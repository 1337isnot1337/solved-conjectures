# Independent review of the continuum rainbow-star theorem

On 27 November 2026 UTC a separate Codex reviewer in WadeDavis’s research session checked Cinna Davis’s original Problem 6 proof, the balanced-biclique strengthening and the connected abelian Cayley construction. All three written arguments pass. Earlier independent review covered the first two results. The finite checks exercise the constructions. The infinite-cardinal conclusions use the written proofs. [Earlier independent review](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1021#issuecomment-7219208826)

## Statement and cardinal transfer

The primary source fixes one edge labelling before quantifying over all countable vertex colourings. The witness must be one vertex incident to all labels in its own colour class. Problem 6 asks for a continuum-sized graph in ZFC for every prescribed finite bound on odd-cycle lengths. The paper credits its own triangle-free case, the proof using CH and the unconditional example on the successor of the continuum. Its unstarred property permits edges of different labels at different vertices. [Definition 6.3, p. 448](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=24) · [Problem 6 and prior cases, p. 462](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=38)

With each template edge oriented once, its target assigns a label at most once to any prescribed pair of sites. The number of nonempty incoming representative sets of size at most $`\mu`$, together with their label functions, is at most $`\lambda^\mu=\lambda`$. Repeating each code at $`\lambda`$ distinct sites therefore fits in the fibre. If every fibre had a colour missing among its incoming fibres, choosing one such colour at every vertex would properly colour every template edge. A template of chromatic number greater than $`\mu`$ consequently has one fibre whose entire colour profile occurs on the incoming side. Choosing one representative per colour gives a single admissible code against all their proposed deficient labels. Its $`\lambda`$ copies cannot all belong to the deficient sets because $`\mathop{\mathrm{cf}}(\lambda)>\mu`$, by König’s theorem for infinite $`\mu`$. The inequality also holds for every positive finite $`\mu`$.

For $`\lambda=2^\mu`$, the classical Specker template has chromatic number $`\mu^+`$, at most $`\lambda`$ vertices and the promised odd-girth bound. Projection sends an odd cycle to an odd closed walk of the same length, which contains an odd cycle no longer than that walk. The star property excludes any proper $`\mu`$-colouring. Projection gives the matching upper bound on chromatic number. [Definition 8.2 and Lemma 8.3](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=32)

## Exclusions and the Cayley witness

In a well-ordered template each site has at most $`\mu`$ earlier neighbours. If a side of a $`K_{\mu^+,\mu^+}`$ had $`\mu^+`$ vertices in one fibre, every opposite vertex would be earlier, exceeding the cap at any site in that fibre. Both sides therefore project to $`\mu^+`$ distinct template vertices. Their images are disjoint because fibres are independent. In the Specker template, regularity permits the first free coordinate of each side to exceed all fixed coordinates of both sides. Placing the free coordinates of one side entirely before those of the other, then reversing the order, forces each fixed-prefix length to equal both $`n^2`$ and $`n^2+n`$. They are distinct for $`n\ge1`$.

In the Cayley graph, the singleton basis vectors induce a labelled copy of the original witness. Every generator determines one edge label. A closed odd walk gives an odd multiset of generating edges with even incidence at every vertex of the original graph. The edges of odd multiplicity form a finite Eulerian subgraph with an odd number of edges and thus contain an odd cycle. In a putative infinite biclique, two vertices on one side cannot differ in four coordinates, since their common neighbours would choose two coordinates from a fixed four-element set. After translation, the nonzero vertices on that side are an infinite pairwise-intersecting family of two-sets. They have a common coordinate, which must occur on the opposite side as well. Removing it recovers an equally large biclique in the original graph.

Component translations preserve labels. A colouring of the zero component avoiding the required star would translate to every component and contradict the embedded original witness. The one-colour case gives the lower bound on component cardinality. A proper colouring of the original graph induces a homomorphism to the Boolean group on its colour set, giving the chromatic upper bound. Finite cubelike hulls and their homomorphism properties are prior. Here the construction preserves the rooted labels, cardinal multiplicities and infinite balanced-biclique exclusion together. [Beaudou, Naserasr and Tardif](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.00776) · [Cores of cubelike graphs, §4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2020.103092)
