# Source and proof audit

## Exact target and credit

Luis Ferroni, Roberto Pagaria and Lorenzo Vecchi, [The Poincaré polynomial of the type B analogue of the moduli space of stable pointed rational curves](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01956), [version 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01956v3), 16 November 2026. The target is Conjecture 4.13, for every rank. The paper supplies the type B space and its actual degree-`n−1` polynomials, as well as the compositional series used in the proof. It reports finite evidence through rank 300. This proof does not rely on that evidence.

Gergely Bérczi and Young-Hoon Kiem, [Real-Rootedness of the Poincaré polynomials of the moduli spaces of stable pointed rational curves: an AI-assisted proof](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.29151v2), proved the separate type A theorem. Their deformation and crossing argument is the model for the final step here. Their degree bound for the type A deformation does not apply to the cubic-degree type B deformation. The type B proof finds an auxiliary function with one fewer rational pole and uses its derivative to bound the real zeros that the type B deformation can have.

Written discovery by Cinna Davis's repository assistant (Lyra), with the initial derivative deformation developed during a background research pass. Original problem, geometric setup and generating-function inputs are credited to Ferroni, Pagaria and Vecchi; the moving-root framework is credited above. No external endorsement or peer review is implied.

## Proof audit

- **Source, signs and scaling.** Starting from Theorem 4.15's first composition directly, expand at its series parameter zero, where the left argument has derivative one. At the real slice, the source's normalization gives a factor of `2^n`, not `1`, when converting the analytic derivative to the polynomial. The formula for `X_q` differs from the composition argument by its value at `y=1`, so all differentiation is unchanged.
- **Algebra.** Apply the chain rule in the decreasing coordinate and differentiate the numerator factors directly. The leading cubic-step multiplier in the original deformation is positive; no derivative is identically zero. For the auxiliary numerator, the two coefficients reaching the same parity slot agree in sign. This avoids any claim that the original cubic coefficient operator is variation diminishing on arbitrary inputs; it is not.
- **Counting with multiplicities.** The factor `x^{n+1}` adds a fixed zero at the origin. Count separately on each open half-line so that its artificial multiplicity is never counted. Transfer any actual multiplicity at zero instead using the first identity in (6). On the faster tail, the weighted function tends to zero, yielding one extra interior critical point even when that half-line has no original zero. This is the improvement needed for every nonsymmetric parameter. At the symmetric parameter the even numerator terms vanish; no tail vanishing of the weighted function is assumed.
- **All ranks and crossings.** Fixed-parameter positive roots are simple by matching lower and upper counts; the ordered roots are globally continuous on the positive parameter interval. The uniform small-parameter root bound follows from the nonzero limiting leading coefficient. At the other end compact-uniform convergence to a nonzero constant excludes bounded roots. Degree counts force one crossing per branch, so interlacing does not require monotonicity of the branches.
- **Reproducible cross-check.** `python3 verify.py` expands the original compositional identity independently with exact rational arithmetic. It compares 132 source-series coefficients and checks the pole-cancelling identity and the auxiliary coefficient sign pattern at 352 rank/parameter pairs. This is a transcription check, not finite evidence substituted for the universal argument. No Lean or external referee check has been done.
