# Review of the uniform packing cutoff

Part I, §7 item 8 asks whether five occurrences distinguish any pair of patterns with different packing enumerations, or whether a small bound depending on pattern length suffices. Its examples involve overlaps up to two less than pattern length. The new construction rules out every absolute bound, including five, with equal pattern lengths and equal overlaps. It gives a linear lower bound on a length-dependent cutoff. The question about a useful upper bound depending on length remains open. [Primary question, item 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29846v1#page=45)

I independently reconstructed the poset imposed by literal windows. At each shift three letters move, one in each chain, so all comparisons are generated by the three increasing chains and the two cross-chain endpoint conditions in each window. Removing the forced initial and final segments leaves two ballot posets. Before the overlap between these posets reaches two middle-chain vertices their extensions concatenate uniquely, including when there is a single shared endpoint.

At the first overlap containing two shared vertices, at most one free letter from each poset can enter the gap between them. If both do so there are exactly two interleavings, because neither poset constrains one free letter relative to the other. Exactly $`C_{j-1}`$ extensions of each poset put its free letter there: in one block the last pair is forced, and in the other the first pair is forced. Thus the extra contribution is $`C_{j-1}^2`$. Shifting the middle-chain length by one gives the claimed first disagreement for arbitrary $`L`$. This proves a family of delayed disagreements. [Proof](PROOF.md)

The exact checker builds the ordering constraints directly from the original windows and counts linear extensions. Its default replay passed $`2\le L\le8`$ and every occurrence count through the first disagreement. At $`L=5`$, the sixth counts were 19,188 and 17,424. I also compared the source’s packing definition, its Catalan special case, the companion paper and the separately published counterexamples to the packing-maximum conjecture. The latter work explicitly excludes item 8 from its claim. The indexed and exact-phrase searches located no earlier proof that an absolute cutoff fails.
