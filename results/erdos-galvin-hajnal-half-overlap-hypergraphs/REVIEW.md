# Independent mathematical review

I reviewed the four files at `5ff4e08a9054b1fa5f9e7dccae88c9c43cd0f798`. The rectangle proof answers the entire original ZFC question affirmatively. The threshold and simultaneous-width arguments also pass. This is an independent written review within the repository.

**Target and prior results.** The scanned original on printed p. 506 asks about every integer 3 ≤ n < ω. The object has 2n-element edges and intersection at most n. The original counts edges. The construction has both edge count and support size exactly aleph-two. The n = 2 example is already in original Theorem 15.15. The original boundary under GCH is in Theorem B(b)/Corollary 12.4, and under Martin’s axiom the incompatible multiplicity conclusion is Theorem 5.6. The impossible range also follows directly from the two closure chains. Both parameter choices in Problem 12, the (8,5) and (7,4) cases, fall in the proved range. [EGH, pp. 427, 445, 485, 506, 509](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=82)

**Pair coloring and collisions.** Shelah’s Theorem 1.1 at aleph-two applies in ZFC. Applying it to pairwise disjoint singleton blocks enumerating any set of aleph-two rows realizes every prescribed pair color there. Composing with the surjection onto the finite sets of columns preserves this property. Each countable vertex coloring supplies a monochromatic column set of the desired size in each row. There are at most aleph-one choices for its ordered data consisting of the color and the column set, so one choice persists on aleph-two rows. The chosen rectangle for the correctly labeled pair is then monochromatic. Distinct edges project to distinct two-element row sets, hence can collide in at most one row and in at most the smaller width of columns. The column coloring attains the upper bound. At all regular successors the actual needed square-bracket input also follows from Rinot–Todorčević, Theorem 2.6, with both large sets equal. Their first page credits Todorčević’s earlier ordinary-square theorem. [Shelah, Theorem 1.1](https://shelah.logic.at/files/198837/572.pdf#page=3) · [Rinot–Todorčević, pp. 119–121](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/89041)

**Boundary checks and extensions.** Trimming one vertex from an even rectangle at width at least two preserves each row in the projection and every column of its label. This gives distinct odd edges and preserves the column upper bound, including uniformity three. For each finite edge, its last layer in a continuous closed chain exists, and at most the permitted overlap many vertices can sit below that layer. A second such argument within that layer puts at least two vertices of every edge into a single injectively colored difference in the impossible range. Reusing a common palette across differences is legitimate. In the all-width construction the fixed vertex color occurs infinitely often in every selected row even for a singular column cardinal when the palette is smaller. Fixing the finite set of columns separately for each width and deleting fewer than the successor cardinal of prior row endpoints permits the advertised disjoint recursion.

**Source corrections incorporated in publication.**

Their Corollary 3.3 has precisely the half-overlap parameters and a large *conflict-free* chromatic number. In the equal-width case of their Theorem 3.1 the edges are unions of two whole finite blocks. For block size at least two, assigning two colors in every block properly colors every edge. Their Lemma 3.4/Corollary 3.5 already uses an onto pair coloring to make odd-width examples for the conflict-free question. The new step forces one *ordinary monochromatic* rectangle with the chosen common column set. Their Proposition 1.1(3) also exhibits the distinction between the two chromatic numbers directly.
