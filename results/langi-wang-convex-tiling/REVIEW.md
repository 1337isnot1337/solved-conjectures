# Review of the disk-window tiling theorem

## Statement and source

The window is a closed Euclidean disk centered at the origin. A tile contributes only when the disk contains the whole tile. The hypotheses are a common positive lower bound on inradius and a common upper bound on the circumradius-to-inradius ratio. They allow arbitrarily large tiles. The lower and upper limits of the mean are separate questions. The journal permits straight angles at genuine tiling junctions. [Definition 2.1 and Question 5.1 in the journal](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2026/1/rnaf372/8408215) · [Question 5.6 in arXiv v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10622v4#S5)

Cinna Davis recorded the [original proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/6be048195c8960380060d5697ed8154173a2cedf) and the [logarithmic-density supplement](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/b4dc8280eae649dc2ab44fc86c993fce88bce438). On 26 November 2026, separate Codex reviewers audited the mathematics and checked the source and prior literature. This was an internal review. There is no Lean formalization or external referee report. [Literature check](LITERATURE.md) · [Tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/361)

## What the review checked

- **Lower mean.** Shrinking a tile about its contact with a bounded disk provides a disjoint inball in a larger bounded disk. This proves local finiteness before the proof uses polygonal sides. The planar dual graph counts all junction-subdivided sides, which also bounds the geometric side count. Cauchy–Schwarz on the packed annulus bounds the number of tiles intersecting a typical circle. The quadratic upper bound on the number of contained tiles provides arbitrarily large annuli with controlled growth, so the outside-neighbor count is negligible along a sequence of disk radii. This also gives a lower bound of one quarter, up to a vanishing error, on the fraction of low-sided tiles along this sequence. [Lower circular average](PROOF.md#lower-circular-average)
- **Unbounded upper mean.** The four wedges retain the actual outer polygon edges and each contains its stated disk. For angular width $`\Delta\le\pi/4`$, the outer polygon inradius exceeds the inner circumradius by at least $`r\Delta`$. Each convex transition quadrilateral contains a disk of radius $`r\Delta/4`$ and has diameter at most $`5r\Delta`$. The next square strictly contains the test disk. At the test radius every transition tile extends outside the disk, and only the old tiles and four wedges count. Choosing the polygon size after the old tile count is known gives one tiling with infinite upper limit. The argument already works with geometric sides. [Construction](PROOF.md#infinite-upper-circular-average)
- **Exceptional radii.** For each fixed multiplicative enlargement, only a bounded number of large outside neighbors fit in the enlarged disk. The remaining neighbors appear in the increment of the contained-tile count. Integrating the difference of the shifted log counts bounds the exceptional upper logarithmic density by a constant tending to zero with the enlargement. [Logarithmic-density proof](LOG-DENSITY.md)
- **Convex minimax consequence.** A diameter chord and transverse width supply an inscribed triangle. A bounded perimeter-squared-to-area ratio gives the required fatness and inradius bounds. The polygonal isoperimetric inequality applies to a low-sided tile and the regular hexagonal tiling attains the resulting supremum. The corollary is for the Euclidean convex class. [Minimax argument](PROOF.md#convex-minimax-isoperimetric-corollary)
- **Corrected normed-plane remark.** An earlier draft inferred a bound on the lower average isoperimetric ratio from the lower average side count. The source makes a different implication. A lower side-count average at most six gives a lower bound on the **upper** average isoperimetric ratio. The paper requires an upper side-count average at most six to bound the **lower** average isoperimetric ratio in this way. The published proof uses the former implication. [Journal §5, Remark 5.1](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2026/1/rnaf372/8408215) · [Preprint §5, Remark 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.10622v4#S5)

## Reproducible geometry check

Run `python3 check_sectors.py` in this directory. The checker constructs a 512-sided stage in floating-point coordinates. Across its four wedges, seven coarsening rings and 1,016 sectors, it checks the area sums, convexity, supporting-line clearance of the proposed inballs, diameter bound and disk-membership decisions. Normal and optimized Python produced the same counts. Inflating the claimed inball, shrinking the radial step and reversing a half-edge each caused the optimized checker to fail in separate mutation tests. The analytic inequalities cover every stage size. [Coordinate checker](check_sectors.py)
