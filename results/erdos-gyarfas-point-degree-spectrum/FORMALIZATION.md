# A linear deficit in the point-degree spectrum

For a nontrivial finite linear space on $n$ points, write $f$ for the number of different point degrees. Lean proves $17f+13\le16n$ for every such space. It also constructs the extremal function $f_3(n)$ as the attained maximum over labelled spaces and proves that $n-f_3(n)$ eventually exceeds $C\sqrt n$ for every real constant $C$. This answers Gyárfás’s Section 3, Problem 2 negatively. [Abstract bound](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumBound.lean) · [Extremal function](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumExtremal.lean) · [Original paper, Section 3](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/101_Gyarfas_ErdosProblemsOnIrregularitiesOfLineSizesAndPointDegrees.pdf).

For any finite set of at least three points in the real affine plane, the same inequality holds when each degree counts distinct real lines spanned through that point. Lean constructs each line as a mathlib affine span, proves that its intersection with the finite set determines it uniquely through a chosen point, and also covers collinear sets. This gives a positive answer to Section 3, Problem 3, with a missed proportion of at least $1/17$. [Real linear space](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumPlane.lean) · [Equality of the line counts](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumRealDegrees.lean) · [Answers to both problems](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumTheorems.lean).

The proof counts incidences and ordered pairs in two disjoint sets of equal size. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the resulting degree deficit. Selecting representatives of the lowest and highest degrees and summing their gaps gives the constant 17. [Incidence identities](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumIncidence.lean) · [Two-set inequality](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumComparison.lean) · [Ordered representatives](../../lean/Conjectures/ErdosGyarfasSpectrumRepresentatives.lean).
