# Source, priority and verification

## Target and scope

András Gyárfás, *Erdős Problems on Irregularities of Line Sizes and Point Degrees*, Bolyai Society Mathematical Studies 11 (2002), pp. 367–373, MR1954734. Section 3 begins with the extremal problem of Erdős and Makai (1992). The sentence immediately preceding Problems 2 and 3 attributes both questions to Paul Erdős and the author. The printed inequality in Problem 2 on p. 371 is a **lower** bound $f_3(n)\ge n-cn^{1/2}$. Its setting includes abstract linear spaces, as defined on p. 367. Problem 3 asks for a positive proportional deficit in the real plane. The new proof answers both signs in one theorem. It leaves the best constant in the extremal problem open. [Primary paper, §§1 and 3](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/101_Gyarfas_ErdosProblemsOnIrregularitiesOfLineSizesAndPointDegrees.pdf) · [Author’s bibliography, item 101](https://www.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/index_files/publications_2000_2009.htm)

The two-set inequality and its consequence were developed in Cinna Davis’s repository during the independent audit of the adjacent point-degree problems. The same source’s Problem 4 minimizes the maximum multiplicity of any individual degree. Its rational example supplies a lower bound of $3n/4+O(1)$ different degrees for both the planar and abstract extremum. The upper bound proved here applies to all spaces and answers a different conjecture. [Earlier construction](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/43152d525a05947dff68a735c5298ac8a8a3dfae/results/gyarfas-planar-degree-repetition/PROOF.md) · [Discovery issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/788)

## Bounded search, 26 November 2026

- The 2002 paper’s Theorem 3 excludes at least a constant times $\sqrt n$ degrees and leaves open whether this order of deficit can be achieved. Its argument uses a single point of minimum degree. Our proof uses two whole sets of point degrees. [Gyárfás, pp. 370–371](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/101_Gyarfas_ErdosProblemsOnIrregularitiesOfLineSizesAndPointDegrees.pdf)
- Erdős, Duke, Fowler and Phelps count the number of possible degree sets and multisets *over all* pairwise balanced designs of a fixed order. Exponentially many possible sets are compatible with every individual spectrum omitting a fixed proportion of the degrees. Their near-exact bound for the number of distinct *block sizes* in one design concerns a different statistic. [Erdős–Duke–Fowler–Phelps, pp. 53–54 and §2](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1985-12.pdf)
- In the real plane, Lund, Pham and Thu’s Theorem 1.9 bounds exceptional centers with few radial directions when no line contains a sufficiently large fraction of the point set. This is a prior result relevant to Problem 3. Its geometric hypotheses exclude arbitrary abstract finite linear spaces, the setting of Problem 2. The abstract proof uses neither a realization theorem nor a crossing-number inequality. [Lund–Pham–Thu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07431)
- Searched the source title, authors with and without accents, MR number, “distinct point degrees”, “degree sets” and “degree spectrum” for finite linear spaces, “replication numbers” for pairwise balanced designs, number of lines through an atom of a rank-three matroid, and dual arrangement formulations. Checked web and arXiv records, the author’s bibliography, and title searches of OpenAlex, Crossref and Semantic Scholar. The article’s bibliographic indexing is incomplete. Its author-hosted printed source was used to verify the inequality signs. Searches of both repository branches, issues and pull requests found the separate planar repetition result, the block-size questions, and graph conjectures involving the same authors. [Author’s publication list](https://www.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/index_files/publications_2000_2009.htm) · [Coordinating issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/156#issuecomment-7188055856)

These checks located no prior proof of a linear deficit in the abstract setting. They establish the extent of the search. They cannot exclude unpublished or differently indexed work.

## Exact finite check

`python3 results/erdos-gyarfas-point-degree-spectrum/verify.py` enumerates every labelled nontrivial linear space on three through seven points. It chooses every possible family of lines of size at least three with disjoint pair sets, then fills all uncovered pairs with two-point lines. This produces each labelled space exactly once, including non-realizable spaces such as the seven-point Fano plane. Degrees are counted from the lines. All 8,778 systems satisfy the theorem. For orders up to five it also checks (1) for all disjoint nonempty equal-sized sets. For orders six and seven it checks the extremal representatives whenever at least two degrees occur. The total is 10,315 two-set comparisons. The all-order conclusion follows from the written proof. [Checker](verify.py) · [Proof](PROOF.md)
