# Scope, sources and verification

## Published statement

Foster Tom and Aarush Vailaya's [journal paper](https://doi.org/10.37236/14403), *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* 33(2), #P2.9, was published April 14, 2026. [Conjecture 74, printed page 30](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v33i2p9/pdf/#page=30) asks for the ascent-weighted trace of the forest-triple matrix after identifying vertices 1 and n of a natural unit interval graph. It retains directed edge multiplicities. The same statement is [Conjecture 5.16 in arXiv:2503.19344](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.19344#page=29). The journal paper proves the ordinary trace at q = 1 (Theorem 60) and reports checking the weighted conjecture through eight vertices.

The proof uses the exact published matrix of Definition 21. Propositions 22, 23 and 37 give the one-vertex, path-edge and complete-graph test gadgets; Proposition 25 bounds the diagonal by a statement about empty forest index sets. Propositions 27 and 29 give the evaluation and gluing rules. The original closure domain in Definition 58 is n ≥ 2. For n = 1 the infinite identity has no ordinary trace, and there are no two endpoints to identify. No connectedness assumption is required. If the endpoints are adjacent, the resulting loop rules out every proper coloring; the color matrix diagonal is zero as well. Opposite directed arcs are not collapsed to one arc.

## Prior work checked

On November 24, 2026, searches by both conjecture numbers, title, authors, and phrases involving weighted forest-triple traces found no earlier proof of this exact identity. The author’s October 26 revision of [*Proper circular arc graphs are e-positive*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.33124v3#page=20), §8, still refers to Conjecture 74 as the single-vertex q-trace question and poses the larger-clique extension. Its proved multiple-boundary trace uses q = 1. Tom and Vailaya’s [Tutte symmetric matrix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27129) concerns a different parameter that weights equal-colored endpoints; it does not prove the ascent-weighted trace. Their earlier test-gadget matrix comparison in §4 is credited. This search is not proof of priority.

## Proof checks

The internal review checked the reversal of transfer-matrix product order, both reflections preserving the final weighted function, arbitrary labeled natural unit interval graphs including disconnected inputs, the indexed forest diagonal vanishing, and the finite-variable cutoff. In particular the single-edge transfer can only increase a state above the cutoff: the only reset term involves an elementary symmetric function that vanishes at that cutoff. Hence no infinite-matrix term can re-enter the tested finite corner. The paired rank argument is proved by Vandermonde triangularity at q = 1, before passing to the rational function field. See [issue #338](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/338) for coordination.

The separate standard-library checker compares two direct enumerations, without using the transfer argument: it enumerates the signed forest triples in the published matrix definition and proper colorings of the directed multigraph closure, counting arcs with multiplicity. The default run checks all naturally labeled unit interval graphs on two through six vertices with q = 1, 2, 5 and distinct positive integer variable values. It makes 585 exact integer comparisons, including loops, disconnected inputs, digons and asymmetric graphs. Finite tests do not establish the universal result. There is no Lean formalization or outside review.
