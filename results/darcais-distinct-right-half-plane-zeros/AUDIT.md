# Scope and independent audit

The exact question is whether the right-half-plane zero values themselves form an infinite set in [Charlton–Heim–Neuhauser–Stumpenhusen–Tröger, arXiv:2608.18842v2, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18842v2#S4). Their paper already refutes the Hurwitz conjecture and gives positive density of non-Hurwitz indices. This proof gives the inverse-index scale and thus infinitely many distinct values. It does not claim another refutation or locate the first counterexample.

Independent internal review checked the divisor prefix and second-moment bounds, the fixed-order convolution limit, local uniform convergence and order at most one half, exact zeta constants, and the reciprocal Hurwitz minor of the genus-zero approximants. The strict sign forces an open-right-half-plane root. Rouché's theorem transfers it on the prime-index subsequence. The density enhancement uses Markov's inequality on multiples of the fixed integer and compactness with a uniform strict minor. The fixed convolution asymptotic is credited to Starr and its earlier sources.

Exact searches found related results on polynomial indices and open statements of this distinct-values question, not an earlier resolution. No priority, Lean verification, or outside refereeing is claimed.
