# Partial Lean verification: Nuttall’s conjecture

[Lean module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallThetaKernel.lean) · [Written proof](PROOF.md) · [John Nuttall, equation (1.3) and §4.5](https://publish.uwo.ca/~jnuttall/det3CAEXP_2.pdf)

Lean defines the exact summand at source index `n+1` and sums over all natural numbers. Every summand is positive for nonnegative real arguments. A summable geometric majorant verifies absolute convergence, continuity on the nonnegative half-line, and a uniform exponential decay bound for the kernel itself.

For positive cumulant order `m = n+1`, Lean uses the written proof’s single-integral formula with weight `v^n / n!`. It proves that the integrand is integrable and positive on the open half-line and that the resulting cumulant is strictly positive at every nonnegative argument. This covers the order-one minor of every cumulant kernel in that formulation. Index zero is the literal theta kernel.

The [literal iterated-tail module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallRepeatedTails.lean) separately uses the source recursion `Ψ₀ = Φ`, `Ψₘ₊₁(u) = ∫ t in (u, ∞), Ψₘ(t)` and checks that all iterates are integrable, continuous, strictly positive, and bounded by the same exponential majorant on the half-line. Its order-one endpoint requires only nonnegative arguments.

The [first-mode module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallThetaTail.lean) proves the undifferentiated part of §3. Set $y=\pi e^{4t}$ and $F_0=2\pi^2 e^{9t-y}$. The first term is exactly $F_0(1-3/(2y))$. For every nonnegative argument the remaining terms sum to a nonnegative amount at most $C F_0 e^{-3y}$ for an explicit finite constant $C$. Therefore $\Phi/F_0\to1$, and for all nonnegative arguments Lean also checks $\Phi(t)\le 2\pi^2(1+C)e^{-e^{2t}}$ (the order-zero case of the bound used in §3). Only the two formula definitions gained visibility to the new module; the underlying definitions and proofs were not changed.

The [individual-term derivative module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallThetaTermDerivatives.lean) sets $z=(n+1)^2\pi e^{4t}$ and represents the $j$th derivative of the $n+1$st theta summand by $e^{t-z}P_j(z)$. It checks $P_0(X)=2X^2-3X$ and $P_{j+1}=P_j+4X P_j'-4X P_j$, with exact degree $j+2$ and leading coefficient $2(-4)^j$. These statements hold for every real argument and every order.

The [termwise series module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallThetaTermSeries.lean) proves local uniform summability of the differentiated modes on bounded real intervals. The literal theta series can therefore be differentiated termwise in every order at all real arguments. For each fixed order $j$, Lean checks a bound $|\Phi^{(j)}(t)|\le C_j e^{-e^{2t}}$ for all $t\ge0$. It also bounds the difference from the $j$th derivative of the first summand by $C'_j e^{t-2y}$, with $y=\pi e^{4t}$. This is enough to isolate the exponentially smaller scale of every fixed-order tail, not a formalization of the higher Wronskian asymptotics.

The [literal tail calculus module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallTailCalculus.lean) proves continuity and integrability on every real tail and the exact identity $\Psi_{m+1}'(u)=-\Psi_m(u)$. Lean proves both index ranges: $D^j\Psi_{m+j}=(-1)^j\Psi_m$ and $D^{m+j}\Psi_m=(-1)^m D^j\Phi$, including the endpoint $u=0$. These identities use the literal recursive tails, not the weighted formula.

The [weighted-moment module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallTailMoment.lean) verifies every moment $\int_0^\infty v^k\Psi_m(u+v)\,dv=k!\,\Psi_{m+k+1}(u)$ for nonnegative $u$ and arbitrary natural $k,m$. It uses exact translation for the base case and integration by parts with checked integrability and both boundary limits. At $m=0$ this identifies the weighted formula with the literal recursion for every cumulant order, including order zero and the endpoint $u=0$.

The [differentiated-moment module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallDifferentiatedMoment.lean) proves $\int_0^\infty v^k D^j\Phi(u+v)\,dv=k!\,D^j\Psi_{k+1}(u)$ for all natural $k,j$ and nonnegative $u$, by signed integration by parts and the checked derivative decay. Applying the index shift gives the exact entry used in §4: if $m=k+1+i$, then $D^{i+j}\Psi_m(u)=(-1)^i\int_0^\infty v^k D^j\Phi(u+v)\,dv/k!$.

The [Andréief module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallAndreief.lean) proves the determinant identity for any finite family of functions whose cross products are integrable under a common sigma-finite measure. It expands both permutation sums, applies finite-product integration, and sums the column-permutation signs. Applied to the power and derivative matrices, it gives the exact §4 determinant integral with factorial and row prefactors. The [Vandermonde module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallVandermonde.lean) simplifies the power determinant and proves the two reversal signs cancel, leaving the Vandermonde and the product of the powers of the integration variables. The signed integral comparison and the deduction of the higher cumulant signs remain unchecked. No implication for the separate RH-equivalent problem is claimed. John Nuttall proposed the conjecture; the written proof’s original provenance and priority caveat remain unchanged.

The [Hankel polynomial module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallHankelPolynomial.lean) verifies the polynomial calculus used in §3. For size $s+1$, let $A$ be the Hankel determinant of the first-mode derivative polynomials, $B$ the determinant with the last column shifted by one, and $C$ the determinant with both the last row and last column shifted. With $\delta=4X\,d/dX$, Lean checks the exact identity
$AC-B^2=A\delta^2 A-(\delta A)^2-16(s+1)XA^2$.
If $A\ne0$, the right side has degree $2\deg A+1$ and leading coefficient $-16(s+1)\operatorname{lc}(A)^2$. The [adjacent-minor module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallHankelAdjacent.lean) applies a Schur complement over the fraction field and descends the determinant identity to polynomials. Lean checks the row and column reindexing and proves $A_{n+2}A_n=A_{n+1}C_n-B_n^2$ whenever $A_n\ne0$. The identity then proves by induction that every $A_n$ is nonzero, has degree $2n+\binom{n}{2}$, and has leading coefficient of sign $(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}}$. The normalized polynomial evaluations tend to their leading coefficients. The [full-kernel transfer module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallHankelTransfer.lean) divides each derivative by the common positive factor $E=e^{t-y}$. The difference from its first-mode polynomial is negligible at every inverse polynomial order in $y$. The determinant expansion preserves this approximation, including cancellations between the highest entrywise terms. Thus $	au_n(\Phi;t)/(E^n y^{2n+\binom{n}{2}})$ tends to the nonzero leading coefficient of $A_n$. Lean verifies the resulting strict sign $(-1)^{\binom{n}{2}}$ for the full kernel beyond a common threshold for all sizes up to any fixed $r$. This is not the extended-Chebyshev argument for distinct nodes or the higher cumulant sign theorem.

The [scalar tail comparison module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallTailDominance.lean) checks two estimates independently of the distinct-node sign. For $m\ge s\ge1$ and $\log m\ge1$, the moment majorant in §5 is integrable and its integral is at most $2(2\log m)^m$. The proof splits at $\log m$ and bounds the tail pointwise by $e^{-v}$. Lean also checks that the scalar ratio in §6 tends to zero for any fixed exponent and nonnegative exponential and ratio constants, even after multiplication by a fixed prefactor. This does not yet bound the actual signed determinant integrals by these scalar expressions.

The [uniform derivative-ratio module](../../lean/Conjectures/NuttallDerivativeRatio.lean) checks (14): for each finite order bound $r$, one constant and one threshold work for all $1\le j\le r$. The error in $\Phi^{(j)}(t)/\Phi(t)-(-4y)^j$ is bounded by $C y^{j-1}$, where $y=\pi e^{4t}$. It also checks that the full kernel divided by $e^{t-y}$ is at least $y^2$ when $t\ge0$ and $y\ge3$. The determinant lower bound on the moving box is not part of this module.
