# Proof, scope and prior-art review

## The statement

The [question following Theorem 2.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-024-00702-5) asks whether, for every strictly convex norm in finite dimension and every infinite set, one coloring with countably infinitely many colors gives every color in every isometric copy of that set. An isometric copy here preserves pairwise distances; an embedding need not extend to an ambient isometry. The counterexample does not answer the separate two-color question. The fixed set in the proof is bounded and countably infinite, as it must be to avoid the paper's positive result for unbounded sets.

## Independent check of the construction

- The tag is the second coordinate of the normalized primary difference and is unaffected by either arc parameter. On the chart for the smaller index, the tag determines the larger index's parameter. The two free directions are independent, and substituting the resulting coefficients makes the squared term zero and gives the exact normalized first coordinate. Both arc parameters can vary freely.
- At zero free coordinates the negative height Hessian has the stated positive quadratic blocks. The determinant and Hessian inequalities are strict for each fixed tag interval, so compactness gives a box of positive width; there is no assumption that the widths have a uniform lower bound. On a shared box, the quartic penalty gives a bound proportional to the squared transverse width, matching the degenerating chart curvature. Across distinct primary boxes, both approximation errors are controlled by the tag gap.
- Each secondary prescription is a Euclidean unit vector with itself as the normal. Its scalar-product gap to any other secondary vector, including a different sign or arbitrarily close index, is exactly half the squared distance. It absorbs the common penalty remainder. Primary and secondary cap axes are orthogonal; opposite primary signs are antipodal. The uniform margin in these cases verifies all ordered support comparisons. At zero the margin is also uniform, so zero is strictly inside the constructed body. Coercivity, symmetry and strict convexity give one strictly convex norm; the supremum construction actually puts each prescribed vector on its boundary.
- All primary independent selections have the same fixed distance matrix. For any proposed coloring, either they can avoid a prescribed color or a whole primary arc has that color. In the latter case the corresponding single secondary copy, with all labels, has a tail in that very arc and hence misses a color. No countability, measurability or invariance hypothesis is imposed on the color classes.

Run `python3 -I -S -B -O verify_dim5.py` from this directory. The standard-library checker passed with the exact source formulas, including the Hessian and secondary scalar-product identities, and rational bounds. It also rejected a flipped height sign and an excessive penalty coefficient. This checks algebra, not the proof's continuum quantifiers. There is no Lean verification or external refereeing.

## Source and novelty checks

The original paper's polygonal-norm argument uses independent selections along segments. The construction here uses short curved arcs and retains strict convexity. The paper's planar power-norm theorem, and the repository's [all strictly convex planes](FINITE-SPHERES.md) and [polynomial norms](POLYNOMIAL-NORMS.md) cases, therefore have distinct scopes. The original paper, subsequent related arXiv and citation records, both upstream branches, [issue #856](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/856), and merged [PR #863](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/863) were checked. In particular Gehér–Sagdeev–Tóth's [canonical theorems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.11454) concern finite configurations, not polychromatic countable copies of one infinite set in this setting. The upstream claim on #856 was released after the two partial proofs. The bounded literature search did not find an earlier resolution of the full statement; it does not certify worldwide priority.
