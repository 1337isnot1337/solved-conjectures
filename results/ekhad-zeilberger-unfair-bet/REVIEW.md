# Independent review of the most unfair bet

On 26 November 2026 two project reviewers using Codex checked Cinna Davis’s written proof against the sponsor’s question, the probability formula and the separate larger-alphabet ranking rule. The proof covers any fixed word length and alphabet size. The supremum here is over word pairs of that length. The conclusion about the win gap holds for all sufficiently long random texts at each fixed choice of parameters. This is internal mathematical review. Sponsor adjudication and external peer review remain outstanding. ## Source and scope

The sponsor first asked for a probability formula for a fixed pair. The separate challenge, posed after receiving such a formula, asks which pair is most unfair for a fair coin. The proposed winner is a run of all but one identical letter followed by a different letter, against the constant word. The sponsor’s Maple help for `Mihai` ranks the leading difference of the two win probabilities. It uses units of $`1/\sqrt{\pi n}`$. The routine returns `FAIL` unless the union of the two words uses every alphabet symbol. For a binary alphabet that condition is automatic for distinct words. For larger alphabets the published proof treats the restricted and unrestricted questions separately. The sponsor offers a donation to the OEIS. [Sponsor’s question and sample rankings](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/mamarim/mamarimhtml/litt.html) · [Original Maple code](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/tokhniot/Litt.txt)

In Janson, Nica and Segert, Theorem 1.1, equation (1.8) gives the leading advantage from the difference between the self-overlaps divided by $`\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}`$. Their equation (4.19) gives $`\sigma^2=2q^{-k}V`$ in the notation of the proof. Substitution gives the claimed scale and winning direction. Both exceptional binary cases have equal self-overlaps. Remark 1.2 covers exact fairness in that case. [Janson–Nica–Segert](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.19035v1#S1) · [Equal-autocorrelation theorem](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~arvind.singh/papers/litt_cr.pdf)

## Extremal argument

- For two nonconstant words, omitting shift one from the self-overlaps gives $`x\le M/q-q^{-k}`$. With no shift-one cross-overlap the variance bound is strict. With such a cross-overlap, equal interiors force a constant interior and two unbordered words. Different interiors give disjoint supports after centering both end letters. A centered endpoint lies outside every other translated window, so the projected contribution is orthogonal to every other window and to the residual on its own window. This proves the uniform variance lower bound, including the binary boundary case.
- For a constant losing word, the two cross-overlaps depend only on the two end runs of the other word. The successive increments of the run sum strictly increase. Equality therefore requires a run of length $`k-1`$ at one end and a different letter at the other. Reversal, relabeling and player exchange give the stated symmetries. Finiteness of the set of pairs at fixed $`q,k`$ gives eventual ordering for the win gap.
- If both words together must use all $`q`$ letters and $`3\le q\le k+1`$, a constant loser leaves at most $`k-q+1`$ end-run letters. Concentrating them at one end attains the bound. For a nonconstant loser, shift-one overlaps in both directions would force alternating words with equal self-overlaps. The remaining competitors fall strictly below the proposed score. The endpoint $`k=q-1`$ is included.
- For $`k+1<q<2k`$, there are exactly $`2k-q`$ repeated positions. A border of this length uses every available identification. The losing word is then periodic with $`q-k`$ distinct residue classes and the other word uses fresh symbols. Competitors with shorter borders fall strictly below it by the geometric-series bound in the proof. At $`q=2k`$ every pair is fair, and at $`q>2k`$ no pair uses the full alphabet.

## Checks and priority

The original integer verifier checked 3,154,226 unordered pairs across 18 alphabet and length choices. An independent enumeration identified words that differ only by a relabeling of letters. It checked 120,259 classes of ordered distinct word pairs of lengths two through five, comparing the exact maximum in each of the 24 possible full-alphabet regimes. These include the eight-, nine- and ten-letter cases. The argument above supplies the general bounds. [Original verifier](verify.py) · [Independent check](check_full_alphabet.py)

The bounded prior-resolution search covered the sponsor’s current challenge, work on Litt’s game and overlapping pattern counts, the fixed-pair asymptotic, exact-fairness results, the length-two analysis and searches for the all-length extremal pair under alternative descriptions. We found no earlier proof optimizing the leading advantage over all word pairs for every length. The fixed-pair probability formula is due to Janson, Nica and Segert. The review makes no claim of exclusive priority. [Fixed-pair theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.19035) · [Segert’s two-letter analysis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.16660)
