# A winning opening on the 14-vertex ladder

Lean proves that Player 1 wins the normal-play dominating-set game on $P_2\square P_7$. This grid has even order and both path orders exceed one, so the theorem refutes Conjecture 24 of Fiscus, Hurlbert, Myzelev and Pence. Its four winning openings are the two vertices in column two and the two in column six, numbering columns from one. [Counterexample and opening classification](../../lean/Conjectures/FiscusGridCounterexample.lean) · [Original conjecture, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03448v2#S5).

A position is the finite set $S$ of vertices already chosen. Play stops as soon as every vertex belongs to $S$ or has a neighbor in $S$. The formal recurrence allows every unchosen vertex as a move before that point. The available number of moves decreases by one at each step. The underlying graph is mathlib’s Cartesian product of its two path graphs. Lean verifies its 14 closed neighborhoods against the coordinate representation. [Game and graph](../../lean/Conjectures/FiscusGridGame.lean) · [Neighborhoods](../../lean/Conjectures/FiscusGridMasks.lean).

The certificate assigns a candidate game value to each of the $2^{14}=16{,}384$ chosen sets. The kernel checks the local minimax equation at every set. An induction then identifies every certified value with the finite-set game on the actual graph. The initial position is winning, and the four stated openings leave the opponent a losing position. [All-position certificate](../../lean/Conjectures/FiscusGridCertificate.lean) · [Inductive correctness proof](../../lean/Conjectures/FiscusGridSound.lean).

Independent research and publication runs each strictly rebuilt and replayed all six Lean modules. Each audit checked 80 declarations, including 60 theorems, ten private declarations and all 15 required endpoints. The separate Python strategy checker replays the hand-written responses to all 13 possible second moves.
