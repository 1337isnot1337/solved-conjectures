## Independent checks

Wade Davis reviewed the author's research manuscripts at commits [Cohen and stick](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/1f63736eadb308d42fba6e300587bfec6aba0fca), [independence](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/fa5f37f7febc9d3a27a6b94dff8be13e7b4101a6), [rainbow equivalence](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/5f789f3575e4cb220b751af3a7fdc1add08c8d91) and [asymptotic dichotomy](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/1f702579).

- A common extension of the two intersecting base pairs is distinct from all three base points. A triple on three such extensions gives seven different edges on six distinct vertices. Closing under the union of all possible distinguished base points forces each lower link of the chosen subset to be a matching. Conversely, closing under all endpoints of the at most two pairs of each color ensures that every collision shares the larger endpoint. These two separate closure arguments establish the equivalence for arbitrary triple systems and arbitrary two-bounded pair colorings.
- The bounded-degree argument uses at most $`2k-3`$ matchings for $`k\ge2`$. Each of its finitely many rainbow refinements preserves the host cardinality. Iterating the common-extension assertion produces distinct triples, uniquely assigned by the earlier group and its spoke and the later vertex. Its constants are uniform in the system, so one can minimize separately for each order. Two spokes and two extensions give a four-cycle containing the link path. The stronger countable-colorability conclusion in the Problem 8/E publication comes from Cinna Davis's earlier, distinct proof.
- In the upper construction choose one earlier vertex first. Among uncountably many later vertices, uncountably many have the same countable enumeration index for it. The strong coloring hits this index on a pair of those later vertices. No top pair determines two triples. Thus the upper example works simultaneously at every finite order. Together with the uniform lower bound this establishes the asserted limit under the positive relation.
- In the Cohen proof the finite initial-segment product is countable atomless forcing. Below a condition forcing an uncountable set, the union of all ground-model forced-in sets is uncountable, so one stronger condition admits an uncountable ground-model subset. Distinct ground reals in that set admit a split with uncountably many reals on each side above the decided levels. Choose an apex above the desired layer and a countably infinite selection on the other side. The decided matching stages use finitely many endpoints. The two required first-difference positions are distinct and fresh, and the remaining matching stage can prescribe both a fresh pair and its layer. A fresh zero similarly excludes a clique in the shadow. If a shared pair had color two, it would have the same apex in both triples, contradicting the matching property. These checks give all five canonical clauses of Theorem 10.5 at this host after adding one Cohen real.
