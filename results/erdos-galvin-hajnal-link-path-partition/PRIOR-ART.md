# Prior-art and proof audit

**Scope.** The target is Problem 8/E on p. 473 of [Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal (1975)](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=49). The positive side uses an uncountable independent set on the specific host cardinal $`\aleph_1`$. It does not assert that the forbidden configuration occurs in every uncountably chromatic triple system on arbitrary cardinals, and it is not a claim about an ordinal of countable order type.

- **Original problem and known direction.** Checked the scans of Definition 10.3, Diagram 4, Corollary 10.6 and Problem 8/E. The original text already gives the counterexample under CH. The link pattern is a path with three edges on four vertices, not an expansion of that path with a separate private point for each edge. The problem asks if ZFC proves the *negative* arrow.
- **Positive model, known input.** The complete 18-page [Todorčević paper](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0) was checked against the [archived AMS scan](https://web.archive.org/web/20250306142100/https://www.ams.org/journals/tran/1983-280-02/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0.pdf#page=13). Section 1, Remark (4), printed p. 715, states the countable rainbow-cover property in a ZFC-relative model. Page 703 also discusses a Problem 8, from the earlier two-author Erdős–Hajnal lists. It asks about partitions of pairs with a countable ordinal alternative. The present 1975 Problem 8/E concerns triples and a fixed five-point system. Sections 1–2 and the bibliography give no earlier application to that triple system. [Abraham–Cummings–Smyth, Theorem 2 and Remark 4](https://www.math.cmu.edu/users/jcumming/papers/polyx_revised_dec2006.pdf#page=11) independently identify the forcing result and give their PFA proof.
- **Related rainbow literature.** [Soukup, *Indestructible colourings and rainbow Ramsey theorems*](https://www.renyi.hu/~soukup/setop/otka61600/rainbow.pdf) studies both directions for pair rainbows and gives negative results consistent with Martin's axiom. Its Galvin CH result concerns bounded pair colorings, not the positive implication for the link-path-free hypergraphs. [Zhang, *Some remarks on uncountable rainbow Ramsey theory*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00649) studies other cardinals and discusses rainbow relations for triple colorings; the use here is a coloring of *pairs* to obstruct each triple of a prescribed 3-graph.
- **Triple partition literature.** [Milner–Prikry (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(91)90336-Z) use a model of Todorčević for a different relation, with a countable order type on the first side and a four-element complete hypergraph on the second. [Reiher (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11223), [Komjáth (2001)](https://zanotowane.pl/190/3900/), and [Hajnal–Komjáth (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-007-6231-2) study obligatory subsystems or related classes. Arbitrarily large hosts of uncountable chromatic number and hosts on $`\omega_1`$ with no uncountable independent set are different quantifiers. [Komjáth’s 2021 problem notes](https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2021.56) treat specific pair/graph problems from the earlier Erdős–Hajnal list, not this question.
- **Searches.** Exact problem-number searches using 8/E and author names, combinations of rainbow/polychromatic partitions with canonical intersections and uncountable 3-graphs, and alternative terminology for the cone or suspension over a four-vertex path did not locate an earlier statement of this transfer or a resolution of exact 8/E. This is a bounded search, not a proof of priority. In particular the full mid-2000s specialist literature was not uniformly accessible.

**Internal proof checks.** The local obstruction is non-induced: any path with three consecutive link edges suffices. Pair-connectedness is defined via finite paths even in infinite components. A pair used in more than one component would join the components. At the four-point component, the optional fourth triple is covered by a second color, and no color spills outside the component. The rainbow theorem is applied to an actual total pair coloring (all repeatedly unused pairs receive separate colors) of an $`\omega_1`$-sized host. The CH model supplies the negative arrow, and the relative-consistency model supplies its negation; no large-cardinal hypothesis is claimed.

**A detail about the bound.** On a $`K_4^3`$ component a single two-bounded pair coloring cannot cover all four triples: each repeated color on distinct intersecting pairs can cover at most one triple, but six pairs can support at most three such colors. If one only has the *countable partition* form of the two-bounded rainbow theorem, it still suffices: split one triple off each $`K_4^3`$ component into a separate triple system; the three remaining triples on each such four-set can be encoded by disjoint pair-groups $`(ab,bc)`$, $`(ac,cx)`$, $`(ax,bx)`$ in the notation of the proof. The split-off triples have disjoint pair shadows. Apply the two-bounded theorem to each system and take the common refinement of the two countable partitions. This observation is not needed for the stated result.

## Further comparisons from independent review

The forbidden configuration is the three-edge tight 3-uniform path on five vertices. Searches also used this name, both common numbering conventions for tight paths, and combinations with uncountable chromatic number, countable rainbow partitions and the first uncountable ordinal.

- Füredi, Jiang, Kostochka, Mubayi and Verstraëte’s Theorem 1.4′ bounds the number of triples in a finite system avoiding this path by two thirds of its pair shadow. It supplies a finite extremal estimate. The theorem above instead needs bounded groups of pairs that detect every triple and a set-theoretic model in which their rainbow classes cover the host. [Theorem 1.4′](https://homepages.math.uic.edu/~mubayi/papers/smalltrunk12-10-2017.pdf#page=9)
- Komjáth’s Theorem 4 uses an uncountable host with no uncountable independent set. The excluded configuration in that theorem is the eight-triple complete tripartite system with three classes of size two. A system can avoid the eight-triple configuration and still contain the five-vertex path. [Komjáth, 2001](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004930100021)
- Cummings’s account in the 2005 Oberwolfach report also gives the countable rainbow partition in Todorčević’s consistency model. Abraham–Cummings–Smyth Remark 4 distinguishes the original mixed-support iteration from their PFA proof. [Oberwolfach, pp. 3155–3156](https://publications.mfo.de/bitstream/handle/mfo/2929/OWR_2005_55.pdf) · [Abraham–Cummings–Smyth](https://www.math.cmu.edu/users/jcumming/papers/polyx_revised_dec2006.pdf#page=11)

The separate sharp-density campaign asks about the entire profile of edge counts of finite vertex sets. Its later rainbow characterization also forces a path when no uncountable independent set exists in a positive-rainbow model: two earlier spokes and two later common neighbors give a four-vertex path in the link. The result here proves countable proper colorability in the countable-partition model and has its own earlier component proof. Problem 8/C and 8/D concern the two different configurations in Diagram 5.
