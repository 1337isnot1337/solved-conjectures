# Primary sources, priority and verification scope

## Conjecture identities

Larsson, Saha and Yokoo study finite impartial vector subtraction on the nonnegative quadrant with ordinary normal play. Definition 56 requires eventual outcome periodicity on every line with positive rational slope and infinitely many lattice points. An admissible horizontal shift must preserve the lattice points of the line. Their Conjecture 57 asserts that some finite game fails this condition. Their row and column periodicity result allows the behavior on the oblique line proved here. The explicit game establishes an existence result. The paper’s separate question about Turing completeness remains open in this work. [2024 journal article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2024.114775) · [Preprint v4, Definition 56 and Conjecture 57](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.12458v4)

In §8 of their 2021 paper, Gurvich and Vyalyi’s Conjecture 2 asserts that the losing positions of every finite difference game with nonnegative subtraction vectors form a semilinear set. The two-dimensional counterexample uses a single invariant move set and the ordinary no-move-loses convention throughout the entire quadrant. Their Conjecture 1 instead asks for polynomial-time recognition. The present theorem makes no complexity lower-bound claim. [Gurvich and Vyalyi, *Algorithms* 14, 71](https://doi.org/10.3390/a14030071)

## Bounded prior-resolution search on 27 November 2026

Larsson and Wästlund use a broader invariant-game framework allowing moves that add matches to some heaps. Gurvich and Vyalyi explicitly note that negative differences are essential in the cited hardness construction. The latter paper also records Raskin’s argument on arbitrary semilinear boundary conditions. Fink constructs a three-dimensional lattice game without a rational strategy in the Guo–Miller framework. His explicit Example 2.5 uses mixed-sign differences, including one whose first two coordinates are 5 and −2. It already realizes a Sierpiński pattern. These precedents give computational constructions in related models. [Larsson and Wästlund](https://arxiv.org/abs/1202.0664) · [Gurvich and Vyalyi, §8](https://doi.org/10.3390/a14030071) · [Fink, Example 2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1883#page=10)

Parfenov and Vyalyi’s May 2026 preprint explicitly reports that the earlier nine-move candidate had no proof of nonsemilinearity. Their own three-dimensional hardness result permits arbitrary boundary data. Faria’s September 2026 abstract considers signed subtraction vectors and still poses a periodicity question. Searches by both author groups, the two numbered statements, nonnegative finite differences, planar normal-play games, nonperiodic rational lines, and semilinear winning and losing sets located no earlier resolution in the exact model. A search of both repository catalogues, issues, review PRs and indexed proof declarations located the contributor’s submission as the first public report of this construction. This is a dated search of the available record.
