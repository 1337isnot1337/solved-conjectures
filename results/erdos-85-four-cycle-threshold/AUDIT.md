# Review of two restrictions on four-cycle-free regular graphs

Erdős #85 asks whether the minimum-degree threshold forcing a four-cycle is eventually nondecreasing with graph order. Walters and collaborators reported a finite drop and gave a sufficient route to further drops through regular four-cycle-free graphs on square orders. The present results constrain those regular candidates. The unrestricted existence and monotonicity questions stay open. [Erdős #85](https://www.erdosproblems.com/85) · [Walters and collaborators](https://zenodo.org/records/23519149)

For the leave-component theorem, two distinct vertices have zero or one common neighbor. This gives the displayed adjacency-matrix identity including its diagonal. The centered component indicator lies in the kernel of the squared adjacency matrix and hence in the kernel of the symmetric adjacency matrix. Every vertex therefore has the same number of neighbors in the component, and this integer equals the component order divided by $`q`$. If the component had order $`q`$, it would be a clique in the leave. The unique neighbor inside that component would be isolated in the graph induced by the neighbors of its base vertex. Each of the other $`q-1`$ neighbors must be matched there, contradicting parity. The same pairing gives the parity constraint on triangles drawn across a component. [Leave-component proof](LEAVE.md)

For the deletion-distance theorem, I checked the path charges for a fixed line separately by edge position. A directed first edge fixes its root and the chosen added neighbor. A directed middle edge fixes its root by line intersection after the center of the root line is excluded. Distinct last internal vertices bound the final-edge charges by the deleted degree of the chosen endpoint. Low-degree endpoints and singleton groups together discard at most $`12|D|/q+12`$ roots. Two retained roots sharing a hub give two secant pencils, and a pair of their lines determines at most one off-line point. This gives the square-root lower bound on their number of secants. After the vertical, loop and hub exceptions, a removed edge can be charged by at most two different line centers. The inequality at $`q=1024`$ has strict slack and improves as $`q`$ grows.

Every eligible line consequently receives a unique hub with more than three quarters of its neighbors on that line. The hub’s old line is different and becomes eligible because vertices that lost incident edges have to regain degree. This defines an injective self-map of a finite set of lines whose image omits the eligible loop line. The same incidence properties hold in the indicated even-order affine truncations: the absolute points are collinear, the polarity is injective, and the omitted lines constitute one parallel class. This checks the generalization as well as the coordinate model. [Deletion-distance proof](PROOF.md)

The finite-field replay checked 36,080 proposed added pairs, 1,010,164 old simple paths, 400 seeded charges on the loop line, and 332 further line selections, at orders 4, 8, 16 and 32. The proof gives the stated range $`q\ge1024`$. Run `python3 results/erdos-85-four-cycle-threshold/check_affine_paths.py`. The source and literature check included the original Erdős question, the regular-candidate paper and polarity stability theorems. He, Ma and Yang work at the projective order $`q^2+q+1`$ with a different density hypothesis. I found no theorem in those sources giving this affine deletion bound at $`q^2`$ vertices. A stability theorem bringing every candidate close enough to one of these truncations would be an additional hypothesis.
