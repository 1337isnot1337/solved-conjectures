# Scope of the proposed agnostic VC theorem

**Status.** The fixed-pool bounds hold for arbitrary classes and every deterministic pairing. For iid data, Lean proves the two rates when the ordered empirical minimizer is measurable under independent fair labels on the completed training law. Under the literal all-class interpretation with completed-law Markov learners, a VC-one counterexample rules out even the accuracy bound. A second VC-one class on standard Borel spaces admits an ordinary Borel empirical minimizer, yet the randomized learner in this paper fails for every tie-breaking order. A positive theorem for all such regular classes requires a different construction. The published conjecture remains marked partial in this repository.

## What the authors specify

Conjecture 6 asks for one absolute constant and, for every binary class of VC dimension $d\ge1$ and every $n\ge d$, a proper learner, chosen before the distribution, with original CMI at most $cd$ under every iid law and empirical excess at most $c\sqrt{d/n}$ on every sample. Definition 3 uses the actual output random variable, a fair selector and a random supersample. The paper does not prescribe a sigma-algebra on every possible hypothesis class or replace expectation with an outer-expectation convention. [Problem paper, Definition 3 and Conjecture 6](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/steinke20b/steinke20b.pdf)

The framework paper defines mutual information as KL divergence from the product of marginals and conditional information as an expectation over the conditioning variable. Its proof of Lemma 4.15 chooses the first empirical minimizer in a well-order supplied by Lemma 4.17. That proves consistency of tie-breaking. Neither lemma proves Borel measurability of a selector. These sources justify the exchangeability calculation once the integral exists. They leave open whether the authors would accept the finite-pool formulation alone for arbitrary classes. [Framework paper, Definitions 2.6 and 2.8, Lemmas 4.15 and 4.17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09122)

The framework paper's Conjecture 4.18 specifically asks for distribution-free $`\mathrm{CMI}(A)`$, the supremum over fixed paired pools in Definition 1.1, for every $`n\ge1`$. This statement is proved in Lean with common constant $`19{,}712`$. Its fixed-pool information is an ordinary mutual information of a finite channel with the actual hypothesis names as outputs. The COLT version instead specifies $`\mathrm{CMI}_{\mathcal D}(A)`$ under every iid distribution. The framework paper's comparison between the two notions follows by averaging when the ordinary CMI and required joint laws exist. [Framework paper, Definition 1.1 and Conjecture 4.18](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09122v3) · [Lean source](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/FixedPairing.lean)

## Relation to the authors’ empirical risk minimizer

With jointly measurable evaluation, measurability of the particular deterministic minimizer in the framework paper’s Lemma 4.15 suffices. Give a finite tuple the prescribed labels and run that minimizer. The labels are realizable exactly when its returned classifier makes zero mistakes. On that event it is the first realizing hypothesis in the fixed well-order. Evaluating it on a larger tuple or testing membership in a measurable output set gives the other two required events. Lean derives both quantitative estimates from these hypotheses. The minimizer used for selection and the randomized learner satisfying the rates are two different algorithms. [Formal reduction and rate theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrderedERM.lean) · [Framework paper, Lemma 4.15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09122v3)

The source proves existence of this ordered minimizer as a set function. It does not prove that the function is Borel measurable for arbitrary classes. The standard Borel example below even precludes an everywhere Borel exact minimizer, which would have zero empirical excess.

Any measurable empirical minimizer with the source's global consistency property also suffices. For each feasible trace, use its output on the tuple labeled by that trace. Under deletion, the shorter representative lifts to the full trace of that same hypothesis by global consistency. This gives the representative property used in the information proof. The irrational circle-orbit example precludes every measurable globally consistent minimizer, although an ordinary Borel ERM exists. [Written positive theorem and obstruction](PROOF.md#a-measurable-globally-consistent-erm-suffices) · [Companion paper, Definition 4.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09122v3)

## Why the distinction matters

Take a non-Borel subset $B$ of the Borel interval. Let the class consist of the zero function and the indicators of the singletons in $B$. This is a VC-one class of Borel classifiers. Already on a one-point input the event that trace 1 is realizable is $B$. No ordering of the hypotheses can make that event Borel.

There is a stronger test for the interpretation in which a learner must be a Borel Markov kernel and evaluation is jointly Borel. Feed the learner $n$ copies of $(x,1)$. The probability $p(x)$ that its returned hypothesis predicts 1 at $x$ must be Borel by integration of the joint evaluation event against the kernel. Outside $B$ this probability is zero. On $B$ the empirical infimum is zero, so the all-sample accuracy requirement forces $p(x)\ge1-c/\sqrt n>0$ once $n>c^2$. It would follow that $`B=\{p>0\}`$ is Borel. Here the natural parameter space is the disjoint union of a point and $B$ with its trace sigma-algebra, and evaluation is jointly measurable since it uses equality of real numbers. Section 6 of the [manuscript](paper.pdf) proves the same obstruction with **both** spaces standard Borel, using an analytic non-Borel set. Lean proves the necessary measurability condition for any proper Markov kernel satisfying the empirical bound, both VC-one examples, and the existence of the continuous map with non-Borel range. [Kernel obstruction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/Obstruction.lean) · [Unit interval](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/IntervalExample.lean) · [Standard Borel example](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/BorelExample.lean) · [Non-Borel range](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/NonBorelRange.lean)

This obstruction rules out an unrestricted Borel-kernel theorem on arbitrarily prescribed spaces, even if one changes the algorithm. It does not determine which conventions the problem authors intended. The question for them is whether the all-class fixed-pairing theorem, with the iid implication whenever the scalar statistic is measurable, meets the scope of their problem. Independent review and prize adjudication remain open.

The fixed-pool theorem now bounds the information of every pairing separately, and therefore also bounds its upper integral under any iid pool law. Measurability remains necessary to identify this quantity with ordinary CMI of the output random variable. Separately, Lean proves the global Borel obstruction with Borel finite-trace feasibility. The accompanying note gives the usual uniform-convergence rate for these two-point classifiers and a completed-iid learner attaining both rates. [Fixed-pairing proof](PROOF.md#4-information-in-the-returned-hypothesis) · [Borel trace feasibility](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/borel-traces-and-randomized-selection.md)


## Completion of an iid law

Fix a labeled distribution and write $P$ for its feature marginal. The positive theorem uses the same randomized learner for every distribution. For this particular distribution, suppose its prescribed ordered empirical minimizer is measurable at every size on the completion of the experiment with iid features from $P$ and independent fair labels. Conditioning that experiment on any prescribed labeling costs a positive factor $2^{-m}$. Consequently every trace test on a subtuple of distinct pool positions is measurable on the completion of the actual pool law. This gives the completed training kernel, the samplewise empirical bound, and the integral of the actual whole-output conditional KL. [Fixed-label reduction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/IidOrderedERM.lean) · [Rate theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/IidOrderedRates.lean)

The source framework proves its prior worst-case information theorem on fixed supersamples. Its generalization theorem assumes a measurable loss. Neither establishes the above premise about the ordered minimizer. Later work on a related CMI bound explicitly assumes measurability of its least consistent hypothesis and gives a countable domain as one sufficient condition. [Steinke and Zakynthinou, Theorems 4.12 and 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09122v3) · [Haghifam et al., Appendix F, footnote 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.05275#page=17)

Lean also constructs the conditional kernel directly. Drawing an independent fair selector and then running the learner on its selected rows induces the same joint law as first drawing the pool and then using the conditional kernel in the KL formula. [Formal joint law](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/IidConditionalKernel.lean)

In the other direction, a class of distinct Borel indicators on the unit interval with jointly measurable evaluation and measurable output singletons has VC dimension exactly one. Under the fixed law of a uniform feature labeled one, **every** proper Markov learner on the completed iid sample space has a sample with expected loss one and empirical optimum zero, at every positive sample size. Its hypothesis space uses the trace sigma-algebra of a space of codes for countable intersections of open sets. The proof makes no standard Borel assertion about that subspace. A maximal chain of null sets has union of positive outer measure. Two independent successful training draws would force one returned null set to hit the other draw. This has probability zero. The chain construction has precedents in Durst and Dudley and in Campos, Fernandes, and Rodrigues. [Formal counterexample](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/LabeledNullChain.lean) · [Durst and Dudley, Proposition 2.2](https://www.math.uni.wroc.pl/~pms/files/1.2/Article/1.2.2.pdf) · [Campos, Fernandes and Rodrigues, Lemmas 3.1 and 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.30246)

There are two distinct reasons to retain the partial status. The null-chain class already defeats the uniform-convergence baseline used in the problem paper. For this comparison, normalize Lebesgue measure on a measurable hull of the union. Its outer probability and that of each finite product of the union are one, while each hypothesis still has null support. The uniform empirical deviation is therefore one on an outer-probability-one set at every size. A regularity convention that makes that baseline valid can therefore exclude the counterexample. On classes admitted under such a convention, the positive proof still needs a suitable measurable ordered minimizer or another argument for the learner. The published offer covers a proof or disproof of the authors’ conjecture. No award is recorded. [Uniform-deviation theorem for the same chain](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/NullChainUniform.lean)

The Lean-verified finite-support counterexample restores distribution-uniform convergence in outer probability, with expected deviation at most $`3/(2\sqrt n)+m/n`$ for the class with support size $`m`$. Its hypothesis parameter space has the trace Borel sigma-algebra. [Finite-support counterexample](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/completed-finite-groups.md)

The strongest finite-support argument rules out any almost-everywhere measurable scalar expected loss on the exhibited iid law that stays between the empirical optimum and the permitted excess on every sample. The conclusion is independent of the measurable structure imposed on the hypothesis names or the use of a Markov kernel. Jointly measurable evaluation and a completed Markov kernel imply this scalar condition by kernel integration. [Lean theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/CompletedFiniteGroups.lean)

Lean also formulates the disproof directly for a randomized function of the sample and an independent private seed. The empirical bound fails if the realized empirical loss is measurable on the completed joint experiment and has ordinary seed-wise expectations on every fixed sample. On the exhibited iid law all proper hypotheses have population loss one, so the same measurability condition applies to the generalization difference used in the source paper. [Lean randomized-learner formulation](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/CompletedFiniteGroups.lean) · [COLT paper, Theorem 4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/steinke20b/steinke20b.pdf)

The disproof also follows from the measurability of the actual conditional joint law used to compute iid CMI. It permits any pointwise probability measures for the learner's outputs. A kernel on the completed iid pool need only agree almost everywhere with the prescribed conditional joint law of the selector and output. Lean recovers the learner's completed training kernel by evaluating the joint law at the all-zero selector and dropping the independent ghost column. [Lean conditional joint-law obstruction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/ConditionalJointObstruction.lean)

The disproof still applies when the actual conditional law uses an ambient standard Borel space of output codes. Every trace Borel event of the proper class is the restriction of an ambient Borel event, so measurability of the ambient probabilities recovers the proper learner's kernel. This addresses an ambient output convention. The hypothesis subclass itself still lacks a standard Borel parametrization with jointly Borel evaluation. [Lean ambient joint-law obstruction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/ConditionalJointObstruction.lean)

Lean also treats the actual independently seeded random output in Definition 3. Completed measurability of the ambient output on the iid pool, selector and seed already precludes the empirical rate, with ordinary seed expectations on every sample. Restricting the output experiment to the all-zero selector event gives completed measurability on the first column and seed. This version requires no separate conditional-kernel assumption. [Lean seeded-output obstruction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/ConditionalJointObstruction.lean)

A written construction makes the entire proper name space standard Borel and identifiable by countably many prediction queries. It keeps VC dimension one and separate Borel evaluation. A jointly Borel test of the output code matches selected coordinates and almost surely matches no unused coordinates. At empirical tolerance at most $`1/8`$, this gives actual iid CMI at least $`(3/4)n\log 2`$ for the one-level counterexample. A countable-level version fixes both the class and the iid law and forces CMI to diverge at the conjectured empirical rate. Joint Borel evaluation and the sharp uniform-convergence premise of Theorem 2 both exclude these constructions. [Written standard Borel name obstruction](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/standard-borel-output-obstruction.md)

The standard Borel counterexample can also have evaluation jointly measurable in the completion of every Borel law on names and features, including dependent laws. Assign the nonempty sector supports to universally null names, using Grzegorek's theorem and full-outer matchings of the corresponding cardinality. With the atomless iid sector law, a measurable proper output hits no training feature almost surely. Full-outer-measure fitting sets then force a sample with optimum zero and expected loss one. This gives the negative conclusion using the empirical requirement alone under joint universal measurability. The sharp uniform-convergence premise still excludes the examples. [Written joint universal measurability counterexample](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/universally-measurable-evaluation-obstruction.md)

A fair-label variant rules out both rates even for improper learners. With $`m\ge8n^2`$ sectors, sector collisions have probability at most $`1/8`$. On every injective sector pattern and every label vector, the benchmark has empirical optimum zero on a full-outer-measure set. The samplewise guarantee with tolerance at most $`1/8`$ therefore forces expected training loss at most $`1/4`$. Population loss is one half for every measurable output classifier. A direct information inequality on the actual iid experiment gives $`I\ge n/32`$. The written proof includes this inequality. The existing Lean proof uses the generalization inequality as an explicit premise. This uses the same trace Borel family and the same limitation on sharp uniform convergence. [Written proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/completed-finite-groups.md#fair-labels-and-improper-learners) · [Lean proof](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/FairLabelObstruction.lean)

A countable disjoint union of finite-support families gives one fixed VC-one class and one fixed fair-label iid law for which every candidate absolute constant fails at arbitrarily large training sizes. Proper outputs already fail the empirical requirement under scalar completed measurability. The CMI required of improper outputs with the empirical rate diverges. This class has outer uniform convergence separately under each labeled law, with no distribution-uniform modulus. [Written fixed-class, fixed-law proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/fixed-law-multiscale-obstruction.md)

The fair-label argument uses population loss minus empirical loss. The one-sided inequality in the COLT paper is written in the opposite orientation for a general bounded loss. It gives the required orientation by using the complementary loss, and the companion paper states the corresponding absolute-value bound. The written proof derives the required inequality directly. [COLT paper, Theorem 4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/steinke20b/steinke20b.pdf) · [Companion paper, Corollary 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2001.09122v3)

With measurable realized loss, the two requested rates themselves imply a sharp outer expected uniform-convergence rate for the comparator class. On a fair-label law with the same feature marginal, the empirical guarantee and CMI control the outer expected signed empirical improvement over a fixed comparator. Symmetrization then bounds uniform deviation under the original labeled law. The result applies to improper learners as well. [Written implication and constants](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/uniform-convergence-necessary.md)

With universal measurability of the scalar expected loss on the sample space, **one** VC-one class of disjoint two-point supports defeats every candidate constant. For the same class Lean verifies a measurable envelope for the uniform iid deviation with expected value at most $`2/\sqrt n+2/n`$ for every labeled law and positive size. The disproof tests universal measurability by pushing forward an iid pair law to a larger sample with repetitions. It uses the accuracy requirement on every fixed sample and requires measurability under that dependent pushforward. [Lean fixed-class disproof](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/CompletedFiniteGroups.lean) · [Lean uniform convergence](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/FiniteSupportUniform.lean) · [Proof and scope](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/completed-finite-groups.md#universal-measurability-on-the-sample-space)

On the same two-point class, a proper learner that uses completed iid measurability achieves both rates. Its written bounds are 12 nats for actual iid CMI and an empirical excess of at most $`2/\sqrt n+2/n`$ on every fixed sample. The design uses compactly supported noise to compare the laws of the named outputs under a single-row change. With the sharp outer uniform-convergence premise, the same method gives both rates for Borel supports with finite overlap $`s=O(d)`$, including infinite supports. [Completed-iid learner and bounded-overlap theorem](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/completed-disjoint-support-learner.md)

Lean verifies the full discrete-noise construction for supports of size at most $`m`$ and pointwise overlap at most $`s`$. One learner satisfies $`20s`$ nats of actual CMI under every completed iid law and all-sample expected empirical excess at most $`m/n+8/\sqrt n`$. This includes disjoint two-point supports with $`m=2`$ and $`s=1`$. [Lean expected loss and iid information theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/NoisySupportIid.lean)

For supports of unrestricted size and pointwise overlap at most $`s`$, a sharp outer uniform-convergence bound with constant $`B`$ gives the same $`20s`$ iid information bound and empirical excess $`(16B^2+9)\sqrt{d/n}`$. Lean verifies the reweighting argument that rules out too many iid hits on a null support. If $`s=O(d)`$, the theorem gives both conjectured rates on this class. [Lean sharp-deviation theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/NoisySupportSharpIid.lean)

If a sharp outer expected uniform-convergence bound holds with one numerical constant for every iid distribution and every sample size, a countable subclass suffices for all feature laws dominated by one reference. This conclusion even allows nonmeasurable finite-trace feasibility. The proof uses a product cell of high outer density to turn any positive-outer-probability trace-approximation failure into a violation of the sharp convergence bound under a reweighted iid law. [Written dominated-marginal extension](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/dominated-marginals.md#extension-from-the-sharp-outer-uniform-convergence-rate)

## A standard Borel class where the constructed learner fails

Let $T$ be the circle and $G$ the cyclic subgroup generated by an irrational rotation. A parameter $a$ labels its coset $a+G$ positive in one copy of $T$ and labels only the point $a$ positive in a second copy. This is a jointly Borel VC-one class of distinct classifiers on standard Borel spaces. A positive observation in the first copy singles out a coset. Choosing its first parameter in any global order would give a measurable set containing exactly one point per coset. Its countably many translates would be disjoint, all have equal measure, and cover the circle, which is impossible for a probability measure. [Class and ordered ERM obstruction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitOrderedExample.lean)

This also rules out measurability of the actual randomized learner. Every realized trace has positive mass. Among supported outputs with that trace, the canonical representative is the only possibility, so its membership in any measurable set can be detected from the output law. A single fixed mixture of Haar observations and a labeled atom makes the obstruction apply **at every sample size $n\ge2$**. In this model, the argument for a positive resolution on all standard Borel classes would have to change the learner. The example gives no impossibility result for a different learner on that regular class. [Positive trace mass](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/PositiveTraceReadout.lean) · [Completed iid counterexample to the ordered learner](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitOutputAllSizes.lean)

Lean verifies both rates for the circle class with a different representative rule. More generally, if each realized finite trace can be reconstructed by one of countably many universally measurable proper maps using at most $`k`$ observed features, choosing the first such witness gives a proper learner with whole-output information at most $`197{,}120d+32k`$ nats and empirical excess at most $`583\sqrt{d/n}`$. The short hypothesis supplies the full trace used in the comparison, and the representative changes only when the full witness uses the deleted row. The circle class admits Borel reconstruction maps with $`k=1`$. [Lean reconstruction theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/LocalReconstructionMeasurable.lean) · [Circle application](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitIidRates.lean) · [Proof and scope](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/measurable-local-reconstruction.md)

The circle class admits an ordinary Borel exact empirical minimizer at every sample size. Enumerate all subgroup translates of the finitely many observed circle coordinates, together with a fixed sequence from pairwise different cosets. The list realizes every training trace, and its first least-error index is measurable. Thus measurability of some exact proper learner is insufficient to justify the information proof via global tie-breaking. [Ordinary Borel ERM](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitMeasurableERM.lean)

A written extension for approximate catalogues keeps the information bound $`O(d+k)`$ when the reconstructions read at most $`k`$ features and may miss feasible traces. Its empirical loss is within $`O(\sqrt{d/n})`$ of the best catalogue candidate. A general $`O(d)`$-read catalogue with the required approximation guarantee would therefore suffice. [Written approximate-catalogue theorem](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/hidden-restriction-marks.md)

For every jointly Borel class, using universally measurable proper representatives on all subtuples of size $`O(\sqrt{nd}\log(en/d))`$ gives the required catalogue approximation with that larger feature budget. The resulting distribution-independent learner attains the conjectured empirical rate and sublinear actual iid CMI $`O(\min\{n,\sqrt{nd}\log(en/d)\})`$. [Written sublinear-information bound](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/general-sublinear-information.md)

For real-analytic score families with a dense finite-dimensional parameter image and arbitrary threshold queries, the derivative rank is at most the VC dimension. Countably many reduced parameter presentations and measurable implicit-function reconstruction give the two conjectured rates for proper named outputs. [Written analytic score theorem](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/analytic-score-reconstruction.md)

An improper learner on the same regular classes attains the conjectured empirical rate and a whole-output information bound independent of the sample size. The bound is $`O(dr^2\log^4(er))`$ for $`r=\max(1,\mathop{\mathrm{VC}}(H^*))`$, even for the entire vote tuple. A uniform linear bound for arbitrary dual dimensions and a proper output remain open. [Written voting bound](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/research/research/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/calibrated-catalogue-votes.md)
