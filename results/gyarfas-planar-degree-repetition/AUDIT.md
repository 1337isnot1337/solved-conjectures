# Source, priority and proof checks

## Original question

András Gyárfás, *Erdős Problems on Irregularities of Line Sizes and Point Degrees*, Bolyai Society Mathematical Studies 11 (2002), 367–373, MR1954734. On p. 367, a planar linear space means actual points in the real affine plane with all maximal collinear subsets of size at least two as lines. The full point set cannot be a line. Section 4, Problem 4, p. 371 asks whether some such set has every degree repeated at most twice. The opening paragraph credits Paul Erdős with initiating these questions. Gyárfás explicitly credits Erdős and himself with formulating Problems 2 and 3 immediately before §4. We use Gyárfás’s name for the published Problem 4. The rational construction and its proof were developed in Cinna Davis’s repository for issue #785. [Primary paper](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/101_Gyarfas_ErdosProblemsOnIrregularitiesOfLineSizesAndPointDegrees.pdf) · [Author’s bibliography, item 101](https://www.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/index_files/publications_2000_2009.htm) · [Discovery issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/785)

## Prior-resolution search, 26 November 2026

- Checked the paper’s planar Construction 2 on three parallel lines and its Corollary 3 for abstract linear spaces. The former gives multiplicity three. The latter gets two by replacing collinear sets by other blocks, an operation that supplies no real realization. Problems 2 and 3 in the same paper concern the number of distinct degrees. The separate Erdős–Purdy question counts multiplicities of line *sizes*. [Gyárfás, §§2–4](https://users.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/Cikkek/101_Gyarfas_ErdosProblemsOnIrregularitiesOfLineSizesAndPointDegrees.pdf)
- Searched the exact title, MR identifier, author and coauthor variants, “different point degrees”, “degree repetitions”, “planar linear spaces”, and dual formulations in terms of the number of distinct intersection points on each line in a real projective arrangement. Checked web and arXiv results, the author’s publication list, and title searches in OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar. Bibliographic indexing of this volume is incomplete. The searches located the original paper but no earlier answer to the planar question. [Author’s bibliographic record](https://www.renyi.hu/~gyarfas/index_files/publications_2000_2009.htm)
- Checked a related source of real conic-and-line configurations. Green and Tao count ordinary lines and classify small ordinary-line counts. The regular-polygon Böröczky examples have a transitive set of polygon vertices, hence many equal point degrees. Their ordinary-line bound gives no upper bound of two on the frequency of an individual point degree. The construction here selects secant intercepts asymmetrically and checks the resulting frequencies. [Green–Tao](https://arxiv.org/abs/1208.4714)
- Checked the radial-projection literature under the equivalent formulation that $d(p)$ is the size of the radial projection from $p$, identifying opposite directions. Lund, Pham and Thu bound the number of centers with small projections under incidence hypotheses. A bound on exceptional centers does not supply a configuration with the required frequency at every degree. [Lund–Pham–Thu, Theorem 1.9](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07431)
- Searched both permanent repository catalogues, discovery issues and pull requests by author aliases, exact title, problem and statistic. Existing Erdős–Gyárfás graph-cycle and coloring problems concern different objects. Filename searches of the full Mathlib and formal-conjectures trees supplied no named prior solution. No formal verification was used. [Catalogue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures) · [Discovery coordination](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/156)

The search establishes what was inspected. Unindexed or differently phrased prior work remains possible.

## Reproducible check

Run `python3 results/gyarfas-planar-degree-repetition/verify.py` from the repository root. The checker uses integer homogeneous coordinates and takes a cross product for every unordered point pair. It divides each resulting line equation by its common divisor, fixes its sign and counts distinct incident lines at each individual point. It then compares these literal degrees with the pointwise formulas, the maximum frequency and the number of different degrees. All 116 orders from 16 through 128 and from 255 through 257 passed. The unrestricted result follows from the written count. [Verifier](verify.py) · [Proof](PROOF.md)

## Independent mathematical review

A researcher working on a separate graph-theory campaign reviewed the proof at commit `2861ca08` against the original §4 statement. They rederived the secant intercept by homogeneous coordinates, checked disjoint partners on each axis line of sight, and recovered the two endpoint corrections to the off-axis count. At the first parameter $k=8$ the ties occur at $1,3,5$. The reviewer checked their separation for every larger $k$ and checked the unique shared degree for even orders. Their separate integer computation found the asserted maximum frequency and spectrum size at all 145 orders from 16 through 160. Their own wording and prior-resolution search are in the issue. [Independent review, 26 November 2026](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/785#issuecomment-7187694961)
