# Which vertex orders can witness chromatic incompactness?

For an ordinal $`\tau`$ and infinite cardinals $`\mu<\theta`$, call a graph on $`\tau`$ a witness if it has chromatic number exactly $`\theta`$ and every induced subgraph whose vertices have inherited order type strictly below $`\tau`$ is $`\mu`$-colourable. A cardinal-local witness on $`\kappa`$ has the same global chromatic number and is $`\mu`$-colourable on every set of fewer than $`\kappa`$ vertices. Products and powers of ordinals below are ordinal arithmetic.

**Theorem.** Fix an infinite cardinal $`\mu`$, put $`\lambda=\mu^+`$ and $`\kappa=\lambda^+`$, and let $`\tau`$ have cardinality $`\kappa`$.

1. For global chromatic number $`\lambda`$, a witness on $`\tau`$ exists exactly when a cardinal-local witness on $`\kappa`$ exists in the same universe and $`\tau=\kappa^d\cdot\lambda^e`$ for integers $`d\ge1`$, $`e\ge0`$.
2. For global chromatic number $`\kappa`$, the corresponding orders are exactly $`\kappa^d`$ for finite $`d\ge1`$, again conditional on the cardinal-local witness. This part holds for every regular uncountable $`\kappa`$ and infinite $`\mu<\kappa`$.

For the smaller-chromatic-number follow-up to Erdős Problem 919 the first part gives $`\omega_2^d\cdot\omega_1^e`$. For example $`\omega_2\cdot\omega_1`$ is possible in any universe with the cardinal-local witness. The existence of that witness in ZFC remains the open question in [#975](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/975). [Erdős, printed p. 29](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1969-13.pdf#page=3) · [Square transfer and consistency inputs](PROOF.md)

## The ordinal obstructions

Forgetting the order gives the required cardinal-local witness, since a set of size below $`\kappa`$ has order type below the initial ordinal $`\kappa`$. We use the classical Milner–Rado decomposition: every ordinal below $`\gamma^+`$, for an infinite cardinal $`\gamma`$, is a countable union of sets each of order type bounded by a finite ordinal power of $`\gamma`$. Thus when $`\tau\ge\kappa^\omega`$, it is a countable union of sets of strictly smaller order type. Separate $`\mu`$-colour palettes for the pieces use only $`\mu`$ colours (take successive differences if the pieces overlap). This precludes either global chromatic number. [Milner–Rado (1965)](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-15.1.750) · [Explicit formulation in Erdős–Hajnal–Milner, §1, p. 89](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1978-25.pdf#page=1)

Otherwise take the unique $`d\ge1`$ with $`\kappa^d\le\tau<\kappa^{d+1}`$. Ordinal division gives $`\tau=\kappa^d\cdot\xi+\rho`$ with $`0<\xi<\kappa`$ and $`\rho<\kappa^d`$. A nonzero remainder would partition $`\tau`$ into two strictly smaller orders. If the global chromatic number is $`\kappa`$, any quotient $`\xi>1`$ gives fewer than $`\kappa`$ blocks each of order $`\kappa^d<\tau`$; disjoint palettes use at most $`\max(\mu,|\xi|)<\kappa`$ colours. This proves necessity in part 2.

For part 1 the remainder must again vanish. A partition of $`\xi`$ into at most $`\mu`$ smaller-order subsets partitions the ordered sum $`\kappa^d\cdot\xi`$ into at most $`\mu`$ subsets of smaller order: retain the full blocks whose indices lie in each part. Multiplication by a fixed nonzero ordinal on the left is strictly increasing in its right argument. Since $`\xi<\lambda^+`$, the Milner–Rado decomposition at $`\lambda`$ rules out every $`\xi\ge\lambda^\omega`$. Below that power take $`e\ge0`$ with $`\lambda^e\le\xi<\lambda^{e+1}`$. If $`\xi>\lambda^e`$, division expresses it as fewer than $`\lambda`$ consecutive blocks of size in order at most $`\lambda^e`$, each strictly smaller than $`\xi`$. There are at most $`\mu`$ such blocks. This rules out every quotient except $`\lambda^e`$, including the case $`e=0`$ where the permitted quotient is one.

## Full subsets of a finite power

For a regular infinite $`\gamma`$ and a positive integer $`s`$, a subset of $`\gamma^s`$ has order type $`\gamma^s`$ exactly if it occupies $`\gamma`$ many points in each of a full set of rows of $`\gamma^{s-1}`$. Here each row has length $`\gamma`$; a full set at level one means cardinality $`\gamma`$. For powers of order at least two the criterion follows from the outer-block argument below. To prove it, one may instead split $`\gamma^s`$ into $`\gamma`$ blocks of type $`\gamma^{s-1}`$. If fewer than $`\gamma`$ such blocks contain a set of full type $`\gamma^{s-1}`$, the portion after their bounded indices is a sum of at most $`\gamma`$ ordinals below $`\gamma^{s-1}`$. That sum is at most $`\gamma^{s-1}`$: each term is bounded by $`\gamma^{s-2}\cdot\eta`$ for some $`\eta<\gamma`$, and the sum of $`\gamma`$ such coefficients is at most $`\gamma`$ by regularity. The initial bounded block segment plus this tail has order below $`\gamma^s`$. This proves the criterion by induction in the usual lexicographic finite-tuple coordinates, in which the last coordinate changes fastest. In particular, if a union of fewer than $`\gamma`$ subsets of $`\gamma^s`$ has full order, at least one member has full order: apply the same assertion inductively within full blocks and then regularity to the outer indices.

Use the [finite-power transfer](REVIEW.md#finite-ordinal-powers) for $`\kappa`$. It constructs a graph $`A`$ on $`\kappa^d`$ with the required smaller-order bound, preserving the chromatic number of the cardinal-local witness. This proves sufficiency in part 2 and part 1 when $`e=0`$.

## Adding the smaller cardinal to the order

For each $`e\ge1`$ construct a graph $`B_e`$ on $`\lambda^e`$ with the following two properties. Every smaller-order subset is $`\mu`$-colourable. Between any two full subsets, even equal ones, there is an edge with one endpoint in each. Start with the complete graph on $`\lambda`$.

Given $`B_e`$, choose a bijection $`r:\lambda^e\to\lambda`$ and replace each vertex by a row of order $`\lambda`$. For distinct rows $`i,j`$ with $`r(i)<r(j)`$, join $`(i,s)`$ to $`(j,t)`$ exactly when $`i,j`$ are adjacent in $`B_e`$ and $`r(j)<s`$. The actual row order remains the ordinal order. The smaller-order colouring is the finite-power transfer: the indices of full rows form a smaller-order set in the base, while on nonfull rows the projected graph has fewer than $`\lambda`$ earlier-in-label neighbours and fewer than $`\lambda`$ later-in-label neighbours. Each component has fewer than $`\lambda`$ vertices by regularity, hence can use $`\mu`$ colours. Use disjoint palettes for the two parts. For the cross-edge property, each full subset has a full set of indices of full rows. Pick adjacent indices from the two such sets by induction. Whichever row is earlier in label has cofinally many selected coordinates, so one passes the later label. An arbitrary selected point in the later row now completes the edge.

On $`\kappa^d\cdot\lambda^e`$ take rows indexed by $`j<\lambda^e`$ with inner coordinate $`u<\kappa^d`$. Join two vertices if their inner coordinates are adjacent in $`A`$ and their row indices are adjacent in $`B_e`$. Projection to $`A`$ gives the global upper bound $`\lambda`$. In any colouring with at most $`\mu`$ colours, for each inner coordinate $`u`$ choose a colour occurring on a full subset of its $`\lambda^e`$ row indices, using the finite-power union property. Adjacent $`u,v`$ cannot receive the same choice: the cross-edge property of $`B_e`$ would give adjacent vertices of that colour. This would $`\mu`$-colour $`A`$, a contradiction.

Finally, in a subset of smaller order the full inner rows have an index set of order strictly below $`\lambda^e`$, so their vertices can be coloured by projection to $`B_e`$. There are at most $`\lambda<\kappa`$ nonfull rows. The union of their inner-coordinate projections in $`\kappa^d`$ has strictly smaller order by the finite-power union property. Projection to $`A`$ colours the other vertices. Separate palettes use $`\mu`$ colours. This proves part 1.

The ordinal decomposition is classical, and the triangular cutoff appears in Erdős’s original construction. The earlier repository result gives the pure finite-power lift. The mixed-order product and the two-successor classification give a structural extension of that result. Its proof of the chromatic lower bound uses the cross-edge property of the constructed factor. For arbitrary graphs the analogous product bound fails. The classification here is conditional on the cardinal-local witness. The Dow–Komjáth existence question remains open.
