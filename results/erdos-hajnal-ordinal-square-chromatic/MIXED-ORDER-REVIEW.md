# Review of the two-successor chromatic order-type spectrum

Independent internal written review, 27 November 2026 UTC. Fix an infinite cardinal μ, let λ be its successor and κ the successor of λ. For an ordinal τ of cardinality κ, consider a graph on τ whose chromatic number is exactly λ and whose every induced subgraph of smaller inherited order type is μ-colorable. The asserted ZFC criterion is equivalent to the conjunction of a cardinal-local (κ, λ, μ) incompactness witness and the ordinal equation τ=κ^d·λ^e for finite d≥1 and e≥0. The cardinal-local existence assertion remains a separate set-theoretic question.

## Obstruction

Forgetting the order gives the cardinal-local witness. For τ≥κ^ω, Milner–Rado at κ covers τ<κ⁺ by countably many sets of order type bounded by finite powers of κ. Color disjoint refinements in countably many μ-sized palettes and contradict χ=λ. Below κ^ω choose the largest finite power κ^d≤τ. Division gives τ=κ^d·ξ+ρ, 1≤ξ<κ and ρ<κ^d. If ρ>0, the proper first segment κ^d·ξ and the proper remainder can be colored separately with μ colors altogether.

If ξ≥λ^ω, apply Milner–Rado at λ to ξ<λ⁺ and union the full κ^d blocks indexed by each member of that countable cover. The order of each lifted set is κ^d multiplied on the left of the covering piece's order, which is strictly smaller than κ^d·ξ by strict increase in the right argument. If ξ<λ^ω, choose the largest e≥0 with λ^e≤ξ and divide ξ=λ^e·γ+η, γ<λ and η<λ^e. Unless ξ=λ^e, this partitions ξ into fewer than λ sets of strictly smaller type. Lifting again contradicts χ=λ. The argument includes ξ=1 and the entire range 1<ξ<λ. Milner–Rado supplies both covering decompositions. [Milner and Rado (1965)](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s3-15.1.750) · [Erdős, Hajnal and Milner, p. 89, §1](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1978-25.pdf#page=1)

## Construction

Begin with the established finite-power triangular lift at κ, which supplies a graph H on α=κ^d of chromatic number λ whose every proper order type is μ-colorable. Its graph is already the required witness when e=0. At λ, start the analogous triangular lift at K_λ. Its smaller-cardinality subsets are μ-colorable because λ=μ⁺. Finite iteration supplies B_e on β=λ^e with the same order-local μ bound.

Here is the extra property needed to tensor H with B_e. In B₁ a large set is one of size λ. In the lift from B_e to B_(e+1), call a set large when the indices of its rows having λ vertices form a large subset of B_e. Inductively a coloring of the whole of B_e in fewer than λ colors has a large color class. In fact this holds on every large domain, by choosing in each λ-sized row a cofinal color, coloring its large index set and recursing. The regularity of λ supplies those cofinal colors. Two large subsets always contain endpoints of an edge between them: this is immediate in K_λ; at the next stage use the inductive edge in their large index sets and choose an occurrence in the smaller-label row beyond the other index's cutoff. This works even if the two subsets coincide and if the index bijection does not preserve ordinal order.

Place H × B_e in the ordinal sum of β rows each of order α, with an edge iff both coordinate pairs are edges of their factors. Projection bounds global chromatic number by λ. A hypothetical coloring with fewer colors, restricted to each fiber of B_e over a fixed H-vertex, has a large monochromatic class. Choose its color. Adjacent H-vertices cannot select the same color because the cross-edge property of B_e supplies an edge between the two classes, giving a monochromatic edge of the product. Thus H would be colored below λ.

For a subset of type below α·β, the indices of its rows of full α-type have type below β; otherwise the full rows alone contribute α·β. Project these rows to the corresponding restriction of B_e. Each remaining row projects to a subset of H of type below α. Their union still has type below α, because fewer than κ such sets occur (β has cardinality λ), and the union of fewer than κ sets of type below κ^d cannot contain a copy of κ^d. For the last assertion, any coloring of κ^d with fewer than κ colors has a full-type monochromatic class by induction on d: within each length-κ row take a cofinal color, then apply the induction hypothesis to the coloring of the κ^(d−1) row indices. Color the nonfull part by its projection to H, using a separate palette from the full part. Their combined size is μ. This deals also with κ-sized rows of proper order type.

I found no missing case in the stated two-successor classification. The argument uses extra Ramsey behavior of B_e. An unrestricted multiplicativity assertion for categorical products of infinite-chromatic graphs is false. Hajnal's counterexample and Rinot's successor-cardinal results delineate that distinction. [Hajnal (1985)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02579376) · [Rinot (2017)](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/32173)

The bounded source review included Erdős's original two ordinal-square questions; the ordinal partition literature of Milner, Rado, Erdős and Hajnal; the cardinal-local incompactness results reviewed by Lambie-Hanson and Rinot; and infinite categorical-product counterexamples. Searches by “lesser order type”, chromatic spectrum, finite ordinal powers, ordinal chromatic incompactness, graph products, and the named authors found the historical single-square example, classical ordinal covering theorem and cardinal-local consistency results. I found no prior statement of this exact conditional mixed-power order-type spectrum. It does not settle whether the cardinal-local witness exists in ZFC for μ=ℵ₀.
