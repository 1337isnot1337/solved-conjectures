# Quantitative approximation in negative curvature

There is an approximate length principle even in geometries where exact attainment fails. Fix a closed negatively curved Riemannian manifold M with its metric, and 0<α≤1. For every α-Hölder real function f on M and ε>0, there is a closed geodesic whose absolute normalized f-average is within ε of the supremum and whose length is at most

    C(M,α)(1+[f]_{C^α}/ε).

This holds for all closed geodesics and does not give the same bound after restricting to simple ones. No optimality of the exponent is asserted.

Use the Hölder strong coding of the geodesic flow by a suspension over an irreducible two-sided shift of finite type with a positive Hölder roof r. A suitable reference is [Constantine–Lafont–Thompson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04395), Theorem A and its compact locally CAT(−1) application; rescaling the metric and time if necessary removes the particular upper curvature normalization. The projection π from the suspension to the unit tangent bundle is a surjective flow semiconjugacy. Pull f back from the base to the suspension, and define g(x) by integrating the pulled-back f over the roof segment 0≤t≤r(x). The coding and roof are Hölder, so g is Hölder in the symbolic metric. One way to see this is to integrate after writing t=u r(x), 0≤u≤1: the coded points along those orbit segments depend Hölder-continuously on x, uniformly in u, by the Hölder zero section and bounded-time smooth dependence of the actual geodesic flow. Subtract a constant from f for the signed estimate, making ||f||∞ bounded by diam(M)^α times [f]_{C^α}.

Write β for the largest signed average among invariant probability measures upstairs. This exists by compactness and equals the largest signed average downstairs: every invariant probability downstairs admits an invariant lift. In detail, first lift it to a probability using surjectivity (extend the positive functional from pullbacks of continuous functions), and then take a weak limit of time averages of the lift. By the suspension measure formula a maximizing invariant probability is built from a shift-invariant probability μ and β=∫g dμ/∫r dμ. Therefore q=g−βr satisfies ∫q dμ=0. Its sup norm and its Hölder constant, for a fixed exponent allowed by the coding and α, are at most constants times [f]_{C^α}.

For each n choose a sequence x with ∑_{j=0}^{n−1}q(σʲx)≥0, which exists by shift invariance and mean zero. In a one-step presentation close its block of n symbols with a connector containing at most B additional symbols, where B depends only on the finite irreducible adjacency matrix. This gives a periodic sequence y agreeing with x in positions 0,…,n−1. The difference between the n displayed q-sums is uniformly O([f]_{C^α}): at index j the sequences agree on a symmetric window of radius min(j,n−1−j), so the Hölder errors form two geometric series. The connector costs at most B||q||∞. The periodic roof sum is between n min r and (n+B)max r. Its signed average is therefore ≥β−C[f]_{C^α}/n, and its flow image is a periodic geodesic of length at most (n+B)max r (the prime period may be shorter).

Apply the signed estimate to f or −f according to which has the larger signed optimum. In particular the invariant optimum equals the closed-geodesic supremum. The choice n=max(1,ceil(C[f]_{C^α}/ε)) proves the bound. This uses only the strong coding and elementary finite-block closing, not a claim about the sharp periodic approximation rate.
