# Attainment on compact flat manifolds and compact Lie groups

Steinerberger suggested groups as a testing ground in [Question 1.3](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972718001636). The qualitative property holds on every nontrivial compact connected Lie group with a bi-invariant Riemannian metric, and on every compact connected positive-dimensional flat Riemannian manifold. This contrasts with [hyperbolic nonattainment](HYPERBOLIC.md). No quantitative norm bound for general groups is being claimed.

## A Fourier compactness lemma

Identify a fixed torus T with ℝ^r/ℤ^r. For v∈ℤ^r nonzero, let P_v average along the circle generated by v. It retains Fourier frequency k precisely when k·v=0. Given any sequence (v_n), thin diagonally so the indicator of k·v_n=0 eventually stabilizes at every k. Let Λ⊆ℤ^r be the set where the stabilized value is 1.

Λ is a subgroup of rank ≤r−1: a set of r independent surviving frequencies would simultaneously annihilate v_n eventually. Let H be its annihilator in T. Then on trigonometric polynomials P_{v_n} converges uniformly to Haar averaging P_H. The same follows on all continuous functions by uniform polynomial approximation, since both kinds of averaging are sup-norm contractions.

There is a nonzero w∈ℤ^r annihilating Λ by rational linear algebra. Its circle lies in H, so Haar invariance gives P_H P_w=P_H and hence ||P_H g||∞≤||P_w g||∞ for every continuous g. This is the step that converts a possibly higher-dimensional limit average into an at-least-as-good closed geodesic.

## Tori and compact flat manifolds

On a torus apply the lemma to a sequence of directions whose maximal absolute average tends to the desired supremum. Uniform convergence gives the norm of the limit; the replacement direction w attains at least that norm. For a compact flat manifold use its finite torus cover from the Bieberbach theorem. A closed geodesic downstairs lifts to a closed one after repeating it finitely many times, because it permutes finitely many points of the fibre. Every closed geodesic upstairs projects to one downstairs. Under pullback and along a repetition the normalized average is unchanged. Thus attainment descends.

## Compact connected Lie groups

A geodesic for a bi-invariant metric is a left translate of a one-parameter subgroup. When closed and nonconstant, that subgroup is a circle. Every such circle is conjugate into one fixed maximal torus T. Thus the averages are obtained by using all x,h∈G and integer circle directions v in T, with the restricted function g_{x,h}(t)=f(x h t h⁻¹).

Take a maximizing sequence x_n,h_n,v_n. Compactness gives a subsequence with x_n→x and h_n→h, and uniform continuity gives g_{x_n,h_n}→g_{x,h} uniformly on T. After the diagonal thinning above, the resulting averages converge to P_H g_{x,h}(e). Its absolute value is at most ||P_w g_{x,h}||∞ for some circle w in T. A torus translate where this norm is attained can be absorbed into the base point x, giving another closed geodesic of G. It attains the original supremum.
