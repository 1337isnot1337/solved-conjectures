# Prior-art and scope audit

The target is [Question 1.3 in the original paper](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972718001636), also see [arXiv:1805.02601 §1](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.02601) and §38 of the author's March 31, 2026 list. It asks about generalizing the flat two-torus theorem, including to hyperbolic surfaces and groups. The negative theorem concerns one of the authors' named geometric settings in full, including all genera; it is not a classification of compact Riemannian manifolds. Our positive group theorem specifies bi-invariance and does not assert anything for general left-invariant metrics or word-metric groups.

## Checks on prior work

- Compared the journal statement and the preprint, and searched the title, DOI and arXiv identifier with “hyperbolic”, “maximizing geodesic averages”, “closed geodesic attainment”, and “nonattainment”. Followed MaRDI's bibliographic links, and searched arXiv and MathOverflow for base-point analogues. The author's [2025 Fourier-analysis lecture](https://www.icmat.es/congresos/2025/Escorial/slides/Main_Talks/Steinerberger.pdf) provides another author presentation of the torus theorem. We found no direct resolution of the smooth base-point statement on hyperbolic surfaces in the searched sources.
- [Cantrell, Martínez-Granado and Reyes, Proposition 8.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02585v1) gives a closely related variational mechanism: a Lipschitz distance function on Mineyev's flow space singles out an invariant lamination. That result concerns length functionals from flow reparameterizations. It does not state smoothness on the underlying surface, the absolute-value mean-zero formulation, or nonattainment even among simple closed curves. We cite it as related independently available machinery, not as our result.
- The classical ingredients of the hyperbolic proof are explicitly credited: [Thurston](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0273-0979-1988-15685-6), [Penner](https://www.ams.org/tran/1988-310-01/S0002-9947-1988-0930079-9/S0002-9947-1988-0930079-9.pdf), [Birman–Series](https://www.math.columbia.edu/~jb/bdd-int.no-sparce.pdf), [Sigmund](https://doi.org/10.2307/2373591), and [density of weighted simple multicurves](https://library.slmath.org/books/gt3m/PDF/8.pdf#page=43). We do not claim those tools as new.
- The 2019 paper already establishes exact attainment and a quantitative theorem in dimension two and notes that higher-dimensional variations are possible. No priority is claimed for general flat-torus attainment; the proof is included as a building block. We did not locate the exact instability example, the sharp band-limited growth, or the Hölder approximation exponent in the works searched.

This records a bounded literature audit, not a guarantee that no prior equivalent result exists.
