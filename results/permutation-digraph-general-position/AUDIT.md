# Audit of the permutation digraph disproof

- **Exact source:** [*The general position number of digraphs*, arXiv:2604.15909, §3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15909#S3.SS3). Theorem 3.6 gives the lower bound for word length at least three and degree at least twice the word length. The sentence immediately after its proof conjectures equality. The bound is not itself the conjecture.
- **Later status:** [*The General Position Problem: A Survey*, v5, Theorem 5.60](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19385v5) records both the lower bound and the conjecture, and lists finding the general exact value separately. Searches on 26 November 2026 used the arXiv identifier, Theorem 3.6, author aliases, both degree and alphabet-size conventions, and variants with “sharp”, “counterexample”, “marker”, “alphabet partition”, “diameter” and “permutation digraph”. The original and survey, cited older permutation-digraph papers, indexed subsequent sources and both repository branches were checked. No prior resolution of the exact equality was found. The separate Kautz and oriented-tree targets were not conflated.
- **Attribution:** A two-class alphabet partition is already used in the original paper. Its Theorem 3.5 uses such a partition for length two, and Theorem 3.6 uses a marker set of size two. The disproof verifies the distance argument for *arbitrary* marker counts and finds a strict inequality on the entire conjectured range. [Brunat–Fiol–Fiol (1997)](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(96)00318-4) is the prior source for permutation digraphs and their diameter. The proof here builds the necessary walks and a shortest path of diameter length directly.
- **Proof check:** For short paths a suffix-prefix overlap must preserve the final marker of the first word, absent from every proper prefix of the second. For the upper bound, the only potentially invalid old–new coincidence across a buffer of length one less than the word length is last–first, also impossible. The two bounds hold in both ordered directions and exclude a triple via shortest-path additivity. The strict gap factors with coefficient degree minus twice word length plus one, positive even at the boundary.
- **Executable checks:** `python3 verify.py` checks 6 palettes, 39,312 BFS visits, and 3,358 ordered distances to partner words. `python3 verify.py --extended` checks 13 palettes across six parameter pairs, 1,298,082 BFS visits, and 88,419 ordered distances. Palette-preserving alphabet permutations let one BFS root cover every ordered pair in a given marker family. The checker also tests the reversal geodesic. The infinite claim does not depend on computation. No Lean or external referee verification is claimed.
