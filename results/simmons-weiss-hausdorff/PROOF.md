# The natural Hausdorff measure of irrationally translated bounded continued fractions

**Theorem.** Let $`N\ge2`$, let $`F_N=\{[0;d_1,d_2,\ldots]:1\le d_i\le N\}`$, and let $`\delta=\dim_H F_N`$. For every irrational $`\theta`$,
```math
\mathcal H^\delta\bigl((F_N+\theta)\cap\mathrm{BA}\bigr)=0.
```
More generally, the same conclusion holds for the image of $`F_N`$ under any non-projectively-rational real Möbius map (its pole is excluded). The conclusion holds for the pushforward of any law on the two-digit blocks of $`F_N`$ whose next-block conditional probabilities, given an arbitrary finite past, are uniformly bounded away from zero.

This treats the natural measure left outside the results of [Simmons–Weiss, *Random walks on homogeneous spaces and Diophantine approximation on fractals*, Invent. Math. 216 (2019), Example 9.2, preprint p. 32](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/145241/1/BQ_and_BA.pdf#page=33). Their Bernoulli conclusion was already enough for every irrational target in the later Gravgaard–Lee existence-only mixed-sumset question. Here the argument is not predicated on a Bernoulli law; it exploits instead uniform conditional block probability, the Benoist–Quint topological orbit dichotomy, and a local exclusion of convergence to finite lattice orbits.

## 1. Block lattices and their orbit dichotomy

For $`1\le d\le N`$, put $`T_d=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&d\end{pmatrix}`$, and use the finite alphabet of two-digit blocks $`e=ij`$ with matrix $`M_e=T_i T_j\in\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)`$. Write $`M_w`$ for the prefix product and $`G=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb R)`$, $`\Lambda=\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Z)`$, $`X=G/\Lambda`$. Use $`U(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&t\\0&1\end{pmatrix}`$ and $`L(t)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\t&1\end{pmatrix}`$. The inverse block matrices $`g_e=M_e^{-1}`$ generate a Zariski-dense semigroup $`\Gamma`$, and the group generated is $`\Lambda`$. Indeed $`A=T_1^2`$, $`B=T_1T_2`$ satisfy $`A^{-1}B=U(1)`$ and $`AU(-1)=L(1)`$. The two integral shears generate $`\Lambda`$ and their powers are Zariski dense in the upper and lower full unipotent groups, respectively. The Zariski closure of the semigroup contains $`A,B`$: the Zariski closure of positive powers of a real hyperbolic $`2`$-by-$`2`$ determinant-one matrix contains all its inverse powers by restriction of polynomials to the diagonal torus (a Laurent polynomial cannot have infinitely many distinct roots). A Zariski closure of a semigroup is multiplicatively closed, by continuity of multiplication in each variable. In particular it contains the entire group generated by $`A,B`$.

By [Benoist–Quint, *Mesures stationnaires et fermés invariants des espaces homogènes*, Ann. Math. 174 (2011), 1111–1162, Corollary 1.2(b)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2011/174-2/p08), every infinite closed forward-$`\Gamma`$-invariant subset of $`X`$ equals $`X`$. The quotient by the center may be taken if needed; it does not change the lattice space.

There are only countably many points with finite $`\Lambda`$-orbit. Indeed finiteness for $`x_0=H^{-1}\Lambda`$ implies that $`\{\gamma\in\Lambda:H\gamma H^{-1}\in\Lambda\}`$ has finite index in $`\Lambda`$. Take positive powers of the integral unipotents $`U(1),L(1),L(1)U(1)L(-1)`$ in this finite-index stabilizer. Their conjugates by $`H`$ are nontrivial integral unipotents, whose fixed points are rational projective points (their fixed lines are rational kernels), and hence $`H(\infty),H(0),H(1)`$ are rational projective. Three distinct rational images of $`\infty,0,1`$ force $`H`$ to be projectively rational: take rational column vectors in the first two image lines and multiply them by the rational coefficients expressing a rational vector in the third line. This leaves only countably many possible cosets (a projective class has at most two determinant-one representatives). Every finite forward-$`\Gamma`$-invariant subset is permuted by each generator, hence consists of points with finite group orbit.

## 2. Noncompact paths under conditionally positive block laws

Let $`(e_i)_{i\ge1}`$ have any law on the block alphabet such that for every finite history of positive probability, every next block has conditional probability at least some fixed $`\eta>0`$. Fix $`x_0\in X`$ outside the set of finite group orbit points, and put $`X_n=M_{e_1\cdots e_n}^{-1}x_0`$, so $`X_{n+1}=g_{e_{n+1}}X_n`$. We prove that almost surely $`\{X_n\}`$ is not relatively compact.

First, almost surely its set $`F`$ of subsequential accumulation points in $`X`$ is forward-$`\Gamma`$-invariant. To see this, fix a basis open set $`O`$ of a countable topological basis for $`X`$, and fix a word $`v`$ of length $`\ell`$. If $`X_n`$ visits $`O`$ infinitely often, choose successive visit times at least $`\ell`$ apart, starting beyond any previously fixed time. At every finite such stopping time, the conditional probability of seeing $`v`$ immediately afterward is at least $`\eta^\ell`$. For any $`k`$, the probability that the first $`k`$ trials all exist and all fail is at most $`(1-\eta^\ell)^k`$, by conditioning at the successive stopping times. It follows that infinitely many visits almost surely imply infinitely many prescribed-word successes. Take the countable intersection over $`O,v`$ and initial times. For any $`y\in F`$, choose shrinking basis neighborhoods of $`y`$, each visited infinitely often, and choose increasing success times for a fixed $`v`$. Continuity gives $`M_v^{-1}y\in F`$, proving the claim.

Suppose now that the path is relatively compact. Then $`F`$ is nonempty compact; by the orbit dichotomy and noncompactness of $`X`$, it is finite. The distance from $`X_n`$ to $`F`$ tends to zero, since otherwise some subsequence of the relatively compact path would accumulate off $`F`$. The points of $`F`$ have finite group orbit and are permuted by all generators. For all sufficiently large $`n`$, let $`f_n`$ be the unique nearby point of $`F`$ and write
```math
X_n=\exp(v_n)f_n,\qquad v_n\in\mathfrak{sl}_2(\mathbb R),\qquad v_n\longrightarrow0.
```
This is a unique local chart because the stabilizer in $`G`$ of $`f_n`$ is discrete and $`F`$ is finite. Since the generator alphabet and $`F`$ are finite, the separation of $`F`$ and continuity give $`f_{n+1}=g_{e_{n+1}}f_n`$ for large $`n`$. Chart uniqueness and the bounded adjoint norms of the generators then give
```math
v_{n+1}=\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(g_{e_{n+1}})v_n
```
for all sufficiently large $`n`$. No $`v_n`$ is zero, since $`X_n`$ is in the group orbit of $`x_0`$, disjoint from every finite orbit.

Fix a time $`n_0`$ beyond both thresholds. The displacement $`v=v_{n_0}`$ has determinant zero, since conjugation preserves determinant and $`v_n\to0`$; it is a nonzero traceless nilpotent matrix. Write $`v=c\,u u^{\mathsf T}J`$, where $`u\ne0`$, $`c\ne0`$, and $`J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}`$. This is possible because the image and kernel of $`v`$ are the same line $`\mathbb Ru`$. Since $`g^{\mathsf T}Jg=J`$ on $`G`$, the Frobenius norm of $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ad}}(g)v`$ is $`|c|\|gu\|^2`$. Let $`w_k=e_{n_0+1}\cdots e_{n_0+k}`$ be the first $`k`$ future blocks. Thus $`\|M_{w_k}^{-1}u\|\to0`$. Writing the matrix as
```math
M_{w_k}=\begin{pmatrix}p_{2k-1}&p_{2k}\\q_{2k-1}&q_{2k}\end{pmatrix},
```
the first coordinate of $`M_{w_k}^{-1}u`$ is $`q_{2k}u_1-p_{2k}u_2`$. Since $`q_{2k}\to\infty`$, we must have $`u_2\ne0`$, and the entire future irrational continued fraction is forced to equal $`u_1/u_2`$.

For each possible $`n_0`$, prefix, and candidate $`f_{n_0}`$ with finite group orbit, $`v`$ is fixed by the local chart; at most one continuation can meet this forced value, by uniqueness of the infinite regular continued fraction. The conditional law of any single continuation is zero, since the probability of each next block is at most $`1-\eta`$. There are only countably many choices of $`n_0`$, prefix, finite-orbit point, and fixed local charts (one near each such point suffices). Their union has probability zero. Therefore almost surely the path is not relatively compact.

## 3. Transfer to rational approximation

Let $`x=[0;d_1,d_2,\ldots]`$ be the real coded by the chosen blocks. For a positive-determinant projective map, use $`H=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{pmatrix}\in G`$ and $`\beta=H(x)=(ax+b)/(cx+d)`$. Negative determinant is handled by composing on the output with negation. For $`H`$ not projectively rational, take $`x_0=H^{-1}\Lambda`$; its full group orbit is infinite by §1. By §2 and the [Mahler compactness criterion](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~einsiedl/EFS6.pdf#page=4), some subsequence of the lattices $`M_w^{-1}H^{-1}\mathbb Z^2`$ contains nonzero vectors of sup norm tending to zero.

For a prefix of even length $`n\ge2`$, set $`q=q_n`$ and $`D_q=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(q,q^{-1})`$. The matrix $`K_x=D_qU(-x)M_w`$ has entries of magnitude at most 1, since its top row is $`q(p_{n-1}-xq_{n-1}),q(p_n-xq_n)`$ and its bottom row is $`q_{n-1}/q,1`$. The bounds on the top row follow from the standard consecutive-convergent error estimates $`|q_i x-p_i|\le1/q_{i+1}`$. For $`cx+d\ne0`$, direct multiplication gives
```math
D_q U(-\beta)\mathbb Z^2
=\begin{pmatrix}1/(cx+d)&0\\ c/q^2&cx+d\end{pmatrix}
 K_x\bigl(M_w^{-1}H^{-1}\mathbb Z^2\bigr).
```
The prefactor has bounded sup operator norm for a fixed $`x`$, uniformly in $`q\ge1`$. Thus along the same subsequence there are nonzero vectors of norm tending to zero in $`D_q U(-\beta)\mathbb Z^2`$. The underlying integer vector $`(p,r)`$ has $`r\ne0`$ once the norm is less than 1. Multiplying its two transformed coordinates gives $` |r|\,|p-r\beta|\to0`$, so $`\beta\notin\mathrm{BA}`$. The pole has probability zero as the symbolic law is nonatomic. In particular the translation $`H(x)=x+\theta`$ with irrational $`\theta`$ has the assertion.

## 4. The Hausdorff measure has uniformly positive branching

Let $`\mu`$ be normalized $`\mathcal H^\delta|_{F_N}`$. For these bounded-digit conformal repellers, $`0<\mathcal H^\delta(F_N)<\infty`$ (indeed $`F_N`$ is Ahlfors regular, as recorded in [Simmons–Weiss, §7](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/145241/1/BQ_and_BA.pdf#page=25)). For a prefix $`w`$, its cylinder is the image of $`F_N`$ under the continued-fraction inverse branch $`f_w(t)=(p_{n-1}t+p_n)/(q_{n-1}t+q_n)`$. Since $`0\le q_{n-1}\le q_n`$, the ratio of the largest and smallest absolute derivatives of $`f_w`$ on $`[0,1]`$ is at most 4. Each two-digit cylinder $`F_{N,e}`$ has positive measure, being a bi-Lipschitz copy of $`F_N`$. Consequently every conditional probability is bounded below uniformly in the prefix:
```math
\frac{\mathcal H^\delta(f_w(F_{N,e}))}
{\mathcal H^\delta(f_w(F_N))}
\ge4^{-\delta}\frac{\mathcal H^\delta(F_{N,e})}{\mathcal H^\delta(F_N)}
\ge\eta>0,
```
where the minimum ranges over the finitely many blocks. The pushforward conclusion of §3 applies. On every compact subinterval away from the pole, $`H`$ is locally bi-Lipschitz, so a $`\mu`$-null subset of $`F_N`$ has $`\mathcal H^\delta`$-null image (use a countable compact exhaustion of the pole-free domain). This proves the natural measure assertion.

## Priority and limitations

Simmons–Weiss's Theorem 9.6 gives prior Bernoulli results and their Example 9.2 explicitly does not treat this Hausdorff measure. They also note in Theorem 9.1(ii) a general natural-measure statement for groups not virtually contained in any conjugate arithmetic lattice; an irrational translate of this continued-fraction IFS remains conjugate into the arithmetic lattice and hence lies outside that hypothesis. Existing finite-state Markov random-walk results and Bernoulli measures do not automatically apply to the natural measure of a continued-fraction repeller. A bounded priority search has not so far found this natural-measure conclusion for every irrational translate. The result is non-bad approximability almost surely, not full Gauss normality, a logarithm law, or a Khinchin theorem.
