# Audit and priority record

**Target.** The [original preprint, arXiv:2604.15909v1, Conjecture 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15909v1#S4.SS1) of Ullas Chandran S. V., Gabriele Di Stefano, Grahame Erskine, Haritha S., Elias John Thomas and James Tuite concerns finite connected simple graphs of order at least three. [Survey Conjecture 5.62](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.19385#S5.SS9) refers back to this conjecture and drops the connectedness condition in the abbreviated statement. The original is the target. Matchings with isolates disprove the survey's literal unrestricted phrasing, but are not the new result.

**Priority search, 2026-11-26.** Checked the [original version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15909), original sections 4–5, the [survey version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19385), and the survey's digraph section. Checked arXiv and publisher/discovery indexes by title, authors, identifier, statement number, “at least two distinct entries,” “nonconstant,” and combinations of the lower/upper invariant, path-cover and comparability terminology. Checked the [MathDB graph-orientation index](https://mathdb.com/database?tag=graph-orientations) and indexed MathOverflow/Lean-library matches. Original partial cases include comparability graphs, graphs with a sufficiently long path, subcubic triangle-free graphs, and regular graphs of small degree. No full prior resolution was found in this bounded search. Original Lemma 4.9 supplies the low orientation, and original Theorem 4.4 characterizes the upper invariant by induced comparability subgraphs. The new argument constructs a single partition certifying strict separation: unextendible endpoints form an induced matching plus isolates, and any one internal vertex can be adjoined. The proof gives its orientations directly.

**Repository and scope.** Searched both branch catalogues and open/closed issues and PRs by source, statement and terminology. [#875](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/875) settles Conjecture 5.3 (computational hardness at value two) from the same paper. [#878](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/878) targets Conjecture 4.30, absence of gaps, which this proof does not imply. [#903](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/903) tracks the present result.

**Separate proof audit.** Re-derived the lower bound on every candidate shortcut length from monotone block index and an advance of at most one in the path coordinate. For the upper certificate, considered a shortest path containing three selected vertices, starting its relevant suffix at its first selected vertex. That suffix cannot leave the selected set. A two-arc segment cannot start in the matching because the only potentially additional neighbor, the extra vertex, sends arcs outward. If the segment starts at the extra vertex and uses a matching edge, the last vertex has the required direct incoming arc, including the case when both matching endpoints are neighbors. This audit does not rely on the source's comparability characterization and handles 3-cycles, singleton paths, disconnected graphs, and paths whose endpoints are joined by chords. The written argument is complete; no independent external referee review is claimed.

**Executable regression.** `python3 results/oriented-general-position-spectrum-nonconstancy/verify.py` enumerates all 33,868 labeled simple graphs of orders zero through six. It computes directed shortest path distances by breadth-first search, then checks the low certificate against every subset of size $b+1$ and checks the high certificate directly. The run passed: 33,748 graphs admit separated witnesses, and 120 are matchings with isolates. This is not computer-dependent mathematics. No Lean formalization was attempted.
