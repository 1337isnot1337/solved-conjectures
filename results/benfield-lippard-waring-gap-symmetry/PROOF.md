# Benfield–Lippard's stable Waring-gap symmetry conjecture

Brennan Benfield and Oliver Lippard define `B^e` to be the stable set of positive offsets not representable as a sum of a prescribed number of positive e-th powers. Their [Conjecture 10.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08193v2#S10.Thmtheorem1) asserts that its largest member is twice its cardinality minus one whenever e is a power of two. This is false: e=64 and e=128 both give strict inequality in the other direction. This also refutes [Conjecture 10.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.08193v2#S10.Thmtheorem2) under its intended finite reflection-domain interpretation, since it implies 10.1. We count these as one symmetry conjecture, not two unrelated resolutions. There is no claim here about Conjectures 10.3, 10.5, 10.6 or priority; see [the parity proof](../benfield-lippard-waring-gap-parity/PROOF.md) for Conjecture 10.4.

## 1. Stable offsets and Frobenius symmetry

Write S_e for the additive submonoid of the nonnegative integers generated by the numbers m^e−1 for all integers m≥2, with zero included. For a positive offset n, j+n is a sum of j positive e-th powers exactly when n is a sum of at most j generators of S_e: terms equal to 1 provide the padding. Thus n is in the stable exception set exactly when n is not in S_e. Nothing about the minimum stabilizing j is needed.

A cofinite submonoid S of the nonnegative integers is called a numerical semigroup. Write F(S) for its largest gap and G(S) for the number of gaps. If 0≤n≤F(S), then n and F(S)−n cannot both belong to S. Hence 2G(S)≥F(S)+1, and equality holds precisely when every such reflection pair has exactly one gap, i.e. when S is symmetric. This includes 0 as a nongap. Thus Conjecture 10.1 is exactly the symmetry assertion for these S_e.

## 2. A finite truncation that is exact

Fix e=64 or e=128 and put a=2^e−1, b=3^e−1, c=5^e−1, D=17^e−1. The arithmetic below is checked by exact integer calculations, independently replayable in [check.py](check.py). Let g=gcd(a,b)=85 and d=gcd(a,b,c)=17; gcd(d,D)=1. Put

    B = (a/g−1)b + (g/d−1)c.

Every residue modulo a that is a multiple of d has a representative in ⟨a,b,c⟩ no larger than B. Indeed, modulo a, the multiples jb for 0≤j<a/g represent exactly the multiples of g. Modulo g, the kc for 0≤k<g/d represent exactly the multiples of d. Choose k to fix the residue modulo g, then choose j for the remaining residue modulo a. Adding multiples of a, all multiples of d beyond B belong to ⟨a,b,c⟩. The same argument adding ℓD (0≤ℓ<d) shows every integer beyond B+(d−1)D belongs to ⟨a,b,c,D⟩.

The exact inequalities are B<7^e−1 and B+(d−1)D<19^e−1. Any prime p satisfying 7≤p≤13 has 17∤p and therefore has 17∣p^e−1 since 16∣e; its generator is redundant by the first bound. Every prime p≥19 has a redundant generator by the second bound. Finally, a composite m=uv contributes no new generator, since (uv)^e−1 = u^e(v^e−1)+(u^e−1); induct on m. This proves the exact semigroup identity

    S_e = ⟨a,b,c,D⟩.

## 3. The nonsymmetric smaller semigroup

Set x=a/d, y=b/d, z=c/d and T=⟨x,y,z⟩. Its gcd is 1 and the preceding bound shows it is cofinite. The three generators are minimal: 1<x<y<z, y is not a multiple of x, and the first check in the table below implies z∉⟨x,y⟩.

Use the three-generator symmetry criterion of [Herzog](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01273309); see also [Numata, Theorem 2.1](https://nihon-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/2000631/files/Numata-Takahiro-3.pdf). If a numerical semigroup is minimally generated by three elements and is symmetric, then for some pair of generators u,v with h=gcd(u,v)>1, the third generator w belongs to ⟨u/h,v/h⟩. We rule this out for every pair.

To test a pair, let X=u/h, Y=v/h, and choose q in [0,X−1] with qY≡w (mod X); since gcd(X,Y)=1, this is possible and unique. If qY>w then there is no nonnegative representation w=iX+jY: every nonnegative j satisfying the congruence has j≥q. The exact checks give h and q as follows; each row also satisfies qY>w.

| exponent | (u,v;w) | h | q |
|---:|:---|---:|---:|
| 64 | (x,y;z) | 5 | 198997453605889410 |
| 64 | (x,z;y) | 1923 | 229627061657430 |
| 64 | (y,z;x) | 256 | 66195206092876816318375305 |
| 128 | (x,y;z) | 5 | 1797117262447424142951374131637808270 |
| 128 | (x,z;y) | 1923 | 9241831223049995711652978025984160 |
| 128 | (y,z;x) | 512 | 1225972396098652645237861052183962306345215133166934804520 |

Consequently T is nonsymmetric, so 2G(T)−1−F(T)>0.

## 4. Nonsymmetry survives the last generator

The remaining exact inequality B<dD implies D∈T. Since gcd(d,D)=1, each residue modulo d can be written uniquely as jD modulo d with 0≤j<d. Within that residue, the elements of S_e are exactly jD+dt with t∈T. In fact, an arbitrary representation has the form ℓD+dt_0, and reducing ℓ=j+dL absorbs LD into T using D∈T. The largest integer missing from this residue is jD+dF(T), so F(S_e)=(d−1)D+dF(T).

If r_j is the smallest nonnegative residue of jD modulo d, the finite initial segment from r_j to jD−d counts (jD−r_j)/d gaps, and thereafter precisely G(T) further gaps occur. The r_j permute 0,1,…,d−1, so

    G(S_e) = dG(T) + (d−1)(D−1)/2,
    2G(S_e)−1−F(S_e) = d(2G(T)−1−F(T)) > 0.

This completes both counterexamples.

## Optional independent certificate for nonsymmetry

This alternative avoids Herzog's theorem and computes the Frobenius number and genus of T directly. Set (L,U,V,H) to the corresponding integer quadruple below.

| e | L | U | V | H |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 64 | 18766804522483320 | 55 | 743739614142711 | 60 |
| 128 | 946318631155207323529554626079834 | 13945 | 364271084930610618508382933752617 | 26520 |

The exact checks are L>V≥0, H>U≥0, LH−UV=x, Ly>Uz, Hz>Vy, and Ly≡Uz, Hz≡Vy modulo x. For any nonnegative pair (i,j), representing i·y+j·z, one may replace (L,0) by (0,U), or (0,H) by (V,0), or (L−V,H−U) by (0,0), whenever the source is componentwise no larger than (i,j). Every move preserves residue modulo x, and strictly decreases weight i·y+j·z. The last congruence and strict inequality follow by adding the first two differences. Thus reduction terminates.

The unreducible pairs fill the rectangle 0≤i<L, 0≤j<H minus the upper-right V-by-U rectangle. It has exactly LH−VU=x pairs. Each residue modulo x is represented by some element of T and hence also by a nonnegative pair, since any copies of x can be discarded modulo x. Reduction preserves residue, so the x unreducible pairs give exactly one per residue. They are necessarily the least weights in their residues: a smaller representation would reduce to the same pair without increasing its weight. Thus they form the Apéry set of T modulo x.

Let W be the largest weight and A the sum of the weights in this shape. By taking both extreme corners and summing the two rectangle contributions,

    W = max((L−1)y+(H−U−1)z, (L−V−1)y+(H−1)z),
    A = y(HL(L−1)−UV(2L−V−1))/2 + z(LH(H−1)−VU(2H−U−1))/2.
    F(T) = W−x, G(T) = (A−x(x−1)/2)/x.

The same checker verifies these formulas give 2G(T)−1−F(T)>0 for both e and prints the resulting positive gap-count defects for S_e.
