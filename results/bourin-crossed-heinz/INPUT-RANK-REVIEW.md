# Independent review of the bounded-input-rank crossed-Heinz reduction

The reviewed FIXED-INPUT-RANK.md has SHA-256 `eaadf7ec99d68e45bc18fb3c30fc23f3440086b0250d9940db915d562734db78`. I found no mathematical gap.

- For an input of rank at most one, every unitarily invariant norm inequality follows from the established operator norm theorem and the positive bound at all Ky Fan ranks at least twice the input rank. In the proof with rank $`r`$, projection onto the input range bounds the appropriate Schatten norm of the second factor by the leading $`r`$ eigenvalue sum with the required exponent. One can also apply the singular-value inequality for the trace norm of a product and then scalar Young term by term. The range-plus-leading-eigenspace test gives the correct lower bound on the denominator. This excludes every possible failing rank outside the claimed interval. [Tian and Xu](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22363v2)
- Perturbing the threshold just above the relevant eigenvalue preserves the strict inequality and bounds the rank of its positive part by $`k-1`$. Avoiding the finitely many shifted eigenvalues of the second input leaves the required resolvent invertible. The positive index bound holds on the intersection of the high spectral space with the kernel of the perturbation of the first input. The refined bound for a positive perturbation holds on the entire high space. When the shifted threshold is nonpositive the low space is empty, since every eigenvalue of the second input is nonnegative and equality with threshold was excluded.
- The real parameter count $`4d^2`$ is correct for four Hermitian matrices. Conic reduction preserves rank-one effects, including their original spectral values, and the identity moment gives an isometry. The high block and retained subspace contain both frames and the whole ranges of the two Hermitian corrections. Thus the two tested ordered products are preserved. A zero-dimensional retained low space allows its removal. The order of the realized pair is always at least the frame rank. [Mädler and Schmüdgen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00957)
- For the operator budget, the complementary block of the shifted operator is strictly positive, even when the perturbation of the first input is indefinite. The common compressed inverse splits as an invertible negative high block plus the positive low moment, so it is invertible as a whole. Its inverse is the Schur complement. Adding a Hermitian term supported on the retained subspace adds precisely that term to the Schur complement. This preserves the Loewner bound for the actual dilated matrix, including singular positive final budgets. The variational comparison needs only that the new numerator retain the chosen frame trace and that its denominator satisfy the original upper bound. Those are exactly the two inequalities proved; no equality of the full matrices or of their entire singular spectra is presumed.
- Keeping the projections of the two frames inside each distinct positive eigenspace of the first input preserves its powers on both frames while it decreases in Loewner order. This yields the asserted second reduction without a multiplicity bound. For a rational exponent the positive common-denominator roots are unique and can be declared by polynomial constraints. The dual threshold/positive-part budget and the isometric trace test give an existential polynomial sentence for strict violation. Quantifier elimination can therefore decide absence of a strict violation as well. The bound independent of the Ky Fan rank applies only to input rank bounded in advance. An arbitrary real exponent still has the reduction; the stated exact decision does not quantify over all such exponents.
- I independently tested the resolvent argument with exact rational arithmetic in 24 finite spectral models, at exponent one third. Each model has a retained high direction and an indefinite perturbation of the first input. Seven or nine distinct low atoms were pruned to four while preserving each of the four moments. A direct elimination in the weighted-coordinate realization recovered the predicted low Schur complement on both sides. All 24 preserved a singular positive budget and also detected a strict failure after a downward perturbation. These calculations check the budget mechanism. They make no claim to produce an instance violating Bourin’s inequality.
- A bounded search on Bourin’s original problem, the subsequent Sababheh–Bourin and Tian–Xu results, rank-constrained inputs and matrix-valued quadrature found the established two-dimensional and norm-specific results and the earlier general quadrature tool. I did not locate this exact gap-independent finite witness result for actual crossed-Heinz inputs. The unrestricted inequality remains open. [Bourin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.1954#page=6) · [Sababheh and Bourin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04567v2)

## Reproducible checks and source comparison

The proof author's [exact check](verify_fixed_input_rank.py) starts from 18 low spectral atoms. Rational reduction preserves two matrix power moments, the identity and the resolvent while retaining 9, 12 and 12 atoms for exponents one half, one third and one quarter. It compares the actual crossed products on the common frames in six pairs of models and 36 weighted-coordinate Schur budgets, including singular and failing controls. The [independent checker](verify_input_rank_schur.py) performs the separate 24-case replay described above.

Question 4's original powers are brought to the normalized form by taking the inputs to power sum and setting the exponent to the first power divided by their sum. Positive powers preserve input rank and the number of distinct positive spectral values. A zero exponent in the original expression with zeroth power taken as identity gives equality. The unrestricted question is still open. Neither the preceding [dimension bound for a specified margin](FINITE-WITNESS.md) nor the [fixed-Ky-Fan-rank approximation](EFFECTIVE-GAP.md) decides exact vanishing at a fixed input rank. The classical matrix-valued quadrature tool and the two independent numerical checks are distinct from the new transfer of the comparison budget to a genuine pair of positive matrices. The proof is written and internally reviewed, with no Lean formalization or external referee report.
