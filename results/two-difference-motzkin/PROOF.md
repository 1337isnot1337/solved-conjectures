# Motzkin density for two forbidden differences in compact abelian groups

Cinna Davis and GPT-6.2 Astra · November 2026 · Unrefereed proof

Let $G$ be a compact Hausdorff abelian group with Haar probability measure, and let $a,b$ be nonzero elements. The Motzkin density $M_G(a,b)$ is the supremum of the measures of Borel sets whose differences avoid $a$ and $b$. If $\widehat G$ denotes the continuous homomorphisms from $G$ to $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$, then

$$
\boxed{M_G(a,b)=\sup_{\chi\in\widehat G}\min(\|\chi(a)\|,\|\chi(b)\|).}
$$

Here $\|r\|$ is the distance to the nearest integer. The theorem treats the complete two-difference case of Candela, Chamizo and Córdoba's Problem 1.1. Their Theorem 2.5 reduces the compact problem to a lattice quotient, and their Theorem 4.1 gives the density when the relation lattice has rank one. We give one proof covering all relation ranks, including finite noncyclic quotients. [Problem 1.1 and earlier results](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09301)

**Prior work on the cyclic case.** Candela, Catalá, Rué and Serra determined the density for two circle differences when at least one is irrational. They bounded the rational case via a finite circulant graph. In May 2026 Carotenuto, Nicoloso and Salvatore published an exact linear-time solution for connected four-regular circulants using odd-cycle transversals. The argument below independently recovers that independence number through a character and applies to general compact abelian groups. [Circle problem](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0081543821040039), [prior exact circulant solution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2026.02.005)

For example, let $G=\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z$, let $a,b$ generate $G$, and write $|r|_N=\min(r\bmod N,(-r)\bmod N)$. The theorem gives the independence number $\alpha=\max_{0\le t<N}\min(|ta|_N,|tb|_N)$. Each independent set of size $s$ implies the existence of a multiplier $t$ with both distances at least $s$.

## The lower bound

For a continuous character $\chi$, set $h=\min(\|\chi(a)\|,\|\chi(b)\|)$. The preimage of $[0,h)$ in the circle avoids both differences. Its measure is $h$ because the image of $G$ is a closed subgroup of the circle and the pushforward of Haar probability is Haar probability on that subgroup. For full circle image this is interval length. For finite image of order $m$, the fraction is again $h$ since $mh$ is integral. This proves the lower bound also when $h=0$.

## The upper bound for finite groups

Let $H$ be any finite abelian group generated by $a,b$, put $N=|H|$, and let $A$ be an independent set in its Cayley graph for these jumps. Write $x(z)$ for its indicator and $\rho=|A|/N$. View the graph as the quotient of the square grid by

$$
\Lambda=\{(u,v)\in\mathbb Z^2:ua+vb=0\text{ in }H\}.
$$

Put $d_1=a$ and $d_2=b$. For the edge in direction $i$ at $z$, define

$$
c_i(z)=1-x(z)-x(z+d_i).
$$

Independence gives $c_i(z)\in\{0,1\}$. It marks the edges with two unoccupied ends. In each direction separately, there are $N-2|A|$ marked edges. Around every unit square the number of marked sides is even, since modulo two each corner indicator occurs twice.

**Assign signs to the marked edges.** On the finite quotient grid, make one dual vertex per square and one dual edge across each marked side. Every dual degree is even. Orient the edges along an Euler tour in each nonempty component. For a marked edge of the original grid, put $w_i(z)=+1$ when the dual arrow passes from the face on the left of the positive grid edge to the face on its right, and $-1$ otherwise. On unmarked edges put $w_i(z)=0$. Count grid edges and squares with their labels even if some endpoints are identified in the quotient. The signed sum of $w$ around a counterclockwise square equals the number of outgoing dual arrows minus incoming dual arrows at its face. Consequently

$$
w_1(z)+w_2(z+a)-w_1(z+b)-w_2(z)=0. \tag{1}
$$

**Integrate and average.** Lift $w$ to the square grid and reverse its sign when traversing an edge backwards. Equation (1) makes its sum along a path depend only on the two endpoints. Interchanging adjacent coordinate steps preserves the sum, as does cancelling a step with its reverse. There is therefore an integer function $h_0$ on $\mathbb Z^2$ with these increments.

For $\lambda=(u,v)\in\Lambda$, the difference $h_0(p+\lambda)-h_0(p)$ is constant in $p$, since its increments in both directions are zero by periodicity. Denote it by $L(\lambda)$. Let $m_i=N^{-1}\sum_z w_i(z)$. Average a fixed path from $p$ to $p+\lambda$ over the $N$ starting cosets. Each positive step in direction $i$ contributes $m_i$, and each negative step contributes $-m_i$. Thus

$$
L(\lambda)=u m_1+v m_2.
$$

On every edge, $w_i\equiv c_i\equiv1+x(z)+x(z+d_i)\pmod2$. In the path sum the internal indicators cancel, and the endpoint indicators agree because $\lambda\in\Lambda$. It follows that $L(\lambda)\equiv u+v\pmod2$. Therefore the map

$$
(u,v)\longmapsto u(1-m_1)/2+v(1-m_2)/2 \pmod1
$$

vanishes on $\Lambda$. It defines a character $\psi$ on $H=\mathbb Z^2/\Lambda$. Finally, $|m_i|\le N^{-1}\sum_z c_i(z)=1-2\rho$, so both $(1-m_i)/2$ belong to $[\rho,1-\rho]$. Evaluating $\psi$ at $a,b$ yields $\min(\|\psi(a)\|,\|\psi(b)\|)\ge\rho$. In the cyclic case $\psi(z)=tz/N\pmod1$ for some integer $t$. This proves the asserted finite formula.

## Passing to a compact abelian group

Let $H=\langle a,b\rangle$ now denote the abstract subgroup generated by the differences in $G$. The restrictions of the continuous characters of $G$ are dense in the character group of discrete $H$, evaluated on $a,b$. To see this, identify each pair of values with a point of $\mathbb T^2$. An integer pair $(u,v)$ annihilates all continuous-character evaluations if and only if every continuous character vanishes at $ua+vb$, which is equivalent to $ua+vb=0$ by separation of points. The closed subgroup theorem for the torus (equivalently, Pontryagin biduality) says that the closure of these evaluations is the annihilator of this integer relation lattice. That annihilator is precisely the pairs arising from homomorphisms $H\to\mathbb T$. Thus each character on a finite quotient of $H$ gives a lower bound on the displayed supremum through its two evaluations. [The torus annihilator argument, Lemma 6.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09301)

Suppose a Borel avoiding set in $G$ has measure $\rho$. If $H$ is finite, some translate of $H$ contains an occupied fraction at least $\rho$, by averaging translations over $G$. Apply the finite result to that independent set.

Otherwise write $H\cong F\times\mathbb Z^d$, where $F$ is finite and $1\le d\le2$. Set $B_n=F\times\{0,\ldots,n-1\}^d$. Averaging over $G$ gives a translate of $B_n$ containing at least $\rho|B_n|$ occupied sites. Let $L$ bound the magnitudes of all free coordinates of both jumps. Delete sites within $L$ layers of any box face, losing at most a fraction $2dL/n$ for $n>2L$. The remaining occupied sites project injectively to $H_n=F\times(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^d$ and remain independent there. Starting in the interior and taking a jump stays in the box, where equality of projected sites implies equality of representatives. For sufficiently large $n$, the images of the two jumps are nonzero. They generate $H_n$.

The finite result gives a character of $H_n$ with both distances at least $\rho-2dL/n$. Pull it back to $H$ and use density of restrictions to bound the supremum over $\widehat G$ by the same quantity from below. Let $n\to\infty$. This proves the upper bound for every Borel avoiding set.

## Computing the value from the relations

Let $\Lambda=\{(u,v):ua+vb=0\}$ in the original compact group. The supremum in the theorem equals the maximum of $\min(\|x\|,\|y\|)$ on the annihilator of $\Lambda$ in $\mathbb T^2$. This yields the following explicit evaluation.

- If $\Lambda=0$, the value is $1/2$, attained on the annihilator at $(1/2,1/2)$.
- If $\Lambda=\langle(u,v)\rangle$ has rank one, put $k=|u|+|v|$. The value is $\lfloor k/2\rfloor/k$, recovering Candela, Chamizo and Córdoba's rank-one theorem. When $k$ is even use $(1/2,1/2)$. When it is odd, choose $x,y$ equal to $\lfloor k/2\rfloor/k$ with the signs of $u,v$ respectively, choosing either sign for a zero coordinate. Then $ux+vy$ is integral. Conversely, if both distances are at least $h$, write $x=(1+e)/2$, $y=(1+f)/2$ modulo one with $|e|,|f|\le1-2h$. Since $ux+vy$ is integral and $u+v$ odd, $ue+vf$ is an odd integer. Thus $1\le k(1-2h)$.
- In rank two, take a lattice basis $(m,0),(s,k)$ with $m,k>0$, $0\le s<m$. The value is the largest of the $mk$ numbers $\min(\|i/m\|,\|j/k-si/(mk)\|)$ for $0\le i<m$ and $0\le j<k$. These are all the characters because the two defining congruences are $mx=0$ and $sx+ky=0$ modulo one. This covers finite subgroups with two nontrivial invariant factors as well as cyclic ones.

## Evaluation without enumerating the finite quotient

In rank two put $N=mk$. The exact independence number and an attaining character can be computed in $O(\log^2 N)$ arithmetic operations on $O(\log N)$-bit integers. The input is the three integers $m,k,s$ describing the quotient. An explicit list of all vertices of a maximum independent set can of course have size proportional to $N$.

For a proposed integer size $1\le h\le N/2$, the first character residue $ki$ must lie in $[h,N-h]$. This restricts $i$ to an integer interval $[I,J]\subseteq[0,m-1]$. At fixed $i$, the possible second residues modulo $N$ are exactly those congruent to $-si\pmod m$. Thus the number of feasible residue pairs is

$$
\sum_{i=I}^{J}\left(
\left\lfloor\frac{N-h+si}{m}\right\rfloor-
\left\lfloor\frac{h-1+si}{m}\right\rfloor
\right).
$$

A positive count is equivalent to feasibility. Binary search finds the greatest feasible $h$. A second binary search on the endpoint $J$ finds the first feasible row, giving its two character residues. For a connected cyclic input $(N,a,b)$, use $k=\gcd(N,a)$, $m=N/k$, and $s\equiv-b(a/k)^{-1}\pmod m$ (take $s=0$ if $m=1$). The relations $(m,0),(s,k)$ hold and their determinant is $N$, so they give the required basis.

For the complexity claim, let $F(n,M,A,B)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\lfloor(Ai+B)/M\rfloor$. Removing the integer quotients of $A,B$ by $M$ adds explicit arithmetic progressions, leaving $0\le A,B<M$. Put $An+B=qM+r$, $0\le r<M$. When $q=0$ the remaining sum is zero. Otherwise $A>0$ and counting the terms at each positive height $j$ gives

$$
F(n,M,A,B)=\sum_{j=1}^q\left(n-\left\lceil\frac{Mj-B}{A}\right\rceil\right)
=F(q,A,M,r).
$$

At each recursion the modulus drops from $M$ to $A$, and the next reduction replaces $M$ by its remainder modulo $A$. This is the Euclidean algorithm. Each binary-search comparison therefore uses $O(\log N)$ arithmetic operations. [Exact evaluator](exact.py)

## Two consequences

**The circle problem.** Write two nonzero rational circle differences as $a/N,b/N$ with their least common denominator $N$. For every $y\in[0,1/N)$, a measurable avoiding set occupies at most $\alpha$ of the points $y+j/N$. Integration gives an upper bound of $\alpha/N$ on its normalized measure. Taking the union of $[j/N,(j+1)/N)$ over a maximum independent set attains it. Thus the displayed formula divided by $N$ is the exact Motzkin density. Together with the authors' Theorem 1.2 for pairs containing an irrational difference, this gives the density for every pair on the circle. Equivalently, the character lower bound of Candela, Chamizo and Córdoba is exact for two circle differences. [Candela et al., Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1134/S0081543821040039), [Candela, Chamizo and Córdoba, Theorem 6.2 and Section 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.09301)

**When the odd-girth bound is sharp.** If the graph has odd girth $k$, put $U=\lfloor N(k-1)/(2k)\rfloor$. Every independent set meets each translated shortest odd cycle in at most $(k-1)/2$ vertices, giving $\alpha\le U$. A connected cyclic graph attains this upper bound exactly when there are $r,s\in[U,N-U]\cap\mathbb Z$ with

$$
br\equiv as\pmod N.
$$

Indeed, the pairs $(ta,tb)$ modulo $N$ lie in the kernel of $(r,s)\mapsto br-as$. This homomorphism is onto by $\gcd(a,b,N)=1$, so the kernel has size $N$. The $N$ pairs $(ta,tb)$ are distinct for the same gcd reason, and hence fill the kernel. The criterion follows from the theorem. For example, at $N=24$, $a=3$, $b=4$, the predicted upper bound from the 7-cycle is 10, but no pair in $[10,14]^2$ satisfies the congruence. The maximum is 9, attained at $t=3$. This is Example 4.4 of the original paper. [Original example](https://upcommons.upc.edu/bitstreams/18c8e105-860c-47a9-a927-cdf120c25909/download)

For a cyclic group in which the jumps generate a proper subgroup, the graph is a disjoint union of isomorphic connected components. With $g=\gcd(N,a,b)$, multiply the answer for $(N/g,a/g,b/g)$ by $g$.
