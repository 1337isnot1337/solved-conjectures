# Review of big-descent Schur positivity

Conjecture 4.9 concerns the fundamental quasisymmetric function indexed by the positions of descents of size at least two, summed over permutations avoiding the increasing pattern. Its quantifiers are all sizes $`n\ge0`$ and all pattern lengths $`m\ge1`$. The theorem also proves positivity for each fixed longest-increasing-subsequence length. [Original conjecture, p. 29](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.15111#page=29) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00026-026-00805-1)

## Independent mathematical review

A constant color in the fundamental function can cover only descents of size one. Its maximal decreasing runs are therefore consecutive value intervals. An ascent between disjoint intervals forces every value of the first to be smaller than every value of the next. Ordering all runs by value, recording their colors and lengths, and then sorting by color reconstructs the permutation and its coloring in both directions. Selecting at most one value from each run identifies its longest increasing subsequence with the longest weakly increasing subsequence of the color word. The ordinary word RSK correspondence gives precisely the asserted Schur series and its first-row restriction.

I derived the content coefficient by differentiating the numerator alternant and subtracting the differentiated Vandermonde. The two contributions to an addable box in row $`j`$ differ by exactly its column minus row. Iteration over standard skew tableaux gives the product of contents. An omitted diagonal cell kills the term. With every diagonal retained, the two components of the skew shape lie on opposite sides of the Durfee square, with incomparable cells between the components. Interleavings supply the binomial factor that cancels when taking normalized skew dimensions. Factoring row and column factorials from their two determinants gives the Pascal minors with the common leg sign. All these minors have nonnegative sign in the simultaneous decreasing index order.

For the remaining signed transform, I checked the tableau corner recurrence before antisymmetrizing. A corner shortens a valid arm, a valid leg, or deletes the last diagonal cell when both its arm and leg have length zero. Permuting leg coordinates moves that last term with precisely the sign of the omitted variable. Substituting $`z_i=x_i/(1+x_i)`$ turns the elementary monomial coefficient of degree $`r`$ in the denominator into $`1-r`$. Its formal inverse is Schur-positive by the Littlewood–Richardson rule. At a strictly decreasing exponent vector the omitted-variable contribution is zero unless the last coordinate is zero, when only the positive last omission survives. Induction over the Durfee size and then the arm total proves positivity for each fixed arm sequence. The first arm records the exact longest increasing subsequence bound. The size-zero and empty-pattern-class cases agree with the source conventions. [Written proof](PROOF.md)

## Reproducible evidence

The supplied program reproduced the content recurrence and Frobenius formula through degree 15 and the direct permutation/Schur extraction through degree eight. A separate program written for this review checked all 362,880 permutations on nine letters against the two-variable Schur expansion, separately for every first-row length and color multiplicity. It also evaluated 36 signed transforms at Durfee sizes four and five using hook lengths and explicit signed permutation sums for the determinants. Both programs use standard Python and exact integers. The formal-series proof covers arbitrary degree. [Original verifier](verify.py) · [Independent checks](check_review.py)

## Source and priority

The original authors’ unrestricted theorem follows earlier work on Eulerian quasisymmetric functions. The new result treats every increasing pattern and refines it by the actual longest increasing subsequence. Searches covered the original article and preprint, the authors’ subsequent work, the related statistics on restricted permutations and tableaux, and the terms big descent sets, Schur positivity and increasing-pattern avoidance. I found no earlier proof of this assertion in those sources. The named real-rootedness questions about big-descent polynomials concern a different statistic and conclusion. The proof is a repository-reviewed, unrefereed result.
