# Target identity and bounded priority audit — 2026-11-26

**Verdict:** no prior resolution of the two exact Brown–Cameron assertions was located in the sources below. This is a bounded, negative search result, not a guarantee of priority. Two attractive alternative targets *did* have earlier counterexamples, one with the same type of construction considered here. They were excluded before doing computational proof work on them.

## Closest literature: what it actually proves

* Brown & Cameron, *On the Unimodality of Independence Polynomials of Very Well-Covered Graphs*, [arXiv:1709.08236v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.08236v1). Conjecture 3.2 was matched to this exact version. The [Discrete Mathematics record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2017.10.007) was used for metadata and citation searches only; the journal full text was not accessed. Cameron's [2019 thesis](https://hdl.handle.net/10222/75412), Conj. 6.1.1 and Problem 6.2.1, repeats both questions. Distinguish preprint Conj. 3.3 on **trees**: unstable trees already occur in Brown–Cameron's 2018 work and answer a different question.
* Brown & Cameron, [EJC 25(1), P1.46](https://doi.org/10.37236/7280), [arXiv:1802.02478](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02478). The published PDF and preprint specify the closed half-plane and ask the density question explicitly after constructing unstable graph families. No arbitrary fixed family of exceptional graphs answers it.
* Brown & Nowakowski, [*Average independence polynomials*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2004.10.001). The reported conclusion that a nonreal root is typical would **not** imply a right-half-plane or even an out-of-sector root. Gubarev, [arXiv:1808.03932](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03932), §13.1, also questions the proof of the nonreal-root claim. We do not rely on either side of that dispute. Gubarev's §10.2 results on some large real roots of the reciprocal PC-polynomial, and the roots of the *expected* clique polynomial, do not constrain all roots of the random sample polynomial. [On the Roots of Expected Independence Polynomials](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.21678) is likewise about a different operation: taking zeros does not commute with taking expectations.
* [Makowsky & Rakita, EJC 2022](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2022.103637), [arXiv:2102.00268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.00268). Their real-rootedness theorem expressly excludes the independence polynomial (see discussion following Theorem 1.15); their Question 4.4 asks for **unimodality**, not log-concavity or stability. Here the violated inequality is near the top and does not produce a valley.
* Follow-up work: [complete bipartite graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24381), [spiders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04694), [well-covered Ferrers graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26962), [symmetric-function approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.04245), and [Lorentzian polynomials](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37553). These deal with special classes or sufficient hypotheses, not a typical unrestricted $G(n,p)$. Constructed nonstable families, including purely imaginary root studies, do not establish a positive density of unstable graphs.
* Classical clique-number threshold estimates and the Hurwitz minor are not claimed as discoveries. The point is that a nonvanishing probability of **at least one** top set, coupled with concentration of the three preceding levels, forces a sign failure. A familiar estimate for a single clique-number location, or a stability test in isolation, is not the conclusion of this argument. Both components used in the proof were derived explicitly.
* Also searched using the statistical-mechanics name “hard-core partition function,” complex fugacity, Lee–Yang zeros, and the random graph model. [arXiv:2610.40510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40510) concerns a zero-free region for random **regular** graphs, not all zeros of dense independent-edge graphs. No equivalent resolution surfaced under this change of terminology.
