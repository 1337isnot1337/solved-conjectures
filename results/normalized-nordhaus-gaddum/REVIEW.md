# Independent review of the normalized connectivity gap

I checked the full reconstructed proof of the square-root upper gap on 27 November 2026. The paper’s §4.2 statement concerns the sum of the second-smallest normalized Laplacian eigenvalues of a graph and its complement. The proof establishes an absolute positive lower bound on the deficit from two, scaled by the square root of the order, for every graph of order at least three. The even-order example in Proposition 8 has scaled deficit tending to the square root of two. The issue credits Cinna Davis with the result and the projected-row and two-regime argument. The present internal review covers the reconstructed manuscript. [Primary statement and sharp-order example](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.12751v1#S4.SS2) · [Original claim](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/495)

1. **Finite orders.** A disconnected graph contributes zero under the stated isolated-vertex convention. Its complement contributes at most $`n/(n-1)`$ by trace and the zero eigenvalue. If both are connected, each is incomplete and contains a nonedge. This gives a zero principal block of order two in normalized adjacency, hence a nonnegative second eigenvalue by interlacing. If that eigenvalue vanishes, the positive semidefinite degree-corrected matrix annihilates the difference of the two nonadjacent vertex indicators scaled by their degree ratio. The adjacency columns must then be proportional and, because their entries are binary and their supports nonempty, identical. Nonadjacency together with equality partitions the graph into independent classes, with every cross-class edge present. Its complement would be disconnected. Thus every fixed order above two has a positive deficit. At order two the connected edge and empty graph have sum two.
2. **Common projection.** The rank-one term in the positive semidefinite bound follows by splitting normalized adjacency into its Perron direction and orthogonal complement. Congruence by the square root of the degree matrix gives exactly the rank-one degree correction stated. Complementary degrees lie in the same two-dimensional span with the constant vector. Compressing both inequalities therefore gives the bound $`L=1+n(\theta+\beta)`$. For any compressed unit vector $`u`$, positive semidefinite Cauchy–Schwarz gives $`|u^{\mathsf T}He_i|^2\le L H_{ii}`$. Supremizing over $`u`$ verifies (1). The required products $`L\theta,L\beta`$ tend to zero along every proposed violating sequence.
3. **Positive limiting densities.** If all degrees were uniformly close to zero or full degree in relative terms, both groups would have positive density. Counting their between-group edges from each side gives incompatible asymptotics. An intermediate neighborhood therefore exists on a subsequence. Its normalized centered indicator is close to the centered degree line. Its coordinates are uniformly of order at most $`1/\sqrt n`$, so any vanishing-density neighborhood has negligible projection on that line. Equation (1) excludes such a row for either graph. With all degrees bounded away from both endpoints, closeness to the two possible normalized indicator directions implies uniform $`o(n)`$ symmetric differences. Symmetry forces the row and column signs to agree or disagree globally except on $`o(n)`$ indices. The resulting partition gives one of the two graphs a cut with $`o(n^2)`$ edges and both side volumes of order $`n^2`$. Its normalized connectivity tends to zero.
4. **Vanishing limiting density.** The slowly increasing typical-degree multiplier makes both terms of (1) vanish uniformly on all typical vertices, who comprise a proportion approaching one. Their unit neighborhood indicators have negligible constant projection. Since they are nonnegative and close to a common line, all have the same sign on that line. Comparison with the minimum-degree neighborhood gives both relative size and near-total overlap, uniformly even for bounded minimum degree. Summing the overlaps identifies a set comprising almost all of that neighborhood whose members have complement degrees $`o(n)`$. For these vertices the dense-complement estimate (1) again applies uniformly. Their complement neighborhoods must approach the same oriented line, since their inner product with the nonnegative minimum-degree indicator is nonnegative. Therefore all but a vanishing fraction of the complementary volume of this set lies on internal edges in the degree-volume count. Its outside volume is of order $`n^2`$, while its own volume is at most a constant times the squared minimum degree and hence $`o(n^2)`$. The normalized cut inequality forces complement connectivity to zero.
5. **Uniformity and sharpness.** Every hypothetical sequence of scaled deficits tending to zero has a density-convergent subsequence, and the two regimes, with a complement exchange if necessary, exhaust those limits. Both contradict convergence of both normalized connectivities to one. Finiteness at each fixed order and the disconnected trace bound give one absolute positive constant. From Proposition 8, the exact deficit on the authors’ even family is $`(\sqrt{2n+1}-1)/n+1/(n-2)`$. Thus the exponent is sharp while its optimal constant remains undetermined.

I found no missing case or unjustified spectral or compactness step. This is an internal review of a written proof without a Lean formalization or external refereeing.
