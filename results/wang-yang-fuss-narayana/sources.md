# Sources and prior-resolution search

The target is Conjecture 4.1 of Yi Wang and Arthur L. B. Yang, *Total positivity of Narayana matrices*, Discrete Mathematics **341** (2018). The journal text specifies $m\ge2$, separately defines both orientations of each triangle, and tests finite sections before posing the conjecture. The earlier preprint includes $m=1$, which the same paper proves. The Fuss–Narayana entries are Armstrong's rank numbers for $m$-divisible noncrossing partitions of Coxeter types A and B. [Journal paper, §4](https://cfc.nankai.edu.cn/_upload/article/files/ca/07/264e89bc4d9bb418a6d91d482b90/ef62459f-bec2-470d-8199-b57a790de20a.pdf) · [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.07822)

On 26 November 2026 I searched the conjecture number, original paper title, both spellings of Fuss–Narayana, and the triangle, square, total positivity and total nonnegativity formulations. I also inspected the citing-title lists from OpenAlex (20 records) and Semantic Scholar (24 records including duplicate versions), then checked the mathematical objects in the closest papers. The search located no previous resolution of the six-array statement. The accessible records leave the usual possibility of an unindexed argument or an indirect specialization. A Hankel matrix uses the sum of its row and column indices to select a polynomial. The conjecture here uses coefficients indexed individually by row and column. For this paper I could inspect the publisher's abstract and bibliography. [Publisher](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms.70450) |

These three papers give exact definitions and their respective positivity conclusions. The product determinant used in the proof is classical. The change-of-basis criterion used for the reversed triangles is Galvin and Pacurar's theorem. The repository's 20 November campaign had already identified entry normalizations and a recurrence in the square's row index. The present argument inserts intermediate columns in the other direction and handles both kinds of triangular boundary. [Krattenthaler, Lemma 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9902004) · [Galvin–Pacurar, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.08658) · [Earlier research note](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/tree/research/research/wang-yang-fuss-narayana) · [Coordination and review](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/98)

## Independent review and catalogue check

An independent researcher from the [planar degree campaign](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/785) checked both square ratios, the order of the intermediate factors across successive column groups, the adjacent-difference minor argument, the limiting triangular test and the restricted-growth condition for the two reversed matrices. The reviewer independently reran the exact checks and reported no mathematical gap. This is an independent research review within the project. [Detailed review receipt](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/98#issuecomment-7188223846) · [Reviewed proof revision](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/af61bc46bbf238ab65693fee279c24af08b15a49/results/wang-yang-fuss-narayana/proof.md)

Immediately before publication I compared the original DOI, preprint identifier, family name and six-matrix statement against both permanent branches and searched again for published proofs. The existing catalogue entry called “Wang–Yang's conjecture” concerns signed forward differences of Xi coefficients. The 20 November Fuss–Narayana research note is the earlier campaign on this same target. The MathDB record reproduces the preprint question and identifies it as unresolved in its source.
