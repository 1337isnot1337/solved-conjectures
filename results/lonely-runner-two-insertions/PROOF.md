# Two unrestricted extra speeds on a short interval

Write ||x|| for the distance from x to the nearest integer. For any integer p>=3 and any two positive integer speeds b<=c, the interval J=[1/(p+2),1/p] contains a time t with both ||bt|| and ||ct|| at least 1/(p+2). This gives a two-speed insertion for all base speed sets of size p-1 that maintain this separation on J, including every Goddyn–Wong multi-acceleration of 1,...,p-1. The added speeds have no upper bound.

Write q=p+2 and delta=1/q, so |J|=2/(pq). If any of the times 1/p, 1/(p+1), 1/q works, we are done. Otherwise each of p,p+1,q divides at least one of b,c, since a nonzero residue with denominator at most q already gives the required separation. One speed must be a multiple of two denominators, whence c>=pq/2 by the pairwise gcds.

Suppose first that p>=11. For b>=2p, the fraction of J on which at least one speed is bad is at most

    4/q + p/b + p/c <= 1/2 + 6/q < 1.

Indeed a speed v occupies a bad length at most 2 delta |J| + 2 delta/v, by splitting into whole periods and one remainder. It remains to consider b<2p. The only possible divisors of b among the three denominators occur when b equals the denominator itself.

* If b=p, then (p+1)q divides c. Take t=1/(p+1)+1/(qc), inside J. The c-phase moves from an integer to delta. The b-phase starts at 1-1/(p+1) and moves by at most 1/((p+1)q), so remains good.
* Otherwise p divides c and p does not divide b. We claim c>=pb/2. For b=p+1 use c>=pq/2. For b=q use p(p+1) dividing c. When b equals none of the denominators use the same divisibility and b<2p. Now take t=1/p-1/(qc) in J. The c-phase is minus delta modulo one. The b-phase at 1/p had distance at least 1/p, and its displacement is at most 2/(pq)=1/p-delta. This proves the claim for p>=11.

For 3<=p<=10 the same measure estimate is strictly below one as soon as b>2pq/(p-2), since c>=b. For every remaining b, intersect J with its exact closed good arcs and choose a longest resulting interval, of length w. The finite loop in two_additions.py checks w>0 in every case. Any counterexample must satisfy c<2/(qw), because otherwise a single open bad arc for c is too short to contain that whole interval. All remaining integer c are checked by direct rational interval intersection. This treats equality endpoints explicitly and imposes no cutoff without a proof.

For the application, let p be the old total runner count and start with a Goddyn–Wong tight base of p-1 moving speeds. An unchanged speed r<=p-1 stays good throughout J. Consider an accelerated speed w=mr with m>=2, put s=p-r, and recall their criterion gcd(r,k)>1 for all s<=k<ms. The interval cannot contain 1, so s>=2. If s>=r, the interval contains at least r consecutive integers, including one coprime to r. Thus s<r. Since r-1 is coprime to r and is at least s, it cannot be in the interval. Hence ms<=r-1. We show (m-1)(p+2)<w<mp, or equivalently p-ms>2m-2 for the first of these inequalities. For m=2, p-ms>=s+1>=3 suffices. For m>=3 we have r/2-2>=s: from r>=ms+1 this follows if (m-2)s>=3, and the remaining case m=3,s=2 requires 2,3,5 all to divide r. Choose an integer coprime to r between r/2-2 and r/2, namely (r-1)/2 for r odd, r/2-1 for r divisible by 4, and r/2-2 otherwise. This integer cannot lie in [s,ms-1], so ms<=r/2. Hence p-ms>=r/2+s>=ms+s>2m-2. The two endpoint phases are strictly between the same consecutive integers and have denominators at most q. By linearity the entire interval J is good. Applying the two-speed result proves the ordinary Lonely Runner bound after insertion. A dilation of all the base and additional speeds preserves the conclusion. The conjecture for arbitrary speed sets remains open.

The tight base family and its gcd criterion are due to [Goddyn and Wong](https://www.sfu.ca/~goddyn/Papers/063tight_lonely_runner.pdf). This note concerns extending it, not priority for its construction.

The corresponding three-speed interval assertion already fails. For p=3 take extra speeds 4,6,10. On [1/6,1/3] the first two leave just [7/36,5/24] and [7/24,11/36]. Multiplying the first by 10 puts it strictly inside (2-1/6,2+1/6), and multiplying the second by 10 puts it strictly inside (3-1/6,3+1/6). Both are therefore excluded. The ordinary five-speed tuple (1,2,4,6,10) succeeds elsewhere, for example at 3/8. Thus the two-speed lemma cannot simply be iterated with three arbitrary speeds on the corresponding prescribed interval. The verifier checks the two surviving intervals and the witness time using rational arithmetic.
