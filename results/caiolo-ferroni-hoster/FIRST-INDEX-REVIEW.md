# Independent review of the first-index counterexample

**Disposition, 27 November 2026.** The argument answers the unnumbered question immediately following Corollary 8.1 in Badalov’s *Prescribed Turán sign patterns for inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomials of matroids*. Corollary 8.1 concerns Badalov’s own examples. The subsequent question quantifies over arbitrary matroids whose ordinary inverse Kazhdan–Lusztig polynomial has degree at least two. The rank-five example meets these hypotheses. [Primary statement, §8](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13417#S8) · [Reviewed proof](FIRST-INDEX.md)

**Source and attribution.** Gao and Xie’s Conjecture 4.2 asked about log-concavity at every internal index. Badalov had already refuted it. His examples realize prescribed negative determinants at indices at least two while their first determinant is positive. Bérczi, Coron, Eur and Li supplied the coalescence method for ordinary Chow and chain polynomials. The previously published dual Chow counterexample supplies the particular matroid, its existence proof and its flat counts. Cinna Davis obtained the inverse-polynomial result from this family in the research commits linked below. The new claim concerns the first index of ordinary $`Q`$. No conclusion about unimodality, an arbitrary prescribed pattern including index one, or inverse-$`Z`$ log-concavity follows. [Gao–Xie](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.15349) · [Badalov](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.13417#S8) · [Bérczi–Coron–Eur–Li](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.17135) · [Original deduction](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/4ac56dc7dc5881cae37a0bc4cc92000496383051) · [Original verifier](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/11b0de79055937ff1fb01d11eb10e5d455afc30f)

**Matroid and coefficients.** The original block-intersection and augmentation arguments apply to every prime power. The incidence correction includes one geometric four-flat and each of the distinct old three-space/auxiliary-point hyperplanes in the block. A rank-four flat cannot be removed twice. Reading Gao–Xie Theorem 1.2 with the restriction below the flat and contraction above gives the written flag formula. As a second route, the original verifier uses palindromicity of the inverse-$`Z`$ polynomial to solve for $`Q`$ in the inversion of Braden–Ferroni–Matherne–Nepal Theorem 2.2. The sign is $`(-1)^{r-d}`$ under inversion, and in even rank the central coefficient is set by the strict degree bound on $`Q`$. Both original calculations were replayed in Python 3.12 with SymPy 1.14.0. [Construction](PROOF.md) · [Braden–Ferroni–Matherne–Nepal](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01086)

**Independent calculation.** The reviewer separately implemented the characteristic recursion for ordinary $`P`$, followed by signed incidence inversion $`Q(x,y)=(-1)^r(P^{-1})(x,y)`$ on an interval of rank $`r`$. The program expands each product over the rank of the intermediate flat and retains the full sequence of internal ranks. Möbius weights are alternating strict-chain counts. It constructs product-lattice flags from the smallest flat using vector-space quotients and unused Boolean elements. The only shared data are the proved matroid and its hyperplane types. The resulting three coefficients at $`q=17`$ and their negative determinant agree exactly with the original two calculations. [Gao–Xie, Theorems 1.1–1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.15349) · [Review calculation](check_first_index_incidence.py)

**All prime powers.** The review implementation evaluates the polynomial formulas at the 97 consecutive integers starting at 17. This determines the defect identically. Indeed, for an uncorrected flag with total rank at most four, each increment of one vector dimension costs at most eight in polynomial degree, and each increment of one Boolean element costs at most four. In choosing Boolean elements after $`j`$ are fixed the factor is a fixed-lower-index binomial in $`q^4-j`$. Thus degree 48 bounds every uncorrected flag. Inside a corrected hyperplane the flag rank is at most three, the ambient dimension at most four, and the Boolean order fixed. Its prefactor has degree at most 19. Degree 48 consequently bounds each coefficient, and 96 bounds their products. In the Newton expansion at 17, all 44 forward differences of $`c_0c_2-c_1^2`$ of orders zero through 43 are positive and all subsequent differences through 96 vanish. The functions $`\binom{q-17}{j}`$ are nonnegative for integral $`q\ge17`$ and the constant term is positive. The coefficient of $`q^{43}`$ is $`1/2`$. Separate forward differences give the exact degrees and leading coefficients of all three $`c_j`$, verifying the stated asymptotic.
