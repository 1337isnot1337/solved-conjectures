## Exact claim and prior work

- [Laurent Bétermin, *Minimal Soft Lattice Theta Functions*, Remark 5.10](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00365-019-09494-x): fixed FCC and BCC, all positive Gaussian scales, optimizing translations. The publisher’s text contains both the geometric assertion and the incorrect explicit BCC coordinate. Section 2 defines BCC using the cubic lattice together with the all-half translate, with an overall factor making the covolume one. The printed representative is neither a deep hole nor a universal minimizer. The corrected coordinate is a direct consequence of the equality-case classification.
- [Bachoc–Moustrou–Vallentin–Zimmermann, *Polarization of lattices: Stable cold spots and spherical designs*](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2026.10169), Theorem 1.3 and §1.6, prove equality between Gaussian minimizers and deep holes of all root lattices for sufficiently large scale, and raise the all-scale question. Those results do not give the all-scale conclusion for FCC or BCC.
- [Caius Folkerts, *Universal cold spots of E8 and related lattices*, §2.2](https://github.com/caiusdfolkerts/research/tree/9fcbca1143471e6e25c337def9897b58377e0b4c/cold_spots), records the equivalent one-variable quartic theta identity. That paper’s higher-rank checkerboard variant is the all-half translate of the even-sum lattice, not the dual used here. Credit for this antecedent does not imply it established the FCC/BCC statement.
- [Bétermin–Petrache](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08716) discuss scale limits and optimization over lattices in shape families. That distinct optimization is not claimed here.

The Nov. 24, 2026 search used the exact remark and authors, “universal cold spots,” ordinary and dual checkerboard notation, “FCC,” “BCC,” and all-scale shifted theta terminology, including current literature and public research records. It found the antecedents above but no earlier proof for the exact both-lattice statement. No absolute priority claim is made.

## Review of the proof

1. **Dual checkerboard.** The product expression follows by summing independently in the two cosets. The log-ratio coordinate is a bijection onto a closed symmetric interval, and its derivative is strictly positive. The derivative of the even logarithmic factor with respect to this coordinate is minus the cosine parameter times a positive weighted mean. On the positive half of the interval that mean is nondecreasing by the covariance identity, so strict concavity follows on both halves by symmetry. At a minimizing configuration, two interior coordinates with fixed sum would give a strictly concave one-dimensional restriction, a contradiction. For odd rank the remaining endpoint imbalance is even; a nonzero imbalance can tie the balanced value only when the free coordinate is an endpoint, not the unique minimum of the reduced one-dimensional Gaussian sum.
2. **Ordinary checkerboard.** An independent derivation verifies the necessary scalar bounds: in the square of the Gaussian sum, write the two summation indices as their half-sum and half-difference. The parity projector gives
   `a(t)^2+b(t)^2=2 a_(2α)(t) a_(2α)(0)` and
   `a(t)^2−b(t)^2=2 a_(2α)(t−1/2) a_(2α)(1/2)`.
   The quartic is their product and is minimized exactly when the absolute cosine is 1, by the Jacobi products. The ratio of the second to the first is strictly smallest at integer translations, proving the endpoint maximum of `|b|/a`. The degree-three ratio bound has a strictly negative derivative away from zero. The Hölder equality cases distinguish odd-sum integers (rank three), those integers and half-integers (rank four), and half-integers only (higher ranks).
3. **Distance interpretation and scaling.** Coordinates rounded to integers give all possible needed parity adjustments. For the dual, the distance is the lower of the two coset distances; the fixed-sum convexity argument leaves at most one fractional coordinate and proves the same equality cases as the Gaussian sum. The squared unscaled distances for the two BCC representatives are exactly 5/16 and 1/4. Overall scaling changes the scale parameter and cannot fix the discrepancy.

The analytical argument is independent of numerical diagnostics. [verify.py](verify.py) makes 40,836 finite Gaussian comparisons using standard-library high-precision decimal arithmetic and checks corresponding distances using exact rational arithmetic. These are supplementary spot checks, not certified infinite sums or a substitute for the proof. No Lean or external referee review is claimed.
