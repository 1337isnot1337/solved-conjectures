# Independent review of the atomic-limit obstruction

**Reviewed written partial theorem, November 27, 2026.** The target is Erdős's relaxation of the planar pinned-distance problem to positive circles centered at sites, under the additional assumption that the number of noncollinear isosceles trapezoids, including rectangles, is at most a fixed constant times the square of the order. The result constrains a hypothetical sequence attaining the mean one-third barrier. It supplies neither a verified counterexample nor a proof that every such sequence is impossible. [Erdős, 1987, p. 168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2) · [Open tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/969)

A second Codex research agent in the WadeDavis session independently read the complete [argument](ATOMIC-LIMITS.md), Appendix C of the arbitrary-collinearities proof, and the marked-carrier transfer in the sparse-trapezoid theorem. The review confirmed the dependence on the global quadratic constant alone at a fixed node. It checked the order of the carrier and axis cutoffs, the incidence bound on intersections of two distinct large carriers, the limiting domination for every Borel null relation, and the use of independent pin–base marginals after conditioning on a point or direction atom. It also checked the decomposition into countably many circles, their coverage by strict-Lipschitz arcs and the separate singleton at radius zero. The two expanding disks in the extraction argument preserve all but a vanishing proportion of full internal pin–base pairs.

The review checked both fiber bounds and both continuum examples. In the diffuse-bearing bound the heavy fiber location has zero global site mass, so the product-null diagonal forbids three copies there. The number of escaped bases has conditional mean three times the escaped mass; the expected heavy-fiber excess is three times the indicated integral. The circle and chord examples satisfy the specified limiting marginals and establish an obstruction to reasoning from these marginals alone. They do not produce a finite planar sequence. This proof has no Lean verification or external refereeing. The supporting scripts are reproducible finite checks.

**Comparison and bounded search.** The two permanent branches, earlier #969 results and issue discussions were compared by the original author, one-third mean threshold, trapezoids, bisector energy, vague site limits, atomic and nonatomic parts, escaping bearings and projections. The mixed-circle paper bounds large exact circles, the uniform-disk note treats a particular limit law, and the residual-weight theorem obtains a gap under additional hereditary hypotheses. The predecessors prove the chosen-carrier estimates and strict-Lipschitz graph obstruction used here. The new argument is the mutual exclusion of singular sides, its consequences for every vague limit under the global bound, and the explicit limits of a marginal-only argument. Bounded literature searches found upper energy estimates and structural alternatives in Lund–Sheffer–de Zeeuw, and bisector and pinned-distance bounds in Lund–Petridis. The continuum pinned-distance and projection literature includes dimension and regularity results under different hypotheses. The inspected statements do not assert the present finite near-extremizer dichotomy. [Lund–Sheffer–de Zeeuw](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6868) · [Lund–Petridis](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00765) · [Shmerkin](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00039-019-00500-9) · [Earlier residual-weight result](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1025)

The [exact checker](check_atomic_limits.py) inspects 300 circle triples in a square-grid fixture and 288 two-side witnesses across its carrier thresholds. It validates the chord Jacobian at 480 rational parameter choices, the full circle-triple Jacobian at 112, and all nine possible heavy/finite/escaped counts once a triple heavy repetition is excluded. A symmetric three-label example checks the sharp marginal value of two thirds.
