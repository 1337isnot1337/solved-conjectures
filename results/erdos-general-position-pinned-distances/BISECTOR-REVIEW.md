# Review of the active-bisector criterion and the disk model

A separate Codex reviewer in the WadeDavis research session checked the two written arguments on 27 November 2026 UTC. They give a sufficient condition for the positive one-third gap and a sharp obstruction to using only limiting pair moments. The full question for all site-centered capacity-three configurations remains open. [Bisector statement and proof](TWO-SIDED-BISECTORS.md) · [Disk construction and endpoint proof](DISK-MOMENT.md) · [Tracking issue #969](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/969)

For the bisector proof, the number of ordered base-coordinate choices in a full radius class is six. A fixed ordered pair within it has one completion per active pin. Thus its mass is its active count divided by six times the number of full classes. For a local triangle, choosing one side and its pin charges at most one triangle to that incidence. The ordinary pairs cost at most the fixed per-pair allowance times the number of base pairs plus the total excess. Nominated pairs give at most the fixed axis allowance times the unweighted incidence bound. Heavy axes have total incidence at most a constant times the sum of the dyadic rich-line estimates, giving the stated vanishing tail after normalization. Truncation followed by weak limits gives absolute continuity of the limiting base-pair law on augmented site coordinates. Restriction of the original nominations to an internal subset is admissible, because a radius with three sites internally already attains the ambient cap. All constants in the subset estimates can depend on the fixed allowances.

The previous farthest-site sampling, tightness and rescaling arguments require regularity of each fixed sample, local counts uniform in the chosen subsets, and independent limiting pin–base marginals. The mixed-circle estimate and the earlier line estimate give that sample regularity despite unrestricted ambient lines and circles. In a terminal node on the Lipschitz graph, coincident bases have zero probability by absolute continuity on the positive nonatomic restricted graph. In an infinite-path node the same statement applies to ties of either limiting empirical rank; every strict pair comparison persists. A set in the resulting double sector lies on a graph of Lipschitz constant below one and cannot supply three distinct bases at one pin-radius. These arguments close the contradiction without the old condition on the carriers of several reflected pairs.

For the disk construction the random common phase makes each arc coordinate individually uniform, even after conditioning on all jitters. The pairwise normalized angle separation stays in the closed interval from one quarter to three quarters. The joint density is at most twelve because the difference of two independent uniform jitters has that supremum. On the equal-distance locus the two coarea Jacobians give the factor radius over base separation. If the accessible arc is wide, separation bounds the pin to a segment of length proportional to that separation. For a narrow arc the two bases both lie within a constant times squared separation of the boundary. Two such points at separation at most a small radius occupy product area bounded by a constant times the fifth power of that radius. This supplies the claimed weak endpoint for the constructed density.

In the reverse direction a pair co-farthest from a pin is an edge of the upper paraboloid subdivision for a sample in general position. Its probability for a specified ordered sample pair is bounded by the expected hull size divided by the square of the sample size, up to a constant. The line-of-chord Jacobian and the disk cap area integral give the cube-root hull-size bound. Conditioning a mate on the pin and one independent sampled base leaves the other sampled sites independent of that pair. Hölder's inequality for the truncated density at exponent five halves now gives a contradiction in the strong endpoint space. Weak convergence and duality transfer a putative uniformly bounded finite moment to such a prohibited strong density. The construction itself makes no finite counterexample claim.

The exact checker passed 435 capacity-three grid subsets, 30,744 optimal axis-nomination tests, 12,405 co-farthest sample graphs and 36,000 discrete angular triples. The asymptotic claims use the written arguments. There is no Lean formalization or external referee report. [Finite checker](check_bisector_moments.py)
