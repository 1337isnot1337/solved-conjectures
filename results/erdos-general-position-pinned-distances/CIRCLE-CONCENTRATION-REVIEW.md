# Independent review of the mixed-circle incidence bound

27 November 2026 UTC. The manuscript reviewed was `erdos-circle-concentration.md`, SHA-256 `99c690130a0868429f6330e8530cde5cd9c8c89442041800f969ec8feaf5f392`. The proof of the mixed isosceles bound and the stated consequence for site-centered capacity three pass the checks below. They give a necessary condition for a hypothetical near-extremum; they leave the strict positive-gap question open.

1. **One double cover.** In unit-circle coordinates $`u,v`$, multiplying the squared-distance equality by $`uv`$ indeed gives $`v^2-[z+(1-|z|^2)u+\bar z u^2]v+u^2=0`$. For $`0<|z|\ne1`$ the first discriminant factor has distinct nonzero roots $`z,1/\bar z`$. The second factor differs by $`4u`$, hence does not vanish at either. In particular $`z`$ is a simple root of $`D_z`$ even at the tangency radius $`|z|=3`$, when the second factor can have a double root. If $`D_w/D_z`$ is square, the simple root $`w`$ of $`D_w`$ must occur in $`D_z`$, giving at most four choices of $`w`$ for each $`z`$. The excluded origin has at most two witnesses per pin.
2. **Two covers and ordered parameter identifiability.** Two distinct nonsquare classes give a biquadratic field. The two monic equations in $`v,v'`$ give an affine algebra free of rank four over $`\mathbb C[u]`$. Its injection into the generic field proves irreducibility without excluding any fiber over finite $`u`$. The equation viewed in $`u`$ is $`(1-\bar zv)u^2-(1-|z|^2)vu+v(v-z)=0`$. At $`v=0`$ the leading coefficient is one and hence projective $`u=\infty`$ is impossible. The remaining $`u=0`$ gives $`v'=0,w`$, both of which occur in the affine irreducible locus. Projectivity of the auxiliary $`u`$-line proves that the closure cannot add further finite values. Equivalently, its resultant with the second quadratic at $`v=0`$ is $`[v'(v'-w)]^2`$. Interchanging the roles retrieves $`z`$. Thus distinct nonexceptional *ordered* pairs give distinct irreducible curves. The resultant is nonzero and of bidegree at most $`(4,4)`$; an irreducible plane factor defining the actual image retains these bounds.
3. **From geometric collisions to incidence counts.** Each fixed $`u,z`$ has at most two on-circle $`v`$. Fixed $`z,w,v,v'`$ allow at most two $`u`$ because $`1-\bar zv\ne0`$ on the unit circle for off-circle $`z`$. Therefore a witness selection in the second factorial moment costs at most a factor two. Exceptional parameter pairs contribute $`O(n^2)`$. A common Cayley chart in the two coordinates preserves bidegree and distinctness. If necessary, a fixed pole may be avoided by rotating the normalized configuration, or removing a single on-circle site at cost $`O(n)`$ for its roles as pin/base. A plane curve not fixed by conjugation has at most 64 real points by Bézout. Every remaining real curve has bounded-degree decomposition into simple arcs and boundedly many isolated or cut points, since the defining complex curve is irreducible and has no constant coordinate. Distinct curves meet at most 64 times, giving the two Pach–Sharir hypotheses with 65 degrees of freedom; arcs from the same original curve have disjoint interiors. The displayed exponent $`n^{3-1/129}`$ for $`\sum_us_u(s_u-1)`$ gives $`n^{2-1/258}`$ after Cauchy–Schwarz, absorbing $`\sum_us_u\le n^2`$. [Pach–Sharir incidence theorem](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/abs/on-the-number-of-incidences-between-points-and-curves/D82C6E430EE5402A3FE18C724A0C14B1)
4. **Capacity-three application.** Each ordered pair of distinct sites on the given circle can have at most one further on-circle site on its pin-radius circle, since the two circles have different centers. Every such pair in a three-site radius class therefore has at least one off-circle witness. This bounds three-site pairs by the mixed estimate. A one/two-site class accounts for respectively one/two ordered pairs, and $`2A_1+A_2\ge (A_1+2A_2)/2`$. This proves the displayed deficit with no restriction on collinear sites or on circles whose center is not a site. [Erdős, printed p. 168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2)

**Direct quantitative improvement.** After establishing family-specific injectivity, the real curves lie in the fixed projective linear space of bidegree-at-most-$`(4,4)`$ coefficients, of dimension 24. Theorem 1.3 of Sharir–Zahl applies with $`s=24`$, no common components and $`M\le n^2`$ points and curves. The leading balanced incidence exponent is $`(7s-6)/(5s-4)=81/58`$, hence the mixed triplet count is $`O_\epsilon(n^{55/29+\epsilon})`$; e.g. $`O(n^{1.9})`$. This version needs no real-arc decomposition. The smaller intrinsic dimension at most four is available too: complexify $`z,\bar z,w,\bar w`$ as independent parameters; the resultant coefficients have an image closure of dimension at most four and bounded degree. For each fixed factor bidegree the multiplication map of projective coefficient spaces has finite fibers by UFD, and its preimage of that closure has dimension at most four. The possible defining irreducible factors consequently belong to a bounded-complexity family of dimension at most four. One may use the union with its conjugate for a real family and a fixed Cayley change. The same incidence theorem then yields $`O_\epsilon(n^{15/8+\epsilon})`$. Both improvements require the manuscript’s elimination/identification result. [Sharir–Zahl, Theorem 1.3 and Definition 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.07877#page=5)

**Pure-resultant version of the four-parameter argument.** Homogenize both quadratics in the eliminated variable $`u`$. The only affine $`(v,v')`$ at which they can share the projective root $`u=\infty`$ is $`(1/\bar z,1/\bar w)`$. This point itself belongs to the actual image closure: with $`t=1/u`$ the first equation reads $`1-\bar zv-(1-|z|^2)vt+v(v-z)t^2=0`$; at $`t=0`$, its $`v`$ derivative is $`-\bar z\ne0`$. The holomorphic implicit function theorem therefore gives a local finite $`v(t)`$ through $`1/\bar z`$, and independently the same construction provides $`v'(t)`$ through $`1/\bar w`$. For $`t\ne0`$ these form incidence-locus points with finite $`u`$. The zero set of the nonzero homogeneous-$`u`$ resultant in the affine $`(v,v')`$ plane is thus precisely the irreducible $`\Gamma_{z,w}`$. Over $`\mathbb C`$ it equals a nonzero constant times $`F_{z,w}^k`$ for the irreducible implicit polynomial, by unique factorization and the Nullstellensatz. Stratify by the finitely many possible bidegrees and exponents. The coefficients of the resultant come from a polynomial map in the four complexified parameters. The power map $`[F]\mapsto[F^k]`$ has finite fibers (in fact it is injective on the relevant projective space), so its inverse image of the four-dimensional image closure has dimension at most four. Apply the fixed Cayley transform to bihomogeneous coefficients at their actual bidegrees. Taking the union of each resulting complex family with its conjugate gives a family defined over the reals with dimension and degree bounded as required. The four-parameter bound and its $`15/8+\epsilon`$ mixed-triple exponent follow. This argument also explains why an apparent isolated pole in a resultant computation contributes no extra curve component.

**Bounded prior search.** The closest circle/general-set source, McDonald–McDonald–Passant–Sahay, bounds the union of the distances between two sets; the text of Theorem 1.1 requires at most one point on a circle concentric with the fixed circle and discusses the necessity of this extra condition. Pach–de Zeeuw treat two prescribed algebraic curves and a global distance set. Sheffer–Zahl–de Zeeuw rule out a heavy circle subject to a sublinear *global* distance count. Raz–Sharir–de Zeeuw's trivariate Elekes–Szabó theorem has three scalar Cartesian variables; the comparator site here ranges over an arbitrary finite set in the plane. Neither it nor the generic incidence theorem alone verifies that these projected curves cannot repeat. A bounded search for mixed shared-pin isosceles triples, two vertices on a fixed circle and an arbitrary third set found no earlier theorem giving this conclusion verbatim.
