## Local counts and farthest-site sampling

- Fixing the pin and one base determines the two mates and exchanging base labels preserves the law. Fixing two ordered bases and an axis containing at most $`K`$ pins leaves at most $`K`$ completions. The normalized good-quadruple count is of order $`n^2`$, so bounded-richness sublaws are dominated uniformly by a constant times $`K`$ times the independent-pair law.
- A single axis reflects disjoint pairs on one perpendicular carrier. Two pairs on distinct parallel carriers would be concyclic. For a dyadic base-carrier size $`t`$, the pair weight is $`W\ll s^2/t+st`$ and each axis has weight at most $`t`$. Summing point–line incidences by dyadic axis weight gives $`p^{2/3}W^{2/3}t^{1/3}+pt+W`$. At $`S=n^{5/9}`$ the growing mixed term sums to $`O(n^{17/9})`$. The decreasing terms sum to $`O(p^{2/3}s^{4/3}+s^2)`$. Beyond $`S`$, two large sides give a vertex at an intersection of two of $`O(n^{4/9})`$ lines. A fixed pin and vertex specify at most one counted triangle. These estimates are uniform over both subsets. The lowest rich-line level $`t=2`$ also follows by counting pairs. [Solymosi and Tóth, Theorem 6](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~gasarch/TOPICS/erdos_dist/soly.pdf)
- For any fixed sample size, the probability of a repeated site or collinear triple is $`o(1)`$ by the maximum-collinearity result. For a regular sample the farthest cells have disjoint interiors, convex boundaries and at most a linear number of edges in the hull size. The same bound holds after clipping and subdividing each individual cell. Choosing the frame and sweep direction after fixing the finite ambient set handles accidental alignments. A pin on a clipping or subdivision boundary can use any incident piece. Strictly beating its owner there persists into the interior.
- A trapezoid needs at most seven original sites to survive deletion. Its owner and top and bottom supporting inequalities keep every open vertical fibre between its wall abscissae fixed. Retaining both active edges at either generating vertex keeps that vertex feasible and extreme after deletion. A new vertex in the open slab would change one of the fixed straight fibre boundaries. The fixed frame needs no sampled sites. Triangular end pieces satisfy the same argument.
- Exchangeably separating $`2m`$ labels into two halves retains a given set of at most seven defining labels with probability bounded below independently of large $`m`$. Every surviving piece avoids all conflict sites in the independent additional half, conditionally with probability $`(1-b)^m`$. The inequality only uses this necessary condition for surviving the additions. Bounding $`b^k(1-b)^m`$ yields the two moments with $`k=2,4`$. Distinct full pieces that survive are distinct pieces in the half, since pieces carry their owner.
- For each fixed pin a tie among $`2m+1`$ independent sampled radii costs $`O(m^2/n)`$. On a strict winning good pair all three bases lie in one assigned conflict set. Since the pin fractions sum to one, Hölder bounds the mixed sum by $`(\sum b^4)^{1/3}`$. Taking the cube root after expectation gives $`1/(2m+1)\ll m^{-4/3}(\mathbb Eh_m)^{1/3}+m^{-2}\mathbb Eh_m+o_n(1)`$ for fixed $`m`$. This forces a positive uniform lower hull fraction. The order of the two limits is essential.

## Concentration, ranks and the last obstruction

- Choosing exactly $`\lceil bn\rceil`$ pins in a minimum enclosing disk of that occupancy avoids an assumption about boundary ties. A base outside radius $`R+2`$ forces its two mates outside $`R`$. The localized triangle bound gives $`be\ll b^{2/3}e^{4/3}+e^2`$ for escaped mass $`e\le1-b`$. Division by $`e>0`$ contradicts the choice of $`b`$ near one. The manuscript only needs a tight limiting probability on the plane. Every atom has mass at most $`b`$ by the smaller-disk condition and the open-set inequality for weak limits.
- A child's surrounding annulus has limiting mass zero. The near-uniform individual mate marginals suffice to discard mates in that annulus before dividing by the fixed positive internal-pair probability. The restriction of the parent law agrees asymptotically with the child's internal quadruple law. Countably many children and nodes admit the nested diagonal construction. Independent paths continue into the same child for $`l`$ levels with probability at most $`b^l`$, giving almost-sure finite separation. Positive similarities preserve the recovered directions.
- The direction sectors can avoid all limiting independent-pair boundary atoms and all finite pair directions. A convex hull contributes a comparable chain for each run of edges in a sector. With fixed sample size, independent-pair direction convergence transfers the largest-chain statistic. The two empirical ranks each converge marginally to a uniform law. Their values are functions of the path up to null sets, by comparing against an independent path. Reverse Fatou and compactness give a nonzero chain submeasure dominated by the rank law. Closure of coordinatewise comparability extends it to all pairs from the support. This gives a measurable positive-mass set of paths.
- A positive terminal portion has non-atomic coordinate measure and its support lies on a graph of slope at most $`\tan(\pi/8)`$. Appendix C excludes it. On surviving paths the increasing node filtration generates the paths. Martingale convergence therefore gives a positive-mass node with conditional chain probability exceeding $`1-10^{-4}`$. The restrictions of either rank to the node remain atomless because the node has positive probability.
- With probability at least $`1-4\cdot10^{-4}`$ all four limiting rank pairs at that node lie on the closed chain. With every single-rank comparison strict, the six comparisons persist on an open neighborhood of the quadruple. Four distinct finite sites in that double sector would lie on a graph of slope less than one. No circle about a graph site can meet it at three distinct sites. Thus some rank tie is forced. Independent pin–base laws exclude pin–base ties. A specified base pair on a bounded-richness axis has limiting sublaw dominated by $`O(K)`$ times an independent pair, so cannot tie in either rank. This argument does not assume unrestricted domination of a pair of bases.
- For the resulting exceptional side, choose a large intermediate cutoff $`B`$, then a large fixed richness cutoff $`K`$, then a small fixed heavy-carrier coefficient. The small- and intermediate-carrier losses total $`O(n^2(B/K^2+B^{-1/3}+c)+nB)`$. Removing intersections of large carriers affects $`o(n)`$ sites. Removing orientations of mass greater than $`n\sqrt{s_n/n}`$ costs $`o(n^2)`$ pin–pair incidences by the single-active-parallel-carrier property. Both endpoints of the side are matched except for arbitrarily small error. Taking the exchangeable labels and the rank-chain error together leaves matched-label probability as required. This uses finite flags before extraction or, equivalently, weak convergence of the bounded-richness submeasures.

## Appendices

In Appendix A the displayed identity for the rational inverse follows by subtracting the two quadratic equations in the parameter. The common infinite pole with ratio tending to one specifies infinity even when the finite poles coincide. An affine reparametrization must have slope one by the behavior at infinity. The pole location and constant term force the translation to vanish. Distinct ordered function pairs thus yield distinct irreducible curves of degree at most three. Ten points determine at most one such curve by Bézout, with bounded multiplicity after splitting it into real simple arcs. Pach–Sharir with ten degrees of freedom gives $`O(n^{3-1/19})`$ for the second factorial moment. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $`O(n^{2-1/38})`$ for the collinear-pin count. The radius deficit then excludes a line of positive site density. [Pach and Sharir](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/combinatorics-probability-and-computing/article/abs/on-the-number-of-incidences-between-points-and-curves/D82C6E430EE5402A3FE18C724A0C14B1)

In Appendix B, injective projection along nonperpendicular source–target pairs transfers popular-midpoint intersections to source subsets. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $`E(2U,-B)\gg t^3`$ and then $`E(U)\gg t^3`$. Balog–Szemerédi–Gowers and the difference-to-sumset estimate supply a subset of size comparable with $`t`$ whose sumset has size $`O(t)`$. Boundedly many removals capture a positive density of good line pairs. Summing overlaps over targets leaves two nonparallel source lines sharing many projected coordinates on many target orientations. Their dot-product ratios are distinct on distinct unoriented target directions. The sums of points on consecutive populated rays occupy disjoint sectors in a Cartesian product with $`O(t^2)`$ elements. This bounds the number of such directions and contradicts its growth. [Reiher and Schoen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10245v2) · [Solymosi](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1040v3)

In Appendix C, closed-ball record radii control the exponentially weighted tails. An area cover and Cauchy–Schwarz lower-bound the normalizing integral, and differentiation on the graph bounds the error at a fixed scale before passing to small radii. The graph's strict one-sided growth follows by subtracting squared distances and using its Lipschitz bound on both the sum and difference of the vertical displacements. The swap of pin and base costs at most $`e^{1/100}`$. After discarding the larger-target size regime by this swap, the larger-source regime costs $`O(n^2/A+ns_n)`$. Comparable dyadic groups of positive mass remain, with $`\Theta(n/T)`$ source lines and $`o(n/T)`$ of any common orientation. Appendix B applies. For the closed rank chain the final carrier argument uses constant weight one, making the swap exact up to the pin–base total-variation error.

## Exact finite cross-check

The separate standard-library checker `check_erdos_654_localization.py` exercises two places where a counting error would affect the proof. It compares radius grouping and rational circumcenter enumeration on two fixtures with no four concyclic points. For every base subset and every pin and integer cutoff it assigns the triangle to a small-axis incidence or to a unique large-carrier intersection. It tests for collisions and checks that multiple endpoint pairs on one axis share a carrier. It also enumerates the diameter and circumcircle candidates for a smallest occupied disk, including tied minimizing choices, and checks that every strict escaping base forces all three bases outside the smaller threshold.

Both ordinary Python and `python3 -O` passed. Counts were 1,536 base subsets, 143,872 pin–subset–cutoff cases, 32 multiple-pair axes, 1,664 small-carrier charges, 192 large-carrier charges and 126 smallest-disk checks involving 42 strict escaping pairs. The mathematical argument above supplies the asymptotic estimates.
