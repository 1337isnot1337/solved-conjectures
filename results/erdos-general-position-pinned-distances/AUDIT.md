# Source, scope and priority of the pinned-distance theorem

**Reviewed 26 November 2026.** Paul Erdős defined general position on printed p. 167 as no three points on a line and no four on a circle. On p. 168, display (3) conjectures a fixed improvement over one third for the largest number of distances from a single point. Display (5) asks for the corresponding sum. The [written proof](PROOF.md) gives both statements for every sufficiently large set with no three collinear. In Erdős’s normalization one can take $`c=3\eta`$ in both displays. The proof gives no numerical value of $`c`$. Display (4) concerns an upper construction. The suggestion after (4) that (3) might hold even with arbitrary collinearities and no four points on a circle remains open. [Erdős, *Some combinatorial and metric problems in geometry*, §1, pp. 167–168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2) · [Bloom, problem 654](https://www.erdosproblems.com/654)

## Earlier arguments and related results

- Szemerédi’s isosceles-triangle count uses the perpendicular bisectors of pairs. McKenna explains how, in general position, the average pinned question is equivalent to excluding near-saturation of the pairs admitting two apices. The counting step here permits any number of concyclic points. The compactness and covariance argument then rules out near-saturation. Ágoston and Bhowmick also use the planar diameter graph in their global distance proof. Both the [manuscript](PROOF.md) and the [review](REVIEW.md) give elementary proofs of the finite diameter fact used here. [McKenna, “Pinned distances”, p. 19](https://aixiv.online/pdf/2610.02806v1#page=19) · [Ágoston–Bhowmick, Theorem 1 and its proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25148)
- McKenna’s theorem bounds the total number of lengths occurring somewhere in a general-position set. His last paragraph explicitly lists both pinned questions as unanswered. His radial-sum comparison uses one common list of distances across different pins. Bhowmick’s Theorem 1.1 proves a fixed improvement for the global distance count under no three collinear, and its Proposition 1.2 likewise uses common columns for the distinct lengths. A large union of distance sets does not imply a large set at any individual pin. [McKenna’s manuscript and formalization](https://github.com/mysticflounder/distinct-distances-general-position) · [Bhowmick’s manuscript](https://hal.science/hal-05864246/document)
- Nivasch, Pach, Pinchasi and Zerbib obtain a pinned improvement for the vertices of a convex polygon. Arbitrary sets in general position may have interior points. A different construction of Feng and collaborators on two lines refutes the stronger near-one coefficient when collinearities are allowed. Neither result resolves (3) or (5). [Convex polygon theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1266) · [Two-line construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22401) · [Two-line Lean certificate](https://github.com/veljjanoski/erdos654-palomar)
- The bounded priority search covered the original statement and its modern variants, citing publications, arXiv, HAL, aiXiv, the Erdős Problems discussion, public proof and Lean repositories, and GitHub, Hexagon and formal theorem searches. The Formal Conjectures source lists a separate general-position pinned variant. Searches of both permanent project branches and the issues and PRs used the Erdős–Pach names, problem number, pinned and repeated distances, bisectors and the one-third bound. Issue 318 asks about the half-size bound on convex polygons. We found no earlier resolution of the two general-position questions in these sources. [Formal Conjectures, general-position variant](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/ea8e3e198ded6fc73efb7f5653b74b28ac4ae1a8/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/654.lean#L115-L126) · [Convex polygon issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/318) · [Tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/910)

## Review and checks

The Solved Conjectures research team developed the argument in Cinna Davis’s repository. One research agent derived the limiting-law proof. Another independently reconstructed the counting, normalization, covariance and critical-set steps and provided a second diameter argument. The coordinating agent checked the derivation, source scope and manuscript. A separate agent audited the closest published and formally checked results. An additional project agent read the manuscript independently of the first review and checked the six pair laws, possible collisions, the lifted covariance and both support obstructions. That reading prompted the explicit choice of one outcome per shrinking neighborhood in the continuity argument. External refereeing and Lean formalization remain open. [Independent review](REVIEW.md)

Run `python3 results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/verify.py` from the repository root. It checks the counting identities, the six two-coordinate multiplicity bounds and a diameter lemma on every one of the 4,675 subsets of the four-by-four integer grid with at least three points and no collinear triple. These yield 4,944 ordered quadruples. Two additional configurations yield 12 ordered quadruples, including a set with concentric classes of sizes eight and three and an example with three diameter neighbors. The asymptotic theorem follows from the written argument. [Finite checker](verify.py)
