# Independent review of the sparse-pair criterion

27 November 2026 UTC. An independent reviewer in the WadeDavis research session checked the [proof](THIN-EXCEPTIONAL-PAIRS.md) against the [published sparse-trapezoid argument](SPARSE-TRAPEZOIDS.md). The theorem gives a sufficient condition for a positive gap in the **mean** under the site-centered capacity of three. The coefficient of the global quadratic trapezoid bound is arbitrary but fixed; the residual weight condition is imposed on each subset. The general site-centered conjecture and the global quadratic hypothesis by itself remain open.

**Tail and local powers.** Choose one carrier of maximal multiplicity on each bisector of the subset. The squared sum of the numbers of unchosen pairs is bounded by six times the global trapezoid count for every subset. A family of unchosen pairs of size small compared with the ambient number of pairs contributes a vanishing incidence fraction under this bound. For a fixed richness cutoff, the low-richness part is at most the cutoff times the pair count. The high-richness part follows from Cauchy–Schwarz and the point–line rich-axis estimate. The order of limits is the site count first and then the cutoff. This reasoning includes the sparse exceptional pairs and the heavy axes, whose total unchosen pairs after the square-root cutoff are at most a constant times the ambient size to the three-halves power.

For the remaining pairs, the endpoint weight on every dyadic multiplicity band bounds the unweighted point–line incidences on that band. In the sum of the main terms Hölder with exponents three halves and three gives a convergent inverse-square logarithmic weight. The two additive terms charge the total pair count and at most the square root of the ambient number of sites times the pin count. This gives exactly the two leading powers needed by the earlier farthest-sample and tightness argument. Their constants are independent of the positive-mass descendant node in the rescaling tree. The global quadratic constant affects only a vanishing error when the incidence-tail argument is used. On a fixed node it also controls the separate loss of unchosen pairs in the marked-carrier step, where the existing proof permits node-dependent cutoffs. The single-carrier convention used by the source–target counting argument is preserved.

**Two concrete implications.** If a base pair lies outside the maximum carrier, at most half the axis's pairs lie on its carrier. Thus an axis with more than twice the fixed transverse multiplicity bound has only exceptional unchosen pairs. This proves the stated bounded-multiplicity corollary.

The generic independent similarities used for the polygon example exclude all cross-polygon cyclic quadruples, collinear triples and equal-distance triples with a site pin. Each excluded condition is a nonidentity polynomial in the similarity parameters. A pair of vertices of a polygon can be mapped to any prescribed ordered pair of distinct points, covering the only mixed four-site configuration without a singleton polygon. In a regular polygon each chord determines one reflecting axis. Distinct chord pairs on the same axis have distinct carriers. Two pairings of one four-set would make a rectangle, which an odd regular polygon cannot contain. Thus the cyclic pair count is exact. Cauchy–Schwarz gives the stated uniform lower bound on dense slices. The [integer checker](check_sparse_pair_moments.py) exhausts 43,688 subsets through 15 vertices. The regular-polygon family separates the old hereditary hypothesis from the new sufficient condition under arbitrary fixed global coefficients; its mean itself approaches the full set size.

The bounded duplicate search compared the incidence literature, both related repository directories and [issue #969](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/969). The result is a reviewed written partial theorem with numerical coincidences checked by exact integers. It has no Lean formalization or external referee report.
