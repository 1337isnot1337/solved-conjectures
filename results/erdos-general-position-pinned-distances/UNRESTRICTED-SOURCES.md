# Priority and scope of the unrestricted one-third pinned-distance bound

**Audit on 27 November 2026 UTC.** We looked for a proof that there are absolute $`c>0`$ and $`n_0`$ such that every planar $`n`$-point set with no four points on a circle and $`n\ge n_0`$ has a pin determining more than $`(1/3+c)n`$ positive distances. We searched separately for the bound $`\sum_{x\in P}d_P(x)>(1/3+c)n^2`$ in the absence of any collinearity restriction. The bounded search located no prior proof of either statement. This is a priority finding. A proof of the sum bound would answer the numbered maximum question affirmatively by averaging.

## Exact original question

Erdős introduces general position as no three points on a line and no four on a circle on printed p. 167. Display (3), p. 168, asks for a maximum above $`(1+c)n/3`$. Display (4) asks for an upper construction. Immediately after (4), Erdős explicitly proposes (3) with the sole hypothesis that no four are on a circle. He then proposes the further relaxation that only circles centered at sites have multiplicity at most three. Display (5) asks for the corresponding sum above $`(1+c)n^2/3`$. The proposed unrestricted average theorem transfers this sum to the first relaxation. It makes no assertion about the site-centered-only hypothesis. The change in conventional normalization absorbs a factor of three into the unspecified constant. Erdős–Pach revisit the general-position question; the 1997 near-one target is separately recorded on the source bibliography. [1987 original, §1, pp. 167–168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2) · [Erdős–Pach, 1990, p. 267](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02122780) · [Bloom’s source listing, including Erdős 1997, p. 530](https://www.erdosproblems.com/654)

## Earlier results and public claims

- Feng and collaborators, §3.1 and Theorem 3, construct sets of $`n=4m`$ points on two perpendicular axes, $`m\ge10`$, with at most $`3m-1`$ distances from any pin and no four sites on a circle. This disproves the near-one assertion and is consistent with the positive-gap lower bound. It also upper-bounds any putative sharp asymptotic coefficient by $`3/4`$. The exact optimal coefficient remains a separate problem. ## Catalogue and credit

The prior package at `results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/` already proves the average bound assuming no three collinear, with arbitrarily many points allowed on a circle. As hypotheses on point sets, the prior and proposed results are incomparable. Their union is a clean catalogue statement: the average bound holds if *either* no three are collinear *or* no four are cocircular. Take the smaller of the two absolute constants and the larger of the two thresholds. Preserve the prior proof, review, author credit, checker and package URL. Rank 4 on `main` and rank 3 on `research` already represent this 1987 question; amend the existing row and preserve each branch’s rank and result count. It is accurate to say the second theorem extends the 1987 general-position positive gap to arbitrary collinearities with no four cocircular. It does not replace the equally important earlier arbitrary-cocircularity theorem.
