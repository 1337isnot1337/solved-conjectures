# Review and scope of the radial-profile reduction

The underlying open target is the positive mean one-third gap for site-centered capacity three under a global quadratic bound on noncollinear isosceles trapezoids. The [one-node argument](RADIAL-PROFILES.md) proves that no diffuse mass disappears between separated logarithmic radius scales about any sequence of centers. The [recursive argument](RADIAL-SCALE-TREE.md) makes independent finite samples separate in geometric coordinates and reduces the remaining gap to the appearance of a positive expected convex-hull fraction along growing samples. It leaves the existence of this hull lower bound open. [Erdős, 1987, p. 168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2) · [Tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/969)

A second Codex agent in the WadeDavis research session independently checked the annular rank tie, estimates on the seven intersecting dyadic bins, full mass in the positive ranked-bin limits and the grouping by bounded relative offsets. The reviewer checked disjoint shrinking disks across different charts, the uniform child mass bound, internal near-extremality after conditioning, and finite-depth separation of independent pairs. The proof distinguishes independent pins and bases from potentially singular base–base laws. At the reviewer's request the recursive proof includes the identity expressing both global empirical ranks as conditional integrals of a recovered direction against an independent path and the rotation which excludes a zero projection. Terminal chart coordinates and successive child labels generate the required path filtration. General auxiliary bin ranks need not be measurable in that filtration. These arguments are written proofs; no Lean formalization or external refereeing is claimed.

The exact source check included the arbitrary-collinearities scale tree, the sparse-trapezoid carrier transfer, the recent single-scale atomic-limit result and the earlier uniform disk coupling. The earlier scale tree obtained tightness of a normalized planar limit from a local triangle count, which is currently unavailable under the sole arbitrary-coefficient global bound. The new argument instead takes the annular ranks in the single-scale null-side theorem, covers every radial profile, and secures separation for all the independent sampled paths that are needed by the existing chain argument. General concentration-compactness uses concentration functions and profiles; the problem-specific step here is the exclusion of vanishing annular mass using two coincident rank sides. The literature check compared the global-bisector and classical concentration-compactness statements. [Previous one-scale theorem](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1031) · [Lund–Sheffer–de Zeeuw](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.6868) · [Lions, 1984](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIHPC_1984__1_2_109_0.pdf)

The [standard-library checker](check_radial_profiles.py) verifies 1,800 radial rank controls, including 900 whose moving center differs from the pin, and 1,300 seven-bin controls. It exhausts 31,250 subsamples in a hull-representative fixture with duplicates and collinear points. The finite checks corroborate the elementary steps; they do not replace the weak-limit or conditional-law proofs.
