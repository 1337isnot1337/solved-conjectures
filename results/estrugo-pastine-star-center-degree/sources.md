# Sources and priority audit

Checked 26 November 2026. The named target is a published open **question** in graph Erdős–Ko–Rado theory.

| Record | What was checked |
| --- | --- |
| Estrugo and Pastine, 2021 | Their paper proves positive results for caterpillars and bounds the locations of centers in lobsters. The degree-gap wording used in this package is the explicit formulation by Hurlbert and Kamat. The [2020 author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.00732) and [journal record](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2021.03.013) are distinct versions. |
| Hurlbert and Kamat, 2022 | Their final paragraph attributes to Estrugo and Pastine the question about a maximum $r$-center of degree $\delta(G)+o(|G|)$. Their numbered Conjecture 4.1 immediately above is the separate leaf-center assertion for trees without degree-two vertices. [Author-hosted published PDF, §4](https://glennhurlbert.github.io/Papers/IFISTpub.pdf) |
| Feghali, Johnson and Thomas, 2018 | Theorem 1.4 prescribes an absolute center degree and a distance to the leaves. In the proof the number of branches at each neighbor of the root is taken sufficiently large for the prescribed parameters. Their concluding paragraph announces further rooted joins with non-leaf centers. Neither assertion supplies a linear lower bound on the degree of **every** maximum center as a function of order. The dependence on order distinguishes the present target from [issue 777](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/777). [Paper and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.07741) |
| Hurlbert, 2025 preprint and 2026 survey chapter | Section 4, Theorem 34 and the discussion after Theorem 37 record the absolute-degree examples, the leaf-center conjecture and positive graph families. [Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16144) |
| Later results on largest stars | Zhang, Ran and Huang analyze leaves, cycles and specific caterpillar families. The perfect-tree result covers equal branching at each level. Their conclusions do not give the linear-degree obstruction or a universal sublinear upper bound. [Zhang–Ran–Huang](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2023.11.035) · [Perfect trees](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16953) |

OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar both returned the same six citing works for the Estrugo–Pastine paper. The two additional records concerned the EKR property for sparse or very well-covered graphs, which compares stars with *all intersecting families*. The present comparison is among individual stars. [OpenAlex citing records](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:doi:10.1016/j.dam.2021.03.013) · [Semantic Scholar citing records](https://api.semanticscholar.org/graph/v1/paper/DOI:10.1016/j.dam.2021.03.013/citations?fields=title,year)

The search included the authors and variations of “largest independent-set star,” “max r-center,” “degree gap,” “linear degree,” “minimum degree,” and “degree proportional to the order,” including graph complements and clique blow-ups. GitHub issue searches and both permanent branches were checked. The existing [Holroyd–Talbot research](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/348) concerns a different assertion. This bounded search found no previous proof of the quantitative tree result. The counting proof also gives the stronger fixed-rank obstruction for general graphs without relying on a known non-leaf example.
