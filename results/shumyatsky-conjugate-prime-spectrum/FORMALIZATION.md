# Lean verification of Shumyatsky’s conjugate prime-spectrum proof

The existing [proof](PROOF.md) answers Kourovka Notebook Question
17.125. This partial verification retains the original contribution
from [aadrianvv](https://github.com/aadrianvv) in [PR #652](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/652)
and the source credits in the [audit](AUDIT.md).

- [Prime-spectrum reduction](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyPrimeSpectrum.lean).
  Lean uses the actual natural cardinalities of groups and their
  subgroups. It proves that minimality descends along surjections,
  that every finite group contains a minimal subgroup with the same
  prime spectrum, and that conjugate generation on minimal groups
  implies the exact original statement. The final cardinality step
  proves that a quotient-surjecting subgroup of a minimal group is the
  whole group once its order contains every kernel prime.
- [Cycle-label equations](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyCycleLabels.lean).
  Lean verifies equations (1) and (2) for arbitrary finite lists of
  paired edge labels in a group. Given the starting conjugator label,
  the labels already chosen, and a consistent reference cycle, the
  missing edge label exists uniquely in the prescribed class under the
  quotient homomorphism.

- [Factor action](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyFactorAction.lean).
  Lean reconstructs an automorphism of the actual finite direct product
  from its permutation of coordinate subgroups and their induced
  automorphisms. It checks the source-label multiplication law, proves
  that equation (1) is equivalent to conjugacy of these automorphisms,
  and identifies a fixed permutation with prescribed outer classes as
  an actual right coset modulo inner automorphisms of the product.

- [Simple factors](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskySimpleFactors.lean).
  Every nontrivial normal subgroup of a direct power of a nonabelian
  simple group contains a coordinate factor. The factors are exactly the
  minimal nontrivial normal subgroups. Consequently every automorphism
  of the finite power permutes the factors and has the label form above.
- [Walk labels](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyWalkLabels.lean).
  Lean evaluates arbitrary words of forward and reverse factor moves on
  the product. A closed word acts on its base factor by the product of
  its successive labels. Words of the two chosen automorphisms and their
  inverses are in their generated subgroup. For a selected occurrence
  with the other transports fixed, each word label in the prescribed
  outer class determines a unique edge label in its prescribed class.

- [Cyclic transport](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyCyclicTransport.lean).
  With a given indexed partition into paired cycles, Lean pastes the
  conjugator labels at all vertices, including the return edges. It
  proves that forcing each reserved final label preserves the reference
  outer class and that the resulting equations give a conjugator of
  the actual product automorphisms.
- [Factor stabilizer](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyStabilizer.lean).
  Restriction from the actual product automorphisms stabilizing a
  factor to its automorphism group is a homomorphism. If the labels
  of two closed words generate a group containing the inner
  automorphisms of the centerless factor, Lean proves that the
  subgroup generated by the two product automorphisms contains every
  prime of the factor order in its own prime spectrum.

- [Permutation cycles](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyPermutationCycles.lean).
  Every finite permutation has a partition into its actual cycles,
  indexed from any chosen representatives by their consecutive powers.
  The cyclic successor agrees with the permutation even on fixed points.
- [Spanning tree](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyCycleForest.lean).
  The cut cycles form an acyclic graph of paths. Transitivity of the
  actual subgroup generated by the two permutations gives a connected
  graph, so the paths extend to a spanning tree. An acyclic supergraph
  cannot add an edge inside a cut cycle unless that edge is already a
  surviving path edge.

- [Directed tree moves](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyDirectedTreeMoves.lean).
  For transitive conjugate permutations on at least two factors, Lean
  selects one directed move per tree edge, a distinct unused return
  move in every second cycle, and two remaining distinct moves outside
  both sets. The proof distinguishes loops and parallel moves by their
  generator and tail, including a two-cycle whose opposite moves
  share endpoints.

- [Independent closed words](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyClosedMoveRoutes.lean).
  A route to a selected move and back through the tree gives an actual
  word in the two permutations and their inverses, fixing its starting
  factor. Lean evaluates the word's actual automorphism label and
  realizes any two prescribed outer-compatible labels independently.
  Subsequent changes to the reserved return edges preserve both labels
  if the tree and the two selected moves keep their prescribed labels.

- [Cycle completion](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyCycleCompletion.lean)
  and [product completion](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyProductCompletion.lean).
  Lean starts each paired cycle immediately after its chosen return
  move and completes the actual conjugacy by changing only those
  return labels. The chosen conjugator and all changed labels stay
  in their original outer classes.
- [Multifactor lift](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyMultifactorLift.lean).
  If any two outer classes admit lifts generating a subgroup containing
  the inner automorphisms, Lean proves the multifactor prime-spectrum
  lift. It constructs the two actual inner-coset adjustments and
  proves that their conjugate pair captures every prime of the
  centerless factor order.

- [Simultaneous assembly](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskySocleAssembly.lean).
  Lean proves distinct minimal normal subgroups commute. For a given
  direct-product kernel, the individual inner adjustments combine in
  the kernel and retain their exact effects on each normal subgroup.
  The local prime-spectrum lifts then give a conjugate generating pair
  of the actual prime-spectrum-minimal group.
- [Prime-power kernel lift](../../lean/Conjectures/ShumyatskyPrimeKernelLift.lean).
  Given conjugacy of the prime-free complements, Lean proves both
  cases of the original lift across a kernel with only one prime in
  its spectrum. The proof handles a nontrivial centralizing kernel
  element and the case where the first generator has trivial kernel
  centralizer.

The passage from the original finite group to these structural hypotheses
remains, including the classical complement-conjugacy input, the actual
socle decomposition, and the almost-simple generation and singleton-factor
arguments.

The pinned Lean 4.35.0 gate strictly recompiles each module, audits
all declarations owned by it, and replays the objects with the stock
checker. The earlier scopes, the thirteen-module multifactor closure, and both new group-theoretic scopes passed.
