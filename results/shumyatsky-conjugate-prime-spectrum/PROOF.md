# Two conjugate elements with the full prime spectrum

**Theorem.** Every finite group $`G`$ has conjugate elements $`a,b`$
such that $`\pi(\langle a,b\rangle)=\pi(G)`$, where $`\pi(X)`$ is the
set of primes dividing $`|X|`$. This is Shumyatsky's Question 17.125 in
the Kourovka Notebook. [Kourovka Notebook, version 50, p. 123](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.0300v50#page=123)

The main step lifts a conjugate pair across a nonabelian minimal normal
subgroup. Two independently chosen automorphism labels, obtained from
closed walks on the simple factors, suffice to realize the prime spectrum
of one factor. The construction applies to all minimal normal subgroups
at once.

## Structure and group-theoretic inputs

Call $`G`$ prime-spectrum minimal if no proper subgroup has the same
prime spectrum. In any finite group choose a subgroup of least order
with the full prime spectrum. A pair of conjugate generators there
is conjugate also in the original group. It therefore suffices to
prove that every prime-spectrum-minimal finite group is generated by
two conjugates.

The class of prime-spectrum-minimal groups is closed under quotients.
If $`G/N`$ had a proper subgroup with its full prime spectrum, the
inverse image in $`G`$ would be a proper subgroup with prime spectrum
$`\pi(N)\cup\pi(G/N)=\pi(G)`$. These two observations are due to
Maslova and Revin, who also proved the reduction to trivial soluble
radical. [Maslova and Revin, Lemmas 1 and 4, Proposition 6](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/timm977)

We use the following results, for finite groups throughout.

- Gaschutz's lifting lemma says that when $`d(L)\leq 2`$, any ordered
  generating pair of a quotient of $`L`$ lifts to a generating pair
  of $`L`$. Here $`d(L)`$ is its least number of generators.
  [Wilson, Theorem 2.5.12](https://www.dpmms.cam.ac.uk/~grw46/LectureNotes.pdf)
- If $`L`$ is almost simple with socle $`S`$, then
  $`d(L)=\max\{2,d(L/S)\}`$. This is the case of the
  Lucchini and Menegazzo theorem used below.
  [Lucchini and Menegazzo, Rend. Sem. Mat. Univ. Padova 98 (1997)](https://www.numdam.org/item/RSMUP_1997__98__173_0.pdf)
- An almost simple group with cyclic outer quotient is generated by
  two conjugates. Every proper quotient is cyclic, so this follows
  from Corollary 4(ii) of Burness, Guralnick and Harper.
  [The spread of a finite group](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2021.193.2.5) |
  [Accepted manuscript](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/26396)
- The Schreier conjecture gives solvability of the outer automorphism
  group of any nonabelian finite simple group. A subgroup of
  $`\operatorname{Aut}(S)`$ of order coprime to $`|S|`$ is cyclic.
  [Spaeth and Vallejo Rodriguez, Theorem 5.1](https://www.uv.es/cavaro3/download/SVCoprime.pdf)

## Lifting across a permuted product of simple groups

Let $`S`$ be a nonabelian finite simple group, let $`I`$ have size
$`k\geq 2`$, and write $`W=\operatorname{Aut}(S^I)`$ and
$`B=\operatorname{Inn}(S^I)`$. Fix $`x_0,g_0\in W`$. Suppose the
permutations of $`I`$ induced by $`x_0`$ and $`g_0x_0g_0^{-1}`$
generate a transitive group on $`I`$. We show that there are
$`x\in x_0B`$ and $`g\in g_0B`$ such that the stabilizer of a factor
in $`\langle x,gxg^{-1}\rangle`$ induces an automorphism group
containing $`\operatorname{Inn}(S)`$ on that factor.

Fix identifications of the simple factors with $`S`$. In the wreath
product description of $`W`$, each automorphism $`z`$ has a
permutation of the factors and, for every $`i\in I`$, a label
$`\ell_i(z)\in\operatorname{Aut}(S)`$ carrying the factor at $`i`$
to the factor at $`z(i)`$. Thus

```math
\ell_i(uv)=\ell_{v(i)}(u)\ell_i(v).
```

The right coset $`zB`$ fixes the permutation and the image of each
label in $`\operatorname{Out}(S)`$. Within this coset the labels can
be varied independently over their prescribed cosets modulo
$`\operatorname{Inn}(S)`$.

Write $`y=gxg^{-1}`$ and $`y_0=g_0x_0g_0^{-1}`$. The permutations
of $`x`$ and $`y`$ are fixed by the cosets. Fix an $`x`$-cycle $`C`$
with initial point $`i`$ and length $`m`$, and the corresponding
$`y`$-cycle $`g(C)`$ with initial point $`g(i)`$. The condition
$`gx=yg`$ is equivalent along the cycle to the label equations

```math
\ell_{x(j)}(g)
=\ell_{g(j)}(y)\ell_j(g)\ell_j(x)^{-1}
\qquad (j\in C).
\tag{1}
```

Fix $`\ell_i(g)=\ell_i(g_0)`$. After a full traversal, (1) is
consistent exactly when

```math
\ell_{g(i)}(y^m)=
\ell_i(g_0)\ell_i(x^m)\ell_i(g_0)^{-1}.
\tag{2}
```

Consequently, after choosing all $`x`$ labels, we may choose all but
one $`y`$ label per $`y`$-cycle arbitrarily in the cosets prescribed
by $`y_0B`$. The remaining label is uniquely determined by (2).
It lies in its prescribed coset because (2) holds for $`x_0,y_0,g_0`$
and the chosen labels agree with their outer images. Equation (1)
then defines the other labels of $`g`$ on $`C`$. Their outer images
agree with $`g_0`$ by the same equation. The construction works
independently on every $`x`$-cycle, and the resulting $`g`$ is in
$`g_0B`$ and satisfies $`y=gxg^{-1}`$.

Make a directed multigraph on $`I`$ with one $`x`$-edge $`i\to x(i)`$
and one $`y`$-edge $`i\to y(i)`$ at every point, retaining loops and
parallel edges. Its undirected graph is connected by transitivity.
Let $`c`$ be the number of $`x`$-cycles, also the number of
$`y`$-cycles. Take the forest of $`x`$-edges obtained by deleting one
edge per $`x`$-cycle. Extend it to a spanning tree with $`c-1`$
$`y`$-edges. A $`y`$-cycle always has an edge outside this tree,
including cycles of length one and two. Reserve one such edge per
$`y`$-cycle for the label forced by (2). Fix all the tree labels
to their initial values in $`x_0,y_0`$.

There are two distinct non-tree edges whose labels can be chosen
freely and independently in their prescribed outer cosets. If
$`c\geq 2`$, use two of the omitted $`x`$-edges. If $`c=1`$, use
the omitted $`x`$-edge and one of the $`k-1`$ unreserved $`y`$-edges.
In each case every $`y`$-cycle's reserved edge adjusts to satisfy
(2), so these choices preserve conjugacy.

Choose a root $`i_0`$ of the tree. For either of the two chosen edges,
walk from $`i_0`$ to its tail in the tree, traverse the edge, and
return to $`i_0`$ in the tree. Use $`x^{-1}`$ or $`y^{-1}`$ when
traversing a directed tree edge backwards. The resulting word belongs
to the factor stabilizer in $`\langle x,y\rangle`$. Its automorphism
label on $`S_{i_0}`$ is the chosen edge label multiplied on either
side by fixed tree transport labels. As $`\operatorname{Inn}(S)`$
is normal, each of the two labels of the resulting words ranges
over its full prescribed outer coset, independently of the other.

Let $`E`$ be the subgroup of $`\operatorname{Out}(S)`$ generated by
the two outer images of those word labels, and let $`L`$ be its
inverse image in $`\operatorname{Aut}(S)`$. The group $`L`$ is almost
simple with socle $`\operatorname{Inn}(S)`$, and $`d(E)\leq 2`$.
The theorem of Lucchini and Menegazzo gives $`d(L)=2`$, so
Gaschutz's lemma lets the two word labels be chosen to generate $`L`$.
This proves the lifting assertion. In particular, the order of the
resulting subgroup $`\langle x,y\rangle`$ is divisible by every prime
in $`\pi(S)`$, since a subgroup of it maps onto an automorphism
group containing $`\operatorname{Inn}(S)`$.

## The case of one simple factor

First let $`L`$ be prime-spectrum minimal and almost simple with
socle $`S`$. The subgroup $`L/S`$ of $`\operatorname{Out}(S)`$ is
soluble. It therefore has a Hall subgroup on the set of primes
$`\pi(L/S)\setminus\pi(S)`$. The inverse image of this Hall subgroup
in $`L`$ has prime spectrum $`\pi(L)`$. By minimality the inverse
image is $`L`$, so $`(|L/S|,|S|)=1`$. Schur-Zassenhaus gives $`S`$
a complement in $`L`$, and the complement is cyclic by the cited
coprime-automorphism theorem. Thus $`L/S`$ is cyclic.

For such an $`L`$, let $`c`$ generate $`L/S`$, and let $`d\in L/S`$.
We can choose conjugate generators $`a,b=gag^{-1}`$ of $`L`$ with
$`aS=c`$ and $`gS=d`$. Take any conjugate generating pair
$`a_1,b_1=g_1a_1g_1^{-1}`$ given by the spread theorem.
Their images in the cyclic quotient are equal and generate it.
Choose an exponent $`e`$ coprime to $`|a_1|`$ such that
$`(a_1S)^e=c`$. This is possible by the Chinese remainder theorem.
Take the required unit residue class modulo $`|L/S|`$ and require
$`e`$ to be 1 modulo each prime dividing $`|a_1|`$ that does not
divide $`|L/S|`$. Then $`a=a_1^e,b=b_1^e`$ still generate $`L`$.
Replacing $`g_1`$ by $`g_1a^j`$ leaves $`b`$ unchanged, and for
some $`j`$ it gives the prescribed image $`d`$.

## Completion of the proof

Suppose $`G`$ is a smallest prime-spectrum-minimal finite group
that is not generated by two conjugates. It is nontrivial and has
trivial soluble radical by Proposition 6 of Maslova and Revin,
applied to the quotient-closed class of all prime-spectrum-minimal
groups. Here is the reduction. If $`N`$ were an abelian minimal
normal subgroup, it would be a $`p`$-group. Lift conjugate
generators of $`G/N`$ to a conjugate pair $`x,y`$. If
$`H=\langle x,y\rangle<G`$, the only prime it can miss is $`p`$,
and hence $`H`$ is a complement to the normal Hall subgroup $`N`$.
If $`x`$ centralizes a nonidentity $`v\in N`$, replace $`x`$ by
$`xv`$ and $`y`$ by the corresponding conjugate. The new pair
still surjects onto $`G/N`$ and its generated subgroup has order
divisible by $`p`$. Otherwise $`C_N(x)=C_N(y)=1`$, and $`H`$ is the
unique complement containing $`x`$. In fact any complement is
$`nHn^{-1}`$ for $`n\in N`$ by Schur-Zassenhaus, and
$`x\in H\cap nHn^{-1}`$ forces $`x^{-1}n^{-1}xn\in H\cap N=1`$,
so $`n=1`$. Conjugating $`y`$ by a nonidentity element of $`N`$
puts it outside $`H`$. Together with $`x`$ it still surjects onto
the quotient and therefore generates $`G`$, since a proper
supplement must again be a complement. Either case gives a
contradiction. If the soluble radical were nontrivial, it would
contain an abelian minimal normal subgroup.

Put $`M=\operatorname{Soc}(G)`$. It is the direct product of its
minimal normal subgroups $`N_i`$, each of the form $`S_i^{k_i}`$
with $`S_i`$ nonabelian simple. Conjugation by $`G`$ is transitive
on the $`k_i`$ factors of each $`N_i`$ by minimal normality.
Distinct minimal normal subgroups centralize one another.
By minimality of $`G`$ and quotient closure, $`G/M`$ has
conjugate generators $`\bar x,\bar g\bar x\bar g^{-1}`$.
Choose lifts $`x_0,g_0`$ in $`G`$.

For every $`i`$, the action of $`M`$ on $`N_i`$ is precisely
$`\operatorname{Inn}(N_i)`$. Multiplying either lift by elements
of $`N_i`$ alters its $`i`$-th action freely in its inner coset
and leaves its action on every $`N_j`$ for $`j\neq i`$ unchanged.
We prescribe the two actions independently for each $`i`$.

- If $`k_i\geq 2`$, the factor permutations induced by the two
  quotient generators generate a transitive permutation group
  since they generate $`G/M`$ and $`M`$ fixes the set of factors
  pointwise. Apply the lifting assertion to the images of
  $`x_0,g_0`$ on $`N_i`$. With those choices, the stabilizer of a
  factor in the restricted action of the generated group has an
  image containing $`S_i`$.
- If $`k_i=1`$, the image $`L_i`$ of $`G`$ on $`S_i=N_i`$ is
  an almost simple quotient of $`G`$, hence prime-spectrum
  minimal. Its outer quotient is cyclic by the preceding argument.
  The images of the two quotient generators of $`G/M`$ are equal
  there and generate it, so the image of $`\bar x`$ is a generator
  $`c`$. Prescribe the conjugate generators constructed above for
  $`c`$ and the image $`d`$ of $`\bar g`$. They are realized by
  changing the two lifts within their inner cosets.

After these independent changes, write $`H=\langle x,gxg^{-1}\rangle`$.
It maps onto $`G/M`$. In each case an image of a subgroup of $`H`$
contains a copy of $`S_i`$, and thus $`\pi(S_i)\subseteq\pi(H)`$.
It follows that

```math
\pi(H)\supseteq\pi(G/M)\cup\bigcup_i\pi(S_i)=\pi(G).
```

Prime-spectrum minimality forces $`H=G`$, completing the proof.
