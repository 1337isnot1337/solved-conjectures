Two independent readings checked the counting and thinning arguments in the [short well-order theorem](SHORT-ORDINALS.md), the [SOCA equivalence](SOCA-CONVERSE.md) and the [chromatic obstruction and Fox construction](COUNTABLE-DECODERS.md). The grid and countable order prescriptions used in the lower bound have a [separate proof review](GRID-REVIEW.md).

- **Short well-orders.** Below the ordinal square each coordinate splits into countably many blocks whose points have countably many predecessors within the block. Closure under each partial block-rank function and its inverse aligns the block orders with the background enumeration on a club. The two hypothetical comparison maps are continuous off the diagonal. Applying the semi-open coloring axiom successively makes the two predicates simultaneously constant. The resulting nonconstant graph has a well-order as a linear extension of its representing partial order. The earlier ZFC replication theorem therefore excludes it in an uncountable induced subgraph of either factor of a Soukup witness.
- **Sharpness.** The grid theorem produces a six-vertex standard example inside every uncountable restriction of one three-order graph. Its three displayed extensions realize the stated poset. Every transitive orientation of the chordless six-cycle must alternate. Each extension can reverse at most one of the three incomparable diagonal pairs, so an intersection of two orders cannot realize it. Fox supplies the star partition of the complete graph. Moving the center from the first to the last point in each star block certifies membership with two order comparisons. With initial-ordinal enumerations the total order type is bounded by the ordinal square even for uncountably many uncountable stars.
- **Converse and lower endpoint.** Two disjoint basic open sets give a rectangle certificate by four block comparisons. Adding a real label to the topology preserves second countability. The resulting uncountable condensation core has no open agreement graph between a real labelling and an order of type $`\omega_1`$. Any one edge would yield an open rectangle with an uncountable side below a single ordinal. The first-differing-bit example attains the lower endpoint $`\omega_1\cdot2`$. Strictly shorter orders have at most countable tails above their initial blocks, so all coordinates align on an uncountable set after a simultaneous thinning.
- **Chromatic obstruction.** If every word at a fixed level had one countably chromatic side, taking the finite tuple of the chosen colorings would produce an uncountable induced finite-order presentation. The mixed words instead have an infinite branch. Every prefix of that branch carries uncountably chromatic graphs of both answers. For Fox’s construction the two-sign certificates work on realized profiles, the first-disagreement decoder is globally open, and uniqueness of a possible common limit refers to the space of disagreement vectors.
