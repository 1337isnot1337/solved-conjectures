# Countably many comparisons in Soukup’s graph problem

Assume the bounded proper forcing axiom (BPFA). Suppose $`G`$ is a graph on $`\omega_1`$ such that both $`G`$ and its complement are uncountably chromatic and their categorical product is countably chromatic. Let $`Y`$ be any uncountable subset of its vertices. Choose any countable sequence of linear orders on $`Y`$ and orient each unordered pair by an additional fixed order. Write its comparison signs in the chosen sequence as a point of $`2^\omega`$.

**Theorem.** There is a single $`p\in 2^\omega`$ such that for every finite prefix of $`p`$, the edges of $`G[Y]`$ with that prefix form an uncountably chromatic graph on $`Y`$, and the nonedges with that prefix also form an uncountably chromatic graph on $`Y`$. This holds for every sequence of orders whether or not the sequence encodes adjacency.

**Proof.** Call a finite sign word mixed when both graphs it selects have chromatic number $`\aleph_1`$. Fix a length $`n`$. If no word of this length is mixed, choose for each word a countable proper coloring of one of its two graphs. The joint coloring has only countably many values, so some joint color class $`Z\subseteq Y`$ is uncountable. Within $`Z`$ the chosen side has no pairs for each word. Adjacency on $`Z`$ is consequently a Boolean function of the first $`n`$ comparisons and the orienting order. Reversing a pair and then orienting it again gives the same value. Such an induced graph is excluded by the [finite Boolean order theorem](BOOLEAN-ORDERS.md). Thus there is a mixed word at every length. Prefixes of a mixed word are mixed because enlarging an edge set cannot lower its chromatic number. The finitely branching tree of mixed words has an infinite branch, which gives $`p`$.

In particular the realized edge and nonedge signatures have intersecting closures in Cantor space. Even a decoder continuous on the realized signatures must fail to extend continuously to their closure if it represents a witness under BPFA. Indeed every neighborhood of $`p`$ meets both sides. The sign sequence $`p`$ need not itself be realized.

## A two-comparison certificate for each pair

In ZFC every graph on at most $`\aleph_1`$ vertices admits a representation by countably many **well-orders** with a fixed open acceptance set in the space of comparison signs. Both possible values have certificates using two signs on the set of realized signatures. This explains why continuity only on that set cannot extend the finite-order obstruction. The earlier [countable representation with an unrestricted decoder](BOOLEAN-ORDERS.md) covers all sets of size at most the continuum.

Apply Jacob Fox’s decomposition of the complete graph on $`\aleph_1`$ vertices into countably many star forests, restricting to the given set if necessary. Split forest $`n`$ into its edges belonging to the given graph, placed at address $`2n`$, and its remaining edges, placed at address $`2n+1`$. Both parts are star forests. Each unordered pair belongs to exactly one address. [Fox, *An infinite color analogue of Rado’s theorem*, Theorem 4, pp. 4–5](https://people.dm.unipi.it/dinasso/ULTRABIBLIO/Fox%20-%20An%20infinite%20color%20analogue%20of%20Rado%27s%20theorem.pdf#page=5)

For each address well-order the components of its spanning star forest, including isolated vertices. Choose a center for each component and a well-order of its remaining vertices. Form two ordinal sums in the same component order, placing each center before its leaves in the first and after its leaves in the second. Their comparisons disagree exactly for the edges of this forest. This also covers an isolated vertex and a single-edge component. Thus each vertex pair has exactly one disagreeing comparison pair. Its address parity decides adjacency.

In the raw sign space accept precisely when the first disagreeing address is even. This fixed acceptance set is open because every accepting word has a finite witnessing prefix. On realized signatures a disagreement at a specified address alone certifies its parity, so each value is relatively open there. Equivalently map the raw signs to their comparison disagreements coordinate by coordinate. The realized images are unit vectors. In this latter space zero is the only possible common accumulation point of the two answers, and is such a point whenever both answers occupy infinitely many addresses. For a putative BPFA witness on any uncountable vertex subset both answers do: a finite union of star forests has finite chromatic number, while both induced factors have chromatic number $`\aleph_1`$ by the finite-order obstruction. The quotient description distinguishes the unique limiting disagreement vector from the potentially many raw sign sequences lying above it.

The chromatic obstruction follows from the repository’s reviewed partial theorem for Lajos Soukup’s Problem 1.4. The representation is a consequence of Fox’s prior star decomposition. The original existence question in ZFC remains open. These arguments are written proofs. [Soukup, Problem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08027v1#S1)
