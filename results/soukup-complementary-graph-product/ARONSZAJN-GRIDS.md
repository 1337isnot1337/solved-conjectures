# Finite grids and countable order patterns on Aronszajn lines

**Theorem (ZFC).** Let $`X`$ be an uncountable subset of an Aronszajn line $`A`$ and let $`r:X\to\mathbb R`$ be injective. For every positive integer $`k`$ there are $`k`$ disjoint successive open intervals in $`A`$ and $`k`$ disjoint successive open intervals in $`\mathbb R`$ such that all $`k^2`$ corresponding cells contain uncountably many vertices of $`X`$. An Aronszajn line here has size $`\aleph_1`$, has neither an uncountable increasing nor an uncountable decreasing well-ordered sequence, and has no uncountable suborder that embeds in the reals.

## The grid argument

Every nonempty subset of $`A`$ has countable cofinality and coinitiality, since otherwise an $`\omega_1`$-long monotone sequence can be constructed recursively. Every uncountable subset $`Z`$ has a point with uncountably many elements of $`Z`$ on both sides. Indeed, the suborder of elements with only countably many predecessors in $`Z`$ has a countable cofinal subset and is countable by taking the union of the corresponding countable initial segments. The same applies to points with countably many successors. Each of the two uncountable sides contains a bounded open interval, with endpoints on that side, that meets it uncountably. To see this, use countable coinitial and cofinal subsets to cover all but at most the endpoints by countably many such intervals. In particular every uncountable subset of an interval meets two strictly separated interior open subintervals uncountably.

Let $`S\subset A`$ be countable and closed under the following choices. For a rational open interval $`I\subset\mathbb R`$ and $`a,b\in S\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}`$, suppose there are uncountably many $`x\in X\cap(a,b)`$ with $`r(x)\in I`$. Choose the endpoints of two strictly separated open intervals inside $`(a,b)`$, each still containing uncountably many such $`x`$. The preceding paragraph justifies the choices, and iterating countably often constructs $`S`$.

The points in $`A\setminus S`$ determine only countably many distinct cuts of $`S`$. Otherwise, representatives of uncountably many different cuts would embed in the real line. Explicitly, enumerate $`S=\{s_i:i\lt\omega\}`$ and map each realized cut to the sum of $`2^{-i-1}`$ over the elements of its lower part. Distinct lower parts are strictly nested, so this map is strictly increasing on the realized cuts. Choose a cut class $`E`$ containing uncountably many points of $`X`$. Its neighborhoods below are the intervals $`(a,b)`$ with endpoints in $`S\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}`$ containing $`E`$. They are closed under finite intersection.

Put $`N=k^2`$. Choose $`2N-1`$ strictly separated bounded rational open intervals $`I`$, in each of which $`r(X\cap E)`$ is uncountable. This follows by selecting that many distinct condensation points of the uncountable real set and disjoint rational neighborhoods. For each such $`I`$, every neighborhood of $`E`$ contains an $`S`$-endpoint interval wholly on one side of $`E`$ in which uncountably many $`x\in X`$ have $`r(x)\in I`$. The two intervals supplied by closure are disjoint. If one contains a point of $`E`$, it contains the whole cut class and the other is on a single side. If both avoid it, either one works. For a fixed $`I`$, at least one of the two sides works in **every** neighborhood. Otherwise intersect a neighborhood excluding all such left intervals with a neighborhood excluding all such right intervals. Hence at least $`N`$ of the real intervals admit the same side in every neighborhood.

Reverse $`A`$ if necessary so this side is the left, and retain $`N`$ real intervals of this type in increasing order. Given any permutation of these intervals, successively choose an $`S`$-endpoint interval on the left of $`E`$ rich for the next prescribed real interval, entirely to the right of the previously chosen interval. At each step use a neighborhood of $`E`$ with left endpoint at the previous right endpoint. This gives $`N`$ successive Aronszajn intervals with uncountably many vertices in the prescribed pairs of intervals. For $`1\le p,q\le k`$, assign the real interval of rank $`(q-1)k+p`$ to the Aronszajn interval of rank $`(p-1)k+q`$. Group the Aronszajn intervals into $`k`$ consecutive rows and the real intervals into $`k`$ consecutive columns, taking each group's open interval hull. The designated uncountable small cell in each row and column proves the theorem. Reversing the Aronszajn row labels restores the original orientation if needed.

## Sharp finite palettes

Give $`X`$ a further well-order of type $`\omega_1`$, independently of the two given orders. For every positive integer $`k`$, the three orders realize all $`(k!)^2`$ pairs of relative order permutations on each uncountable subset. The grid cell for any prescribed row and column contains an unbounded set of indices in the ordinal enumeration. Taking one element from each of the $`k`$ required cells in the desired ordinal order proves the claim. A coloring of unordered $`k`$-sets is **decoded from finitely many orders** if its value depends only on the joint relative order pattern of the $`k`$ points in a fixed finite list of linear orders on the vertex set. Under the bounded proper forcing axiom (BPFA), the [simultaneous three-order theorem](BOOLEAN-ORDERS.md) gives one uncountable restriction of any uncountable set on which all those orders are copies or reversals of at most three representatives. Sort a $`k`$-set in one representative. Only a permutation in each of the other two remains, so every such coloring takes at most $`(k!)^2`$ values there. The same restriction works simultaneously for every finite arity and every decoder from the given orders. Conversely, color each $`k`$-set in the ordinal, real and Aronszajn example by its two full permutations. The grid shows that no uncountable subset omits a color. This lower bound holds in ZFC for every $`k`$.

More precisely, let $`t`$ be the number of nonempty basis families retained by the BPFA reduction, among ordinal, real and Countryman coordinates. On every uncountable subset of that restriction the $`t`$ representatives realize all $`(k!)^{t-1}`$ joint patterns for every $`k`$. The three-type case is the preceding grid argument. For a real order and a Countryman order use the same grid with two coordinates. For an ordinal order and either remaining type, split the nonordinal suborder into $`k`$ successive intervals each containing uncountably many vertices, and meet these intervals in any ordinal order. The single-type case has one relative pattern. If the original list is empty, all decoders are constant.

## Countably many prescribed comparisons

Fix the uncountable set $`X`$, its Aronszajn and real orders, and any well-ordering of $`X`$ of type $`\omega_1`$. There exists a sign for the Aronszajn order with the following property. Given **any** linear order and **any** well-order on the labels $`\mathbb N`$, there are distinct vertices $`x_0,x_1,\ldots`$ that are increasing in the signed Aronszajn order in their natural label order. Their real order induces exactly the prescribed linear order on the labels, and their ordinal order induces exactly the prescribed well-order. The sign may depend on $`X`$ and the labelling. It is chosen once before either countable prescription. The assertion applies anew in every uncountable subset.

Use the same countable set $`S`$ and cut class $`E`$ as in the grid proof. The uncountable real set $`r(X\cap E)`$ admits a family of disjoint rational open intervals, each still containing uncountably many of its points, ordered from left to right like the rationals. To construct it, choose a bounded open interval meeting this set uncountably. Inside it choose three strictly separated bounded rational open intervals that all do so. Reserve the middle interval and repeat inside both outer intervals at every node of the infinite binary tree. The reserved intervals have no endpoints in their linear order: each node has a reserved descendant on either side. They form a dense order: if one node is in an outer subtree of the other, an appropriately placed descendant of the inner node lies between the two; for nodes in opposite subtrees their common ancestor lies between them. The resulting countable dense order without endpoints has type $`\mathbb Q`$.

Give each reserved interval one of its available sides at $`E`$, as in the grid argument. One side contains a subfamily still ordered like $`\mathbb Q`$. Indeed, if the first color is dense on the whole rational order it itself contains such a copy; otherwise a nonempty open rational subinterval is entirely of the second color. Fix that common side and orient the Aronszajn order so that intervals on that side approach $`E`$ from below. Every countable linear order embeds into $`\mathbb Q`$ by choosing rational positions for successive labels between their previously placed finitely many lower and upper neighbors. Choose a different real interval $`I_n`$ for each label according to that embedding for the prescribed real order. In the natural order of $`n`$, choose successive signed-Aronszajn intervals $`J_n`$ on the chosen side, so each set of vertices with Aronszajn coordinate in $`J_n`$ and real label in $`I_n`$ is uncountable. This uses a finite previous bound at every stage. Each of these sets is unbounded in the ordinal enumeration. Finally visit the labels in their prescribed well-order and choose one vertex from each set above all earlier chosen ordinal indices. The supremum of countably many previously chosen indices is below $`\omega_1`$, including at limit stages. These choices realize the three simultaneous orders. The construction uses one uncountable cell per chosen label.

## Sources and scope

The grid and countable prescription are ZFC results. The sharp upper bound uses BPFA through the preceding common three-order theorem. These form a partial structural supplement to the campaign on Soukup’s complementary-graph question. The unrestricted existence problem in ZFC remains open. The arguments are written proofs without Lean formalization. [Soukup, Problem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08027v1#S1) · [Five-element basis under BPFA, Krueger and Moore, §12](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03581v2#page=21) · [Independent review and checks](GRID-REVIEW.md)

Galvin and Shelah's Theorem 2 gives the pair case for precisely these three order types. Their Lemma 7 separates two uncountable blocks in agreeing directions and Lemma 8 iterates the agreement to rationally many blocks; reversals give the corresponding opposite arrangements. The grid supplies arbitrary finite permutations, including order patterns that cannot be obtained from singletons by successive two-block direct or skew sums. The 1973 introductory results for arbitrary colorings have a broader class of admissible colorings. [Galvin and Shelah, pp. 168, 171–172](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95020/23.pdf#page=5)
