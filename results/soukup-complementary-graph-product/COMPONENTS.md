# Components of a product-color class

This lemma describes a single color of the symmetric refinement in the [order-dimension proof](ORDER-DIMENSION.md). It applies before choosing ordinal enumerations or restricting the graph to a club.

Let $`R`$ be a bipartite graph on disjoint sets $`L,U`$. Form an auxiliary bipartite graph on the unordered pairs of distinct points of $`L`$ and of $`U`$. Two pair vertices are adjacent when the corresponding two-by-two subgraph of $`R`$ has a matching of size two. A pair is *matchable* if it has at least one neighbor in the auxiliary graph. With no isolated endpoints, a pair fails this test exactly when both endpoints are leaves sharing their only neighbor.

**Component lemma.** Every unmatchable pair is an isolated auxiliary vertex. Two matchable pair vertices lie in the same auxiliary component exactly when the unordered multisets of their endpoints’ original $`R`$-components agree. The two pair vertices may belong to different shores.

A matching step preserves that multiset. If the endpoints belong to different original components, connect each endpoint to the requested endpoint in its own component by a finite walk. The two walk lengths have the same parity because each source pair and each destination pair lies on one shore. Pad the shorter walk by backtracking on an incident edge until the lengths agree. Synchronized steps then give auxiliary adjacencies; the two original components keep the moving points distinct.

In a single original component take a shortest path between a matchable pair of same-shore points. Its length is even. If its length is at least four, move both endpoints inward by one edge. The new endpoints are distinct, remain matchable by reversing that step, and have distance two less. It remains to connect the matchable pairs at distance two. Write such a pair as the endpoints of a path $`a,v,b`$, called an angle here. At least one of $`a,b`$ has a neighbor other than $`v`$.

For a fixed nonleaf neighbor $`b`$ of $`v`$, every angle at $`v`$ using $`b`$ links to the angle with endpoints $`v,u`$, where $`u`$ is any other neighbor of $`b`$. Use the disjoint edges $`av`$ and $`bu`$. If $`v`$ has two nonleaf neighbors, the angle using both connects their classes. Thus all matchable angles centered at a given $`v`$ are in one auxiliary component. Two adjacent nonleaf centers link their classes by the same disjoint-edge step, choosing one further edge at either end. In a connected graph the vertices of degree at least two induce a connected subgraph if there are any, because internal vertices of a shortest path have degree at least two. A star has no matchable pair inside its component. These observations cover every case.

## Consequences for complementary graphs

For a fixed value of the symmetric color $`f`$ in the [replication argument](ORDER-DIMENSION.md), take its incidence relation as $`R`$ on two copies of the vertex set. Each auxiliary adjacency equates the graph bits of the corresponding pairs. The lemma lists exactly the equalities enforced by the matching steps of this single relation. When both shores carry the same graph, one must also identify the two copies of each underlying unordered pair.

Within any original component, all distinct pairs of nonleaf vertices on the same shore are matchable. Consequently these vertices form a homogeneous set in the underlying graph. In a witness each such set is countable. The component therefore has a countable vertex cover: take its nonleaves on both shores, adding one endpoint in a component consisting of a single edge. This conclusion is intrinsic to the specified color class.

Enumerate one such cover per component and assign each edge to a covering endpoint of least index, breaking ties consistently. Fixing an index and the shore of the designated center gives one star per component at most. Read these stars as partial functions from outer endpoints to centers. Each function preserves the adjacency bit on pairs with distinct images. This decomposes the given color class on its entire domain into countably many such partial functions and transposes.

For comparison, the **existence** of a regressive function description on $`\omega_1`$ has a short direct proof. Enumerate the predecessors of each ordinal $`\beta`$ with natural-number indices. For each index and each symmetric product label, map $`\beta`$ to the corresponding predecessor when that label occurs there, leaving the function undefined otherwise. The countably many partial functions enumerate every predecessor, and equal labels preserve the bit whenever the images differ. Conversely their graphs, their transposes, and the diagonal give a countable product coloring by assigning an index and an orientation, with a separate diagonal color. Same-index entries with a repeated image have no product edge, and entries with distinct images have equal bits. The factor chromatic requirements are separate. Sierpiński’s classical square cover already gives the enumeration without the adjacency requirement. [Kubiś and Vejnar, §1](https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.1402)

The [finite checker](verify_components.py) constructs the matching-step graph independently of the predicted partition on all 65,536 labelled relations with four vertices on each shore. It includes isolated endpoints, common-neighbor leaves, stars and disconnected supports.
