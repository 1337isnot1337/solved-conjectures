# Finite Boolean combinations of order comparisons

A graph on a set $`X`$ has a **finite Boolean order representation** if it admits finitely many linear orders $`L_1,\ldots,L_d`$ on $`X`$ and a Boolean function such that the adjacency of distinct $`x,y`$ is determined by the bits $`[x\lt_{L_i}y]`$. The formula may use arbitrary Boolean operations and the orders can be independently reversed. For an undirected graph the value agrees at a realized sign vector and its bitwise complement, since these arise from exchanging $`x,y`$. Give each unrealized complementary pair any common value. This supplies a decoder invariant under simultaneous reversal on the entire sign cube.

**Canonical form.** Assume the bounded proper forcing axiom. Every uncountable graph on at most $`\aleph_1`$ vertices with a finite Boolean order representation has an uncountable induced subgraph which, or whose complement, is the comparability graph of a partial order of dimension at most three. More generally, on an uncountable subset, any finite tuple of orders is determined by individual reversals and repetitions of at most three representative orders. A coloring of unordered pairs decoded from the tuple consequently uses at most four colors on that subset. Four is optimal by the classical three-order coloring of Galvin and Shelah.

**Application to Soukup's problem.** Under the same axiom no uncountable induced subgraph of either factor of a witness has any finite Boolean order representation. In an extension by one Cohen real the whole witness has a representation with two orders. The ZFC existence question for witnesses without restrictions on their representation remains open. [Finite-dimensional obstruction](ORDER-DIMENSION.md) · [Cinna Davis's positive construction](ONE-COHEN.md) · [Soukup's original problem](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08027v1#S1)

## Three representative orders

Fix a Countryman line $`C`$ and a real suborder $`R\subseteq\mathbb R`$ of cardinality $`\aleph_1`$. Under BPFA every uncountable linear order contains a copy of one of $`\omega_1,\omega_1^*,R,C,C^*`$. Apply this to each of the finitely many orders in turn, retaining an uncountable subset at each stage. Thus on the last subset each coordinate embeds, after an optional reversal, in $`\omega_1`$, the reals, or $`C`$. A previously selected embedding remains valid on further restrictions. [Krueger and Moore, Corollary 1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.03581v2#S1)

Within each of the three types pick one reference coordinate. For each other coordinate of that type, thin to an uncountable subset where its comparisons agree with the reference up to a fixed sign.

- For two embeddings in $`\omega_1`$, choose an increasing sequence of length $`\omega_1`$ in both coordinates. At each stage, countably many earlier ranks have been used. The set of candidates whose value is too small in either coordinate is countable because the embeddings are injective.
- For two embeddings in the reals, the second coordinate as a function of the first is an injection on an uncountable set of reals. It is strictly increasing or strictly decreasing on an uncountable subset. The relevant size-restricted open coloring axiom follows from the bounded forcing axiom for a countably closed forcing followed by ccc forcing, and hence from BPFA because such two-step iterations are proper. One can apply it to the graph of the injection in the plane, declaring two points adjacent when the two orders disagree. This is an open relation. An uncountable clique gives decreasing order, and a countable cover by independent sets gives an uncountable increasing set. [Asperó and Bagaria, §3.1, pp. 184–185](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(00)00058-0)
- For two embeddings in $`C`$, place their paired images in $`C^2`$ and take an uncountable subset in one of its countably many coordinatewise chains. Injectivity makes the two comparisons agree there. [Countryman square property](ORDER-DIMENSION.md)

The finitely many thinnings preserve every comparison already synchronized. The resulting signed-repetition bound of three is sharp for tuples: put a well-order of type $`\omega_1`$, an uncountable real order and a Countryman order on the same underlying set. No two can agree up to reversal on an uncountable subset. A real order has no uncountable well-ordered or reverse well-ordered suborder, and a Countryman order is an Aronszajn line, which also omits uncountable real suborders. Galvin and Shelah proved in ZFC that for any such triple, all four possible pair orientations relative to the well-order occur on every uncountable subset. Their Specker type is an Aronszajn order. Their result supplies the matching lower bound of four colors. [Galvin and Shelah, Lemma 6 and Theorem 2, p. 171](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95020/23.pdf#page=5)

## Classifying the symmetric decoder

Substitute the fixed signs and repetitions into the Boolean function. If fewer than three orders remain, repeat an order in unused coordinates and let the new function ignore these coordinates. The case of no orders gives a constant graph. A comparison vector in three coordinates and its simultaneous reversal define the same graph value. There are four resulting classes, labelled by the two bits recording whether coordinates two and three agree with coordinate one.

A decoder with no accepted classes or with all classes accepted gives an empty or complete graph. If it accepts just one class, reverse the second and third orders as dictated by that class. Two vertices are then adjacent exactly when all three signed orders agree on the pair. This is the comparability relation of the intersection of those orders. The case of three accepted classes is its complement. If the decoder accepts exactly two classes, they form an affine line in the two-bit plane. Such a line fixes the first bit, the second bit or their exclusive-or. These three choices specify agreement or disagreement of one pair among the three orders. After a reversal the graph is the comparability graph of the intersection of that pair. Thus every decoder gives the claimed comparability or complementary comparability restriction. The same four sign classes bound the number of colors for any unordered-pair coloring.

A finite list of unary predicates or named vertex constants in a quantifier-free order formula does not change the canonical form. Discard the constants themselves, then thin to one uncountable class for all unary values and all comparisons of a variable to each constant in each order. The remaining decoder depends only on the order comparisons of the two distinct variables.

The [finite-dimensional theorem](ORDER-DIMENSION.md) rules out the resulting induced graph in a witness. Davis's Cohen construction already uses a Boolean function of two order comparisons. Hence the opposite consistency results apply throughout the finite Boolean class. [Exhaustive decoder check](verify_boolean_orders.py)

## Equality on every infinite ground set of size at most the first uncountable cardinal

Under BPFA the chromatic number of the categorical product of such a graph and its complement equals the smaller factor chromatic number. On a ground set of cardinality $`\aleph_1`$, the uncountably chromatic case follows from the obstruction. If the smaller chromatic number is countable or finite, one class of its proper coloring has cardinality $`\aleph_1`$. It is a clique in the other factor. Pair the identity with an injection of the entire ground set into that clique. This gives an injective homomorphism from the smaller-chromatic factor to the product. Projection to either factor gives the upper bound. On a countably infinite ground set, infinite Ramsey gives an infinite clique in one factor. That factor is countably chromatic, so the same injection applies to the other, which has the smaller or equal chromatic number. These smaller-cardinality arguments hold in ZFC for every graph. The one-Cohen example witnesses failure in size $`\aleph_1`$.

## Countably many comparisons with an arbitrary decoder

In ZFC, every graph on at most continuum many vertices has a representation using countably many orders and an arbitrary decoder on countable bit sequences. Inject its vertices into the binary sequence space. For each finite binary word take one pair of orders with all vertices in its prefix cylinder placed before the outside vertices in one order and after them in the other. Use the same background order inside both blocks. The two comparisons of a pair disagree exactly when that cylinder contains one of its two codes. At all sufficiently long levels the codes lie in separate cylinders, and the two odd cylinder prefixes determine their unordered pair of infinite paths. Hence the resulting signs identify the unordered vertex pair, and any edge relation can be assigned from them. This argument imposes no measurability condition on the decoder. The unrestricted countable version therefore includes the whole original problem.
