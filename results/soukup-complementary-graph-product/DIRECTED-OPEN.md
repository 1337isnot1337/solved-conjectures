# A ZFC obstruction from induced matchings

Let a **witness** be a graph on $`X`$ of size $`\aleph_1`$ for which the graph and its complement are uncountably chromatic and their categorical product is countably chromatic. Lajos Soukup asks whether ZFC proves that a witness exists. The following necessary conditions hold in ZFC for both factors of any witness. They apply to every uncountable induced subgraph. [Soukup, Problem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08027v1#S1)

**Induced matching theorem.** For either factor and every countable coloring of its edges on an uncountable vertex subset, some color contains a countably infinite matching induced in the original graph. In particular the least number of chain subgraphs covering the edges there is $`\aleph_1`$. A chain graph is a bipartite graph in which the neighborhoods in one shore are nested under inclusion.

**Proof.** Write $`H`$ for the chosen factor restricted to an uncountable set $`Y`$. The [replication theorem](ORDER-DIMENSION.md) gives an uncountable index set $`I\subseteq Y`$ and disjoint uncountable subsets $`A_i\subseteq Y`$ such that all pairs between distinct fibers $`A_i,A_j`$ have the same adjacency as $`i,j`$. Every uncountable vertex subset contains an edge and a nonedge. In each $`A_i`$ choose one edge $`e_i`$. Uncountably many choices have the same color. Their index set has no uncountable clique, so the Erdős–Dushnik–Miller partition relation gives a countably infinite independent set of indices. The corresponding edges are disjoint and there is no edge between endpoints from different chosen edges. [Dushnik and Miller, Theorem 5.22](https://fa.ewi.tudelft.nl/~hart/onderwijs/set_theory/material/dushnik_miller-partially_ordered_sets.pdf#page=7)

Given a countable covering by chain subgraphs, color each edge by a member of the cover containing it. A chain subgraph cannot contain two edges of a matching induced in the ambient graph. Their two vertices in one shore would have incomparable neighborhoods, since both possible cross edges are absent even in the ambient graph. The theorem rules out the cover. The stars centered at the vertices give a cover by at most $`\aleph_1`$ chain subgraphs. [Chain subgraph covers and induced matchings](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-021-00904-w)

**Oriented open relations.** Suppose the vertex set carries a second countable Hausdorff topology and an arbitrary linear order $`W`$. Assume there is a relatively open directed relation $`O`$ on the off-diagonal square such that, for $`x\lt_W y`$, the vertices $`x,y`$ are adjacent exactly when $`(x,y)\in O`$. This representation cannot occur on any uncountable induced subgraph of either factor of a witness.

Indeed, cover $`O`$ by rectangles $`U\times V\subseteq O`$ with $`U,V`$ disjoint basic open sets. There are only countably many. The edges from the rectangle are the pairs with $`u\in U`$, $`v\in V`$ and $`u\lt_W v`$. These form a bipartite chain graph since the neighborhoods in $`V`$ are final segments of the same linear order. The chain graphs cover every edge, contradicting the theorem. Openness on pairs in their chosen orientation suffices as well, by extending a relatively open subset of the space of forward pairs to the off-diagonal square.

## Normalizing short ordinal comparisons in ZFC

Suppose adjacency is given by a symmetric, relatively open acceptance set for countably many comparison signs, each supplied by a well-order of type strictly below $`\omega_1^2`$. On an uncountable subset the graph has an oriented open presentation with a well-order $`W`$ of type $`\omega_1`$. Moreover it has a symmetric open comparison presentation there with countably many well-orders of type at most $`\omega_1\cdot2`$. The same conclusions hold starting inside any prescribed uncountable subset.

To see this, divide each coordinate into its countably many consecutive blocks of type at most $`\omega_1`$. Enumerate the starting vertex set in type $`\omega_1`$ as a background chronology. Recursively select $`\omega_1`$ vertices in increasing chronology. After each selection exclude all predecessors of that vertex in its own block in each coordinate, as well as its predecessors in the chronology and the vertex itself. At any countable stage the exclusions form a countable set. Thus each block order on the selected set agrees with the chronology $`W`$. [Equivalent club argument](SHORT-ORDINALS.md)

Give each selected vertex its block label in each coordinate and an auxiliary injective real label. Give the set the topology inherited from the product of the real line with the countably many discrete countable sets of labels. For a hypothetical ordered pair $`x\lt_W y`$, each comparison sign has a continuous value on the whole off-diagonal square. It is given by the order of distinct block labels, and is forward when the two labels agree. The resulting comparison map is continuous. Extend the acceptance set openly from the realized signatures to Cantor space and take its inverse image. This gives the directed open relation $`O`$ required on forward pairs.

To recover a symmetric decoder in two-block well-orders, cover $`O`$ by disjoint-coordinate basic rectangles as above. Use $`W`$ as one coordinate. For each basic $`U`$, take one order listing $`U`$ first and its complement second, and one listing these blocks in the other order. Keep $`W`$ inside each block. Each of the two orders has type at most $`\omega_1\cdot2`$. Orient the input pair forward by $`W`$. Forward then backward in the two comparisons for $`U`$ certifies that its first vertex lies in $`U`$ and its second outside $`U`$. Backward then forward certifies the opposite. For a disjoint rectangle $`U\times V`$, use the first test on $`U`$ and the opposite test on $`V`$. Their conjunction certifies membership of the oriented pair in that rectangle. The union of these finite certificates is open, and ordering the input by $`W`$ makes the predicate symmetric on raw profiles. This proves the normalization.

It follows in ZFC that no uncountable induced subgraph of either factor of a witness has such a presentation in orders all strictly below the ordinal square. In any model with a witness the least possible ordinal upper bound on the coordinate types in an open presentation of either induced factor is therefore exactly $`\omega_1^2`$. The upper bound follows from [Fox’s star-forest representation](COUNTABLE-DECODERS.md) and the [ordinal length calculation](SHORT-ORDINALS.md). The separate [canonical form equivalent to SOCA](SOCA-CONVERSE.md) applies to all graphs in the class. This ZFC obstruction uses the replication property of witnesses. The unrestricted existence question remains open.

The argument uses Cinna Davis’s replication theorem for this campaign, the classical partition relation, and Fox’s decomposition. A bounded source check covered Soukup’s original paper, the literature on chain covers and induced matchings, the Dushnik–Miller partition theorem, and the coloring-axiom and order-representation sources in the [ordinal review](ORDINAL-REVIEW.md). It located no previous statement of this hereditary matching requirement or its forcing-free consequence for the ordinal lengths of a witness.
