# An equivalent form of the semi-open coloring axiom

Write $`\mathrm{SOCA}_{\aleph_1}`$ for the statement that every open graph on a separable metric space of size $`\aleph_1`$ has an uncountable clique or independent set. Here openness means that the symmetric edge relation is open off the diagonal. A comparison decoder reads the signs of an ordered pair in a fixed countable sequence of well-orders. Its answer is symmetric on the realized signatures of distinct vertices. An edge predicate open relative to those signatures is the restriction of an open subset of Cantor space. [Abraham, Rubin and Shelah, §1](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95641/153.pdf) · [Ongay-Valverde, *Ken’s colorful questions*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0166864122002954)

**Theorem.** Over ZFC the following three assertions are equivalent.

1. $`\mathrm{SOCA}_{\aleph_1}`$ holds.
2. Every graph on $`\aleph_1`$ vertices with an open comparison decoder using well-orders of types strictly below $`\omega_1^2`$ has an uncountable induced subgraph that is constant, or whose edges are the pairs on which an order of type $`\omega_1`$ and an injective real labelling agree or disagree.
3. The same conclusion holds when every well-order used by the decoder has type at most $`\omega_1\cdot 2`$.

These conclusions can equivalently be required on every uncountable subset. Restrict the original presentation, retaining the upper bounds on the order types. The [short well-order theorem](SHORT-ORDINALS.md) proves the first implication. The second follows from the bounds. We prove that the third implies the axiom.

Let $`H`$ be an open graph on a separable metric space $`X`$ of size $`\aleph_1`$. Fix a countable base and any background well-order of type $`\omega_1`$. For each basic set $`U`$ take two well-orders, one listing $`U`$ before its complement and one listing it afterward. Retain the background order inside each block. Both well-orders have type at most $`\omega_1\cdot 2`$. Their comparison signs disagree exactly for a pair with one endpoint in $`U`$. For disjoint basic sets $`U,V`$ with $`U\times V`$ contained in the edge relation, require disagreement for both $`U`$ and $`V`$. The two disagreements certify an edge. Every edge has such a pair of basic neighborhoods, since the topology is Hausdorff and the symmetric edge relation is relatively open. Taking the union of these certificates gives a symmetric open decoder.

Apply assertion 3. A constant restriction gives the required homogeneous set. Suppose an uncountable restriction $`Y`$ instead has edges given by agreement between a real labelling $`r`$ and a well-order $`W`$ of type $`\omega_1`$. Reversing the real labelling covers disagreement as well. Give $`Y`$ the finer topology induced by $`y\mapsto(y,r(y))`$ in $`X\times\mathbb R`$. This topology is second countable and preserves openness of the graph. The union of its countable basic open sets is countable. In the remaining uncountable subspace $`D`$, every nonempty open set is uncountable. There are $`u,v\in D`$ increasing in both orders: otherwise $`r`$ would embed the reverse of an uncountable well-order into the reals. Choose open neighborhoods $`U,V`$ of these vertices in $`D`$ with $`r(U)`$ strictly below $`r(V)`$ and with every cross pair adjacent. Every element of the uncountable set $`U`$ then precedes $`v`$ in $`W`$. All strict initial segments of $`W`$ are countable. This rules out the nonconstant alternative for an open graph and proves the axiom.

## The first ordinal threshold

In ZFC, if every coordinate has type strictly below $`\omega_1\cdot 2`$ on $`\aleph_1`$ vertices, discard the countable tail after the first $`\omega_1`$ elements of every coordinate. On what remains, each point has countably many predecessors in each coordinate. Recursively choose $`\omega_1`$ points, excluding the previously chosen points and their predecessors in every coordinate. Each step excludes only countably many points. All coordinates now agree. Every symmetric decoder, even one with an arbitrary acceptance set, is constant on this restriction.

At $`\omega_1\cdot 2`$ there is a ZFC example with no uncountable homogeneous subset. Label the vertices injectively by infinite binary sequences with their lexicographic order and let $`W`$ be a well-order of type $`\omega_1`$. Take $`W`$ as one coordinate. For each digit position take two more orders, sorting by that bit in opposite directions and preserving $`W`$ within each bit class. The first position where these two comparisons disagree is the first differing digit of the two labels. The comparison in its zero-first order gives the lexicographic direction. Accept precisely when this agrees with $`W`$. Every acceptance has a finite certificate, and reversing the input pair preserves the answer. The resulting classical Sierpiński graph has no uncountable homogeneous set: the lexicographic order on binary sequences embeds in the reals, which have no strictly increasing or decreasing sequence of length $`\omega_1`$.

Together with the [upper threshold and its six-cycle example](SHORT-ORDINALS.md), this separates the ordinal ranges in which homogeneous restrictions, ordinal/real agreement restrictions, and unrestricted graphs occur. The grid and chromatic applications remain [scoped results for Soukup’s question](BOOLEAN-ORDERS.md).
