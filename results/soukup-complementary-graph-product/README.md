# Soukup’s complementary graph product problem

**Lean status:** none.

Lajos Soukup asks whether ZFC proves the existence of a graph on the first uncountable cardinal whose graph and complement are both uncountably chromatic while their categorical product is countably chromatic. In this product, two ordered pairs are adjacent when their first coordinates are adjacent in the graph and their second coordinates are adjacent in its complement. The general ZFC question remains open. [Soukup, Problem 1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08027v1#S1) · [Earlier handout, Problem 8.1](https://danieltsoukup.github.io/academic/norwich_handout.pdf#page=3)

Cinna Davis’s construction works after adjoining a single Cohen real. The witness comes from two linear orders on the same set. Its edges are the pairs on which those orders agree, so it is the comparability graph of their intersection. Under the bounded proper forcing axiom, neither a witness nor its complement has an uncountable induced subgraph that is the comparability graph of a partial order of finite dimension. In particular, for any partial order of finite dimension on at most the first uncountable cardinal, that axiom makes the chromatic number of the product equal to the smaller of the two factor chromatic numbers. The Cohen example violates this equality in dimension two. [One-Cohen construction](ONE-COHEN.md) · [Finite-dimensional obstruction](ORDER-DIMENSION.md)

Allowing arbitrary Boolean functions of finitely many order comparisons gives the same obstruction and the same chromatic equality on every infinite ground set up to the first uncountable cardinal. Every such graph restricts on an uncountable subset, up to taking its complement, to the comparability graph of an order of dimension at most three. The associated four-color bound for finite-order pair colorings is sharp by Galvin and Shelah’s 1973 construction. [Boolean order theorem](BOOLEAN-ORDERS.md) · [Source comparison and independent review](BOOLEAN-ORDERS-REVIEW.md) · [Decoder check](verify_boolean_orders.py)

ZFC implies chromatic product equality for graphs represented by a relatively open predicate on countably many well-orders, whenever every coordinate has type strictly below the square of the first uncountable ordinal. In either factor of any witness, every countable edge coloring of any uncountable restriction has a monochromatic countably infinite matching induced in the whole restriction. Consequently its edges need uncountably many chain graphs to cover them. [Matching and directed open relations](DIRECTED-OPEN.md)

The ordinal bound is exact in every model with a witness. At the square all graphs on that cardinal have such a representation. The classification below the square is itself equivalent to the semi-open coloring axiom. [Sharp threshold](SHORT-ORDINALS.md) · [Equivalent axiom and first threshold](SOCA-CONVERSE.md) · [Chromatic obstruction and star-forest construction](COUNTABLE-DECODERS.md) · [Independent review and checks](ORDINAL-REVIEW.md)

For any injection from an uncountable subset of an Aronszajn line into the reals, every finite square grid size occurs with uncountably many points in each cell. This gives the sharp bound of the square of the factorial of the arity for colorings determined by finitely many orders under BPFA. The written proof also gives a countable order-pattern theorem in ZFC. [Grid proof](ARONSZAJN-GRIDS.md) · [Independent review and checks](GRID-REVIEW.md)

A ZFC description of the product color classes gives a countable family of regressive partial maps preserving adjacency on distinct images on the whole first uncountable ordinal. An exhaustive finite check compares the component formula with the relation generated by disjoint edge pairs.
