# Independent review of Cinna Davis’s stable-core reduction

The reviewed target is the first question in §12 of Erdős’s 1987 paper, p. 227, attributed there to Péter Komjáth. It asks for a proper two-coloring of an arbitrary family of countably infinite sets with finite pairwise intersections and no singleton intersection. The second question in that section has a different forbidden intersection size. [Cinna Davis’s research manuscript](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/58f398f88a9a2743d24732742461e5cca3e9f5dc/work/846-komjath-property-b/LOW-CODEGREE-PEELING.md) proves that ordinary Property B is equivalent to Property B for its transfinite stabilized subfamily. The implication leaves the possible nonempty stable obstruction open. The existing main result in #863 concerns the impossibility of requiring two points of each color and supplies a proper coloring of its constructed family. [Erdős, §12](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-28.pdf#page=5) · [Earlier distinct result](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/863)

**Mathematical review.** A single witness assignment suffices for the full transfinite union. Given a finite tuple, use the first stage at which its codegree becomes countable and take the members still containing it at that stage. Every infinite restriction of any subsequently discarded edge has a tuple that places that edge in the assigned countable family. The assignment is an ordinary set and can be included in every elementary submodel. A finite tuple from the model belongs to it and its assigned countable list lies inside it. An outside peeled edge therefore has only finitely many points in the model. Restrictions to successive layers stay countably infinite and pairwise almost disjoint. The witness assignment transports through the injective map from the original edges to the layer remainders. Concatenating the inductively produced orders gives finite overlap with the full union of earlier peeled edges, including at singular cardinals.

The same argument gives this well-order for every essentially disjoint countable-uniform family by assigning a point to the unique cofinite tail containing it. The repair recursion flips a point of a monochromatic peeled edge outside every earlier peeled edge. Once an edge has both colors it is henceforth untouched. If a color originally present in a core edge is lost, take any originally present point which was flipped. The peeled edge causing this flip was monochromatic just before it. Its intersection with the core edge has a second point, which keeps the original color and remains permanently protected. This argument handles arbitrarily many prior flips and a possible purported loss at a limit stage. The independent review found both directions valid. An input coloring may change on the core’s ground set while its edges stay split in the final coloring.

**Attribution for the one-step criterion.** The low-codegree criterion by itself is a direct specialization of the 1986 Main Lemma 1.1 of Hajnal, Juhász and Shelah. Take their ideal to be the finite sets and their semi-ideal to consist of sets all of whose finite subsets have uncountable codegree. This semi-ideal is closed under unions of increasing chains since finite subsets of such a union occur in a single stage. Its subsets lying in a member of the family are finite by the one-step hypothesis. For an infinite cardinal and a ground set of that size, the number of members whose intersection with the ground set is outside the semi-ideal is at most that cardinal. Sum the countable edge lists over its finite subsets. Essential disjointness is additive over disjoint ground blocks after finite deletions; induction on ground cardinal supplies the smaller cofinite restrictions, and on countable ground the hypothesis itself implies countably many edges. These verify all three clauses of the published lemma. It applies at the countable uniformity as printed. Credit the static implication and the filtration method to this prior work. [Hajnal–Juhász–Shelah, Main Lemma 1.1, p. 370](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95149/249.pdf#page=2)

Menachem Kojman’s 2015 paper uses fixed intersection parameters and develops a general method of finding filtrations. His Theorem 4.7 and the earlier bounded-intersection results cover important special cases. Lajos Soukup’s paper derives consequences from hereditary essential disjointness and uses cardinal bounds on almost-disjointness in other uniformities. Neither source gives the least-stage countable assignment for an arbitrarily long peeling, followed by the relative one-point repair and the exact stable-core equivalence. The finite Lovász theorem and its quantitative versions concern finite hypergraphs. The finite ordering or a finite edge obstruction does not by itself extend to the infinite edges here. Searches by property B, forbidden singleton intersections, countable codegrees, rich or stabilized cores, hereditary essential disjointness, semi-ideals, transfinite filtrations and recoloring found no earlier exact stable-core theorem. This is a bounded source finding. [Kojman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.1307) · [Soukup](https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.1971) · [Quantitative Lovász criterion](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.04968)

**Elementary consequence.** Within almost-disjoint countably infinite families, a countable union of essentially disjoint subfamilies is essentially disjoint. Choose pairwise disjoint cofinite tails separately in each layer. Each point belongs to at most countably many chosen tails, so the intersection graph of tails has countable components. Enumerate each component in order type at most omega and remove from each tail its intersections with all its earlier neighbours. Only finitely many earlier tails occur and every intersection is finite. This gives disjoint cofinite tails across all components. Thus a sigma-essentially-disjoint stable core gives essential disjointness of the whole family. This elementary closure argument warrants no separate novelty claim.

**Reproducible checks.** A [finite regression](verify_core_repair.py) exhausts the legal choices of a flipped point for ordered sets on up to five vertices. It checks 348,768 complete repair histories and 1,446,380 initially split core-edge instances. In 844,156 of these instances the final coloring changed on the core edge. A negative control exhibits loss of a color when a cross-intersection may be a singleton. The written proof supplies the transfinite conclusion. This supplement has no Lean formalization.
