# Steinerberger's greedy-energy Fourier conjecture

## Result and scope

In [Steinerberger, *Some Open Problems*, March 31, 2026 revision, §20, p. 22](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf#page=22), the rule on the circle $\mathbb T=\mathbb R/\mathbb Z$ adds $x_{n+1}$ to minimize $\sum_{a=1}^n f(x-x_a)$, using the periodic Bernoulli polynomial $f(x)=x^2-x+1/6$ on $[0,1]$. Its explicitly proposed Fourier estimate is

$$
W_n:=\sum_{\ell=1}^n\frac1\ell
\left|\sum_{a=1}^n e^{2\pi i\ell x_a}\right|
=O(\log n).
$$

This estimate is false. Starting at any one point and choosing any minimizer at each step, the same exact Fourier magnitudes result. For $\ell\ge1$ and $k=v_2(\ell)$,

```math
\left|\sum_{a=1}^n e^{2\pi i\ell x_a}\right|
=2^{k+1}\left\|\frac{n}{2^{k+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}.
\tag{1}
```

In particular, using natural logarithms,

$$
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{W_n}{(\log n)^2}
=\frac{1}{6\log 2}.
\tag{2}
$$

The rule initialized at an arbitrary multi-point seed can behave differently. It is unnecessary to analyze that variant to refute the stated estimate: (2) holds for every permitted singleton initialization and every tie-breaking rule. The logarithmic energy and potential observations preceding the conjecture in §20 are separate; they are not refuted.

## Dyadic block structure

Let $G_j(\theta)=\theta+2^{-j}\mathbb Z/\mathbb Z$ be a grid of $2^j$ points. Summing the quadratic polynomial directly gives
```math
\sum_{x\in G_j(\theta)}f(t-x)=2^{-j}f(2^j(t-\theta)).
\tag{3}
```
Indeed, set $M=2^j$ and let $u$ be the fractional part of $M(t-\theta)$. The fractional parts in the sum are $(u+b)/M$ for $0\le b<M$; the sums of $b$ and $b^2$ give $f(u)/M$. The set of minimizers in (3) is the midpoint coset $G_j(\theta+2^{-j-1})$.

Write $n=\sum_{j\in J}2^j$ in binary. We claim that the first $n$ greedy points decompose, in order of insertion, into consecutive translated grids $G_j(\theta_j)$ with $j\in J$ in decreasing order. They have the further property
```math
G_i(\theta_i)\subset G_j(\theta_j+2^{-j-1})
\qquad(i,j\in J,\ i\lt j).
\tag{4}
```
For $n=1$ this is immediate. If the property holds at $n$, the midpoint cosets of the active grids are nested: for $i<j$, $\theta_i$ is in the midpoint coset of the $`j`$th grid by (4), and $2^{-i-1}$ and $2^{-i}$ are integer multiples of $2^{-j}$. Thus the midpoint coset of the smallest active grid is contained in all the others. Each block potential in (3) reaches its minimum on its own midpoint coset, so the minimizers of their sum are exactly that smallest midpoint coset.

Append an arbitrary chosen minimizer as a singleton. If the old singleton bit is occupied, the new point and old singleton constitute a two-point grid. If the next bit is also occupied, that two-point grid lies in the old two-point grid's midpoint coset, and it equals that coset by cardinality. The union is a four-point grid. Continue this binary carry until an unoccupied bit is reached. At each step the new carry-grid and each of its constituent points lie in the midpoint cosets of all still-active larger grids, by (4) for the old points and by minimization for the new point. This proves (4) at $n+1$, and completes the induction.

This is a direct proof for the specific kernel, not a novelty claim for the dyadic greedy construction. [Pausinger, *Greedy energy minimization can count in binary*, Theorems 2.1 and 5.5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10231-020-00990-7) gives the broader structure for symmetric strictly convex kernels and shows that the classical base-two van der Corput sequence is an admissible output. [Kritzinger, *Uniformly distributed sequences generated by a greedy minimization of the $`L_2`$ discrepancy*, arXiv:2109.06298v4, Theorem 4 and Algorithm 3, page 15](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.06298v4#page=15) also explicitly identifies the classical sequence for the corresponding periodic-$`L_2`$ algorithm with minimum-point tie-breaking.

## Exact Fourier magnitudes

Fix $\ell=2^kq$ with $q$ odd. The Fourier sum of the block $G_j(\theta_j)$ vanishes if $j>k$ and is $2^j e^{2\pi i\ell\theta_j}$ otherwise. A smaller block $i<j<k$ has the same phase as the $`j`$th active block, since (4) gives
```math
\theta_i-\theta_j\equiv 2^{-j-1}\pmod{2^{-j}},
```
whose product with $\ell$ is an integer when $j<k$. If $k$ is active, its phase is instead opposite to all those of lower blocks, since the product then is a half-integer. Let $r=n\bmod 2^k$. If $k$ is inactive, the magnitude is $r$. If it is active, the magnitude is $2^k-r$. In both cases this is $`2^{k+1}\|n/2^{k+1}\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}`$, proving (1) regardless of the grid translations or tie-breaking.

## Sharp asymptotic for the conjectured sum

Let $`a_j=\|n/2^j\|_{\mathbb R/\mathbb Z}`$ and $`L=\lfloor \log_2 n\rfloor`$. Grouping (1) by $`v_2(\ell)`$ gives
```math
W_n=2\sum_{k=0}^L a_{k+1}
\sum_{\substack{1\le q\le n/2^k\\q\ {\rm odd}}}\frac1q
=\sum_{k=0}^L a_{k+1}(\log n-k\log 2)+O(\log n).
\tag{5}
```
The second equality uses the estimate $\sum_{q\le t,\ q\ {\rm odd}}q^{-1}=\tfrac12\log t+O(1)$ uniformly for $t\ge1$, by the difference of the full and even harmonic sums.

Since $a_{j-1}=\min(2a_j,1-2a_j)$, we have $2a_j+a_{j-1}\le1$. Summing over $1\le j\le K$ and using $a_0=0$ shows $\sum_{j=1}^K a_j\le K/3+1/6$. Abel summation with the decreasing nonnegative weights in (5) consequently gives
```math
W_n\le \frac13\sum_{k=0}^L (\log n-k\log2)+O(\log n)
=\frac{(\log n)^2}{6\log 2}+O(\log n).
```
To attain the leading term take $n=1+4+\dots+4^m$. For $1\le j\le L+1$, the residue $n\bmod 2^j$ has alternating binary digits, so $a_j=1/3+O(2^{-j})$ uniformly. Explicitly the fractional part is $(1-2^{-j})/3$ for $`j`$ even and $(2-2^{-j})/3$ for $`j`$ odd. For $j=1$ either nearest-integer expression agrees. Inserting these into (5) gives equality in the leading term with an $O(\log n)$ remainder, proving (2). At $n=2^L$, (1) gives $W_n=1$, so the result is about the sharp upper order along all prefixes, not a lower bound along every prefix.

## Related bounds and priority

From (3) the potential is uniformly bounded:
```math
\left\|\sum_{a=1}^n f(\cdot-x_a)\right\|_\infty
\le \tfrac16\sum_{j\in J}2^{-j}<\tfrac13.
```
The unnormalized energy is
```math
\sum_{a,b=1}^n f(x_a-x_b)
=\frac12\sum_{j\ge1}a_j^2=O(\log(n+1)).
```
For example this follows from the Fourier coefficients $\widehat f(\ell)=1/(2\pi^2\ell^2)$ for $\ell\ne0$, (1), and $\sum_{q\ {\rm odd}}q^{-2}=\pi^2/8$. The sum over $j>L+1$ is bounded by the geometric decay there. On alternating binary prefixes the energy is of logarithmic order. This squared Fourier statistic is classical diaphony, equivalently the periodic $`L_2`$ discrepancy in this setting; its logarithmic order for van der Corput is not asserted as new. See [Kritzinger, op. cit., version 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.06298v4) and the literature cited there.

Pausinger's dyadic characterization and existing $`L_2`$ discrepancy work are prior ingredients. The claim addressed here is the different truncated, weighted **absolute** Fourier sum in Steinerberger's stated conjecture. Targeted searches for that exact sum, the explicit conjecture, and its van der Corput counterexamples did not locate a prior treatment. The argument and the leading constant (2) are given independently of any priority assertion.
