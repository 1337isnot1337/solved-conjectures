# Steinerberger’s greedy-energy Fourier conjecture

## Review outcome

The four-module development proves the full negation of the original logarithmic Fourier conjecture. It gives one actual infinite sequence allowed by the stated greedy rule and proves that its actual truncated weighted absolute Fourier sum is not asymptotically bounded by any constant times the logarithm of the prefix length. No finite-prefix test or unproved greedy-admissibility hypothesis replaces this result.

The mathematical and final source review passed. One bibliographic discrepancy was found and corrected by the proof author: arXiv:2109.06298 version 4, dated 8 January 2022, places the relevant statement at Theorem 4 about Algorithm 3 on page 15, rather than Theorem 6. The corrected Sequence module and scope document now name that exact version and statement. No mathematical declaration changed. ## Proof-chain review

The admissibility proof starts from the exact two-point Bernoulli identity `kernel t + kernel (t−1/2) = kernel (2t)/2`. Its proof handles both fractional-part regions, including their boundary. The global lower bound is attained at one half. The even and odd potential recurrences then give a strong-induction proof that every next van der Corput point globally minimizes its actual preceding potential. This proves admissibility for every index without finite enumeration or an unproved structural characterization.

The Fourier argument rotates a prefix by its next point. `rotatedSum_eq` expresses the rotation as multiplication of the actual complex sum by a unit-modulus character, and `rotatedSum_norm` preserves the magnitude. Even and odd recurrences prove an exact amplitude at every frequency of the form `4^j(2q+1)` on the alternating-binary prefix lengths `n_m=1+4+⋯+4^m`.

The growth module proves that the selected frequencies are injectively indexed by their scale and odd multiplier, and that the entire finite rectangle `j<d`, `q<2^d` lies inside the actual truncation at `n_(2d+1)`. Thus the lower bound does not count a frequency twice or use frequencies beyond the conjecture's upper limit. Positivity justifies discarding all other terms.

Each dyadic block of odd harmonic terms contributes at least one quarter. The resulting bound is

```math
W_{n_{2d+1}}\ge d^2/8.
```

The prefix-size bound gives

```math
\log n_{2d+1}\le (2d+2)\log 4.
```

For arbitrary `C≥0` and `N`, choosing an integer `d` above both `N` and `32 C log 4+1` makes the quadratic lower bound strictly exceed `C log n` at a prefix beyond `N`. The terminal `IsBigO` contradiction extracts a positive eventual bounding constant and removes real norms using the established nonnegativity of the statistic and of the logarithm on natural arguments. It therefore proves failure arbitrarily far out, not merely at the exceptional values `n=0` or `n=1`.
