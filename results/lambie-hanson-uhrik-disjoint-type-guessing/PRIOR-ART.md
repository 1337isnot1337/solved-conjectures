# Prior-art and proof audit

**Exact source.** [Lambie-Hanson and Uhrik, *Hajnal–Máté graphs, Cohen reals, and disjoint-type guessing*](https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12261), *Mathematika* 70 (2024), e12261, Question 6.1. Checked its [author preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.01828), §§2, 5, 6, against the published question. It asks for ordinary guessing for arbitrary sequences of uniformly depth-bounded types. It does not require the strong version or specify a numerical bound on the widths.

The paper already proves a uniform **strong** positive theorem for bounded widths (Theorem 2.7), strong guessing without bounds from club guessing (Theorem 2.8), and failure for unbounded original depth under PFA$(\omega_1)$ (Theorem 5.2). We do not claim any of these as new. The paper identifies bounded depth with unbounded widths as the unresolved case. The new parameter measures how far the shorter-side interleaving reaches into the other prefix. An unbounded-width family working in ZFC is $01\,0^{k+1}1^{k+1}$; the unbounded-width family $0^{k+1}1\,0\,1^{k+1}$ fails under PFA$(\omega_1)$. Both have original depth one; the exact theorem treats all sequences, not just these examples.

**Consistency input.** PFA$(\omega_1)$ is the restriction to proper posets of size $\aleph_1$, with at most $\aleph_1$ dense sets. Its ZFC-relative consistency is identified in the source and explicitly recalled on p. 2 of [Dobrinen, Krueger, Marun, Mota, and Zapletal](https://pedromarun.github.io/files/consistencyMM%28omega_1%29.pdf). No inference from the consistency of full PFA is needed. For the other direction the source proves strong guessing under club guessing, which follows from diamond; the latter holds in the constructible model.

**Search.** Searched by exact question number, both title spellings (with and without the hyphen), the arXiv identifier, both authors, bounded depth, unbounded width, and forcing with the topic. Checked [Lambie-Hanson’s publications](https://clambiehanson.github.io/papers.html), arXiv listings, and searched Hexagon. No earlier solution of the exact question or two-parameter theorem was located. This is a bounded search rather than a priority certification.

**Proof checks.**
- Normalize each word separately; the guess accepts its opposite, so orientation loses nothing. Depth counts precisely the upper-side points below the final lower point. The new parameter counts lower-side points before the last of those markers.
- In the positive proof only a fixed number of model endpoints is needed. Choosing the lower ladder ordinal itself below the first tail point of the upper ladder automatically controls an arbitrarily long lower tail. That short argument establishes ordinary, not strong, guessing.
- The exact flag of the upper ladder is equivalent to the entire normalized type test: all later upper points are beyond the cutoff, and all lower points must precede it. Requiring exact flags for all colored vertices takes care of both orientations automatically.
- When reducing to a model below a cut, a flag with a marker outside that cut is vacuous for an inner ladder. For all other flags, a cutoff outside the model acts exactly like no bound. Conversely a flag inside the model cannot hit an outside ladder that satisfies the guard, and for unguarded outside ladders it can be checked on the same-color prefix proxy. These are the two directions needed for the union, not merely one.
- Fresh colors can have arbitrarily large reach even after discarding any finite set. Their exact flags do not forbid their own ladders, because the disjointness check fails on the attached tuple.
