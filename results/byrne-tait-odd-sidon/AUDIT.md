# Review of the odd-length Sidon-set bound

The journal version of Byrne and Tait’s Theorem 1.5 gives a density gap at even word lengths for groups with a normal abelian subgroup of fixed index. Their Question (1) asks about odd lengths. In arXiv v1 the same question is numbered (2), and its Question (1) is different. Here every ordered word of the prescribed length, including words with repeated letters, must have a different product. The proof answers the journal question at every odd length and removes the normality hypothesis. [Journal statement and questions](https://doi.org/10.4153/S0008414X26102314) · [Preprint, concluding questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.07750v1)

## Independent mathematical review

Let $`m`$ be the size of the set and $`h`$ the index. Any pair of distinct commuting letters would give a collision by swapping their first two positions and using the same suffix. Each point stabilizer in the action on left cosets is an abelian conjugate, so at most one letter fixes each coset. The average permutation matrix fixes constants and preserves the zero-sum subspace. Removing the constant eigenvalue from its trace gives a real number at most $`h/m-1`$. When $`m>h`$, the triangle inequality forces an eigenvalue of modulus at least $`(1-h/m)/(h-1)`$. An eigenvector exists over the complex numbers without a diagonalizability assumption.

I checked the residual average by multiplying permutation matrices in word order. Injectivity gives equal weights to the elements represented by the words. The uniform group average is zero on the zero-sum subspace even for a nonnormal stabilizer, because the action is transitive. Acting on the eigenvector, the two pieces of the uniform average give $`\theta |\lambda|^k\le 1-\theta`$ for word density $`\theta`$. The residual average is a Euclidean contraction. Full density would force every eigenvalue on that subspace to vanish and hence is impossible for $`m>h`$. Separating $`m\ge2h`$ from $`m<2h`$ gives the uniform gap in the published question. The same identity with complementary multiplicities proves the stated bounded-representation variant. [Written proof](PROOF.md)

The exact verifier builds a cyclic rotation action on three modular coordinates, first with a normal abelian stabilizer of index three, then in a direct product with the symmetric group on three letters using a nonnormal abelian stabilizer of index nine. It enumerates all group elements and ordered triples, checks every column of the average/complement identity, and checks the claimed numerical inequality over the rationals. The two groups have orders 375 and 23,958, with 125 and 1,331 distinct triple products respectively. Both tests exercise the strict regime $`m>h`$. I checked the published and preprint versions, both authors’ publication lists, the indexed citing records, and searches using the exact odd-length question and the terms nonabelian Sidon, ordered products, bounded-index abelian subgroup and density gap. The authors’ even-length proof uses a different intermediate set. No earlier proof of the odd-length assertion appeared in the inspected sources.
