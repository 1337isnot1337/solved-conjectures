# Source, scope and attribution

Abreu, Brondani, de Lima and Oliveira proposed the precise finite bound and four-block equality condition in Conjecture 17, page 133 of *A note on the Nordhaus–Gaddum type inequality to the second largest eigenvalue of a graph*, AADM 11 (2017), 123–135. Their Proposition 16 evaluates the candidate family. Here the adjacency eigenvalues are ordered algebraically, counting multiplicity, and the graph or its complement may be disconnected. The statement has meaning for orders at least two. At order two the bound is strict, and at order four the path attains it. The conjecture does not prescribe the exact largest adjacency sum attainable at orders indivisible by four. [Primary source](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/img/doi/1452-8630/2017/1452-86301701123A.pdf#page=11) · [Brondani’s 2017 thesis](https://pep.ufrj.br/images/documentos/Teses/2017/Andre_Ebling.pdf)

**Attribution.** Cinna Davis announced the result, equality characterization and route through a sharp Seidel spread bound in issue #499 on 24 November 2026. The original issue contained the primary source and a description of the method. A search of both permanent branches, the public audit branch, earlier repository pull requests, and locally recoverable historical objects did not locate the full manuscript cited by the issue. On 27 November, WadeDavis and Codex reconstructed the argument. The signed-coordinate reduction, the maximizing equality transfer and the exact odd-order matrix consequence are documented in this reconstruction. The separate internal review covers the full mathematical argument. [Original claim](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/499) · [Independent review](REVIEW.md)

**Earlier work.** Nikiforov established the asymptotic second-adjacency Nordhaus–Gaddum bound, and Nikiforov and Yuan prove the bound $`n/\sqrt2-1`$ for the sum of the absolute values of the two second eigenvalues when $`n\ge15`$. This leaves a finite gap to the expression conjectured in 2017 and does not identify the four classes in its equality case. A 2026 treatment of generalized adjacency Nordhaus–Gaddum inequalities studies related limiting constants. [Nikiforov](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0506260) · [Nikiforov–Yuan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.4365) · [Generalized inequalities](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15941)

Two spectral classifications supply distinct ingredients. Xiong and Hou exclude nontrivial threshold-graph Seidel eigenvalues in the open interval from $`-\sqrt2`$ to $`\sqrt2`$. Haemers and Topcu characterize all Seidel matrices with at most two eigenvalues outside the interval from minus one to one, up to switching and complementation. We apply those theorems to a threshold matrix obtained from an arbitrary maximizing pair of eigenvectors. The sign of the difference of two rank-one spectral projectors appears in earlier work on interval matrices with a free diagonal. Here the zero diagonal is fixed, and the ordering of the ratios of the switched coordinates makes the maximizing signs a threshold creation word. [Xiong–Hou](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amc.2022.127177) · [Haemers–Topcu, Theorem 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.02522#page=4) · [Related interval-matrix sign optimization, Corollary 6](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.09088)

The bounded priority search checked the paper and its four OpenAlex citing records, the original and 2014 adjacency estimates, later work on weighted adjacency, signed complete graphs, Seidel spectra, threshold graphs, exact matrix spreads and generalized graph–complement inequalities. Searches used the authors, DOI, conjecture number, finite expression, matrix class and switching formulation. The collection’s normalized Laplacian problem #495, its combinatorial Laplacian dandelion result, and its general-position Nordhaus–Gaddum entry concern different invariants. No earlier proof of the exact finite adjacency bound with its equality characterization or of the unrestricted Seidel maximum at each order was identified in this screen. [Citing records](https://api.openalex.org/works?filter=cites:W2613793072&per_page=100&select=title,doi) · [Normalized problem](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/495)

The original Xiong–Hou paper has a corrigendum. Li, Wang and Brunetti subsequently record the same stronger gap, and a later threshold-matrix paper derives it as Theorem 3. These provide post-corrigendum support for the endpoint used in the odd-order argument.
