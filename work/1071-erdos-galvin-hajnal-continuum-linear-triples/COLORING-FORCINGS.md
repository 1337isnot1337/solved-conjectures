# Two coloring forcings at the continuum

For [EGH Problem 13](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1071), the natural route to a negative model is to force countable colorings of all linear triple systems on the continuum. The standard small-cardinal forcing and a superficially attractive no-new-reals version behave quite differently.

## Finite conditions have precaliber \(\omega_1\)

Let \(H\) be any linear triple system. Let \(Q\) consist of finite partial maps from its vertices to \(\omega\) with no monochromatic triple on the domain, ordered by extension. Adding a point to the domain can always be done with a fresh color, so the totality requirements are dense.

To check precaliber \(\omega_1\), take an \(\omega_1\)-sequence of conditions. Thin to finite domains of constant size forming a delta system whose common root has one fixed coloring. A pair of conditions that cannot be united has a monochromatic triple with at least two vertices in one of the two domains. Orient the conflict from one condition containing such a pair towards the other. Every fixed condition has at most \(\binom m2\) outgoing conflicts if its domain has size \(m\): each pair has at most one third vertex, and if that vertex is outside the root it is in at most one other tail. Thus this oriented conflict graph has uniformly bounded outdegree. Its finite subgraphs have bounded average degree and hence uniformly bounded chromatic number. Graph compactness and pigeonhole give an uncountable linked subfamily.

A new obstruction involving three linked conditions must have one vertex in each of their three tails. Make a triple system \(K\) on their indices to record these obstructions. For any two indices, there are at most \(m^2\) possible third indices, because a pair of tail vertices determines at most one third vertex. Close a chain of countable initial sets of indices under these countably many pair completions. Each \(K\)-triple has at least two indices in a common latest countable block, so an injective coloring of each block countably colors \(K\). One of these color classes gives an uncountable centered subfamily of \(Q\): a monochromatic triple in any finite union could only use one, two, or three tails, and all three cases have been eliminated. This also explains why the classical forcing-axiom result is easy below the continuum but does not supply the whole collection of continuum many totality requirements.

## The unrestricted countable-condition version collapses the continuum

Instead let \(R\) use countable domains closed under every defined pair completion of \(H\), with countable proper colorings, again ordered by extension. A larger prescribed countable set can be added to the domain: take the countable closure, and use pairwise distinct colors for the new vertices. No triple can have two old vertices and one new one by closedness, and any triple with at least two new vertices is split. Countable descending sequences have a union condition, so \(R\) adds no reals.

Nevertheless this does **not** give a model with \(\mathfrak c>\omega_1\). If the vertex set is uncountable, fix pairwise disjoint countably infinite lists \(B_\xi=(b_{\xi,n}:n<\omega)\) for \(\xi<\omega_1\). Given a ground real \(r\in2^\omega\) and an arbitrary condition, choose \(\xi\) such that \(B_\xi\) avoids its countable domain. On \(B_\xi\) prescribe color \(2n+r(n)\) for the \(n\)-th point. These colors are distinct, with infinitely many unused values. Close the enlarged domain and assign all other new vertices distinct unused colors. Thus it is dense to code \(r\) on one of these \(\omega_1\) lists. The generic total coloring codes every ground real on some list. As there are no new reals, the extension has CH.

The obstruction is not linearity or the pair closure. It is the ability of arbitrary countable conditions to encode an arbitrary real in a single generic block. A successful no-new-reals forcing would need to restrict such variations or use fundamentally different side conditions; none is claimed here.
