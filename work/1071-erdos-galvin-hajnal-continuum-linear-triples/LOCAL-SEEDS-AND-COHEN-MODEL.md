# Local monochromatic seeds for the continuum triple-system problem

Erdős, Galvin and Hajnal ask whether it is consistent that $`\aleph_1\lt\mathfrak c`$ and every linear triple system on $`\mathfrak c`$ vertices is countably chromatic. In a linear system each pair belongs to at most one triple. A proper coloring assigns at least two colors to every triple. We give two sufficient hypotheses for obtaining an uncountably chromatic system, and a model with $`\aleph_1\lt\mathfrak c\lt 2^{\aleph_1}`$ in which both sufficient hypotheses fail. We do not know whether all continuum-sized linear triple systems are countably chromatic in that model. [Original Problem 13, printed p. 509](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=85) · [Cinna Davis’s original construction](STICK-CONSTRUCTION.md) · [Existing forcing analysis](COLORING-FORCINGS.md) · [Open issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1071)

## Two sufficient hypotheses

Assume $`\mathfrak c>\aleph_1`$. Each of the following hypotheses suffices separately.

1. For some cardinal $`\aleph_1\le\mu\le\mathfrak c`$ of uncountable cofinality, there is a family $`\mathcal D\subseteq[\mu]^{\aleph_1}`$ of cardinality at most $`\mathfrak c`$ such that every subset of $`\mu`$ of size $`\mu`$ contains a member of $`\mathcal D`$.
2. There is a family $`\mathcal F\subseteq[\omega_1]^{\aleph_1}`$ of cardinality at most $`\mathfrak c`$ such that every coloring of $`\omega_1`$ by countably many colors has a monochromatic member of $`\mathcal F`$.

The first hypothesis permits a local cardinal $`\mu`$; taking $`\mu=\mathfrak c`$ recovers the input of the earlier proof. At $`\mu=\aleph_1`$ the second hypothesis asks for one monochromatic member somewhere in each partition. An individual family with that property can miss a whole uncountable set: transport the family inside one half of a partition of $`\omega_1`$ into two uncountable sets. The assertion here is a weakening of the requirement on the given family. No separation of the two existence principles is claimed.

The same construction works for both hypotheses. Use $`\omega_1`$ successive layers. Reserve disjoint packets in each layer, each of size $`\mu`$ for hypothesis 1 and of size $`\aleph_1`$ for hypothesis 2. Put a copy of the corresponding seed family on each packet. Seeds are bookkeeping sets. The hyperedges themselves will all be triples. Provide a nonempty initial layer. At each later layer allocate one packet for every countable tuple of pairwise disjoint nonempty seeds from earlier packets, allowing repeated empty slots. This uses at most $`\mathfrak c`$ packets per layer, since the total number of previously available seeds is at most $`\mathfrak c`$ and $`\mathfrak c^{\aleph_0}=\mathfrak c`$. Empty layers or additional unused vertices cause no difficulty; pad with isolated vertices at the end to obtain size exactly $`\mathfrak c`$.

Under hypothesis 1, partition each allocated packet into $`\mu`$ disjoint sets of size $`\aleph_1`$. Under hypothesis 2 use the whole packet as just one such set. On each set $`B`$, copy Todorčević’s coloring $`d:[\omega_1]^2\to\omega`$ taking every color on every uncountable set. For an assigned tuple $`(D_n:n\in\omega)`$, attach each pair of $`d`$-color $`n`$ to a different old point of $`D_n`$ when this slot is nonempty. The injection is taken separately within each $`B`$ and color. Distinct colors use disjoint old seeds. Two triples in the same $`B`$-construction cannot share an old point, and distinct new pairs meet in at most one vertex. Triples from different $`B`$-constructions at the same layer share at most one old point. Between different layers a later triple has two entirely new points. Thus the system is linear. Injective coloring separately on each $`B`$ gives an $`\aleph_1`$-coloring. [Todorčević’s square-bracket coloring](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/117/6408-11511_2006_Article_BF02392561.pdf)

Now suppose a countable coloring $`f`$ is given. For hypothesis 1 let $`I`$ consist of colors with $`\mu`$ occurrences in at least one packet. For each $`n\in I`$ choose a monochromatic local seed in such a packet. All these seeds are old by a common countable layer ordinal. Take a later packet assigned this tuple, with all slots outside $`I`$ empty. The combined number of vertices whose colors are outside $`I`$ is less than $`\mu`$ in this packet, by uncountable cofinality. One of its $`\mu`$ disjoint copies $`B`$ avoids them all. A color $`n\in I`$ occurs uncountably often in this $`B`$. The strong coloring gives a pair of $`d`$-color $`n`$ among them and its assigned old vertex has $`f`$-color $`n`$ as well.

For hypothesis 2 let $`I`$ consist of the colors of monochromatic seeds in any packet. Choose one such seed for each, again using a later packet for their tuple. By hypothesis 2 that very packet has a monochromatic seed; in particular, some $`n\in I`$ occurs uncountably often there. The same pair argument gives a monochromatic triple. It does not require all colors of this packet to belong to $`I`$.

## A model in which every proposed family is split

Start from GCH. Take the product $`P\times C`$ of the ground-model forcings
```math
P=\mathop{\mathrm{Fn}}(\omega_3\times\omega_1,2,{\lt}\omega_1),
\qquad C=\mathop{\mathrm{Fn}}(\omega_2\times\omega,2,{\lt}\omega).
```
Thus $`P`$ has countable partial functions as conditions and $`C`$ has finite partial functions. In the extension the original cardinals are preserved and
```math
2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2,\qquad 2^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_3.
```
Every family of at most $`\aleph_2`$ uncountable subsets of $`\omega_2`$ can be split simultaneously by one set, with both parts of each member having its original cardinality. This applies also to families on $`\omega_1`$.

For the arithmetic, $`P`$ is countably closed and has the $`\aleph_2`$ chain condition under ground-model CH by the delta-system lemma for countable supports. It preserves all cardinals and adds no countable sequences of ordinals. Force with $`P`$ first and then the ground $`C`$, which is still the usual finite-condition Cohen forcing and is ccc. It adds $`\aleph_2`$ reals. In the intermediate model $`\aleph_2^{\aleph_0}=\aleph_2`$ still holds, giving the upper bound by countable names. The product has the $`\aleph_2`$ chain condition as well, since $`C`$ has precaliber $`\aleph_2`$ by the finite delta-system lemma. The $`\aleph_3`$ columns of $`P`$ give distinct subsets of $`\omega_1`$. For the upper bound the product has size $`\aleph_3`$ in the ground model, and a nice name for a subset of $`\omega_1`$ uses at most $`\aleph_1`$ conditions. Under ground GCH there are at most $`\aleph_3^{\aleph_1}=\aleph_3`$ such names.

Fix a name for a sequence enumerating the proposed family. A nice name for its relation on $`\omega_2\times\omega_2`$ uses at most $`\aleph_2`$ conditions. Their countable $`P`$-supports mention a ground set $`S`$ of at most $`\aleph_2`$ columns. The family already belongs to the intermediate extension by $`P\restriction S`$ and all of $`C`$. In the ground model choose an injection $`j:\omega_2\to\omega_3\setminus S`$. In the remaining product let a vertex $`\xi`$ belong to the splitter when the generic value at $`(j(\xi),0)`$ is one. For each member $`A`$ enumerate it without repetitions in its cardinal order $`\kappa=\aleph_1`$ or $`\aleph_2`$ in the intermediate extension. Given a remaining condition, a cutoff below $`\kappa`$ and a bit, choose a point of $`A`$ beyond the cutoff whose assigned column is outside the countably many columns mentioned in the condition. Add its single prescribed bit. This is still a ground-model condition, even though the enumeration was selected in the intermediate extension. Density and preservation of regularity give $`\kappa`$ points of $`A`$ for each bit.

Consequently no family in hypothesis 1 at either possible packet cardinal can refine every full-sized set. Indeed the splitter divides each candidate seed and at least one part of the domain has full size. No family in hypothesis 2 can have the required uncountable chromatic number either. In the terminology of generalized reaping, the construction gives $`\mathfrak r_{\omega_1}=\aleph_3`$ in this model. The fresh-coordinate splitting argument is standard Cohen forcing; its use here tests the proposed sufficient hypotheses. A splitter of uncountable seeds need not split a specified three-element edge. [Fischer and Soukup on generalized reaping](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.02791) · [Chen and Galgon on splitting, hitting and stick principles](https://www.yorku.ca/chenwb37/papers/antichains1.pdf) · [Fuchino, Shelah and Soukup on sticks and product forcings](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9804153)

## A restriction on a history-tree alternative

There are $`\mathfrak c`$ countable sequences from a set of size $`\mathfrak c`$, so one might replace the preassigned uncountable seeds by countable initial segments of an eventual sequence of witnesses. Consider the following specific rule. Nodes form a tree of countable histories of old labels, closed under unions of increasing chains of length less than $`\omega_1`$. A triple consists of two distinct comparable nodes $`s\subsetneq w`$ and an old label that already occurs among the entries of $`w`$. At each stage suppose there are uncountably many allowed immediate extensions, including at every union node along the construction. Nodes and old labels are disjoint vertex sets.

For any *linear* triple system obeying this rule there is a branch of length $`\omega_1`$ on which no pair of distinct limit-level nodes is an edge with any old label. At a stage with current node $`t`$, list each ancestor $`s`$ of $`t`$, including $`t`$, and each old label $`x`$ that has appeared in $`t`$. There are countably many pairs $`(s,x)`$. By linearity each has at most one possible completing node $`w`$ anywhere in the tree. Each strict-descendant completion lies in just one immediate-successor cone of $`t`$. Choose an allowed successor outside all the listed cones. Take unions at limits. If limit-level nodes $`s\subsetneq w`$ on the resulting branch had an eligible edge, its old label $`x`$ would have appeared strictly before the limit height of $`w`$. At a stage below $`w`$ where both $`s`$ and $`x`$ were already available, the cone leading to $`w`$ was excluded. This is impossible.

This conclusion addresses a construction requiring its comparable-pair gadget to work on every branch. It leaves open constructions relating incomparable nodes, introducing new labels at limit nodes, or severely restricting the possible continuations. Eric Li’s result gives a linear system of chromatic number $`\aleph_1`$ of size at most $`2^{\mathfrak c}`$ and resolves different questions on obligatory configurations and spectra. The older theorem quoted by Erdős, Galvin and Hajnal already gives a witness at $`\mathfrak c^+`$. Neither bound supplies a witness at $`\mathfrak c`$ in the unresolved arithmetic regime. [Eric Li, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24882v2) · [Original cardinal bounds, p. 509](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=85)

## Independent review

A second project researcher reviewed the proof at SHA-256 `b8d39b9253b8aae5f8a1ae73f1fcf74e19ba145ccb0e3e15d527c56bfbac7b24`. The review checked the extraction of one monochromatic seed in the very packet assigned the selected tuple, cardinal preservation and the nice-name support bound for the entire named family. In the final forcing factor each density argument extends an old countable condition by a single ground-model coordinate. The reviewer also checked that the history-tree argument concerns only comparable nodes on a branch whose old labels appeared earlier. The two local hypotheses extend Cinna Davis’s seed construction. The session coordinator proposed the product model to test those hypotheses, and the proof and review were developed during the same research session. The consistency question remains open.
