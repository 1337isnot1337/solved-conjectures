# The ordinal threshold for open order representations

Let $`X`$ have cardinality $`\aleph_1`$. A countable list of well-orders gives each ordered pair of distinct vertices a sequence of comparison bits. An **open representation** of a simple graph accepts exactly its edges using an open subset of the binary sequence space. It suffices for the set of accepting profiles to be relatively open among the realized profiles, since such a set extends to an open set of the whole space. Reversing the pair complements every raw bit. Both orientations of each realized profile give the same verdict. Intersect the accepting open set with its image under simultaneous reversal to obtain a symmetric open extension. A decoder that first orients the pair by one distinguished coordinate also fits, since this normalization is continuous.

**Theorem.** Assume the size-$`\aleph_1`$ semi-open coloring axiom, or SOCA. If all orders of an open representation have ordinal type strictly below $`\omega_1\cdot\omega_1`$, every uncountable vertex subset has an uncountable restriction of one of the following forms. Its induced graph is constant, or its edges record agreement or disagreement of a well-order of type $`\omega_1`$ with a real order. Here a real order embeds in the real line. In particular, graphs with such representations cannot witness Soukup's problem. Under the same axiom the ordinal bound is sharp. There is a specific graph that has an open representation with orders of type at most $`\omega_1\cdot\omega_1`$ and has none with all types strictly smaller. This conclusion about the boundary holds regardless of the existence of an unrestricted Soukup witness.

SOCA in the proof says that an open symmetric relation on distinct pairs of a separable metric space of size $`\aleph_1`$ has an uncountable homogeneous subset. We use this only on subspaces of the Baire space. Such subspaces embed topologically in the real line. The bounded proper forcing axiom implies this size-restricted principle through the size-restricted open coloring axiom. [Abraham, Rubin and Shelah, §1, p. 132](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95641/153.pdf#page=10) · [Asperó and Bagaria, §3.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0168-0072(00)00058-0)

## Countably many blocks and two open relations

Start with any uncountable vertex subset, enumerate it anew in type $`\omega_1`$ and use this enumeration as the background order. Restrictions of the given well-orders still satisfy the bound. For each coordinate split its order into consecutive blocks of type $`\omega_1`$ followed by a possible smaller block. Division by $`\omega_1`$ on the left gives a countable ordinal quotient, so the entire coordinate has only countably many blocks. Each block has an injective rank map from its vertices into $`\omega_1`$.

There is a closed unbounded set $`D\subseteq\omega_1`$ of ordinals closed under all the partial block-rank maps and their partial inverses. For unboundedness, start above any ordinal and repeatedly increase the countable bound past all images of smaller ordinals under the countably many maps; its countable limit has the required closure. Suprema of increasing limit sequences of such bounds again have the closure. If $`\alpha\lt \beta`$ lie in $`D`$ in one block with rank map $`f`$, closure at $`\beta`$ gives $`f(\alpha)\lt \beta`$ and $`f(\beta)\ge\beta`$, the latter by inverse closure. Thus every block order agrees with the background order on its surviving vertices.

Code each vertex of $`D`$ by its sequence of block labels. In each coordinate identify the countable set of labels with distinct natural numbers and retain the known strict order among these labels. If one code occurs uncountably often, every pair there has the same signature in its background orientation. Otherwise restrict to one vertex per code, leaving an uncountable set $`Z`$ of distinct codes in the Baire space. Order these codes by their images in a fixed topological embedding in the reals. The resulting real order strictly separates the two points of a pair by open neighborhoods.

On an unordered pair of codes, sort the two codes in that real order. Produce two hypothetical signatures of this sorted pair. On different block labels each signature uses their fixed order. On equal labels the first signature gives the sorted pair forward background order, and the second gives it reverse background order. Each signature is continuous on distinct pairs. Near a given pair, the real sorting is locally constant as a choice of the first and second point, and each queried block label is a discrete coordinate. Pulling the symmetric open decoder back under each of these maps gives two open pair relations. Apply SOCA first to one and then to the other. Both hypothetical verdicts are constant on the resulting uncountable set. The true verdict there selects between just these two constants according to agreement of the real and background orders. This proves the canonical restriction.

For a nonconstant canonical restriction, reversing the real order if needed expresses its graph as the comparability graph of the intersection of the background and real orders. The background well-order is a linear extension. The preceding ZFC replication argument rules out such an uncountable induced graph in either factor of a Soukup witness. It also rules out every uncountable homogeneous induced subset. It follows that SOCA alone gives the chromatic product equality for this representation class on every infinite ground set of size at most $`\aleph_1`$. The smaller-chromatic-number cases use the full-size clique argument. [ZFC linear-extension obstruction](ORDER-DIMENSION.md) · [Chromatic equality on smaller ground sets](BOOLEAN-ORDERS.md)

## Sharpness at the ordinal square

The star-forest representation from Fox's edge partition gives every graph on $`\omega_1`$ an open representation by countably many well-orders. Each order is a sum over at most $`\omega_1`$ components enumerated in initial ordinal order. Enumerate the leaves of each star in their initial ordinal order. With the center first or last, a block has type at most $`\omega_1+1`$. The whole order therefore has type at most $`(\omega_1+1)\cdot\omega_1=\omega_1\cdot\omega_1`$. [Star-forest decoder](COUNTABLE-DECODERS.md) · [Fox, Theorem 4 and §4](https://people.dm.unipi.it/dinasso/ULTRABIBLIO/Fox%20-%20An%20infinite%20color%20analogue%20of%20Rado%27s%20theorem.pdf#page=5)

For the lower bound put an ordinal order of type $`\omega_1`$, a real order and an Aronszajn order on the same set, and join two vertices when the three orders agree on their relative order. Every uncountable subset induces a chordless cycle of length six on some six of its vertices. To see this, take the height-two poset with lower vertices $`a_1,a_2,a_3`$, upper vertices $`b_1,b_2,b_3`$, and $`a_i\lt b_j`$ exactly when $`i\ne j`$. Three linear orders realize it. The order indexed by $`i`$ lists the other two lower vertices, then $`b_i`$, then $`a_i`$, then the other two upper vertices, using increasing subscripts within the outside lists. Each required relation holds throughout. The incomparable lower-upper pair with index $`i`$ is reversed exactly in the $`i`$th order, and two elements in either shore are ordered both ways in the lists indexed by their two subscripts. The grid theorem realizes these three relative orders on six vertices of every uncountable set. Their comparability graph is the six-cycle. [Aronszajn grid theorem](ARONSZAJN-GRIDS.md)

This six-cycle cannot be the agreement graph of two linear orders. Their intersection would orient its edges transitively. Since the graph has no triangles, a vertex cannot have both an incoming and an outgoing edge. Connectedness therefore forces one bipartite shore below the other. The represented poset is the displayed one up to duality and relabelling. A linear extension can reverse at most one of its three incomparable pairs $`a_i,b_i`$, since reversing two would give $`b_i\lt a_i\lt b_j\lt a_j\lt b_i`$. Two linear orders cannot realize the poset. A disagreement graph of two orders gives the agreement graph after reversing one order, and a constant graph also omits the induced six-cycle. The canonical restriction now shows that the three-order example cannot have an open representation in strictly shorter well-orders under SOCA.

The coloring axiom, Fox's star-forest partition and the six-vertex standard example are prior ingredients. A bounded comparison with sources on semi-open colorings, permutation graphs, star arboricity and ordinal lengths of countable order representations located no preceding statement of this cutoff for open decoders. This is a written structural result toward the representation question in the existing Soukup campaign. It leaves the unrestricted ZFC question open.
