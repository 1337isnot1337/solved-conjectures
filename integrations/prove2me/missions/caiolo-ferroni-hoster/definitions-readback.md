For any ambient type $P$ and matroid $M$ on $P$, the simplicity predicate holds exactly when every finite subset of $P$ with at most two elements is independent in $M$, including the empty set and all singletons. For any field $F$, any $F$-module $V$ whose additive group is abelian, and any type $X$, with $F,V,X$ in a common type universe, the point type is the disjoint union of the one-dimensional $F$-subspaces of $V$ and $X$. For any matroid $M$ on this point type and any natural number $r$, a flat of rank $r$ is a flat of $M$ whose extended rank equals the finite value $r$. For any subset $A$ of the point type and any list $s=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$ of natural numbers, a flag bounded by $A$ consists of flats $A\supseteq B_1\supseteq\cdots\supseteq B_m$ of extended ranks $r_1,\ldots,r_m$, respectively. The inclusions allow equality, and the empty list has exactly one flag for every $A$. For $F,V,X$ in the base type universe, the real-valued count $f_M(s)$ is the natural cardinality of the flags bounded by the ground set $E(M)$, viewed as a real number, using $0$ for an infinite flag type. The signed sublists of any natural-number list are obtained by independently retaining or omitting each position, preserving relative order, and giving each result sign $(-1)^k$ when $k$ positions are omitted. At each recursive step, the sublists retaining the first position precede those omitting it. Distinct choices of positions may give repeated entries, and the empty input gives just the empty list with sign $+1$. Let $\mathcal S$ be the list obtained from the signed sublists of $(4,3,2,1)$ by dropping the signs. For any commutative ring $R$ with unity, $x\in R$, natural number $t$, and natural-number list $s=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)$, let $d_0,\ldots,d_m$ be the successive differences along $(t,r_1,\ldots,r_m,0)$, with subtraction truncated at zero. For a natural number $d$, put $a_d(x)=-(-1)^d\sum_{0\le j\le d}x^j$ and $c_d(x)=-(-1)^d\sum_{0\le j<d}x^j$, so that $a_0(x)=-1$ and $c_0(x)=0$. The augmented weight is $\prod_{i=0}^{m}a_{d_i}(x)$, and the Chow weight is $c_{d_0}(x)\prod_{i=1}^{m}a_{d_i}(x)$. For the empty list, the sole difference is $d_0=t$ and the product over $1\le i\le m$ is $1$. For any function $f$ from lists of natural numbers to $R$, the dual Chow and dual right augmented expressions sum $f(s)$ times the respective Chow and augmented weights over $s\in\mathcal S$, both with $t=5$. The corresponding matroid polynomials take $R=\mathbb R[T]$, $x=T$, and $f(s)$ equal to the constant polynomial with value $f_M(s)$. The matroid polynomial definitions apply to every $M$ on the specified point type, with the weights starting at $5$ in every case.
