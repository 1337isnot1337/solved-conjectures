## Integer root lift

The six `RootInteger` modules convert modular root covers into an actual
integer relation. They bound a product of 111 factors, use the product of
distinct primes to force a zero integer factor, and recover either two
distinct rows of the literal relation catalogue or a permuted positive
`{x,3*x,4*x}` triple. The hypotheses still require a root cover at every
chosen prime and the explicit prime-product inequality.

All six integer-lifting sources passed local warning-as-error compilation.
The repaired final lift uses the frozen v2 source. The `root-integer` task checks all 29 modules in its closure,
including every transitive project theorem and independent kernel replay.

`PrimeData` records the 230 root primes and 574 plane/prime pairs.
`PrimeProducts` supplies the proposed numerical product inequalities at
coordinate bound 279841. Its dedicated hosted task is the first complete
gate for these literal data and proofs.

## Assembly interfaces

`FiniteAssembly` proves the exact reduction from bounded-root coverage,
canonical-plane coverage, and bounded-map coverage to sporadic exhaustion,
assuming the four-speed gap. The gap is already an accepted unconditional
theorem. `RootReduction` converts the integer-root outcome to the canonical
representation required by the assembly. Both sources remain candidates;
the `finite-assembly` task checks their complete dependency closure.

The missing concrete input is still complete root coverage across 230 primes.
The plane certificates are being generated and checked separately. Three
isolated root-block probes measure the guarded bit-search implementation at
small and large primes; they do not claim a full root cover.

Every hosted task recompiles its local dependency closure, checks owned
theorems against the standard three-axiom whitelist, and replays every local
module using the unmodified pinned kernel checker. Generation and source
compilation alone are not recorded as full verification.
