# Review of the real roots of book domination polynomials

The one-page book is a four-cycle. Its domination polynomial has a double zero at the origin and no other real zero. The earlier counterexample explicitly leaves the classification for two or more pages open. Rather’s Proposition 5.3 gives two existence intervals for even orders. The present proof settles the number, location and multiplicity of every real zero for every order, addressing the real part of Alikhani, Brown and Jahari’s root question. [Rather, Proposition 5.3 and Conjecture 5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08998v2#S5) · [Earlier counterexample and scope](https://the-omega-institute.github.io/trureturing-mdbook/Library/Geometry/rather2026bookroots.html) · [Alikhani–Brown–Jahari, Question 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1401.2092#page=12)

I rederived the endpoint-conditioned generating function and checked the double root at zero separately at one and two pages. For even orders the divided polynomial decreases across the near-zero interval. On the middle interval its positive term is bounded by the endpoint values. Beyond two, the derivative of the difference of powers is strictly positive and its limit is infinite. For odd orders both exterior regions have positive sign in the transformed variable. On the remaining interval the substitution $`u=t/(1-t)`$ gives a strictly convex function diverging at both ends. The first summand has positive logarithmic second derivative. For the second, the explicit lower bound on $`v''/v'`$ and the positive term $`2f'/f`$ prove positivity of the second derivative. The rational test points place the minimum strictly below one, yielding two simple crossings. [Proof](README.md)

The supplied verifier passed direct domination counts through seven pages, exact real-root isolation through 48 pages, and the rational sign constants. In a separate symbolic check I differentiated both factors of the odd-case ratio, checked the substitution as an identity of rational functions at nine odd orders, and counted roots by Sturm’s theorem in the four intervals cut out by $`-2,-1/2,0`$ at each of the first twelve orders. All counts agreed. The asymptotic range of orders is covered by the analytic proof.
