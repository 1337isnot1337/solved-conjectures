# Independent review of the uniform lower-cutoff theorem

On 27 November 2026 UTC, a separate Codex reviewer in the WadeDavis research session examined the extension to every $`\alpha\ge a`$ for sufficiently high rank. The joint limiting regimes include both parities and arbitrarily rapid growth of $`\alpha`$ relative to $`N`$. The argument establishes the stated exact minimizers in those regimes. [Half-line proof](HALF-LINE.md) · [Compact-interval calculation](PROOF.md) · [Source audit](SOURCE_AUDIT.md)

1. **Entropy at the mean.** Strictly positive discrete Gaussian masses have decreasing adjacent ratios. Every order-two minor of their Toeplitz kernels is nonnegative. Finite-intermediate-index Cauchy–Binet followed by entrywise convergence preserves that sign for the convolution. For its integer mean $`m`$, concavity of the interpolated logarithm gives $`\log f(m)\ge\mathbb E\log f(S)`$. Adding an independent uniform variable contributes $`1/12`$ to the variance and converts discrete entropy exactly into differential entropy. The maximum-entropy Gaussian bound therefore gives the required lower bound at zero even before the shifted site parameters have been bounded. Corollary 4.3 of Bobkov, Marsiglietti and Melbourne independently supplies the convolution assertion. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1017/S096354832100016X)
2. **Dimensionless penalty.** After pairing opposite integers about a half-integer, the factor remaining after the common quadratic shift is a weighted average of $`\cosh((2k-1)z)`$, at least $`\cosh z`$. On $`|z|\le\alpha/2`$, the Gaussian prefactor loses at most $`|z|/2`$ in the logarithm. For bounded $`z`$ and large $`\alpha`$, a quadratic lower bound follows from the positive continuous extension of $`\log\cosh(z)/z^2`$. The compact-interval midpoint argument covers the remaining scales.
3. **Odd parity and first localization.** The baseline contributes the exact factor $`e^{\alpha/(4N)}`$ in odd $`N`$. It can be large. Keeping it unexpanded allows comparison with the tilt $`e^{\alpha Nu^2}`$ through the inequality $`(J+s)^2-j_0^2\ge-2j_0|s|`$. The resulting linear cost per coordinate is $`|z_i|/N`$. Completing the square at small $`z_i`$ costs only $`O_a(N^{-1})`$ overall. Together with the entropy lower bound and the midpoint upper local probability, this first bounds every $`z_i`$ uniformly.
4. **Uniform local limit.** On this bounded box the two neighboring lattice atoms retain positive probabilities independent of $`N,\alpha`$. Gaussian tails uniformly bound arbitrarily high centered moments. These estimates give the Fourier envelope over the whole inversion interval and the relative error $`O_a(N^{-1})`$ at bounded integer offsets from the mean. The variance ordering $`V\le Nv_*`$ then gives the stronger bound $`\sum z_i^2=O_a(N^{-1})`$. Finally, centering the sum and using the bounded first derivative of the tilted mean bounds $`J`$. Each of these bounds is uniform for the unbounded scale interval.
5. **Exchange and conditional curvature.** Swapping two dimensionless deviations at fixed original tilt preserves their sum and therefore stays in the primal hyperplane. The endpoints are equivalent by a permutation followed by a root-lattice translation. On the entire segment, adding a common dimensionless increment of size $`O_a(N^{-1/2})`$ restores the zero mean. For the two-coordinate marginal, the Fourier bound after omitting two factors is uniform, including their tails. Its conditional variance differs from $`2v_*`$ by $`o_a(1)`$. Factoring the Hessian as $`4\alpha^2(\mathrm{Var}-1/\alpha)`$ is important on an unbounded interval. The variance gap inside the parentheses is uniformly positive, which gives the strict convexity needed for equality.
6. **Final levels and fixed ranks.** The comparison expands only the logarithm of the midpoint *probability* ratio. Its $`o_a(N^{-1})`$ remainder stays uniform for the bounded admissible offsets, while $`\alpha(j^2-j_0^2)/N`$ stays exact. In particular, the common scalar phase classes with nearest indices give precisely the minima even along $`\alpha/N\to\infty`$. An absolute steep-scale threshold across all ranks follows by applying the result with $`a=1`$, then taking a finite maximum of the fixed-rank thresholds. Bachoc, Moustrou, Vallentin and Zimmermann’s uniqueness near each deep hole and strict separation away from the deep holes are the parts supplying the exact minimizer set for those remaining ranks. The normalization is the standard root length squared two. [BMVZ, Theorems 1.3, 2.1 and 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08819v2#S2)

The rank threshold and the dimension-independent steep-scale cutoff are existential. The separately [reviewed small-scale argument](SMALL-SCALE-REVIEW.md) handles scales tending to zero, completing the classification at all parameters in sufficiently high ranks.
