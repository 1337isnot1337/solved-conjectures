* At a scalar translate with common residue $`k/N`$, integer lifts identify the lattice weights with $`N`$ independent midpoint discrete Gaussians conditioned on their integer sum being $`k`$. Replacing $`k/N`$ by $`1/2`$ inside the energy contributes only the constant $`\alpha(k-N/2)^2/N`$ to the mass exponent. Conditional permutation invariance centers $`K_1-K_2`$ and makes the log Hessian a scalar on the standard representation. Gaussian tails justify differentiation.
* If $`Y=K-1/2`$, then $`\mathbb E(Ye^{itY})=\psi'(t)/i`$ and $`\mathbb E(Y^2e^{itY})=-\psi''(t)`$. Thus the insertion for the squared difference divided by two is $`\psi^N[-(\log\psi)'']`$, with the sign of the cross term positive. Inversion at $`S=k`$ gives the cosine weight in the manuscript. The midpoint characteristic is real and even, and vanishes simply at both endpoints. For even $`N`$ the weight is nonnegative, and for odd $`N`$ the factor $`\cos(t/2)`$ is also positive throughout the interior. The logarithmic singularity of its second derivative is of order inverse squared distance to an endpoint; multiplication by $`\psi^N`$ is integrable even for $`N=2`$.
* Poisson summation at half-integers gives the alternating reciprocal Gaussian sum. Jacobi's product extraction, the coefficients after twice differentiating the logarithm and the factor $`2`$ in each paired hyperbolic cosine agree exactly. Away from zero the weighted curvature strictly exceeds the midpoint variance. Dividing the second derivative on $`e_1-e_2`$ by its squared norm two gives the asserted positive dimension-independent bound. At fixed scale the span-one local limit for $`N-2`$ unconstrained variables at a bounded shifted center gives the limiting two-coordinate variance. Domination at all shifts follows by the absolute Fourier bound on the largest mass and the positive asymptotic central probability. This establishes sharpness in the limit of ranks, with strict inequality in each finite rank. [Jacobi products](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.5)
* On a negation-invariant multiset of deviations modulo one, the determinant-one phase constraint forces an even multiplicity of phase minus one. Opposite pairs therefore have real lifts summing to zero, up to a root-lattice translation. Since the midpoint characteristic is real on the real axis, the complex offsets form conjugate pairs. Their product is real and even. For each Jacobi factor the triangle inequality is strict at every interior point if its phase is nonintegral. The Gaussian prefactor exactly cancels the displacement energy in modulus. The classification on this locus and its equality case follow. In even coordinate dimension the prior conjugation-invariant calculation already gave this minimizer. In odd coordinate dimension the earlier invariant locus was centered at the identity, whereas the present loci surround the two nonreal deep phases.
* In the even adjacent-level interpolation, integrating the derivative of $`\psi^N\sin(st)`$ removes the Gaussian tilt derivative; the zero boundary values hold for every $`0\lt s\le1`$. The remaining integrand has fixed positive sign on both half intervals. In the odd comparison, the reference midpoint characteristic at $`\alpha/N`$ has parameters $`q^N,N\pi/\alpha`$. Exactly the terms with indices divisible by $`N`$ cancel in the differentiated ratio. Even after normalization the ratio tends to zero at the right endpoint with order $`N-1`$ and is strictly decreasing inside. The one-variable Fourier coefficient ratio at $`3/2`$ and $`1/2`$ is $`e^{-2\alpha/N}`$, while $`\cos(3t/2)/\cos(t/2)=2\cos(t)-1`$. Strict positive covariance for two decreasing functions gives the claimed sign after restoring the tilt.
* The reciprocal lattice of $`A_{N-1}`$ splits into the $`N`$ cosets $`c_l+A_{N-1}`$, with pairing $`-kl/N`$ modulo one and covolume $`\sqrt N`$. Hence the real finite Fourier transform, period, reflection multiplicities and nonzero-class strict maximum at zero are as stated. For $`N=6,7`$ the coefficients of the displayed transformed difference have signs positive, negative, positive, including the singly counted middle term for $`N=6`$. Their sum is zero; positivity of the last known difference forces the first adjacent gap to be strict. For $`N=5`$ the neighbor and maximum exhaust all three distinct levels.
