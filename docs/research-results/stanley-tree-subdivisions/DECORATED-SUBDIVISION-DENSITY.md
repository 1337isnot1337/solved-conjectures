# Independent subdivision of a tree with arbitrary terminal decorations

Fix a finite tree skeleton with at least two hubs, each of degree at least three, and $`r\ge4`$ leaves. At each leaf prescribe a rooted cap $`C_i`$, either a single vertex or a tree whose root has at least two children. The arm is attached to its designated hub and has $`u_i`$ unary vertices followed by this cap. Its total order is $`a_i=u_i+d_i`$, where $`d_i=|C_i|`$. Its attachment to the cap root is a path of $`u_i+1`$ edges. Choose **any subset** of the internal skeleton edges to vary. Give the other internal edges fixed positive integer lengths. Write $`p`$ for $`r`$ plus the number of varying internal edges.

**Theorem.** Among all choices of the $`p`$ independent integer parameters (the exterior orders and the chosen internal lengths) in $`[1,t]^p`$, all except $`O(t^{p-1})`$ yield a tree identified among all finite unmarked trees by its ordinary chromatic symmetric function. Infeasible exterior orders below the cap size can be counted among the exceptions. The implicit constant depends on the fixed data. More precisely the exceptions lie in a finite union of bounded-width slabs perpendicular to nonzero rational vectors in parameter space. No comparability of the lengths or restriction on the number of leaves or shape of the caps is required.

This includes a fixed complete core with only its outer arms stretched, and independent subdivision of every skeleton edge with prescribed terminal decorations. It extends the [arbitrary-core theorem](ARBITRARY-CORE.md), whose unrestricted-cap density estimate assumed a common balanced length window. The input carries no branch labels or marked vertices. Stanley's original question for all individual trees remains open. [Original paper](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1995.1020)

## Recognizing the rival and locating the long paths

Use the [two-scale fringe recognition](ARBITRARY-CORE.md#recognizing-the-ends-on-a-competing-tree). Once the smallest exterior order exceeds the fixed cap threshold, every rival with the same chromatic symmetric function has the same multiset of rooted caps, the same number of exterior arms and the same multiset of skeleton hub degrees. Its suppressed skeleton is therefore one of finitely many trees with $`r`$ leaves, all internal degrees at least three. Its interior lengths can initially be arbitrary. The inputs also give equal orders, equal two-part cut histograms, and equal signed reduced pair-distance measures. The last follows from the [ordinary path histogram and signed second difference](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md). For positive real lengths, this signed measure determines the [global arclength distance law](METRIC-TREE-DISTRIBUTION.md).

Contract the fixed internal edges of the target skeleton; call the resulting tree $`P`$. Its $`p`$ edge labels are precisely the exterior arms and varying internal edges. All its internal degrees are at least three. Write $`A=(A_e)`$ for the vector of their parameters, with $`A_e=a_i`$ for an exterior edge. For a subset $`I\subseteq E(P)`$ put $`s_I=\sum_{e\in I}A_e`$ and
```math
g(A)=\min_{I\ne I'}|s_I-s_{I'}|.
```
The events of a small arm or of $`g(A)`$ below any specified constant lie in finitely many bounded-width rational slabs.

We show that, outside another such collection, each competitor skeleton can be contracted to $`P`$ so that the retained parameters differ from the corresponding target parameters by less than $`\epsilon g(A)`$ each, while the sum of its contracted lengths is less than $`\epsilon g(A)`$. Contracted edges must be internal. Here $`\epsilon>0`$ is any fixed rational number, to be chosen small depending only on the skeleton size. Rival exterior parameters are measured as their total arm orders.

First consider a sequence with scale $`T\to\infty`$ and $`A/T`$ converging to a vector of positive rationally independent coordinates. The same argument applies when the varying lengths are real and the recognized fixed caps are ordinary unit trees. The total length of either tree is the sum of its exterior orders and internal lengths. Thus all rescaled rival lengths are bounded. Pass to a subsequence on which its skeleton and cap placement are fixed and every length divided by $`T`$ converges. The metric limits are obtained by contracting edges of limiting length zero, including all fixed cap edges. Indeed an arclength sample first chooses an edge in proportion to its length, then a uniform fraction of that edge. This coupling on the finite edge set proves convergence of the two-point distance laws, including under contraction. Equality of the laws before taking limits gives equality afterwards.

The target limit is $`P`$ with its independent edge lengths. The rival limit has at most $`r`$ geometric leaves. In a contraction of a tree every endpoint of positive degree one is the image of an original skeleton leaf: a contracted connected set of hubs only has boundary degree $`2+\sum(\deg v-2)\ge3`$. The fixed caps can first be contracted to their terminal points. The [minimum-leaf reconstruction theorem](ROBUST-HUB-DISTANCES.md#the-minimum-leaf-representative) now identifies the entire rival limit with $`P`$. There are exactly $`r`$ different leaf images, one from each skeleton leaf. Such a leaf cluster cannot include a hub, since with exactly one original leaf its boundary degree would be at least two. Every other cluster consists solely of hubs and has boundary degree at least three. Thus every remaining positive rival edge corresponds individually to an edge of $`P`$. All contracted lengths divided by $`T`$ tend to zero, and the corresponding retained lengths have the same normalized limits. As $`g(A)/T`$ tends to a positive number, the required bounds hold for large terms of this subsequence. In particular a sequence failing the localization throughout cannot have this limiting direction.

Here the bounded-width quantitative conclusion uses only the order and signed reduced pair measures before reconstruction. For each possible rival skeleton and cap assignment, the reduced vertex weights are fixed. Its reduced pair distances are affine linear forms in the rival parameters with integer constants contributed by the fixed caps. The target has the analogous list. Equality of signed measures can be expressed as a finite union of systems of rational linear equalities: partition the two lists into classes, require equal values within each class, and require that the two sums of weights agree class by class. Further coincidences between balanced classes preserve equality. Include equality of total lengths, the positivity lower bounds and the two-scale minimum orders. A failure of all admissible localizations is also a finite union of rational polyhedral conditions. There are finitely many contractions and edge bijections; for each one a failed bound uses a coordinate difference or a sum of contracted lengths, and $`g`$ is the minimum of finitely many absolute linear forms. Subdivide by their signs and order. Projection of this finite family to the real target coordinates gives rational polyhedra by linear elimination. Closed inequalities suffice for failure of the strict localization bounds; positive skeleton and cap-connection lengths have their fixed unit lower bounds.

No one of these nonempty projected polyhedra has a full-dimensional recession cone. Such a cone in the nonnegative orthant would contain a positive direction with rationally independent coordinates. A ray in that direction from any point of the polyhedron would give positive real candidate pairs failing localization, contrary to the preceding argument. A rational polyhedron is a bounded polytope plus its recession cone. A nonzero rational vector orthogonal to the latter therefore has bounded scalar product on the whole polyhedron. This proves the claimed slab containment without a uniform continuity assumption about tree reconstruction. Each fixed slab contributes $`O(t^{p-1})`$ integer points in the box, by fixing all coordinates except one with nonzero coefficient.

## Exact lengths from oriented edge cuts

Fix a localized rival. For a unit edge cut enter *both* component orders in the histogram $`A_j^*`$, twice at a balanced split. Form $`D_j=A_j^*-A_{j+1}^*`$. Along an oriented internal path of length $`b`$ the successive component orders are an interval $`M,\ldots,M+b-1`$. This orientation contributes a negative impulse at $`M-1`$ and a positive one at $`M+b-1`$ to $`D_j`$. The exterior stem and attachment to cap $`i`$ contribute the outward interval $`[d_i,a_i]`$ and its complemented interval. Cuts inside a cap have one bounded side.

Assign each impulse to the subset of active edge labels whose lengths it includes to leading order. Explicitly, for a retained edge $`e`$, oriented towards one side in $`P-e`$ whose edge set is $`I`$, the minimum-minus-one has label $`I`$ and the maximum has label $`I\cup\{e\}`$. One sees this by summing complete exterior orders and internal lengths in each component. Partial contributions at an end of the cut path differ by only a bounded constant or the total contracted length. The same description applies for an exterior edge with the cap side treated as the empty set. Cap-internal cuts and the constant exterior endpoints have labels empty or full. For an edge contracted at a hub $`v`$ of $`P`$, both endpoints of its oriented interval have the same label: the union of the incident directions at $`v`$ on that side, each direction including its first edge and all edges beyond it.

All impulses for a given label are within $`g(A)/4`$ of its subset sum, on both trees, when $`\epsilon`$ is sufficiently small and $`g(A)`$ exceeds a fixed constant. Indeed a component size is a sum of the complete parameters on its side, at most one end adjustment on the cut path, a bounded number of fixed vertex and cap offsets, and contributions bounded by the total contracted length. The error due to retained-parameter discrepancies is at most $`p\epsilon g(A)`$, and that from contracted lengths at most $`\epsilon g(A)`$ apart from the fixed target edges. One can take $`\epsilon=1/[10(p+2)]`$ and then enlarge the fixed threshold. Windows for different labels are disjoint.

At a hub $`v`$, the sets of edges in its directions partition $`E(P)`$. A nonempty proper label for a retained-path endpoint partitions the edge set into one direction and all other directions at its hub. The two edge-induced connected parts have exactly that hub in common, so it is unique. Since the hub has degree at least three, the single direction is unique too. Consequently each such label identifies its single oriented endpoint and its sign. A contracted internal cut instead divides the directions at its hub into at least two on each side: each of the two connected pieces of the contracted hub cluster contains a hub of original degree at least three, and has one boundary edge towards the other piece. Its other boundary edges number at least two by the degree sum. Thus none of its labels coincides with a nontrivial retained-path endpoint label.

The unique positive impulse with the singleton exterior label $`\{i\}`$ gives the exact exterior order $`a_i`$. This applies unchanged when that arm is longer than half the tree. The two impulses for a chosen orientation of each retained internal edge give its exact length as the difference of their positions. Both component orientations were retained throughout. The recovered parameters are the target values for every retained edge. Subtracting their sum from total length shows that the sum of all contracted internal lengths now agrees exactly with the fixed target value.

The full oriented histograms of retained internal cuts can be subtracted because their interval endpoints are now known. The total contribution of all exterior attachments and stem cuts depends only on the separate multisets of the recovered orders and cap sizes: the outward count at $`j`$ is $`\#\{i:a_i\ge j\}-\#\{i:d_i>j\}`$. Add the complemented counts. Cap-internal cuts and their complements are also known from the cap multiset. The residual consists only of contracted internal cuts. Every remaining oriented cut size identifies its subset of incident directions at its unique hub, by the same disjoint windows. For a given split count only the orientation omitting one fixed terminal label. The multiplicity of the split is its path length in the corresponding finite hub cluster. Distinct reduced cluster edges give different splits of the incident directions. The [hierarchy reconstruction from splits](ARBITRARY-CORE.md#recovering-the-entire-core-from-labeled-splits), applied with those directions as labelled external terminals, recovers the entire cluster including its internal lengths. Thus both trees have the same core and the same exterior orders at the same positions. Only the assignment of cap shapes to those positions remains.

## Reading each cap from the connected-subset polynomial

The chromatic symmetric function determines the polynomial $`C_T(x,y)`$ counting nonempty connected vertex sets by size and number of edges on their boundary. Put $`Y=y/(1-x)`$, and use the rooted attachment polynomial $`F_C=y+x\prod F_{\rm child}`$ (the empty product is one). Define
```math
N_C=x^{-|C|}(F_C-Y).
```
The attachment factor of an arm of order $`a`$ and stripped cap $`C`$ is $`Y+x^aN_C`$. These nonzero normalized factors distinguish the rooted stripped caps by the [rooted polynomial and unary-extension identity](TWO-HUB-SUBDIVISIONS.md#distinguishing-one-transposition-without-size-restrictions). [Connected-subset invariant](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03980v3)

For every nonempty connected set of skeleton hubs actually in the vertex set, the paths between its adjacent included hubs are present in full. A path of length $`b`$ to an excluded adjacent hub contributes $`y(1-x^b)/(1-x)`$ from its possible initial segments, each with one boundary edge. Each included hub contributes $`x`$, each fully included internal path contributes $`x^{b-1}`$, and each incident exterior arm contributes the attachment factor above. This expresses the contribution as a sum indexed by subsets of the active edge labels, with each summand $`x^{s_I}`$ times a rational function of bounded exponent range after clearing denominators.

For precision, connected sets meeting no hub are in a single exterior arm or inside one internal path. On a chain of $`b`$ open vertices with both end vertices excluded the interval contribution is $`y^2\sum_{j=1}^{b}(b+1-j)x^j`$. Multiplication by $`(1-x)^2`$ leaves only bounded shifts of the exponents zero and $`b`$, with coefficients allowed to depend linearly on $`b`$. Inside an exterior arm, a set meeting its cap root contributes the rooted cap choice $`F_C-y`$, one boundary edge towards the absent hub, and a geometric sum of stem prefixes. Sets omitting the cap root lie inside the cap or in the open stem. Thus these terms involve at most one active edge label. A single fixed factor $`x^B(1-x)^B`$ with sufficiently large $`B`$ consequently makes the whole expansion polynomial, with uniformly bounded degree shifts about each $`s_I`$. The bound depends only on the fixed caps, core size after long paths are suppressed, and the fixed total length of contracted edges, now the same on both trees. Increasing the fixed threshold on $`g(A)`$ separates all these windows in the ordinary univariate $`x`$-degrees with coefficients in $`\mathbb Q[y]`$. Equality of the actual connected-subset polynomials therefore gives equality of each window coefficient on the two trees.

Take all retained internal labels and all exterior labels, or all exterior labels except one $`i`$. In either case only the term for *every* hub included can contribute. Indeed a connected proper set of hubs omits a component reached through a missing adjacent hub. Following the internal tree away from its attachment reaches a terminal hub of the complete skeleton, with at least two exterior arms. Their two labels are both absent. Terms meeting no hub contribute at most one exterior label, and $`r\ge4`$.

Write $`H_0=1+\sum b`$ with the sum over the contracted internal lengths only. The full-core factor is $`x^{H_0+\sum b_e}\prod_j(Y+x^{a_j}N_{C_j})`$, with the latter sum over retained internal lengths. Before clearing the common denominators, the two isolated coefficients are respectively
```math
x^{H_0}\prod_jN_{C_j},\qquad
x^{H_0}Y\prod_{j\ne i}N_{C_j}.
```
Their ratio, multiplied by $`Y`$, determines $`N_{C_i}`$. Repeating for each exterior position determines every rooted cap. This proves isomorphism of the full ordinary trees outside the stated slabs. The remaining general case includes exceptional assignments of the parameters and arbitrary trees without the long exterior subdivisions.
