# Sources, attribution and scope of the marked visibility theorem

The ambient question is Stanley's tree-isomorphism conjecture, introduced in his 1995 paper on the chromatic symmetric function. This project studies reconstruction from a weaker invariant, the unlabelled histogram counting unordered vertex pairs at each distance. The method gives information about which subdivisions the histogram distinguishes without a supplied degree sequence. All competitors here are finite trees. [Stanley's original paper](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/100.pdf) · [Martin–Morin–Wagner, Corollary 5](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339)

The new statements are generic identification for arbitrary degree patterns under an explicit incidence condition, uniqueness of the minimum-leaf representative for every generic topology, recovery of a common subtree after boundedly many leaf deletions, and exclusion of a single extra leaf in marked comparisons. Arbitrarily many high-degree vertices and arbitrarily high individual degrees are permitted. One can construct examples by prescribing any finite tree of high-degree vertices and attaching sufficiently large trivalent branches in each remaining direction. The arbitrary sparse-branch case, the exceptional integer length vectors, and Stanley's unrestricted question remain open.

## Earlier results and bounded priority check

* Gutman's Theorems 3 and 4 relate the Schultz, modified Schultz and Wiener polynomials of a tree. Their combination yields the signed second difference used here. Martin, Morin and Wagner recover path counts and the degree sequence from the chromatic symmetric function. The present target takes the ordinary path counts alone as its input. [Gutman](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/Article.aspx?id=0561-73320530001G) · [Martin–Morin–Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339)
* Buneman's tree-metric theorem identifies the subtree spanned by labelled points from their pairwise distances. Here it is applied only after the unlabelled signed data have identified actual leaf labels and their full distances. Pachter and Speyer recover trees from labelled subtree weights of fixed cardinality. [Buneman](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/the-recovery-of-trees-from-measures-of-dissimilarity/) · [Pachter–Speyer](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/records/2v174-rtt49)
* Bartha, Burcsi and Lipták study the multiset of weights of paths in edge-weighted reduced trees. Their Proposition 11 includes homometric examples. Proposition 15 reconstructs a specified star's edge weights. Theorem 20 enumerates compatible labellings of a supplied topology from its pair-path weights. The ordinary distance histogram of a subdivision also counts pairs with endpoints at every inserted bivalent vertex. The present comparison supplies neither the rival topology nor its number of leaves. [Bartha–Burcsi–Lipták](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2016.10)
* Mémoli and Needham study global and local distance distributions. Their Theorem 12 concerns a local distance-distribution invariant on metric trees, and Lemma 4.18 already recovers a degree-square coefficient from a global law. Evans and Lanoue sample leaves and study the joint distribution of successive spanned subtree lengths. The metric version here uses one distance between two arclength samples and supplies a global comparison across finite tree topologies. [Mémoli–Needham](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09646) · [Evans–Lanoue](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01091)
* The earlier project results prove the generic conclusion with full degree data, give decision and finite-size reductions for the histogram alone, and solve all-one-quartic topologies independent of the sizes of the branches. The new proof retains the earlier local factorization in its one-extra-leaf argument, extends leaf visibility to arbitrary defect profiles, and uses quotient coordinates and negative variation to give the new conclusions. [Path certificate](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md) · [Fixed-excess reduction](FIXED-EXCESS-REDUCTION.md) · [One-quartic result](QUARTIC-DISTANCE-RIGIDITY.md)

On 27 November 2026 the repository checks covered the research and publication branches, the open [original research issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/315), and the [one-quartic publication](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1046). Bounded primary-source searches covered homometric trees, generic subdivisions, global arclength distance distributions, the Wiener/Hosoya path polynomial, minimum-leaf reconstruction, labelled and unlabelled tree distances, and marked distance profiles. These checks found no prior resolution of the new statements. They do not establish that unindexed or unpublished arguments are absent.

## Research contributions

The result continues the repository's existing Stanley campaign. The two project researchers working on the preceding quartic theorem and the higher-degree follow-up arrived independently at the quotient argument retaining all but four times the surplus number of leaves. The quartic researcher supplied the negative-variation minimum-leaf lemma. The higher-degree manuscript derives the common spanned subtree, clean-leaf profile, arbitrary-degree criterion, and the extension of the one-split argument. A separate researcher reviewed the complete package and replayed the included checker. [Independent review](ROBUST-HUB-REVIEW.md) · [Finite checks](verify_marked_visibility.py)
