## Independent mathematical review

The two fringe saturations force exactly the required terminal blocks in each competitor. Contraction is legitimate because all the blocks are pendant. The degree histogram after subtraction leaves two hubs, including when their degrees coincide with one another or with degrees inside the caps. This reuses the terminal-block argument of the [one-hub theorem](HUB-SUBDIVISIONS.md).

The reviewer checked the signed differences at both ends of each central interval and at the reflected end of an exterior tail. In every possible competitor the first $`d`$ orders occur before any of these perturbations. Ties are resolved by collecting exactly the first $`d`$ orders with multiplicities. The sum $`S`$ is therefore common to the target and every competitor, before the full exterior order list is known. Strict inequalities exclude an equal-split coefficient from the scan. The recovered list in the target has all $`r`$ arms strictly below $`S`$.

The reviewer also checked the giant-component threshold against a cut anywhere on the central path, including a single edge between the hubs. The available rooted product suffices for all the required logarithmic derivatives, including repeated orders and unit caps. For connected subsets meeting only one hub, exactly one boundary edge leads towards the other. For subsets missing both hubs, external edges to the attachment vertex must still be counted. These facts give the sum and product of the two rooted attachment polynomials; their factors recover even repeated arm types. The equal-degree case permits interchanging the hubs. The family of one-edge coincidences marks a limitation of the scan, without asserting equality of chromatic symmetric functions.

The coordinating reviewer derived the transposition identity independently of the grouping proof. The proof author checked it by summing every new topmost vertex in each unary chain, including the boundary edge towards its parent. The normalization by cap order correctly exchanges the total arm orders for unequal caps. Equality of the normalized factors would make the larger stripped cap a unary extension of the smaller one, excluded by rooted completeness unless they coincide. Regarding the far hub and its two arms as one cap shows the identity distinguishes the displayed one-edge ambiguity.

## Additional independent reconstructions

The [arbitrary-length proof](TWO-HUB-FREE-LENGTHS.md) was read independently in the same session. The reviewer checked the highest boundary contribution from subsets meeting both hubs, the separate single-vertex factors, and the strict upper bound on every other contribution. The exponent of the term retaining every arm depends only on the tree order and cap orders. Each next processed leaf-count class leaves a single Laurent sum after division by a known nonzero factor. Its coefficients give the entire multiset of lengths of that cap type. All exterior lengths and the central length are independent under the condition on cap shapes.

The [long-arm proof](DOMINANT-ARM.md) was developed by the coordinating reviewer and separately checked by the proof author. The midpoint histogram counts a balanced cut twice. A competitor's residual has the same sign pattern at the two endpoints, hence only the two listed length assignments can occur. Clearing the two denominators in the exchange identity retains the period factor for every subsequent regrouping. Coprime cancellation bounds the two central lengths by the degree of an explicit nonzero polynomial, independently of the short-arm allocation.

## Earlier results and scope

Wang, Yu and Zhang's arXiv version 3, Theorem 1.2, identifies trees with exactly two vertices of degree at least three, and Theorem 4.2 recovers connected-subtree size and boundary. Arbitrary caps here may themselves have many branch vertices. Aliste-Prieto, Martin, Wagner and Zamora recover the generalized degree sequence, including this connected-subtree distribution. Eisenstat, Gordon and Redlich's Definition 3.7 and Theorem 3.9 give exactly the rooted attachment factor and its completeness after adjoining a unary root. These are existing dependencies. The grouping argument recovers two products of these rooted factors from ordinary unrooted information. [Wang, Yu and Zhang, arXiv v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03980v3) · [Aliste-Prieto, Martin, Wagner and Zamora](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10333) · [Eisenstat, Gordon and Redlich](https://www.davideisenstat.com/cv/EisenstatGR08.pdf)

Martin, Morin and Wagner recover the degree sequence and ordinary spiders. Huryn and Chmutov's Theorem 2 distinguishes two 2-spiders, which have a single hub with legs terminating as paths or cherries. Crew recovers twig lengths and trunk order. A bounded comparison by author, paper and variant terms for homeomorphic trees, subdivided trees, generalized spiders, two branch vertices and rooted attachments found no previous theorem giving this unpromised two-hub recognition with general caps and the stated range. Both permanent branches contain the same original question and the earlier single-edge, one-hub and repeated-branch partial work. This result extends that single project. [Martin, Morin and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339) · [Huryn and Chmutov](https://msp.org/involve/2020/13-1/involve-v13-n1-p08-p.pdf) · [Crew](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04417)

## Reproducible finite checks

The [checker](verify_two_hub_subdivisions.py) scans 18,984 folded arm profiles with two or three exterior arms at each hub, including 14,649 with all orders below the detected cutoff. Across 23 small joins it enumerates 46,080 forests and 92,137 nonempty vertex subsets. It compares the giant components with the independently computed rooted product, evaluates the entire connected-subset polynomial at a collision-free integer encoding and tests every grouping of each given arm collection. It also checks 36 decorated cap assignments, including repeated shapes and sizes, and 27 instances of the one-edge ambiguity. The [boundary-layer verifier](verify_two_hub_free_lengths.py) reconstructs all lengths and the central distance in 127 joins with one to three cap types, including single and repeated unit factors. The [long-arm verifier](verify_dominant_arm.py) checks 5,408 oriented cut profiles, 108 decorated transpositions and 224 regroupings, including 168 instances separated by the degree bound. These are written proofs without Lean formalization or external refereeing.

Run `python3 verify_two_hub_subdivisions.py`, `python3 verify_two_hub_free_lengths.py` and `python3 verify_dominant_arm.py` from the directory containing the checkers.
