# Independent review of recognition from a leaf-rich centroid

On 27 November 2026 UTC a separate Codex reviewer in the WadeDavis research session checked the proof manuscript with SHA-256 `2de950860bbc3def45a73bec7a602703d02da44ba3de11d3ce3f8b19705780b0`. The ordinary chromatic symmetric function identifies, among arbitrary trees, each tree with a unique centroid carrying at least one fewer leaf than the order of its largest centroid branch. The check includes the case of equality and self-consistent recovery of the attachment from ordinary invariants.

1. The balanced-edge coefficient determines whether the centroid is unique. For a unique centroid every component of a subforest exceeding half the vertices contains it, giving the usual first truncation. The maximal variable seen is precisely the largest branch order $`b`$. All off-centroid components have order at most $`b`$, so the same extraction extends to weight $`n-b-1`$ in every competing tree.
2. Differentiating the ordinary forest polynomial in $`x_1`$ distinguishes a singleton component and yields the sum of the vertex-deleted forest polynomials. Substituting $`x_i=(z-1)^{i-1}`$ returns one monomial $`z^{n-1-\deg(v)}`$ for each removed vertex, giving the ordinary degree histogram. Gonzalez, Orellana and Tomba, Theorem 4.4, identifies the separate leading star-basis partition with the sizes of the components formed after removing internal edges. For $`n\ge3`$ these are precisely the pendant-neighbor counts of nonleaves plus one. [Primary theorem, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15986v1#S4)
3. No off-centroid vertex can have more than $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors. If the centroid is at the threshold $`b-1`$ for $`b\ge2`$, it has at least two further nonleaf branches by uniqueness, and hence degree at least $`b+1`$. This transfers to every competitor because all other degrees are at most $`b`$. A degree-$`b`$ off-centroid vertex must be the attachment of an order-$`b`$ branch and must see every other vertex in that branch, all leaves; conversely $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors exhaust the available branch positions and force that same configuration. Subtracting the degree-$`b`$ count from the count of $`b-1`$-leaf nonleaves then detects the threshold at the centroid. This works when $`b=2`$ as well. The order-one branch case is exactly a star.
4. If the common marked branches have order sum $`K`$, their product $`H`$ has constant coefficient one, and divides the centroid truncation formally. For $`K\ge b`$ all degrees of the remainder’s rooted polynomial are recovered. For $`K=b-1`$ one top homogeneous layer $`f`$ alone is missing. Applying root-component restoration to the missing $`Hf`$ gives $`f\Phi_{K+1}(H)`$ coefficientwise, and the second polynomial is a nonzero ordinary forest polynomial independent of $`f`$. This determines $`f`$ by integral-domain cancellation. Factoring the recovered rooted child polynomial uniquely reconstructs the residual rooted tree and reattachment restores the full tree.

This result imposes no diameter or rooted-shape restriction on the other branches. It is a written partial result toward Stanley's general question. [Stanley, §2](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/100.pdf) · [Rooted polynomial reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08186)
