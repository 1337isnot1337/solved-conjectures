# Independent review of balanced-edge arm reconstruction

27 November 2026 UTC. A separate Codex reviewer checked the manuscript whose SHA-256 digest is `6561be33d84c5ef710852349195d99e0256e00c76cdb484d24b9120af8c42e33`. The sufficient condition includes its equality endpoint and permits competitors without distinguished edges.

- For a rooted end of order $`s`$, the degree-$`s`$ part of $`Q`$ is $`U`$, and that of $`XQ`$ is also $`U`$. Thus $`G`$ has degree at most $`s-1`$. The cut-edge and retained-edge cases applied to homogeneous pieces of $`G_A,G_B`$ reduce separately to two end-rooted paths of strictly positive orders. Their join is a single path, giving the identity $`U_T=[HG_AG_B]_n`$ with no assumption of balance.
- Passing to the path generators $`y_i=H_i`$ is triangular and invertible over the integers in every finite weight. If all factors have index at most $`M`$, then after subtracting the contributions at weights below $`k`$, the coefficient of $`y_{n-k}`$ is exactly $`F_k`$ whenever $`n-k>M`$. A restoring generator of smaller index cannot put $`y_{n-k}`$ in its factor. The nonconstant stripped factors have singleton coefficient minus one in their greatest variable; the greatest singleton coefficient of the product consequently cannot cancel. A path unit contributes no singleton.
- Every competitor has an edge splitting the same pair of orders: the ordinary coefficient of $`x_ax_b`$ counts the two-component forests and each is produced by a unique cut edge. Initially $`M=b-1`$ suffices because $`a\le b\le a+1`$. The first extraction therefore sees every candidate's greatest index and the number of factors attaining it. An alternative pair cannot conceal a greater index outside the first extraction.
- For two ends attaining index $`L`$, all competitor products have degree at most $`2L`$. At $`n=3L`$ the only missing term in the entire restored homogeneous identity is $`y_LF_{2L}`$; division determines it. Unique factorization applies with normalized constant coefficient one. If just one factor attains $`L`$ then $`F=(-y_L+S)B`$ is at most linear in $`y_L`$. Only the restoring term $`y_LF_{n-L}`$ can contribute the second power of $`y_L`$ in $`U_T`$. Its coefficient equals $`-[B]_{n-2L}`$, including the borderline case $`n=2L+l`$. Lower layers of $`B`$ are seen in $`[y_L]\tau_{n-L-1}F`$. Core degrees obey $`L\le b-1`$, so $`n-2L\ge1`$ and $`n-L-1\ge L`$ even at the tightest permitted near-balanced size.
- In ordinary variables, the singleton coefficients of $`HG_E`$ through the actual order of $`E`$ count all its fringe vertices, including the root in the top weight. Beyond the actual order the singleton coefficient is one. At its normalized degree $`d`$ the stripped core contributes exactly its $`d`$ proper vertices, and every further level contributes one singleton from the core root, a unary ancestor, or the extension of the formal series. Conversely if exactly $`m`$ vertices have fringe order at most $`m`$ and the tree has larger order, take a minimal vertex with fringe order greater than $`m`$. Its proper descendants exhaust the $`m`$ small vertices, and no off-path vertex can survive outside this fringe since such a branch would have another leaf. All remaining ancestors are unary. Thus the observed equality rules out any competing partner of degree greater than $`m`$. Division through degree $`n-L-1`$ recovers all of the larger factor. The unknown allocations of unary vertices to the two ends give the same unmarked tree because the total internal connector length is known.
- For the path-conjoined corollary $`n\ge 2g-1`$ places the larger end in a half of size at least $`g`$; when both end orders equal $`g`$, the enlarged order is necessarily at least $`2g`$. The other end fits the smaller half and all available path vertices fill the two deficits. The sorted normalized degrees are bounded respectively by $`g-1`$ and $`h-1`$. The bound $`2g+h-3`$ therefore suffices. Paths, a singleton end and equal-order halves create no exceptional assignment.

Qi, Tang and Wang’s path-conjoined graph construction concerns expansions for particular ends. The path-basis polynomial specialization sends each path generator to a single variable, whereas this inverse uses the path generators independently. The rooted forest-factor and rooted chromatic reconstruction literature takes a specified root as part of its input. A bounded search of these sources and the established ordinary-CSF chain and star-basis reconstructions found no earlier theorem giving this endpoint and all-competitor recognition for arbitrary rooted ends. The full Stanley question remains open.
