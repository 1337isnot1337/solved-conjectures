# Independent review of simultaneous subdivision at a branch vertex

On 27 November 2026 UTC a separate Codex reviewer in WadeDavis’s research session checked the unmarked reconstruction theorem, the independent-length quantifier and the closest prior statements. For a vertex of degree $`r\ge3`$ the sufficient hypothesis is that every arm has order at least $`\max(3,L+1,2rL)`$, where $`L`$ is the maximum unary-stripped arm order minus one. The absolute lower bound on each subdivision length depends only on the chosen vertex and the original arms. The proof permits a single arm to exceed the combined order of the others by any amount. This is an internally reviewed written partial theorem for Stanley’s tree-isomorphism question. The general question remains open.

## Recognition without a supplied root

For $`m=rL`$ take $`M=L+1`$ and $`K=\max(3,M,m)`$. On the target $`2K\lt n`$, and cutting a single edge leaves the hub in a component of order greater than $`K`$. Thus the nonroot size counts through $`K`$ at any centroid can be read from two-part coefficients. An equal split causes no ambiguity at these indices. Its existence also has no effect on the low rooted forest polynomial, because a component larger than half the tree contains both centroids.

At each of the two scales $`B=M,K`$ the target has precisely $`r`$ disjoint fringes of order $`B`$, and their $`rB`$ vertices exhaust the vertices with fringe order at most $`B`$. Both statements transfer to every competing tree. Every leaf lies in one of the smaller blocks. Following a leaf of a larger block finds a smaller block within it by laminarity. Counting the blocks gives one per larger block. A child off the path from the two block roots would contain an uncovered leaf. Each larger block therefore consists of a cap of order at most $`M`$ extended by unary roots.

Every vertex outside the larger blocks has descendant order greater than $`K`$, apart from the provisional root where the child-product identity is applied directly. At this cutoff the ordinary top layer of every intervening subtree vanishes. Recursing down its child products gives the product of the $`r`$ block factors. Dividing by $`H^r`$ recovers the entire product of normalized caps because its weighted degree is at most $`rL\le K`$ in every competitor. Distinct nonunit caps are normalized irreducibles, and the known number of blocks supplies the number of unit caps.

The degree contribution of a block includes its unique edge to its parent. Subtracting all the reconstructed block histograms from the known ambient degree sequence leaves exactly one vertex of degree $`r`$ and vertices of degree two. Contracting the blocks to leaves then gives a spider with that single branch vertex. This identifies the arm structure in every possible reconstruction, even when some cap also contains vertices of degree $`r`$.

## Arm lengths and cap multiplicities

Write $`b,c`$ for the two largest arm orders and $`D=n-1-b`$. The difference between consecutive two-part coefficients at $`i,i+1\lt n/2`$ gives the multiplicity at $`i`$ minus the multiplicity at $`n-1-i`$. The latter term is zero through $`i=c`$ since $`D-c`$ is the sum of $`r-2`$ further positive lengths. Both coefficients exist through that stage since all reconstructed arm lengths are at least $`K\ge3`$. Taking just the first $`r-1`$ multiplicities in ascending order handles tied maxima. The remaining order follows from the total. This proof avoids taking a difference across the middle of an even split.

In a forest with more than $`b`$ vertices in a component, that component contains the now-recognized hub. Exactly the root polynomial through weight $`D`$ is recovered by erasing these parts. In the logarithmic derivative at $`y_j=0`$, multiplicities enter additively even for repeated caps at a repeated size. Derivation in $`y_j`$ costs $`j`$ units of weight. When $`j+m\le D`$ and $`m\le j`$, multiplying by $`H\rvert_{y_j=0}`$ gives the reciprocal of the cap factor for each arm of current maximum size $`j`$. The product of the distinct cap factors clears these denominators within weight $`m`$. Reduction modulo any one nonunit factor isolates its individual coefficient, and then the unit coefficient is determined.

If $`b-c\lt m`$, the inequality $`D-c\ge2m`$ makes even the first derivative visible at the largest size. If $`b-c\ge m>0`$, the unique longest factor agrees with $`HG`$ through weight $`j+m`$ for every $`j\le c`$. Its cap has degree less than $`j`$. Its extra contribution to the normalized derivative is therefore exactly one through weight $`m`$. Subtracting one at each smaller size recovers the other assignments, and the remaining cap belongs to the largest arm. This includes equality at the cutoff. For all unit caps the arm orders already determine the tree.

## Comparison with earlier work

Martin, Morin and Wagner recover ordinary spiders and the vertex-degree sequence. Huryn and Chmutov’s Theorem 2 distinguishes any two 2-spiders, whose legs end in a path tip or a cherry. Crew recovers trunk order and the lengths of pendant path twigs. The new theorem allows any finite collection of rooted caps, including multiple branch vertices within a single cap, and compares a target against every tree using its ordinary invariant. Wang, Yu and Zhang treat trees with exactly two branch vertices. Aliste-Prieto, de Mier and Zamora recover a tree from rooted data. These earlier theorems and the repository’s single-edge subdivision and pendant-leaf results give different hypotheses. A bounded search under generalized spiders, 2-spiders, homeomorphic trees, rooted attachments, ordinary chromatic symmetric functions and simultaneous edge subdivisions found no prior all-competitor theorem with the stated independent lengths. ## Independent finite checks

A standard-library checker evaluates exact forest polynomials by retaining or cutting each edge and independently derives the degree histogram by the singleton derivative. It checked every one of the 2,288 unlabelled trees of orders 1 through 13 and found 2,486 saturation-scale pairs, including 84 pairs at distinct scales, 59 with nonpath caps and 390 with a single outer hub. It also recovered 27,027 sorted arm profiles, including 8,554 tied maxima, 98 arms larger than half the order and 139 profiles within one vertex of an equal split. The second checker exercises 84 cap assignments in ten collections of arm sizes. These finite computations support the written proof.
