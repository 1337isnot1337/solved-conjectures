I found no mathematical gap in the stated sufficient threshold. Given any tree and any one of its edges, let $`c,d`$ be the orders of the two endpoint components. When the subdivided tree has at least $`4\max(c,d)-3`$ vertices, its ordinary chromatic symmetric function distinguishes it among all trees of that order. The input supplies no edge labels, candidate core or subdivision lengths.

- Stanley's signed power-sum expansion gives the ordinary spanning-forest polynomial since deletion of edges in a tree determines the component count. Restoring the root component of each spanning subforest of a rooted tree of order $`b`$ yields the weight-$`b`$ part of $`XP_D`$, with $`X=\sum_{i\ge1}x_i`$. Thus $`(1-X)Q_D`$ has zero weight-$`b`$ part. Every truncation of expressions involving $`X`$ is finite. Adding a unary root replaces the new $`P`$ by the old $`Q`$, and removing the zero top layer proves $`G`$ is unchanged. [Stanley, §2](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/classes/Hopf.algebra/bulletins/stanley.1995.pdf) · [Rooted factorization precursor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08186)
- If stripping unary roots leaves a single vertex, direct calculation gives $`G=1`$. Otherwise the root has at least two children and the remaining order $`r`$ is at least three. No off-root component has order $`r-1`$. Multiplication by $`-X`$ therefore makes the coefficient of the singleton $`x_{r-1}`$ exactly $`-1`$ and the weight bound excludes every term multiplying that variable. This unit leading coefficient proves irreducibility in the polynomial ring. Its maximum variable and weighted degree both equal $`r-1`$. The identity $`Q_D=\tau_b[G_D/(1-X)]`$ restores the entire rooted factor at the known original arm order.
- Given a two-arm truncation at the common order $`p`$, the coefficient of the greatest singleton in $`G_AG_B`$ is minus the number of factors with that maximum. Other monomials cannot contribute to this coefficient. Every possible arm has degree at most $`p-1`$, so an alternative product cannot hide a greater maximum above the cutoff. Consequently the detected $`L`$ bounds the degree of both alternative factors. If $`2L\le p`$ the complete product is known. Unique factorization recovers normalized nonunit factors with multiplicities. Knowing the number of arms restores unit factors, including when both arms are paths. Their common order resolves every ambiguity about the stripped unary parts.
- For odd $`n=2h+1`$ the hypotheses give $`h\ge\max(c,d)`$ and $`h\ge2(\max(c,d)-1)`$, including $`c=d=1`$. Allocating $`h-c`$ and $`h-d`$ interior sites to the two sides leaves exactly one inserted hub. Its removal gives two arms of size $`h`$. In every competing tree any component with more than $`h`$ vertices contains its unique centroid. The corresponding truncation has $`[x_h]=2`$, which forces two full arms exhausting all noncentroid vertices.
- For even $`n=2h`$ and $`g=\max(c,d)\ge2`$, $`p=h-1\ge2g-2\ge g`$. Each endpoint of the central balanced edge is an inserted interior vertex. The $`x_h^2`$ coefficient detects the unique balanced edge also in every competitor. A connected component larger than a half must retain the central edge, so removing that large component recovers the product of the two endpoint branch polynomials through weight $`p`$. Its singleton $`x_p`$ occurs twice. Each half has exactly $`p`$ available nonendpoint vertices, hence must have exactly one such child. A tree with $`g=1`$ is a path and the same calculation works for even orders at least four. The sole order-two tree is immediate. These arguments also allow the two endpoint trees to differ in both size and shape.

The bounded comparison found previous work on rooted forest reconstruction, twig-length data, prescribed classes with few branching vertices, and CSFs of path-conjoined graphs. Their hypotheses or outputs differ from unlabelled reconstruction among arbitrary competitors after one long internal subdivision. This review supplies no general isomorphism decision for the original unsubdivided tree.
