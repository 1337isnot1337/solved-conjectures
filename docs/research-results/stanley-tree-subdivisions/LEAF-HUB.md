# Recovering a tree from a centroid with many pendant leaves

Let $`T`$ be a finite tree of order $`n\ge3`$ with a unique centroid $`v`$. Write $`b`$ for the maximum order of a component of $`T-v`$. If at least $`b-1`$ leaves are adjacent to $`v`$, the ordinary chromatic symmetric function determines $`T`$ among all trees. The branches apart from those leaves may have arbitrary shapes, depths and multiplicities. The bound compares the leaf count with the largest branch, even if both form a vanishing proportion of $`n`$.

The input contains no marked vertex or specified branch orders. Codex derived this partial result in the WadeDavis research session, 27 November 2026 UTC. Stanley’s question for arbitrary trees remains open. [Stanley, §2](https://sites.math.washington.edu/~billey/classes/Hopf.algebra/bulletins/stanley.1995.pdf)

## Recognizing the centroid and its leaf count

Use Stanley’s forest polynomial $`U_T`$ with one factor $`x_i`$ for each forest component of order $`i`$. Its coefficients count spanning subforests, and it is equivalent to the ordinary chromatic symmetric function on trees. An equal-split edge is detected by $`[x_{n/2}^2]U_T`$ for even $`n`$. Otherwise the centroid is unique. Every connected component of order greater than $`n/2`$ must contain it. In the forest monomials having such a component delete its variable, obtaining the centroid-rooted forest polynomial $`P_T`$ truncated through weight $`\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor`$. Here $`P`$ omits the root component, the weight of $`x_i`$ is $`i`$, and equal monomials retain all forest multiplicities. The largest variable index in this truncation is $`b`$. A whole top branch supplies $`x_b`$, and every off-root component is in some branch. Now delete the component variable in all forest monomials containing a part greater than $`b`$. Its component contains the centroid and it is unique. This gives $`P_T`$ through weight $`D=n-b-1`$ in every competing tree. [Earlier centroid argument](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/456ae8829ccf210cd911c737c252ea1679af10cf/research/stanley-tree-isomorphism/CENTROID-EDGE.md)

The degree histogram comes from the ordinary CSF. For a direct verification, differentiate $`U_T`$ with respect to $`x_1`$ to get $`\sum_{w\in T}U_{T-w}`$. The occurrences counted are precisely distinguished singleton components. Under $`x_i=(z-1)^{i-1}`$ a forest with $`e`$ edges has value $`z^e`$ by summing its edge subsets. Thus the derivative gives $`\sum_w z^{n-1-\deg(w)}`$. Another known invariant is the multiset of the numbers of pendant neighbors of the nonleaf vertices. Indeed the sizes of the components after deleting the internal edges form the smallest indexing partition in the star-basis expansion. Each such component is a nonleaf vertex and all its pendant neighbors. [Martin, Morin and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339) · [Gonzalez, Orellana and Tomba, Theorem 4.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.15986v1#S4)

For $`b=1`$ the tree is a star. Let $`b\ge2`$. Any vertex $`w`$ other than $`v`$ and all its pendant neighbors are in a single branch of order at most $`b`$, since $`v`$ is a nonleaf. If the maximum pendant count exceeds $`b-1`$, it occurs at $`v`$ in every competitor, and the count there is known.

It remains to recognize a centroid with exactly $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors when that number is the maximum. It has at least two nonleaf branches. If its only nonleaf branch had order $`b`$, then $`n=2b`$ and its attachment edge would give an equal split. Consequently its degree is at least $`b+1`$. All off-centroid degrees are at most $`b`$. This vertex of degree greater than $`b`$ is therefore the unique centroid in every competitor, by the degree histogram and the already recognized branch bound.

Off the centroid, degree $`b`$ is equivalent to having $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors. To see the forward direction, within an order-$`b`$ branch there are at most $`b-1`$ other sites, and only its top site is adjacent to the centroid. Degree $`b`$ forces the full branch to be the top site and its $`b-1`$ children, all leaves. Conversely a site with $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors uses all $`b`$ places in its branch and is the sole possible attachment to the centroid. Thus the number of nonleaf vertices with $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors minus the number of vertices of degree $`b`$ is exactly the indicator that the centroid has $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors when its degree is greater than $`b`$. Both counts are ordinary-CSF data and the indicator transfers to every competitor. Denote the common known number of pendant neighbors at the centroid by $`l`$.

## Restoring the last layer

More generally, suppose a known multiset of rooted branches of combined order $`K`$ is present at the centroid of every competitor. Write $`H`$ for the product of their rooted branch factors. For any rooted tree $`C`$ the factor is $`Q_C=P_C+U_C`$, with constant one. Removing the listed branches leaves a rooted tree $`R`$ of order $`r=n-K`$, and $`P_T=H P_R`$ by the cut-or-retain rule at the root. Formal division recovers $`P_R`$ through weight $`D`$ from the centroid truncation. If $`K\ge b`$, this includes its full weight range through $`r-1`$. The complete rooted polynomial reconstructs the rooted tree by factoring its child product. [Aliste-Prieto, de Mier and Zamora, §3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.08186)

If $`K=b-1`$ then $`D=r-2`$. Write $`P_R=P_{\mathrm{known}}+f`$, where $`f`$ is the unknown homogeneous part of weight $`r-1`$. Define $`\Phi_j(m)=x_{j-w}m`$ for a monomial $`m`$ of weight $`w<j`$ and extend linearly. Restoring root components gives the exact identity
```math
U_T-\Phi_n(H P_{\mathrm{known}})=f\,\Phi_{K+1}(H).
```
For each monomial of $`f`$ the omitted root size is one in $`R`$, so the restoring factor on the product of that monomial and a weight-$`t`$ monomial of $`H`$ is $`x_{K+1-t}`$. The divisor is the nonzero ordinary polynomial of the marked branches attached to their common root. Division uniquely recovers $`f`$. Factor $`P_R`$ to recover its rooted branches and reattach the known multiset. In the leaf application $`K=l`$ and $`H=(1+x_1)^l`$.

For an endpoint example take an arbitrary rooted tree with $`b\ge3`$ vertices, a separate order-two rooted branch, and $`b-1`$ singletons at a new hub. The hub has exactly $`b-1`$ pendant neighbors and maximum branch order $`b`$, while $`D=b+1<2b`$. The arbitrary large branch is recognized among all competing trees. The same construction works with any additional nonleaf branches of order at most $`b`$. The earlier centroid factor extractions alone can require $`D\ge2b`$ for a unique branch of size $`b`$.

## Bounded comparison with existing results

The star-basis leading partition and the ordinary degree sequence are prior invariants used above. The rooted forest polynomial distinguishes trees with a supplied root and full rooted data. The rooted chromatic specializations of Loehr and Warrington also supply a complete invariant when the root color is fixed. They assume a marked input. Orellana and Tomba attach a fixed number of leaves to *every* vertex to relate the full problem to weighted proper trees. The one-hub result above allows a single application of the ordinary invariant and unrestricted other branches. A bounded search for ordinary CSF reconstruction after padding a single root with pendant leaves, rooted star attachment, and marking by a large leaf component did not return a prior theorem with this recovery criterion or endpoint. [Rooted chromatic specialization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.05392) · [Orellana–Tomba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18940) · [Awan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13464)
