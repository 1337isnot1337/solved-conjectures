# Independent review of regular-value certificate attempts

Reviewed 23 November 2026 (America/Chicago) by the all-multiples worker. This checks [REGULARITY_CERTIFICATE_ATTEMPTS.md](REGULARITY_CERTIFICATE_ATTEMPTS.md), Sections 1–7. It is a mathematical review, not formal verification.

Sections 1–3 correctly distinguish pointwise solvability from multiplicity. Formula (1) is exactly root-support inclusion. In the high-order example `P=t^M,Q=t^(M+1)`, all derivatives through the stated cutoff vanish at zero, while genuine derivatives of `1/t` satisfy the equations elsewhere. The proposed value remains unconstrained at zero. The promised-regularity example built from `F=t^M` has the same defect even though its actual quotient is the constant `M`; therefore the missing regular-value condition cannot be replaced by merely promising that some regular extension exists. The bounded-denominator-order exception is correctly stated and uses precisely the derivative order that is absent in the unrestricted setting.

Section 4's initial-form calculation is exact. At weights `(w(t),w(x),w(z))=(1,0,2)`, both supplied gate equations retain only `x-1` up to sign, so there is a torus point. Their sum is `z-t`, whose initial form is `-t`, making the full initial ideal incompatible with a torus point. Thus the example refutes the displayed generator-wise feasibility certificate and does not assert hardness of every possible valuation certificate.

Section 5 keeps the needed complexity distinction: collapsing a fixed polynomial hierarchy does not repair a false formula. I checked the mathematical applications of that distinction here; this review does not independently re-audit every historical statement in the separately cited 2004 source.

Section 6 is a sound positive formulation. In the forward direction, choose the inverse `w=1/Q(t)` on the punctured nonvanishing locus. In the reverse direction, approximate image membership forces `t` to the target point and `Q(t)w` to one, so the quotient values converge to the prescribed finite number. A univariate rational pole cannot admit such a bounded subsequence. Constructibility of the polynomial image justifies using either complex Euclidean or Zariski closure. The circuit size, two source variables, and three output coordinates are correct. The example `P=Q=t,alpha=0,v=1` sharply separates closure membership from exact HN feasibility.

The conclusion is restricted approximate image membership for a univariate quotient. It supplies neither an ordinary HN formula nor fixed-level complex polynomial-hierarchy membership, and the note correctly avoids the false multivariate generalization in which one bounded approach would imply regularity.

No mathematical correction was identified. For any statement about the current best general APS complexity bound, the discrete counting-hierarchy result announced in arXiv:2610.00644v1 should be distinguished from the arbitrary-complex-coefficient BSS question; the present note makes no conflicting best-bound claim.

The added Section 7 also passes. For each fixed input format and bounded running time, the complete machine computation and HN correctness statement are first-order field statements with the finite machine constants as parameters. An algebraically closed extension preserves them. Symbolic execution on rational functions of the infinitesimal uses shared rational circuits; after clearing denominators, each equality branch is a polynomial identity test, whose nonidentity condition is the displayed ordinary HN query. The claim is about field decisions and does not claim to compute algebraic witness functions.

The valuation-ring limitation is exact: one-variable field-definable subsets of an algebraically closed field are constructible and hence finite or cofinite. Both the valuation ring and its complement are infinite. The same finite/cofinite argument excludes defining the embedded constant field. The equation `epsilon c=1` demonstrates the failure of silently restricting transferred witnesses to the valuation ring. Finally, the qualification about bounded circuit encodings is necessary and correctly included; field-element nondefinability does not by itself give a lower bound on formulas for degree-bounded representations.
