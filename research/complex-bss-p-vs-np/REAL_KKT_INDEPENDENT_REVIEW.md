# Independent review of the real critical-point extension

Reviewer: all-multiple-attack agent. Date: 23 November 2026.

Reviewed the complete `REAL_KKT_RADICAL_EXTENSION.md`, Sections 1–11, including its local-component and finite-Boolean-verifier extensions. No defect was found in this review. This is an internal written-proof review, not a Lean verification or external peer review.

The following obligations were checked independently.

1. **Bounded witnesses.** The bounded-witness sets over a compact real box are closed, since bounded complex witness vectors have convergent subsequences. Their union is the box. The stated Baire/nested-ball argument supplies a real open region with uniformly bounded true witnesses. The ball constraint therefore makes the perturbed maximization nonempty and compact.
2. **Generic active gradients.** For each finite constraint subset, a critical component of the map `(x,w)->(x,F_I)` has image dimension below the target dimension by the characteristic-zero differential-rank argument. A nondominant map has a proper algebraic image closure for the same purpose. Positivity of offsets and the permitted radius interval leave real open parameter sets, so the finitely many nonidentity conditions can be met simultaneously. Removing the maximal power of `t` from their product gives a fixed real box on which all sufficiently small positive `t` are regular.
3. **Stationarity.** At a maximizer, independent active gradients define a smooth equality manifold. Inactive constraints stay strict locally, so the objective is stationary on that manifold. Its differential is a linear combination of the active gradients. Multiplier signs are unnecessary for the complex incidence used later.
4. **Dimension of every critical component.** In each reduced component's function field, the multiplier-weighted sum of active equation differentials has the form `dy+A dx-B dt=0`, with dy coefficient one. It is a linear combination of zero differentials; no derivative of a multiplier was dropped. Characteristic zero gives projected transcendence degree at most `n+1`, including for singular or nonreduced displayed systems.
5. **Central hypersurface factor.** Components with nondominant `(x,t)` projection can be excluded on a fixed smaller box for all small positive `t`. For the remaining components the projected closures are hypersurfaces. Their finite product vanishes at every maximizing point. Uniform convergence of the maximal outputs then gives a polynomial identity at `t=0`; one central factor contains `y-f(x)`.
6. **Actual components after specialization.** Intersections of the selected total component with another total component have strictly lower dimension. Their generic `t` fibers have dimension at most two below the total selected component, whereas every component of the selected fiber has codimension one. Thus selected fiber components remain actual incidence components outside finitely many parameters. The dense constructible image also remains dense in each generic hypersurface fiber component. These are the conditions needed by the finite-branch jet lemma.
7. **Local-component extension.** The chosen smooth point can lie over a real base point because its projected good set contains a complex Zariski-open subset and real points are Zariski-dense. Its output is real because `f` has real coefficients. A local holomorphic witness section exists by submersion and transverse slicing. A small ball excludes all other components, so the compactness and maximizing-output arguments remain valid with only the chosen actual component.
8. **Boolean paths and inequalities.** Fixing a Boolean challenge and a finite accepting BSS path gives polynomial equality and inequation conditions of size `O(T)`, including defined-division conditions. Full path assignments across all finitely many challenges produce a finite union of basic constructible sets in the original witness coordinates. No inverse witnesses are added. The closure of each such open subset of an equation variety is a union of actual irreducible components, which makes the local-component theorem applicable.

The restrictions in the note are necessary and are retained: the local geometric hypothesis requires an actual component; the finite-Boolean argument does not allow independent existential complex witnesses after each Boolean challenge; and the final result is a certificate-format obstruction, not a proof of HN hardness or `P_C != NP_C`.

The later BLOCK_KKT_FINITE_EXISTENTIAL_EXTENSION.md uses an additional whole-block multiplier argument to allow the extra local complex existential block. That later extension is reviewed separately in BLOCK_KKT_REVIEW.md; the restriction described above records the scope of the single-block argument reviewed here.
