# A relative-invariant cofactor can cancel a balanced logarithmic coefficient

Research checkpoint, 23 November 2026. This is a precise test of an invariant-theory extraction proposal. It gives an exact cancellation already for three ternary quadrics. It does not construct a small multiple of the universal asymptotic resultant, and it does not claim cancellation of the BCJS permanent coefficient. Its use is to rule out a blanket assertion that a relative-invariant cofactor cannot cancel a first balanced coefficient of a resultant logarithm.

## 1. The degree-six invariant

For a triple of ternary quadratic forms, let `J6` be the homogeneous degree-six invariant normalized in Domokos–Fehér–Rimányi, *Equivariant and invariant theory of nets of conics with an application to Thom polynomials*, Journal of Singularities 7 (2013), 1–20, revised 1 January 2013, Remark 5.3(i) and Theorem 5.4. The primary version is [the published PDF](https://real.mtak.hu/10061/1/domokos-feher-rimanyi.pdf), DOI `10.5427/jsing.2013.7a`.

That paper proves that the invariant ring for `SL_3 x SL_3` is freely generated by `J6` and a degree-twelve invariant `J12`. In particular, not every relative invariant is a polynomial in the degree-twelve resultant.

Use the action `q(x) -> A q(Bx)`, with `A,B` invertible. Homogeneity and special-linear invariance give

```math
J_6(Aq(Bx))=(\det A)^2(\det B)^4J_6(q),
\qquad
R_3(Aq(Bx))=(\det A)^4(\det B)^8R_3(q).
```

For example, decompose `A` as a scalar matrix times a determinant-one matrix and use total degree six for the first formula. The scalar ambiguity is a cube root of unity and disappears because the exponent is a multiple of three. The variable transformation is the same calculation, with quadratic forms scaling by the square of the variable scalar.

## 2. An explicit three-parameter restriction

Consider

```math
q_1=x^2+2ayz,\qquad
q_2=y^2+2bxz,\qquad
q_3=z^2+2cxy,
\qquad u=abc.
```

Normalize the resultant to equal one on `(x^2,y^2,z^2)`. Then

```math
R_3(q)=(1+8u)^3.                                    \tag{1}
```

Here is a direct product calculation. On the chart `z=1`, the first two equations have the four-dimensional quotient algebra with basis `1,x,y,xy` and relations `x^2=-2ay`, `y^2=-2bx`. Multiplication by `xy` is zero on its one-dimensional zero-eigenspace and has eigenvalue `4ab` with multiplicity three. More explicitly its matrix in the displayed basis is

```math
\begin{pmatrix}
0&0&0&0\\
0&4ab&0&0\\
0&0&4ab&0\\
1&0&0&4ab
\end{pmatrix}.
```

The leading forms of the first two equations are `x^2,y^2`, whose binary resultant is one. The elementary resultant product formula therefore identifies the ternary resultant with the determinant of multiplication by `1+2cxy`, which is (1). The computation on the generic parameter open extends as a polynomial identity.

On the same family,

```math
J_6(q)=1-20u-8u^2.                                  \tag{2}
```

For reproducibility, use the coefficient columns `x^2,xy,xz,y^2,yz,z^2`. The coefficient matrix is

```math
\begin{pmatrix}
1&0&0&0&2a&0\\
0&0&2b&1&0&0\\
0&2c&0&0&0&1
\end{pmatrix}.
```

Its nonzero three-column minors are

```math
u_{123}=-4bc,\quad u_{124}=-2c,\quad u_{136}=2b,
\quad u_{146}=1,\quad u_{235}=-8u,
\quad u_{245}=-4ac,\quad u_{356}=-4ab,\quad u_{456}=-2a.
```

Substitution in the explicit invariant from the cited Remark 5.3(i) gives

```math
-8J_6=64u^2-8-32u+64u+64u+64u
     =64u^2+160u-8,
```

which proves (2). The minor determinants and this substitution were also checked with exact symbolic arithmetic; the mathematical check is the displayed calculation.

## 3. Positive multiplicities can produce exact cancellation

The polynomial

```math
F=R_3^5J_6^6
```

is a relative invariant and a nonzero polynomial multiple of the resultant. Its restriction to the family above has constant term one. The coefficient of `u` in its formal logarithm is

```math
[u]\log F=5\cdot24+6\cdot(-20)=0.                  \tag{3}
```

Consequently its degree-three `abc` coefficient also vanishes. This cancellation uses positive integer multiplicities for both factors. A cube-root character filter in the single coordinate `a` detects no degree-three character-one term in `F` or `log F`, although it detects a nonzero such term in the resultant itself.

For the invariant-theory route, (3) is the exact failed inequality: relative invariance supplies no positivity or common-sign rule for these logarithmic coefficients. Passing to a group eigenvector does not by itself prevent a cofactor from canceling such a coefficient. The compensated diagonal torus also fixes both invariants, so that smaller symmetry cannot remove this example.

The family is only a finite-dimensional test. The BCJS hard tensor uses a different, large grid specialization and a degree that grows with the grid. Any proof that the relevant BCJS coefficient survives all suitable asymptotic semi-invariant cofactors must establish a property of that coefficient and those cofactors that goes beyond relative invariance. This note leaves that possibility open.

## 4. Arbitrarily many coefficients can be flattened on this family

The same example is not limited to one canceled coefficient. Fix any integer `d>=0` and any `e>=1`. On the displayed family set

```math
t(u)=\frac{J_6(u)^2}{R_3(u)}.
```

Then `t(0)=1` and `t'(0)=-64`. The one-variable inverse function theorem supplies a holomorphic local inverse `u=u(t)` near one. Let `p_d(t)` be the degree-`d` Taylor polynomial at one of

```math
R_3(u(t))^{-(e+d)}.
```

Define the global polynomial

```math
F_d=R_3^{e+d}p_d(J_6^2/R_3).
```

This is a polynomial multiple of `R_3^e`: after expanding `p_d` into powers of its argument, the smallest exponent of `R_3` is at least `e`. Since `R_3` and `J_6^2` have the same character, `F_d` is a relative invariant. On the three-parameter family,

```math
F_d(q(a,b,c))=1+O(u^{d+1}),\qquad u=abc.             \tag{4}
```

The Taylor remainder and the nonzero constant term of `R_3` prove (4). Horner evaluation of the homogenized polynomial `R_3^d p_d(J_6^2/R_3)` uses `O(d)` gates once `R_3,J_6` are available; multiplication by `R_3^e` costs `O(log(e+1))` gates. At this fixed dimension both input invariants have constant-size circuits. Thus relative invariance and a short expression do not give any fixed-order survival rule on this family. As before, this construction does not produce a small circuit for a growing universal resultant: its direct analogue would require circuits for the growing invariants themselves.
