# A fixed gap for bounded collinearity in Erdős Problem 654

For each fixed integer $`M\ge2`$ there are $`c_M>0`$ and $`N_M`$ with the following property. If $`P`$ consists of $`n\ge N_M`$ points in the plane, every circle contains at most three of them, and every line contains at most $`M`$ of them, then the sum of the numbers of distinct positive distances from its individual points exceeds $`(1/3+c_M)n^2`$. Some point therefore determines more than $`(1/3+c_M)n`$ distances. The unrestricted [Problem 654](https://www.erdosproblems.com/654) needs one positive constant valid for all $`M`$. [Research issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/922)

## Farthest pairs in a fixed sample

Suppose there is a sequence with $`n\to\infty`$ and total pinned count at most $`(1/3+o(1))n^2`$. Write $`\mu_n`$ for uniform measure. All but $`o(n^2)`$ ordered pin-site pairs $`(x,y)`$ have exactly three sites at distance $`|x-y|`$ from $`x`$, by counting the deficit from three in the radius classes. Call these pairs good. Sample $`X,Y_1`$ independently and uniformly, condition on a good pair and label its two other sites $`Y_2,Y_3`$ in random order. The three bases are exchangeable. The pin-base laws are $`o(1)`$ in total variation from $`\mu_n^2`$. Each base-pair law is dominated by $`C\mu_n^2`$, with any fixed $`C>M/2`$ for large $`n`$, since the perpendicular bisector of a base pair contains at most $`M`$ pins.

Fix $`m\ge3`$. Sample $`X,T_1,\ldots,T_m`$ independently from $`\mu_n`$. For each good $`(X,T_j)`$, let $`Z_j`$ be a random one of its two mates, with auxiliary randomness independent of the other sites. With probability $`1-o(1)`$ all pairs are good. For a farthest $`T_j`$ from $`X`$, the sites $`T_j,Z_j`$ are co-farthest from $`X`$ among $`T_1,\ldots,T_m,Z_j`$. The subprobability law of $`(T_j,Z_j)`$ on good pairs is dominated by $`C\mu_n^2`$ and is independent of the other $`m-1`$ sites. Summing the co-farthest events and replacing this pair by two independent sites gives

```math
1-o(1)\le C\,\mathbb E[\deg(T_{m+1})]\le\frac{6C}{m+1}\mathbb E[h_{m+1}]+o(1).
```

Here edges join co-farthest sites, $`h_k`$ counts the convex-hull vertices of $`k`$ independent sites, and the expectation uses $`m+1`$ independent ordinary sites. With probability $`1-o(1)`$ they are distinct with no three collinear, since a fixed pair determines a line containing at most $`M`$ sites. There are no four cocircular. In this case the graph projects from the upper hull of the paraboloid lift, has at most $`3h_{m+1}`$ edges, and the degree estimate follows by exchangeability. The exceptional graphs have bounded size at fixed $`m`$. Details of the planar argument are in [the farthest-site calculation](FARTHEST-SITES.md).

## Successive rescalings

Fix $`b<1`$ with $`(1+C)(1-b)<1`$. In each set translate and dilate a smallest ball containing at least a fraction $`b`$ to the unit disk. The tightness argument in [the farthest-site calculation](FARTHEST-SITES.md) shows that the fraction outside the disk of radius three tends to zero. After a subsequence the sites have a compact limiting distribution. Every atom has mass at most $`b`$, since a disk of radius less than one cannot contain a fraction $`b`$, and open disks satisfy the lower bound under weak convergence.

Start a tree of rescaled sets at $`Q=P`$. At a node with sets $`Q_n`$ of positive limiting relative size, normalize them by the same rule and let $`\lambda_Q`$ be their limiting distribution in those coordinates. At every atom $`a`$ take a child consisting of the sites of $`Q_n`$ in a ball $`B(a,r_n)`$. Choose $`r_n\downarrow0`$ sufficiently slowly that the mass of this ball and that of $`B(a,3r_n)`$ both tend to $`\lambda_Q(\{a\})`$. This follows by using a sequence of common continuity radii for the two balls. For countably many atoms, use a growing finite prefix with disjoint child balls and diagonalize. If a child is absent from an initial prefix, give it the empty set at those indices.

Each child inherits the near-full set of good pairs. For two sites $`x,y`$ in the child having mates inside the parent, both mates lie in $`B(a,3r_n)`$ by the triangle inequality. The chance that a mate belongs to its difference from $`B(a,r_n)`$ is $`o(1)`$ since each mate has near-uniform marginal on the parent. The child has fixed positive relative mass, so only a vanishing fraction of its internal pairs fail to have their two mates inside. Its good-quadruple law thus has the same independence and domination estimates. This also shows that conditioning the parent quadruple on its pin and first base belonging to the child gives the child's quadruple law up to $`o(1)`$ in total variation. Repeat for each child. There are countably many nodes, and successive subsequences and a diagonal subsequence suffice, listing ancestors before descendants. Every child has relative mass at most $`b`$ in its parent.

Encode a site by its node membership indicators and its normalized planar coordinate at each node it visits, using a single dummy value elsewhere. These are countably many compact coordinates on taking the one-point compactification of the plane. Pass to weak limits of the single-site and quadruple laws in this product. Write $`\mu`$ for the limiting site law. Conditional on visiting a node $`Q`$, its coordinate has law $`\lambda_Q`$. At an atom $`a`$ it visits exactly the corresponding child almost surely, by the definition of the balls and their limiting masses. At a nonatomic coordinate the path ends. Distinct independent paths separate at different planar coordinates at a finite visited node almost surely. Indeed the probability of still sharing a child after $`l`$ levels is at most $`b^l`$, and two independent draws cannot coincide at a nonatomic coordinate. The same separation holds for any pair of labels of a limiting quadruple, by domination. Here and below a path includes its terminal coordinate if it has one.

For a pair separating at node $`Q`$, its Euclidean unit direction is the normalized difference of its two distinct $`Q`$-coordinates. This is the limit of the original finite directions because all normalizations use only translations and positive dilations. More formally, record the finite directions as extra compact coordinates before passing to the joint laws. Conditional on both membership indicators at $`Q`$ being one, the relation between the direction and the difference is closed away from zero. The countable union over separating nodes identifies the limiting directions almost surely. This applies both to independent finite samples and to a quadruple. Conditional on pin and first base visiting $`Q`$, the quadruple law agrees with the limit of the finite quadruple laws internal to $`Q`$, by the preceding conditioning observation. All four marginal path laws there equal $`\mu`$ conditioned on visiting $`Q`$.

## A positive-measure set of comparable pairs

Partition oriented directions into eight fixed sectors of angle $`\pi/4`$. Choose their rotation so that no boundary has positive probability under the limiting direction of an independent pair, and so that the boundary directions avoid every finite pair difference. This excludes at most countably many rotations. Call a pair comparable for a sector if one of its two directions belongs to that sector. The hull of a finite sample breaks into at most eight chains when its oriented boundary edges are grouped by sector. All pairs in a chain are comparable since the sector is a convex cone. The sum over sectors of the largest comparable-subset sizes is therefore at least the hull size. Direction convergence and the bound on $`\mathbb E[h_m]/m`$ show that for one fixed sector and arbitrarily large $`m`$, the independent $`m`$-sample from $`\mu`$ has expected largest comparable-subset size at least $`m/(48C)`$.

For clarity, one can extract a measurable set from this sample statement by ordinary ranks. Write the sector as the intersection of two homogeneous half-planes. For each finite set append to a site's coordinates the two empirical ranks, divided by $`n`$, of the defining linear forms. Take another subsequence. The limiting ranks $`U,V`$ each have uniform distribution on $`[0,1]`$. Almost surely the order of either rank on two independent samples equals the order of its original linear form, which is determined by the direction at the first separating node. In fact each rank is a measurable function of the path alone. For $`U`$, compare with an independent path and rank $`U'`$. Its probability of satisfying $`U'<U`$, conditioned on the first path and both of its ranks, equals $`U`$ by uniformity; this comparison is almost surely a function of the two paths. The same applies to $`V`$.

In these ranks comparability means coordinatewise comparability. An infinite independent sample has some realization with empirical rank measures converging to the rank distribution $`\rho`$ and with largest chain fraction of positive limsup, by the sample bound and the reverse Fatou inequality. Along a subsequence take the empirical submeasures of its largest chains, normalized by the entire sample size. Compactness gives a nonzero weak limit $`\sigma\le\rho`$. Coordinatewise comparability is closed, so every two points of $`\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\sigma`$ are comparable. Its inverse image $`S`$ in path space therefore has positive measure and almost every independent pair from $`S`$ is comparable for the fixed Euclidean sector.

## Where the comparable points can lie

No positive part of $`S`$ can terminate at a nonatomic coordinate of any node $`Q`$. Otherwise its terminal coordinates carry a positive nonatomic measure dominated by $`\lambda_Q`$. Its support lies on a Lipschitz graph with constant less than one, in coordinates along the bisector of the sector. In fact almost every pair of these sites has its difference in the closed double sector, and closedness extends that condition to the support. Projection along the sector axis is injective there and the slope bound is $`\tan(\pi/8)`$. Remove the countably many atoms of $`\lambda_Q`$ from the graph to obtain a Borel portion $`H`$ with positive $`\lambda_Q`$ mass and no atoms.

The local argument in [the farthest-site calculation](FARTHEST-SITES.md) excludes this even when $`\lambda_Q`$ has atoms elsewhere. At a density point of $`H`$, take arbitrarily small balls $`B`$ with $`\lambda_Q(3B)\le27\lambda_Q(B)`$. The pin and first base land in $`B\cap H`$ with probability asymptotic to $`\lambda_Q(B)^2`$. Their mates lie in $`3B`$, and the chance that either is in $`3B\setminus H`$ together with the first base in $`B`$ is $`o(\lambda_Q(B)^2)`$ by pair domination. The three bases on $`H`$ are distinct almost surely because this portion has no atoms. A circle centered on a graph of slope bound less than one meets the graph at most once on each side of the pin, which is impossible here.

It follows that almost all of $`S`$ consists of infinite atom paths. On those paths the successive node labels generate the full path information, since the normalized coordinate at every node is its next child's atom. The martingale convergence theorem applied to membership in $`S`$ therefore gives a node $`Q`$ with $`\mu(S\mid Q)>3/4`$. Explicitly, one can keep the terminal coordinate on paths already terminated when defining each level of the increasing filtration. Its union then generates all path coordinates, and its conditional probability on a surviving node is $`\mu(S\mid Q)`$.

A quadruple sampled internally in $`Q`$ has positive probability of placing all its points in $`S`$, by the four marginal laws and the union bound. Pair domination transfers almost-sure pair comparability to all six pairs on this event. The limiting directions avoid the sector boundaries almost surely, by the generic choice and the same domination. Consequently a positive probability of all six directions being comparable would persist in the original finite quadruples, by convergence of their directions. For four distinct actual points pairwise comparable in a double sector of aperture $`\pi/4`$, the points lie on a graph of slope bound $`\tan(\pi/8)<1`$. They cannot consist of a pin and three points equidistant from it. This contradiction proves the asserted fixed-$`M`$ gap.
