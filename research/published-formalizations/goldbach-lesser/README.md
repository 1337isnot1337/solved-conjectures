# Goldbach’s lesser conjecture

## Published disproof

Christian Goldbach proposed the prime-plus-twice-square assertion in his letter to Leonhard Euler dated 18 November 1752. M. A. Stern, *Sur une assertion de Goldbach relative aux nombres impairs*, Nouvelles annales de mathématiques, série 1, 15 (1856), 23–24, [primary source](https://www.numdam.org/item/NAM_1856_1_15__23_0.pdf), p. 23 states the universal assertion and p. 24 records that Stern and his students found 5777 and 5993 as exceptions. The modern convention omits 1 from the primes; the candidate explicitly permits 1 and proves the stronger disproof. It also provides the modern odd-composite, n>17 version.

## Exact scope and proof

The universal proposition quantifies over every odd natural n and asks for naturals p,a with p prime or 1 and n=p+2a². Taking a nonnegative square root loses no case, since integer squares are invariant under sign. The full negation follows from either witness.

For 5777 any such a is at most 53, since 2·54²=5832>5777. For 5993 a is at most 54, since 2·55²=6050>5993. All 54 and 55 residuals are products of two integers different from 1, and none equals 1. The candidate contains explicit factor pairs generated by exact integer trial division and checks them in the Lean kernel; no external primality assumption is needed. An independent finite JavaScript calculation confirmed each residual admits a nontrivial product. The endpoint negates the universally quantified original proposition, not an isolated claim about a fixed range.
