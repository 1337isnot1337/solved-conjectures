# Source, priority, and mathematical review

## Exact target

[Bóna–Pantone, *Permutations avoiding sets of patterns with long monotone subsequences*, Conjecture 6.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.06918v2#S6.SS3) predicts an asymptotic equivalent $C9^n/n^3$ for the class $A_{5,4}$. Their Theorem 2.1 determines the exponential rate of a broader class, and their Theorem 4.1 describes structural restrictions, not this asymptotic. The primary paper's nearby Conjectures 6.1 and 6.2 concern different forbidden sets, and Conjecture 6.4 is the different $A_{5,5}$ case. The guessed D-finite equation remains a separate claim.

A targeted search on Nov. 24, 2026 covered both author names, the original title, Conjecture 6.3, both asymmetric-index notations, the exact four forbidden patterns, and recent public entries for the avoidance sequence. It found the primary question and nearby work but no earlier proof of the precise asymptotic equivalence. The proof credits the authors' rank-code and decomposition inputs, Regev's fixed-strip asymptotic, the classical RSK correspondence, and signed reflection. No exclusive historical priority claim is made.

## Independent proof checks

1. The tail of rank-$`d+1`$ entries decreases. An increasing witness of length $d+1$ ending in that tail uses exactly one tail entry. Its preceding rank-$`d`$ front entry is a marker. Every later tail entry must be above that marker and below the witness's last entry. The smallest tail entry therefore lies above every marker below the first (largest) tail entry, and the tail uses a single gap immediately after a marker. Conversely this condition makes every such witness and later tail entry legal. A terminal gap needs no upper marker. It contributes to the finite formula but at most one to each fixed-gap frequency.
2. At fixed gap length, normalized rank-code frequencies concentrate exponentially in the uniform word model. The permutation image is polynomially small by Regev, so it cannot alter the limit. This alone would not supply a bound uniform in gap length.
3. For the uniform bound, rank-by-value agrees with insertion rank for the inverse permutation. Bumping columns go weakly left, so a nonmarker rules out the last row in the transposed recording tableau. Conditional on the removed marker-free block, the remaining walk is still uniform. Increasing every starting gap by the block size guarantees survival of the shortened walk whenever the original survives. The unchanged walk's required endpoint is determined by that block and the shortened endpoint, so a survival-and-point-mass bound applies independently.
4. The Poissonized reflection determinant gives the asserted survival bound. Successive finite differences are triangular determinant-preserving operations, and independent Poisson splitting gives the needed product bound on their first-difference norms. De-Poissonization uses monotone survival. The exponents sum to $(d^2-1)/2$, exactly the factor lost by conditioning on a pair of surviving walks with common endpoint.
5. A generating-function bound in tail length supplies a summable geometric majorant in the binomial-gap formula. In the near-full-front regime the limiting geometric series gives $c_d/[d(d-1)]$; the complementary regime has exponentially small total. Substituting the documented $1234$-avoidance amplitude gives the stated result.
