# A diagonal restriction for balanced cuts that share vertex classes

This argument uses ordinary tensor restriction and partition rank. It works in either case of D.2, with exact circuits over the complex field. Take the graph polynomial of [the flat note](FLAT.md) on the $`h`$-dimensional hypercube, $`n=2^h`$ vertices, with $`\ell`$ bits per edge. Its clause-sum permanent dimension is $`M=3nh\ell\,2^{h\ell}`$. In particular $`\ell=1`$ gives $`M=3hn^2`$.

**Theorem.** Suppose the coefficient tensor is a sum indexed by $`J`$ balanced vertex cuts, with at most $`L`$ product tensors across each cut. Partition the vertices into their $`t`$ nonempty membership classes: two vertices belong to the same class if they are on the same side of each named cut. Then $`t\ge2`$ and
```math
\log_2 L\ \ge\ \ell\left\lceil\frac{n}{4t(t-1)}\right\rceil-\log_2 J
\ \ge\ \frac{\ell n}{4t(t-1)}-t.
```
In particular at $`\ell=1`$, no quasipolynomial-size circuit for this network can have an E.4 decomposition with polylogarithmically many such classes and rank per cut bounded by its circuit size. Also $`J\le(1/2-\varepsilon)\log_2 n`$ implies $`t\le2^J\le n^{1/2-\varepsilon}`$ and $`\log L=\Omega(n^{2\varepsilon})`$ for fixed $`0<\varepsilon<1/2`$, using the **first** inequality and the actual $`\log J`$. These restricted conclusions do not assume the contracting or flat hypothesis.

**Expansion.** Every subset $`S`$ of the cube has edge boundary at least $`\min(|S|,n-|S|)`$. Induct on $`h`$, the single-vertex case being immediate. Split into two $`(h-1)`$-cubes of size $`m`$ with populations $`a\le b`$. Their internal boundaries contribute at least $`\min(a,m-a)+\min(b,m-b)`$ by induction. The matching between halves adds at least $`b-a`$. If $`b\le m/2`$ the sum is at least $`a+b`$; if $`a\ge m/2`$ it is at least $`2m-a-b`$; in the mixed case it equals $`m`$ as a lower estimate. Each suffices.

**Packing trees.** Coalesce all vertices in each membership class to one tensor party. This groups indices by a fixed permutation and preserves the given factorizations since each named cut is a union of classes. In the quotient multigraph keep all parallel wires and ignore loops during tree selection. Mark as terminals the classes of size at least $`n/(4t)`$. Unmarked classes contain at most $`n/4`$ vertices in total. Both sides of every balanced cut contain terminals. Any quotient cut separating terminals has at least $`n/(4t)`$ edges, by the expansion bound applied to the union of classes on one side. Therefore deleting fewer than $`n/(4t)`$ edges leaves all terminals in one component. Choose a tree in that component containing all terminals (at most $`t-1`$ edges), delete its edges, and repeat. Before choosing tree number $`j+1`$ at most $`j(t-1)`$ edges have been deleted. This gives at least $`B=\lceil n/(4t(t-1))\rceil`$ edge-disjoint trees.

**Restriction.** Within each tree impose equality of all used edge labels at each party. At a terminal output their common label, and at other parties sum it away. Project both ends of every unused edge to the coordinate for label zero, including unused loops. These operations are local linear maps on the grouped parties. On each tree the only surviving assignments have one common label, one assignment per label, so the output on the terminal parties is the unit diagonal tensor of dimension $`2^\ell`$. Use the product maps for all trees, which have disjoint edge indices even at shared parties. This yields a diagonal of dimension $`2^{\ell B}`$. Every original product across a named cut remains a product across a nontrivial bipartition of the terminal parties.

**Partition rank of the diagonal.** Partition rank is the least number of product tensors in a sum, with any nontrivial bipartition allowed per term. The diagonal of dimension $`q`$ on $`k\ge2`$ parties has partition rank $`q`$. The upper bound is its explicit diagonal sum. For the lower bound induct on $`k`$, starting at matrix rank. Suppose a decomposition has $`R<q`$ terms, of which $`s`$ isolate the last party. Choose a functional on that party annihilating the $`s`$ isolated factors and nonzero on at least $`q-s`$ basis vectors. Indeed the homogeneous system has at most $`s`$ pivot columns; assign one to every free coordinate and solve the pivot equations. Contract with this functional. The $`s`$ terms vanish, and each other term still splits the remaining $`k-1`$ parties nontrivially. Restrict all parties to the same nonzero coordinates and rescale one party to obtain a diagonal of dimension at least $`q-s`$. Induction would give $`R-s\ge q-s`$, a contradiction. This is the diagonal partition-rank argument of Naslund (JCTA 2020, Lemma 11), included to specify exactly which variable bipartitions it permits. [Naslund, *The partition rank of a tensor and k-right corners in finite vector spaces*, arXiv:1701.04475](https://arxiv.org/abs/1701.04475v3)

The original decomposition has at most $`JL`$ terms, so $`JL\ge2^{\ell B}`$. Finally each cut is determined by a subset of the $`t`$ classes (count each distinct cut name once), giving $`J\le2^t`$. This proves the theorem.

**Limit of this restriction.** If the list includes all balanced cuts, any two different vertices are separated by some listed cut, so all membership classes are singletons. Any diagonal obtained by these local maps with even one such singleton as a terminal has dimension at most $`2^{h\ell}`$ by its one-party flattening rank. The dimension of that party's input space is exactly $`2^{h\ell}`$. Thus arbitrary common diagonal extraction on the ungrouped parties cannot on its own supply a superquasipolynomial lower bound in $`M`$. An unrestricted proof would need to combine incompatible groupings, force shared classes in a circuit decomposition, or use a different monotone quantity.
