# The shift cap cannot depend on poset height alone

This companion strengthens the obstruction to Conjecture 6.9 from [Ferroni–Morales–Panova, *Skew shapes, Ehrhart positivity, and beyond*](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms.70204). Sign evaluation at negative integers merely forces a maximum shift of at least `H−1`, where `H` is the longest chain size. In contrast, the alternating fences `Z_n` are connected skew cell posets with `H=2`, yet every positive product expansion whose shifts are nonnegative has maximum shift at least

`(√2/π^(3/2)+o(1)) √n`.

The result allows arbitrary real shifts, arbitrary positive real weights, and no integrality assumption. It does not refute the distinct geometrically enlarged cap `λ₁+ℓ(λ)−2`.

## 1. A two-coefficient obstruction

Let `P` have `n≥2` elements. Write `c_j=[t^j]Ω(P;t)`. Suppose `c_1>0` and `Ω(P;t)` is a positive weighted sum of products of `n` factors `t+a_i` with all `a_i∈[0,M]`, where `M>0`.

The constant coefficient vanishes. Because all factors have nonnegative constant and linear coefficients, each product must contain at least one zero shift. Products with exactly one zero shift have coefficient of `t` equal to the product of the other shifts. Their ratio of the quadratic to the linear coefficient is

`sum_{a_i>0} (1/a_i) ≥ (n−1)/M.`

Products with at least two zero shifts have vanishing linear coefficient and nonnegative quadratic coefficient. Adding all weighted products gives

`c_2 ≥ ((n−1)/M)c_1.`

## 2. Exact coefficients for alternating fences

Write `b_n=[t]Ω(Z_n;t)`. The underlying undirected Hasse graph of `Z_n` is a path with edges of alternating directions. It is a ribbon with `⌊(n−1)/2⌋+1` cells in one coordinate direction and `⌈(n−1)/2⌉+1` in the other, up to transpose. Ferroni–Morales–Panova Proposition 4.1 gives, equivalently,

`b_n = floor((n−1)/2)! ceil((n−1)/2)! / n!.`

The coproduct identity from their Proposition 3.1 gives

`2[t²]Ω(Z_n;t) = sum_I [t]Ω(I;t) [t]Ω(Z_n\I;t),`

summing over order ideals. A nonempty disconnected induced poset is a disjoint union of two nonempty posets and has vanishing linear coefficient, because its order polynomial is the product of theirs and both have zero constant term. Empty sets also contribute zero. Thus a nonzero summand requires both sides to be connected. In a path, a proper nonempty subset and its complement are both connected exactly when the two are the prefix and suffix of a unique cut edge. For each edge there is exactly one choice of ideal side, the side containing the lower endpoint (no other path edge crosses that cut). Each of the two connected induced posets is an alternating fence or its dual; duality preserves the order polynomial by sending a label `v` to `t+1−v`. Therefore

`[t²]Ω(Z_n;t) = (1/2) sum_{j=1}^{n−1} b_j b_{n−j}.`

## 3. Asymptotic minimum shift

Stirling's formula, separately for odd and even integers, yields

`b_n ∼ √(2π) 2^(−n) n^(−1/2).`

It follows that

`2^n sum_{j=1}^{n−1} b_j b_{n−j} → 2π ∫₀¹ dx/√(x(1−x)) = 2π².`

For completeness, on `εn≤j≤(1−ε)n` the Stirling approximation is uniform, and these sums are Riemann sums for the integral over `[ε,1−ε]`. Globally there is a finite constant `C` with `b_j≤C 2^(−j)/√j`. For `j≤εn` and `ε<1/2`, the scaled endpoint sum is bounded by `C²√(2/n) sum_{j≤εn}j^(−1/2)≤2√2 C²√ε`. The other endpoint has the same bound. Letting `ε` decrease to zero proves convergence.

Consequently `[t²]Ω(Z_n;t) ∼ π² 2^(−n)`, and the necessary inequality from §1 is

`M ≥ (n−1)b_n / [t²]Ω(Z_n;t) ∼ (√2/π^(3/2)) √n.`

This shows that the largest chain alone cannot govern a valid shift cap, even if the coefficient or shift requirements are relaxed to positive real weights and arbitrary nonnegative real shifts.

## 4. Small exact example and source correction

For `n=8`, the coefficient of `t` in `8!Ω(Z_8;t)` is 144 and that of `t²` is 988. Since `988 < 7·144 = 1008`, cap 1 is impossible. Equivalently, its unique expansion in the basis `t^j(t+1)^(8−j)`, ordered from `j=0` to `8`, has coefficients

`(0,144,−20,596,−55,596,−20,144,0)`.

This is not the four-element example mentioned in the paper. The paper claims after Definition 6.8 that `4!Ω(Z_4;t)` fails to lie in `M[{0,1}]`, but its own polynomial in Equation (5.3) gives the exact expansion

`4!Ω(Z_4;t) = 2t(t+1)^3 + t²(t+1)^2 + 2t³(t+1).`

Thus `Z_4` does lie in the cone stated there. The eight-element and asymptotic obstructions use the exact coefficients and do not rely on that sentence.

### Endpoint-basis reduction for future work

For a fixed real `M>0`, the cone of positive real combinations of products with shifts in `[0,M]` equals the cone generated by the `n+1` endpoint products `t^j(t+M)^(n−j)`. Indeed `t+a=(1−a/M)t+(a/M)(t+M)`. Under the substitution `t=My/(1−y)` and multiplication by `(1−y)^n`, the basis products become `M^n y^j`. Thus the relaxed positive-expansion problem at cap `M` is equivalent to coefficientwise nonnegativity of

`(1−y)^n Ω(P; My/(1−y)).`

This equivalence does not guarantee the integer multiplicities required in the original conjecture.
