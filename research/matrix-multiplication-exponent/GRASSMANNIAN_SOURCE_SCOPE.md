# Constraints on two uses of the Grassmannian source

These are elementary necessary conditions for proposed constructions.
They do not exclude a general noncommutative source map or a general
matrix multiplication restriction.

The source and its full coefficient equation are defined in
[GRASSMANNIAN_FROBENIUS_SOURCE.md](GRASSMANNIAN_FROBENIUS_SOURCE.md).

## 1. Commuting coefficient operators cannot give the desired trade

Let $`\mathcal A`$ be a $`p`$-dimensional Frobenius algebra, with
four-party tensor $`\mathcal F(a,b,c,d)=\tau(abcd)`$. The flattening
of $`\mathcal F`$ across $`AB\mid CD`$ has rank exactly $`p`$.
Indeed, it factors through the two multiplication maps
$`\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal A\to\mathcal A`$, which are surjective
because the algebra has a unit, and through its nondegenerate
Frobenius pairing.

Suppose all operator coefficients in the complete trace-four source
equation belong to a single commutative unital complex algebra.
Only finitely many coefficients occur. The subalgebra they generate
has a character into $`\mathbb C`$: quotient by a maximal ideal and
use the following elementary form of the weak Nullstellensatz.
If a field $`K`$ is finitely generated as a complex algebra,
choose a maximal algebraically independent subset
$`\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_t`$ of a finite set of generators.
The remaining generators $`\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m`$ are algebraic
over $`\mathbb C(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_t)`$. Clearing the finitely
many denominators in their monic equations makes them integral
over

```math
R=\mathbb C[\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_t,1/f].
```

Since $`K`$ was generated as an algebra by these elements,
$`K=R[\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_m]`$; thus the field $`K`$ is integral
over $`R`$. This forces $`R`$ to be a field: a monic equation for
$`r^{-1}\in K`$, multiplied by a suitable power of any
$`0\ne r\in R`$, puts $`r^{-1}`$ in $`R`$. If $`t>0`$, choose
$`c\in\mathbb C`$ such that $`\alpha_1-c`$ does not divide $`f`$.
That polynomial is not a unit in $`R`$, a contradiction.
Therefore $`t=0`$, and algebraic closedness gives $`K=\mathbb C`$.
Apply this to the quotient by a maximal ideal of the finitely
generated coefficient algebra.

Applying the resulting character to every
coefficient preserves an exact identity and sends the identity
operator to $`1`$.

The resulting scalar identity is a restriction of $`r`$ full direct
sum copies of

```math
\mathcal F\otimes W_{CD}(Q).
```

The C--D wire lies entirely on one side of the cut, so this source
has $`AB\mid CD`$ flattening rank at most $`rp`$. The full scalar
target

```math
\sum_{i,j=1}^p A_iB_jC_{ij}D_{ij}
```

has flattening rank $`p^2`$. Thus an exact full-target identity
requires $`r\geq p`$.

More generally, if the target C leg is mapped by $`L`$, its
$`AB\mid CD`$ flattening rank is

```math
\#\{(i,j):Le_{ij}\ne0\}.
```

The different D labels make these nonzero rows independent. Hence

```math
\mathop{\mathrm{rank}}L
\leq \#\{(i,j):Le_{ij}\ne0\}\leq rp.                         \tag{1}
```

Consequently the commuting algebra generated by the additive
Satake shifts, on its own, cannot give the required
$`p^{2-o(1)}`$-rank C compression with $`r=p^{o(1)}`$.
The conclusion is about an exact operator identity with all
coefficients in the same commutative algebra. It does not cover
the full noncommutative Grassmannian Weyl algebra, or arbitrary
additional channel projections at A and B.

## 2. Degrees of a coordinate matrix multiplication restriction

The three-party tensor of the special Grassmannian algebra is

```math
\mathcal G=\sum_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
   \tau_0(s_\lambda s_\mu s_\nu)
        e_\lambda^A e_\mu^B e_\nu^C.
```

Its border rank is at most $`p=\binom sk`$, by contracting the
four-party source with the unit at D. A nonzero coefficient has

```math
|\lambda|+|\mu|+|\nu|=d,\qquad d=k(s-k).                     \tag{2}
```

Suppose selected distinct coordinate classes in the three parties
are labelled $`A_{ij},B_{jk},C_{ki}`$, and every one of the $`n^3`$
matrix multiplication cycles has a nonzero coefficient. Write
their degrees as $`u_{ij},v_{jk},w_{ki}`$. Then necessarily

```math
u_{ij}=a_i+b_j,\qquad
v_{jk}=c_k-b_j,\qquad
w_{ki}=d-a_i-c_k                                           \tag{3}
```

for integers $`a_i,b_j,c_k`$.

To prove this, subtract
$`u_{ij}+v_{jk}+w_{ki}=d`$ from the equation with $`j=j_0`$.
It follows that $`u_{ij}-u_{ij_0}`$ is independent of $`i`$,
and its value is $`v_{j_0k}-v_{jk}`$, independent of $`k`$.
Call that value $`b_j`$, and put $`a_i=u_{ij_0}`$,
$`c_k=v_{j_0k}`$. The remaining equation gives the last formula.
All selected degrees must also lie in $`[0,d]`$.

Equation (3) is only a necessary condition. Degree data do not
determine Littlewood--Richardson support. Already in
$`\mathop{\mathrm{Gr}}(2,4)`$, with top class $`s_{22}`$,

```math
\tau_0(s_2s_2\cdot1)=1,\qquad
\tau_0(s_2s_{11}\cdot1)=0,                                  \tag{4}
```

although both triples have degrees $`(2,2,0)`$. Thus eliminating
degree potentials alone cannot certify an embedding or disprove
every embedding.

The number of distinct coordinates immediately requires
$`p\geq n^2`$. For the next bound assume additionally that the
selected coordinate subtensor has exactly the fully weighted
matrix multiplication support, with no off-cycle coefficients.
If the unit is among the selected C coordinates, one gets the
stronger elementary condition

```math
p\geq 2n^2-n.                                              \tag{5}
```

Indeed, the unit slice of $`\mathcal G`$ is the complementary
Schur-basis pairing. The selected A and B sets each have $`n^2`$
elements, so at least $`2n^2-p`$ complementary pairs remain,
and this coordinate submatrix has that rank. A single coordinate
slice of a fully weighted matrix multiplication tensor has rank
$`n`$: it has one nonzero entry in each of its $`n`$ participating
rows and columns. This proves (5). The unit hypothesis is essential
to this particular pairing argument.

No family of coordinate embeddings with quadratic source cost
has been constructed. No superquadratic obstruction follows from
(2)--(5).
