## Nine-fourths theorem and its operational barrier

Alimzhan Amanov, Maxim van den Berg, Jeroen Zuiddam, *Multiplying matrices via polynomials: exposition and barriers*, arXiv:2610.30054v1, 21 October 2026.

Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2610.30054v1

- Theorem 1.1: omega_F <= 9/4 over every field.
- Theorem 4.2: Strassen's characterization, max_phi log_2 phi(<2,2,2>) = omega_F; cited there to Strassen 1988, Theorem 3.8 and Proposition 4.2.
- Lemma 4.3(iv): multiplication-tensor value factors through its three wire values.
- Proposition 4.14: the quadratic constraint on the three wire exponents.
- Definition 5.3: the exact formal preorder generated by the paper's allowed degeneration ingredients and combination rules.
- Theorem 5.4(ii): <n,n,n> <=_formal <r> implies r >= n^(9/4).
- Proposition 5.7: the barrier survives extra rules whenever its operational functionals remain monotone.
- Definition 5.5 and equation (5.2): operational functionals; at theta = (1/3,1/3,1/3), each wire exponent is 3/4.

The listed statements and their scope were read. The full theorem was not replayed in Lean here. The operational functionals are only certified on the formal system; they do not establish a genuine normalized spectral point on all tensors, and therefore do not prove omega >= 9/4.

## Sun's exponent-two claim

Zhi-Wei Sun, *Tensor Degenerations for Matrix Multiplication*, arXiv:2610.13971v1.

- Primary record: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13971v1
- Requested fixed version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13971v1
- Alternate archive identifier: https://chinaxiv.org/abs/202609.00167
- Alternate PDF linked by the archive mirror: https://chinaxiv.org/user/download.htm?filetype=pdf&uuid=ae3fee52d1684d098740665eeee375f3

The primary PDF became accessible at https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13971v1 during the audit. Its header identifies v1 and it has 67 pages. Theorem 1.4 claims the bound border rank(M_(2^n)) <= 2^(2n+1) for every n >= 5 over every field. Theorems 1.1–1.3 give finite-size claims. The complex-field propagation rests on Definition 10.1 and Lemmas 10.3–10.4. The PDF header and index disagree about the date; cite the exact version.

Independently checked: the Walsh minors in Lemma 10.4 are invertible with the stated exceptional determinant 16, and its first-difference identity (10.4) is correct. Unverified: the residual-subspace condition in Definition 10.1(iii) for the bases and its preservation in the next state. Lemma 7.1's isolated first-jet identity does not establish this separate condition. The detailed dependency audit is in `SUN_PROPAGATION_AUDIT.md`.

Two printed local claims fail exact substitution. Appendix A.4's restrictions give coefficient 1 to a retained B_i, although the common coefficient is asserted to be 1/128. Independently found by the barrier-audit worker and checked here, Appendix A.7's crossed map at (alpha,beta,gamma)=(0,4,4) has coefficient 1 at t^(-3) Xbar_L Ybar_L V; its claimed negative-term cancellation therefore fails. Specific local modifications repair these local identities, as proved in `SUN_CROSSED_MAP_AUDIT.md`, but a repaired global certificate has not been verified. Neither discrepancy nor the unresolved propagation calculation proves that omega differs from two.

Independently checked implication: if that border-rank estimate holds for an unbounded sequence of n, the standard border-rank-to-exponent theorem gives omega_C <= log_(2^n)(2^(2n+1)) = 2+1/n. Passing to the unbounded sequence and using omega_C >= 2 gives equality. A bound at one fixed n is insufficient.

## Later independent nine-fourths exposition

M. Levent Dogan, Vladimir Lysikov, Keiya Sakabe, Michael Walter, *Tensor value and the 9/4 bound for matrix multiplication*, arXiv:2611.09716v1, 6 November 2026.

Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/2611.09716v1

The paper gives a proof through fractional symmetrized tensor value, with Lemma 4.12 supplying partial-direct-sum superadditivity. It establishes the same nine-fourths bound without using spectral points. This later work corroborates that theorem's independent mathematical development; its publication after Sun's claim is not a refutation of Sun.

## Older normalized-spectrum reference

Matthias Christandl, Peter Vrana, Jeroen Zuiddam, *Universal points in the asymptotic spectrum of tensors*, arXiv:1709.07851v3, 26 May 2018; JAMS 36 (2023), 31-79.

Primary full text: https://arxiv.org/html/1709.07851v3

Theorem 1.1 gives the compact normalized monotone-semiring-homomorphism spectrum. Remark 1.2 explains equivalence of restriction and degeneration for its asymptotic use. The paper constructs universal quantum spectral points. Merely assigning three distances does not construct such a point; all tensor inequalities and semiring laws remain necessary.

## Broader claim search

Searches covered the exact arXiv identifier, exact Sun title, omega=2 proof/claim terms, and criticism/error/withdrawal terms. No primary public critique of Sun or distinct verified exponent-two proof was found in those results. This is a report of search results, not proof of absence.

Two misleading search leads were checked: the MathDB page titled 'The matrix multiplication exponent equals two' is a problem page whose listed OpenAI submissions claim 9/4 or weaker bounds; Reza Zadeh's arXiv:2610.10593 claims 2.37115924, not two. Neither is an exponent-two resolution.

## Higher-order separation and its existing barrier

Huanyi Zhou, *Asymptotic Rank of Complete Graph Tensors*, [arXiv:2610.34273v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34273v1), 25 October 2026; revised as *Asymptotic Rank of Weighted Graph Tensors via Completely Positive Bounds*, [arXiv:2610.34273v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.34273v3), 11 November 2026.

The primary full texts were accessed. Version 3, Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2 give the many-input polynomial tensor and its interpolation cost. Theorem 3.4 separates any fixed number of shared parties, retains an edge of arbitrary dimension, and uses the same sum-of-squares degeneration independently reconstructed in this project. Corollary 3.5 gives its entropy consequence. These are prior results; this project claims an independent reconstruction, not novelty.

Version 1, Lemma 5.4 is the resource form of that separation. Definition 5.5(v) explicitly includes it among permitted operations. Theorem 5.11 proves the fixed-party operational barrier, including tensor powers, subexponential overhead, and common auxiliary expressions. The separator alone therefore does not escape that barrier. Version 3 removes this operational section and replaces it with conic bounds; Proposition 5.13 retains value 9/4 on the triangle. No theorem here gives a genuine spectral point of perimeter 9/4.

## Current support and quantum results

Keiya Sakabe, Mahmut Levent Dogan, Michael Walter, *Strassen's support functionals coincide with the quantum functionals*, [arXiv:2601.21553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.21553).

The indexed primary full text was accessed. Theorem 1.1 identifies the upper support functional with the singleton quantum functional over the complex numbers; Theorem 1.3 supplies the moment-polytope minimax formula. This proves that the support functionals are universal spectral points. It does not say that every spectral point is one of these functionals. Their maximum on a matrix multiplication tensor consequently remains a maximum over a known family, not over the whole spectrum. The fixed-version endpoint for v2 returned an access error, so no v2-specific theorem numbering is asserted here.

Josh Alman, Baitian Li, Kevin Pratt, *The edge of the asymptotic spectrum of tensors*, [arXiv:2604.01386v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.01386v2), 27 September 2026.

Theorem 1.6, precisely Corollary 8.10, identifies every spectral point satisfying phi(<1,n,1>) = n for all n with an edge support functional. Corollary 8.10 uses Proposition 8.1 to obtain wire exponents (rho,1,1-rho), then Corollary 8.7 for uniqueness. Independently checked consequence: by the wire power law and party permutations, any point with one wire exponent equal to one has perimeter two. Every possible perimeter-greater-than-two witness therefore has all three exponents strictly between zero and one. This theorem leaves the interior unresolved.

Alonso Botero, Matthias Christandl, Thomas C. Fraser, Itai Leigh, Harold Nieuwboer, *On quantum functionals for higher-order tensors*, [arXiv:2604.18283v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18283v2), 18 September 2026.

The pinned primary full text was accessed. Theorem 3.1 separates upper and lower quantum functionals for every laminar weighting outside the singleton family; Theorem 3.14 states that the lower functional for every nonsingleton weighting is not a universal spectral point. Universal extensions exist from the subsemiring where the two agree, but that is an existence result, not equality with the original lower functional on all tensors. These results prevent using arbitrary bipartition entropy mixtures as spectral points without a separate proof.

## Hypergraph subrank and minimum cuts

Peter Vrana and Matthias Christandl, *Entanglement distillation from Greenberger-Horne-Zeilinger shares*, [arXiv:1603.03964v2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1603.03964v2), 30 March 2016; Communications in Mathematical Physics 352 (2017), 621-627, DOI [10.1007/s00220-017-2861-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00220-017-2861-6).

The primary PDF was read. Definition 1 identifies SLOCC conversion with separate linear maps at each party. Theorem 1, page 3, proves conversion cost 1/lambda(H) from the product of binary unit tensors on the hyperedges of any multihypergraph H to the full binary unit tensor. Here lambda(H) is hyperedge connectivity, equivalently the minimum number of hyperedges crossing a nontrivial cut. Thus the asymptotic subrank is 2^lambda(H). The theorem is genuinely about hypergraphs, not only graphs.

For any chosen party subset P, apply it to a multihypergraph whose every edge H lies in P, then pad the restriction maps at the other parties. Spectral monotonicity gives sum_H w_H g(H) >= lambda_P(w) g(P). Integer multiplicities are covered by the theorem; scaling and continuity extend this numerical inequality to rational and real nonnegative weights. A terminal-mincut formula allowing helper parties outside P is a different assertion and is not imported here.
