# The next multiplicity in the square-zero module construction

Status: research. Sections 1--4 prove necessary conditions and exclude
some exact completion ansatzes. They do not construct a smoothing at
multiplicity $`m=n+1`$, and do not exclude every such smoothing.
All statements are over $`\mathbb C`$, unless a characteristic-zero
field is explicitly specified.

## 1. Model and the allowed first nontrivial diagonal term

Write $`L_X=X\otimes I_m`$ on
$`V=\mathbb C^n\otimes\mathbb C^m`$, and consider

```math
N_X=\begin{pmatrix}0&L_X\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad X\in\mathop{\mathrm{Mat}}_n(\mathbb C).
```

A diagonal smoothing means a formal commuting tuple over
$`\mathbb C[[t]]`$ with special tuple $`N`$ and generic tuple
simultaneously diagonalizable over $`\overline{\mathbb C((t))}`$.
Theorem 7 of `SQUARE_ZERO_MODULE_OBSTRUCTION.md` excludes
this for $`n\ge2`$ and $`1\le m\le n`$.

The leading-order calculations in Sections 3--4 of that note show
that, after a common lower shear and the rescaling used there, any
first nontrivial diagonal term gives the special tuple

```math
\Psi_X=\begin{pmatrix}I_n\otimes\Phi_X&L_X\\
0&I_n\otimes\Phi_X\end{pmatrix},
\qquad [\Phi_X,\Phi_Y]=0.                       \tag{1}
```

Its generated unital algebra has dimension at most $`2nm`$ if
the original family is a diagonal smoothing.

**Lemma 1.** Every joint generalized eigenspace of $`\Phi`$ in
(1) has dimension strictly greater than $`n`$ if (1) is itself
the special tuple of a diagonal smoothing.

**Proof.** Separate the joint eigencharacters by one linear
combination of the $`\Phi_X`$. Coprime characteristic factors
lift its spectral projectors to the formal family. This gives a
formal diagonal smoothing of each block

```math
\begin{pmatrix}I_n\otimes\Phi_X|_E&X\otimes I_E\\
0&I_n\otimes\Phi_X|_E\end{pmatrix}
```

on a space of dimension $`2n\dim E`$. For a block family
$`M_X(t)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}A_X(t)&B_X(t)\\
C_X(t)&D_X(t)\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$, multiply by $`t`$
and conjugate by $`\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(t^{-1}I,I)`$.
The resulting blocks are

```math
\begin{pmatrix}tA_X(t)&B_X(t)\\t^2C_X(t)&tD_X(t)\end{pmatrix}.
```

They remain integral and generically simultaneously diagonalizable,
and specialize to $`N_{n,\dim E}`$. Theorem 7 of the previous
note therefore forces $`\dim E>n`$. This is a direct formal
composition, so no unmentioned algebraization step is used.

Consequently, when $`m\le2n+1`$, the tuple $`\Phi`$ has just
one joint eigencharacter. Subtract it and assume the $`\Phi_X`$
are jointly nilpotent. Put

```math
R=\mathbb C[\Phi_X:X\in\mathop{\mathrm{Mat}}_n],\qquad
J=\mathop{\mathrm{rad}}R,\qquad r=n^2,
\qquad k=\dim J/J^2.
```

**Lemma 2.** If $`k\ge1`$, the algebra generated by (1) has
dimension at least

```math
r+1+rk-\frac{k(k-1)}2.                          \tag{2}
```

If $`k=1`$ and the nilpotence index of a generator of $`J`$
is $`s`$, its dimension is at least $`sr+1`$.

**Proof.** The map
$`\varphi:\mathop{\mathrm{Mat}}_n\to J/J^2`$ given by
$`X\mapsto[\Phi_X]`$ is surjective, because the $`\Phi_X`$
generate $`J`$. Modulo $`J`$, the identity and the $`\Psi_X`$
give $`r+1`$ independent directions. Products lie in the first
filtered piece, and modulo $`J^2`$ their upper-right blocks are

```math
X\otimes\varphi(Y)+Y\otimes\varphi(X).          \tag{3}
```

Choose a complement $`U`$ of $`\ker\varphi`$. The image in
(3) is the direct sum of
$`\ker\varphi\otimes J/J^2`$ and the symmetric tensors on
$`U\cong J/J^2`$. Its dimension is
$`(r-k)k+k(k+1)/2=rk-k(k-1)/2`$. These directions are
independent of the preceding $`r+1`$ directions, proving (2).

If $`k=1`$, choose $`H\in J`$ whose class spans $`J/J^2`$.
Nakayama's lemma gives $`J=(H)`$, and iteration through the
nilpotent radical gives $`R=\mathbb C[H]\cong
\mathbb C[z]/(z^s)`$. Write
$`\Phi_X=\ell(X)H+O(H^2)`$, where $`\ell\ne0`$, and choose
$`Y`$ with $`\ell(Y)\ne0`$. For $`0\le p<s`$, the
upper-right block of $`\Psi_Y^p\Psi_X`$, in filtered degree
$`p`$, is

```math
\ell(Y)^pX+p\ell(Y)^{p-1}\ell(X)Y.              \tag{4}
```

For $`p=0`$ the second term means zero. The map in (4) is
invertible: its eigenvalue on $`\ker\ell`$ is
$`\ell(Y)^p`$, and its eigenvalue on $`\mathbb C Y`$ is
$`(p+1)\ell(Y)^p`$. Thus each of the $`s`$ filtered degrees
contains at least $`r`$ independent directions, in addition to
the identity. This proves the second assertion.

**Corollary 3.** Suppose $`n\ge3`$ and $`m=n+1`$. In any
first-order limit (1) arising from a diagonal smoothing, either
$`\Phi`$ is scalar or

```math
\Phi_X=\chi(X)I_m+\ell(X)H,
\qquad H\ne0,\quad H^2=0,\quad\ell\ne0.       \tag{5}
```

Indeed, there is one joint character by Lemma 1. If $`k\ge2`$,
(2) is at least $`3r=3n^2>2n(n+1)`$. Thus $`k=1`$ for a
nonzero radical. If $`s\ge3`$, the second part of Lemma 2
again exceeds $`2n(n+1)`$. Hence $`s=2`$, giving (5).
For $`n=2,m=3`$, the $`k=2`$ estimate is exactly $`12`$,
so this particular corollary does not apply.

The abstract algebra generated by a tuple of the form (5) with
the scalar part removed has dimension $`2n^2+1`$. After taking
$`\ell(X_0)=1`$ and a basis $`X_i`$ of $`\ker\ell`$, its
basis is

```math
1,\ x_0,\ x_i,\ x_0^2,\ x_0x_i
\quad(1\le i<n^2),
```

with $`x_ix_j=x_0^3=x_0^2x_i=0`$ for $`i,j\ge1`$.
Its displayed monomial basis is a finite lower ideal, so the explicit
grid-distraction family in `POLYNOMIAL_SOURCE_OBSTRUCTION.md`,
Section 1, proves that it is smoothable. This does not identify the
displayed action on $`V\oplus V`$ with its regular module.
Smoothability of the abstract algebra alone is insufficient for
the required smoothing of this particular representation.

## 2. Exact commuting completions never suffice

The following exclusion holds for every multiplicity, not just
$`m\le n`$.

**Lemma 4.** Over any characteristic-zero field, suppose a linear
commuting tuple has the exact block form

```math
M_X=\begin{pmatrix}A_X&X\otimes I_m\\C_X&D_X\end{pmatrix}.
                                                               \tag{6}
```

Then one common lower shear conjugates it to

```math
\begin{pmatrix}I_n\otimes\Phi_X&X\otimes I_m\\
0&I_n\otimes\Phi_X\end{pmatrix},
\qquad[\Phi_X,\Phi_Y]=0.                        \tag{7}
```

In particular, (6) is not simultaneously diagonalizable for
$`n\ge2`$.

**Proof.** The weighted family

```math
\begin{pmatrix}tA_X&L_X\\t^2C_X&tD_X\end{pmatrix}
```

is a common conjugate of $`tM_X`$. The first-order classification
in the preceding module note gives
$`A_X=-L_XK+F_X`$, $`D_X=KL_X+F_X`$, where
$`F_X=I_n\otimes\Phi_X`$. Conjugating by the inverse lower
shear with lower block $`tK`$ gives exactly

```math
\begin{pmatrix}
tF_X&L_X\\
t^2(C_X+KL_XK+[F_X,K])&tF_X
\end{pmatrix}.
```

There are no suppressed higher coefficients in this expression.
The second-order lemma therefore makes its lower block zero and
makes the $`\Phi_X`$ commute. Setting $`t=1`$ proves (7).
Equivalently, the original lower block is
$`C_X=-KL_XK+[K,F_X]`$.

For $`X=I_n`$, (7) is
$`\left(\begin{smallmatrix}F&I\\0&F\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$.
If it were diagonalizable, its invariant upper subspace would
make $`F`$ diagonalizable. An eigenvector of $`F`$ then gives
an invariant two-dimensional Jordan block with nonzero upper
entry, a contradiction. This proves the exclusion.

Thus a valid smoothing at $`m=n+1`$ must use higher-order
changes to the upper-right matrix space. It cannot be a single
quadratically weighted commuting completion with fixed upper
blocks $`L_X`$.

The same observation excludes a formal smoothing whose upper
blocks form an exact unital representation of
$`\mathop{\mathrm{Mat}}_n`$ throughout the family. Such a
representation over $`\mathbb C[[t]]`$, specializing to $`L`$,
is conjugate to $`L`$: use the images of the matrix units to
transport a basis of the image of $`E_{11}`$. Apply the same
change of basis on both copies of $`V`$, then apply Lemma 4
over the fraction field. No assertion is made here for arbitrary
perturbed upper-right spaces.

## 3. A hyperplane version of the first-order calculation

For linear maps $`A,D`$ on a subspace of
$`\mathop{\mathrm{Mat}}_n`$, define

```math
\delta(A,D)(X,Y)=XD(Y)-YD(X)+A(X)Y-A(Y)X.       \tag{8}
```

**Lemma 5.** Let $`n\ge3`$, let $`\ell`$ be a nonzero
linear functional, and put $`H_0=\ker\ell`$. If (8) is zero
for all $`X,Y\in H_0`$, then there are a matrix $`K`$ and a
linear functional $`\lambda:H_0\to\mathbb C`$ such that

```math
A(X)=-XK+\lambda(X)I_n,\qquad
D(X)=KX+\lambda(X)I_n
\quad(X\in H_0).                              \tag{9}
```

**Proof.** Independent invertible changes on the left and right
of $`X`$ put $`\ell`$ into diagonal form. More explicitly,
under $`X=P\widetilde XQ`$, replace $`A`$ by
$`\widetilde A(\widetilde X)=P^{-1}A(P\widetilde XQ)P`$
and $`D`$ by
$`\widetilde D(\widetilde X)=QD(P\widetilde XQ)Q^{-1}`$.
Equation (8) is unchanged up to multiplication by $`P,Q`$.
Hence assume that $`H_0`$ contains every off-diagonal matrix
unit.

For off-diagonal $`X=E_{ij}`$ and $`Y=E_{k\ell}`$, the
$`(a,b)`$ entry of (8) is

```math
\delta_{ai}(D_{k\ell})_{jb}
-\delta_{ak}(D_{ij})_{\ell b}
+(A_{ij})_{ak}\delta_{\ell b}
-(A_{k\ell})_{ai}\delta_{jb}=0.                 \tag{10}
```

Given $`a\ne i`$ and $`k\ne a`$, choose the column
$`\ell`$ to avoid both $`j`$ and $`k`$, which is possible
because $`n\ge3`$. Taking $`b=\ell`$ in (10) shows
$`(A_{ij})_{ak}=0`$. Thus $`A_{ij}`$ is supported on row
$`i`$ and the diagonal. Transposing the calculation gives
support of $`D_{ij}`$ on column $`j`$ and the diagonal.

For fixed off-diagonal $`Y=E_{k\ell}`$, take $`a=i,b=j`$
in (10) with $`i\ne k,j\ne\ell,i\ne j`$. It gives
$`(D_{k\ell})_{jj}=(A_{k\ell})_{ii}`$. All these diagonal
entries have one common value. To see this without assuming
independent choices, form the bipartite graph with left vertices
$`i\ne k`$, right vertices $`j\ne\ell`$, and edges
$`i\ne j`$. It is connected: the edge $`i=\ell,j=k`$
exists, every left vertex joins $`j=k`$, and every right
vertex joins $`i=\ell`$. Subtract the resulting common scalar
from both $`A_{k\ell}`$ and $`D_{k\ell}`$.

The remaining matrices have the forms
$`A'_{ij}=e_i a_{ij}^{\mathsf T}`$ and
$`D'_{ij}=d_{ij}e_j^{\mathsf T}`$. Equation (10) becomes

```math
((d_{k\ell})_j+(a_{ij})_k)E_{i\ell}
=((d_{ij})_\ell+(a_{k\ell})_i)E_{kj}.           \tag{11}
```

For fixed $`j,k`$, its first coefficient is zero whenever
$`(i,\ell)\ne(k,j)`$. The allowable indices are
$`i\ne j,\ell\ne k`$. Their bipartite graph is complete
except possibly for the one edge $`i=k,\ell=j`$; each side
has at least two vertices, so the graph is connected. Thus one
number $`K_{jk}`$ works simultaneously for every allowable
$`i,\ell`$, with
$`(a_{ij})_k=-K_{jk}`$ and $`(d_{k\ell})_j=K_{jk}`$.
This proves (9) on all off-diagonal matrix units.

Subtract that common $`K`$ solution and extend the scalar
functional arbitrarily to $`H_0`$. The residual $`A,D`$
vanish on the off-diagonal units. For any diagonal $`X\in H_0`$,
equation (8) against every $`E_{ij}`$ with $`i\ne j`$ gives

```math
A(X)E_{ij}=E_{ij}D(X).
```

This makes $`A(X),D(X)`$ diagonal and equates their $`i,j`$
entries whenever $`i\ne j`$. Since $`n\ge3`$, all these
entries are equal. The residual is therefore a common scalar,
which can be included in $`\lambda(X)`$. This proves (9).

The proof is coefficientwise valid for matrix-valued coefficients
on an additional multiplicity factor, because the $`X,Y`$
actions only use the first matrix factor.

**Corollary 6.** For $`n\ge3`$, the equation

```math
\delta(A,D)(X,Y)=\ell(Y)Z(X)-\ell(X)Z(Y)        \tag{12}
```

has a solution if and only if there are constant matrices
$`P,Q,S`$ with

```math
Z(X)=XQ-PX+\ell(X)S.                           \tag{13}
```

**Proof.** Restrict (12) to $`H_0`$ and use Lemma 5. Subtract
the resulting common $`K`$ and a linear extension of the scalar
part. The remaining maps vanish on $`H_0`$, so they are
$`A(X)=\ell(X)P`$ and $`D(X)=\ell(X)Q`$. Substitution
in (8) gives (12) with $`Z(X)=XQ-PX`$. Equality of the
two alternating products with $`\ell`$ says precisely that
their difference is $`\ell(X)S`$. The converse follows by
the same substitution; the $`S`$ term cancels.

## 4. What the hyperplane calculation does and does not give

After subtracting the scalar part of (5), suppose the upper-right
block of the formal family is
$`L_X+tB_X+O(t^2)`$ and the first-order diagonal block is
$`I_n\otimes\ell(X)H`$. The degree-two upper-right equation
has right side of the form (12), with

```math
Z(X)=[I_n\otimes H,B_X]
```

up to an overall sign depending on which side of the equation is
used. Corollary 6 therefore forces this commutator to be a sum
of left and right basis-change terms and one term in the single
direction $`\ell`$. This is a necessary second-order condition
on a proposed escape through (5).

The statement does not remove every upper-right coefficient by a
formal gauge. In particular, a distinguished-direction term and
later interactions with the commutant of $`H`$ remain. No
iteration excluding $`m=n+1`$ has been established here. A
constructive attempt must supply those higher-order coefficients
and verify simultaneous diagonalizability, not only solve (12).

The finite exact checks in `check_hyperplane_cocycle.py` corroborate
Lemma 5 for $`n=3,4`$ and every possible rank of $`\ell`$.
The general proof is the entrywise argument above and does not depend
on those checks.
