# Lovász’s Hamiltonian path conjecture

**Partial research.** A connected cyclic layered graph with a deck group that is a product of copies of one cyclic prime-power group has a Hamilton cycle that advances around the layers. Each interface can have its own family of allowable translations. This gives Hamilton cycles in vertex-transitive graphs whenever a Hamilton cycle in a suitable quotient has a connected lift. The unrestricted Hamilton-path conjecture remains open. [Issue #404](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/404)

## Target and sources

Lovász’s Problem 11 asks whether every finite connected vertex-transitive graph has a Hamilton path. The November 24, 2026 source check found results on near-linear and linear cycle lengths and a separate odd-order proof claim. The primary sources on the general problem still treat it as open. The original problem appeared in the proceedings of the 1969 Calgary conference, p. 497. [Original source pointer](https://mathconjectures.com/references/Lovasz1969ProblemSession) · [Li–Methuku](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38135) · [Ma–Zhao](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02053) · [Nakanishi](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00646)

Witte proved Hamiltonicity for connected Cayley digraphs of prime-power order. That covers the special case where the allowed translations vary at just one interface, after reducing each pass around the layers to one step in a Cayley digraph. The argument below allows simultaneous variation at arbitrary interfaces, using independently chosen basis directions to join cycles. Priority for this layered construction is provisional. [Witte](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895686900687)

## A connected cyclic lift over a homocyclic prime-power group

Let $A=(\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z)^r$ for a prime $p$ and $k\ge1$, and let there be $\ell\ge3$ layers indexed modulo $\ell$. Vertices in layer $i$ are $(i,a)$ for $a\in A$. Across interface $i$ allow all arcs

```math
(i,a)\longrightarrow(i+1,a+s),\qquad s\in S_i,
```

for a nonempty set $S_i\subseteq A$. If the underlying undirected graph is connected, we prove the directed graph has a Hamilton cycle. If $A$ is trivial the statement is the quotient cycle itself.

**Choose reference arcs.** Pick one $s_i\in S_i$ at each interface and let $t=\sum_i s_i$. Changing the origins of the layers makes the reference shifts zero at all interfaces except the closing one, where the reference shift is $t$. The differences $S_i-s_i$ are unaffected by this change of origins. Let $H$ be the subgroup generated by $t$ and all those differences. Every arc preserves the coset of $H$ in the changed coordinates. Thus connectivity implies $H=A$.

Set $R=\mathbb Z/p^k\mathbb Z$. The reference shifts can be chosen so that $t$ is nonzero modulo $pA$. If it is initially zero there, some allowed difference $g\in S_i-s_i$ is nonzero there by connectivity, and replacing $s_i$ by $s_i+g$ makes the new sum nonzero there. Connectivity still implies generation for the new references. From the new difference sets choose vectors whose residues, together with the residue of $t$, form a basis of $A/pA$. The vectors and $t$ are a basis over $R$, since their determinant is a unit. Use the arc family at each interface to assign its chosen difference vectors to that interface.

Using only reference arcs gives one directed cycle over each coset of $R t$. Indeed each turn around the layers translates by $t$, which has order $p^k$. Initially keep all these cycles. We process the interfaces in turn, using the following cycle join at each one.

**Directed torus ordering.** On a product of $h$ cyclic groups of the same order $m$, with arcs given by the positive standard generators, there is a directed Hamilton cycle for $h\ge1$. In rank one just use the generator repeatedly. For higher rank order the cosets of the first cyclic factor by induction on the remaining factors. In each coset take $m-1$ successive steps in the first generator to visit every point of that coset, then take the next step in the ordering of cosets. The higher coordinates close by induction, and the total first-coordinate displacement is $(m-1)m^{h-1}$, divisible by $m$. This makes a directed cycle visiting every vertex. The rank-one case $m=2$ is used as a directed two-cycle in the ordering of components.

**Join at one interface.** Suppose the current cycles cover exactly one coset of a subgroup $U$ at a time, and all reference arcs at unprocessed interfaces are still present. This holds initially for $U=R t$. At interface $i$ let $W$ be the span of the assigned difference vectors. By the basis choice $`U\cap W=\{0\}`$ and $W$ is a product of cyclic groups of order $p^k$ with those vectors as its standard generators. If $W$ is trivial, leave this interface unchanged.

Within each coset $b+(U+W)$, there is one current cycle for every $U$-coset $b+w+U$ with $w\in W$. In that cycle remove the reference arc at interface $i$ whose source is $(i,b+w)$. Its target is $(i+1,b+w+s_i)$ in the current coordinates. Keep the resulting directed path through that entire $U$-coset. Order the paths by the directed torus cycle on $W$. If the next index is $w+g$, join the end $(i,b+w)$ to the next beginning $(i+1,b+w+g+s_i)$. This arc is allowed since $g$ was selected from $S_i-s_i$. Thus the paths become one directed cycle over the enlarged coset. Every other interface keeps its reference arcs. Repeat for all enlarged cosets and process the next interface.

The final subgroup is $A$ by the basis choice, so the final cycle is directed Hamiltonian. It advances one layer at each step. In rank at least two even the order of a single net translation is too small to produce this cycle by selecting just one shift per interface.

## A path variant and relation to vertex transitivity

The joining step has a variant for a finite abelian deck group without a complement at the final interface. Suppose current cycles cover the cosets of $U$ and reference arcs remain at an interface whose difference set generates $A/U$. The Cayley digraph of the quotient on the projected differences has a directed Hamilton path. To see this for any finite abelian group, choose a nonzero generator and traverse each of its cyclic cosets in order along that generator. Order the cosets by induction in the quotient by the generator’s subgroup. The next quotient step lifts to an allowed translation at every vertex of the current coset, so the entrance positions cause no difficulty. There is no closing step to require an extra voltage condition.

Use that Hamilton path to order the cycles of the layered graph. Choose a reference source in the first coset and for each step lift its quotient difference by an actual allowed difference at the interface. This fixes the next reference source. Break the corresponding reference arc in each cycle and join the paths successively, keeping both global ends. The result is a forward Hamilton path. For a directed cycle the analogous ordering would require that its total chosen displacement be zero in the original group, as the direct complement guarantees in the prime-power proof.

Now suppose a graph admits a semiregular group of automorphisms isomorphic to $A$, so that the group acts freely and transitively on each orbit. Identify each orbit with $A$ by choosing a representative. Edges joining any two orbits are then described by a family of shifts, since a translation of both ends preserves an edge. If the quotient has a Hamilton cycle of length at least three and the edges over its consecutive interfaces give a connected spanning subgraph, the theorem gives a Hamilton cycle in the original graph by orienting those interfaces forward. This implication applies in particular to vertex-transitive graphs with that quotient structure. Finding such a lift in an arbitrary vertex-transitive graph remains unresolved.

The requirement on the deck group matters for the forward cycle statement. With deck group $\mathbb Z_6$, three layers, zero shifts on two interfaces, and shifts $2,3$ on the remaining one, the lift is connected. A forward Hamilton cycle would make six passes and use shift two in some number $j$ of them. The net shift is $18-j$, so closure forces $j=0$ or $6$. Neither constant shift visits all six deck elements. This example still has a forward Hamilton path, using deck order $0,2,4,1,3,5$.

## Exact controls

The [integer checker](verify.py) builds the reference arcs and performs the joins explicitly. One example has four layers and deck group $(\mathbb Z/4\mathbb Z)^3$, with independent shifts at three interfaces. The other has four layers and deck group $(\mathbb Z/9\mathbb Z)^3$, with two basis differences on the same interface. It checks the entire forward cycles on 256 and 2,916 vertices respectively, including closure and membership of every shift in the assigned family.
