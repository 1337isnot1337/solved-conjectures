## Exact problem and earlier results

Bachoc, Moustrou, Vallentin and Zimmermann distinguish a stable cold spot, which minimizes the lattice Gaussian for every sufficiently large parameter, from a universal one, which minimizes for every positive parameter. Their Theorem 1.2 gives a sufficient stability criterion using a transitive deep-hole orbit and spherical 2-design shells. Section 1.6 asks whether their stable examples are universal and separately singles out the unresolved dual root families $`A_n^*,\ n\ge3`$ and $`D_n^*,\ n\ge5`$. The shells in those families have the weaker 1-design property. The present theorem determines **all** minimizing translations of the fixed lattice $`A_n^*`$ for **each** positive Gaussian parameter. The remaining examples in §1.6 and optimization over lattice shapes are separate problems. [Journal article, §1.6 and Theorem 1.2](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2026.10169) · [Source PDF, p. 7](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/16B869EA8216480737BB2BD00B115F9D/S2050509426101698a.pdf/polarization-of-lattices-stable-cold-spots-and-spherical-designs.pdf#page=7).

Rank one is the classical midpoint minimum of the circle heat kernel. Rank two is the equilateral triangular lattice, treated by Albert Baernstein II in *A minimum problem for heat kernels of flat tori*, Contemporary Mathematics **201** (1997), 227–243. Rank three is the body-centered cubic lattice up to similarity, already covered by the repository's FCC/BCC theorem. That sibling also covers every checkerboard dual $`D_n^*`$ in its stated rank range. A change of lattice scale merely reparameterizes the positive Gaussian parameter. The additional all-parameter cases in the present result start at rank four. [Baernstein’s chapter in the proceedings](https://books.google.com/books?id=ws4aCAAAQBAJ&pg=PA227) · [Faulhuber’s calculation of the hexagonal heat-kernel minimum](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.01218) · [FCC/BCC proof and scope](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/tree/a70f25b264ab4261ea18dcf3292dc4432e7bb381/results/betermin-fcc-bcc-cold-spots).

Earlier research by Cinna Davis established large- and small-parameter results for dual type A. Neither argument controlled the entire intervening range. The Leech result concerns a different lattice and a limiting orbit. The separate mixed-hole campaign studies arbitrary lattices with several deep-hole types. The original dual-type-A tracking issue is #372. [Large-parameter proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/069f966bc77c5a962460597e9a6118becd7f50e0/research/lattice-cold-spot-stability/STABILITY.md) · [Small-parameter proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/069f966bc77c5a962460597e9a6118becd7f50e0/research/lattice-cold-spot-stability/SMALL-ALPHA.md) · [Dual-type-A issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/372) · [Mixed-hole publication, PR #963](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/963) · [Leech issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/376).

## Earlier identities and geometry

The exact circular average of the shifted theta product is already **Theorem 4 of Song Heng Chan and Zhi-Guo Liu**, *On a new circular summation of theta functions* (2010). In their notation take the integer multiplier $`m=1`$, the number of factors $`n=N`$, $`y_j=\pi x_j`$ and $`z=\pi t`$, absorbing their nome convention into the coefficients. Division by $`N`$ gives the normalized average in the proof. Jun-Ming Zhu restates this theorem as Theorem 1.1. Zhu's correction concerns a different imaginary-transformation formula. Earlier, Kok Seng Chua connected Ramanujan's circular **power** sums to the theta series of $`A_n^*`$. Chan–Liu's identity supplies the circular sum with arbitrary centered shifts. Neither of the displayed circular-summation theorems compares the resulting coefficient across shift configurations. [Chan–Liu, Theorem 4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jnt.2009.09.011) · [Zhu, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1202.1869#page=1) · [Chua’s dual-lattice circular summation theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-01-06080-4).

The permutohedral Voronoi cell and its deep-hole coordinates are classical. Jacobi's triple product and its multiplication over roots of unity yield the displayed eta product at the centered staircase. The contribution here is the global comparison with every shift at each scale, its full equality classification, and the Hessian calculation. The minimum value follows by evaluating the known product at the proved minimizer. [Baek–Adams, geometry of the permutohedral lattice](https://graphics.stanford.edu/papers/permutohedral/permutohedral_techreport.pdf) · [Jacobi's triple product, DLMF §20.5](https://dlmf.nist.gov/20.5).

Caius Folkerts gives universal Gaussian cold-spot theorems for $`E_8,E_6,E_7^*`$, the Barnes–Wall lattice in dimension 16 and the $`D_n^+`$ family for even $`n\ge8`$. Proposition 6.1 also provides a projection criterion. It requires a complementary point at which **all** fibre Gaussians agree for every parameter, together with an ambient minimizer on that slice. For the most direct projection of $`\mathbb Z^N`$ onto the sum-zero hyperplane, the complementary line is generated by $`\mathbf1`$. Its fibre in residue class $`j`$ is $`(j/N+\mathbb Z)\mathbf1`$, for $`0\le j\lt N`$. Equal Gaussian profiles would force equal nearest distances by the large-parameter limit. A metric circle has at most two points at any prescribed distance from a fixed point. Thus for $`N\ge3`$ the $`N`$ distinct equally spaced residues cannot satisfy the common-fibre hypothesis in this projection. Proposition 7.1 concerns diagonal gluing. Neither the stated family list nor this direct use of the projection criterion supplies the present theorem. [Folkerts’s paper and theorem correspondence](https://github.com/caiusdfolkerts/research/tree/487611b97e9c0b59ee583b451e92130a789b2caa/cold_spots) · [Exact projection hypotheses](https://github.com/caiusdfolkerts/research/blob/487611b97e9c0b59ee583b451e92130a789b2caa/cold_spots/reading/paper_verification/finite_projection_transfer.lean).

## Independent mathematical checks

- **Lattice and parameter.** Write $`H=\mathbf1^\perp`$. For $`u\in H`$, the inner products with the projected standard basis vectors are its coordinates. Hence the reciprocal lattice of $`\pi_H\mathbb Z^N`$ is exactly $`\mathbb Z^N\cap H`$. The Gram matrix of the difference basis is the identity plus the all-ones matrix, of determinant $`N`$. With the Gaussian $`e^{-\alpha\lVert v-x\rVert^2}`$, Poisson therefore gives the prefactor $`\sqrt N(\pi/\alpha)^{(N-1)/2}`$ and the nome $`q=e^{-\pi^2/\alpha}`$. The shift sign has no effect because the reciprocal lattice is symmetric.
- **Global comparison.** The coefficient of frequency $`kN`$ in the product is $`q^{Nk^2}`$ times the constant coefficient, by subtracting $`k\mathbf1`$ from each integer frequency vector. Averaging across the $`N`$ cyclic shifts retains precisely these modes. The staircase product is already invariant. The translated products have equal logarithmic means. This makes the logarithm of the ratio of Gaussian potentials exactly the integral of the pointwise Jensen gap. The theta factors are strictly positive on the real circle, so that gap vanishes precisely when the product is invariant under the cyclic shift. Absolute convergence on bounded complex strips and positivity on a compact real period justify all integrations and real logarithms.
- **Independent equality check.** Write $`q=e^{-\pi a}`$ with $`a>0`$. On the horizontal complex line of height $`a/2`$, the triple product gives one simple zero of the single theta factor per real period, at real part $`1/2`$. The product therefore has zero multiset $`\{1/2-x_j\pmod1\}_j`$ on this line, with multiplicities. Periodicity on the real line extends by analyticity. Translation by $`1/N`$ has full orbit length $`N`$ on the circle. The invariant multiset of size $`N`$ is a single orbit, with multiplicity one. Projecting the resulting common phase shift and integer lifts onto $`H`$ gives exactly the permutations of the staircase modulo $`A_{N-1}^*`$. Conversely these points attain equality by lattice and permutation invariance. This agrees with the real Fourier/Newton proof in the manuscript.
- **Deep holes.** For a coordinate subset of size $`k`$, the projected indicator has squared norm $`k(N-k)/N`$. Every integer vector can be shifted by a constant and expressed as a sum of indicators of nested level sets. For $`S\subseteq T`$ the projected indicators have scalar product $`|S|(1-|T|/N)\ge0`$. The subset inequalities thus imply all the Voronoi inequalities. Ordering an arbitrary linear functional and summing by parts gives its maximum at the correspondingly ordered staircase. The resulting permutohedron has squared circumradius $`(N^2-1)/(12N)`$, and strict convexity gives exactly its vertices as farthest points.
- **Value and Hessian.** The staircase phases have all intermediate elementary symmetric coefficients zero and top coefficient one. Their product of the linear factors $`1+u e^{2\pi ih_j}`$ is therefore $`1+u^N`$ for either parity, giving exactly the stated product evaluation. For a real sum-zero tangent vector $`v`$, the second variation of the integral Jensen gap at the staircase is the integral of the cyclic variance of $`g(t)=\sum_jv_j(\log\theta_q)'(t+h_j)`$. The cyclic mean of $`g`$ vanishes. The logarithmic Fourier coefficient at every $`r\ge1`$ is $`(-1)^{r-1}q^r/[r(1-q^{2r})]`$. Differentiation and the contributions of positive and negative frequencies give the factor $`8\pi^2`$ in the manuscript. The discrete Fourier transform at $`1,\ldots,N-1`$ detects every nonzero vector of coordinate sum zero. This proves a positive-definite Hessian for each fixed scale. No uniform lower bound as the scale varies is needed.

## Reproducing the checks

Run the verifier from the repository root using standard Python 3.9 or newer.

```sh
python3 docs/research-results/dual-a-universal-cold-spots/verify.py
```

The first check uses integer arithmetic to compare the twisted root-lattice theta series through degree 30 with the staircase product for each $`N=2,\ldots,11`$. It enumerates integer coordinates subject to the norm bound, total sum and phase residue. Averaging over primitive characters evaluates each residue by an integer Ramanujan sum. A separate polynomial multiplication computes the Euler quotient coefficients. All 310 coefficient comparisons passed, including degree zero in each rank.

The second check sums Gaussian contributions over a finite box of projected-integer representatives and differentiates those same primal summands to obtain a directional log-Hessian. It compares the mass and Hessian with the Euler product and Fourier expression, and evaluates a displaced point. The nine checks use $`N=3,5,6`$ and $`\alpha=4,9,19`$, with tangent vector $`v=(1,-1,0,\ldots,0)`$. For $`N=5`$ and $`\alpha=9`$, the mass is approximately 0.1405642824, the directional log-Hessian is approximately 19.48089953, and the mass at $`h+0.12v`$ is approximately 1.023638789 times the minimum. These are finite, floating-point diagnostics. The all-rank inequality, equality characterization and positive Hessian follow from the proof.
