# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s individual configuration avoidance problem

## Consequences for Problems 9/A and 9/E, and a single matching for \(T_4\)

This note uses the existing [link-path partition result](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1047), rather than counting its direct consequences as independent discoveries. The [original questions](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=57) concern hosts whose **order equals their chromatic number**, not the unrestricted host in Erdős #593. Write \(G_3(\kappa)\) for the class of finite triple systems appearing in all \(\kappa\)-chromatic triple systems on \(\kappa\) vertices. Let \(T_4\) denote the suspension of the three-edge path, as drawn in the original paper.

### The two original questions

- **9/A:** The conjunction \(T_1,T_3,T_4,T_8,T_9\notin G_3(\aleph_1)\), with the omissions required **individually**, is independent relative to ZFC. CH implies all five omissions by Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal's Theorems 11.6 and 11.11. In Todorčević’s rainbow-partition model the transfer in [#1047](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1047) makes every \(T_4\)-free triple system on \(\omega_1\) countably colourable. In particular \(T_4\in G_3(\aleph_1)\) there. This gives the negative answer to the authors' literal question whether that conjunction is a theorem of ZFC. It does not depend on joint avoidance in Problem 9/C.
- **9/E:** Equality of \(G_3(\kappa)\) over all uncountable \(\kappa\) is not a theorem of ZFC. Start with GCH and force the rainbow-partition model by the set forcing used for [Todorčević’s theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0). Take an infinite cardinal \(\lambda\) in the ground model strictly above the size of this forcing. The forcing preserves \(\lambda,\lambda^+\), since it has the \((|\mathbb P|^+)\)-chain condition. The usual counting of names for subsets of \(\lambda\) gives at most \((2^{|\mathbb P|})^\lambda=2^\lambda=\lambda^+\) such names, so \(2^\lambda=\lambda^+\) still holds in the extension. By EGH Theorem 11.6 there is a \(\lambda^+\)-chromatic \(T_4\)-free system on exactly \(\lambda^+\) vertices. Thus \(T_4\in G_3(\aleph_1)\setminus G_3(\lambda^+)\) in that same model. This does not assert a positive consistency result for the universal equality, and does not decide the complete class \(G_3(\kappa)\).

### A finer structural form of the \(T_4\) obstruction

On \(\omega_1\), every \(T_4\)-free triple system \(H\) is the union of a countably colourable subfamily and a subfamily encodable by **one** two-bounded colouring of the pairs. The published finite-shadow proof instead directly encodes the whole system by three matchings of pairs; this proof singles out the obstruction to a single matching.

Each vertex link is a graph with no ordinary three-edge path. Every nontrivial connected component of such a link is a star or a triangle: if it contains a triangle, any further attachment makes the path; if not, a connected component containing two adjacent non-leaves would either contain a triangle or such a path. The triangle components in links correspond to three-edge tetrahedral fans, \(xab,xac,xbc\). If the fourth triple \(abc\) is present, the four triples form a full tetrahedron, and each of its six constituent pairs has codegree exactly two in the entire \(H\). For example, in the link at \(x\), the component on \(a,b,c\) is a triangle and admits no extra adjacency; the other links give the same conclusion for the three base pairs.

Let \(H_T\) consist of all triples in full tetrahedra. Each of its pairs has codegree at most two. More generally any triple system on \(\omega_1\) with countable pair codegrees is countably colourable: build a continuous chain of countable subsets covering \(\omega_1\), closed under adding every page of every pair in the subset. Such closure remains countable, by iterating \(\omega\) times. Colour each successive countable block injectively with colours in \(\omega\). A monochromatic triple uses distinct blocks; its top-block vertex must already belong to the earlier closed prefix since the other two are there, a contradiction.

For every fan \(\{xab,xac,xbc\}\) missing its fourth triple \(abc\), its three spokes \(xa,xb,xc\) are pairs of codegree exactly two. Its base pairs \(ab,ac,bc\) are private. Indeed in the link at \(a\), the vertices \(b,c\) are both attached to \(x\) and not to each other. They must be leaves of a star centred at \(x\); this gives privacy of \(ab,ac\), and the same reasoning gives privacy of \(bc\). Assign the pairs in the following collision matching:
\[
  xa\leftrightarrow ab,\qquad xc\leftrightarrow ac,\qquad xb\leftrightarrow bc.
\]
Every one of the fan's triples has the specified two pairs made equal. Distinct incomplete fans have disjoint sets of used pairs. A spoke \(xy\) could participate in a second spoke group only in the link at \(y\) (its only other endpoint); making that a triangle would supply the missing fourth triple. A private base pair belongs to exactly one triple, and that triple has exactly two repeated pairs whose common endpoint uniquely determines the fan centre. Thus no other fan can use it.

Every triple outside full tetrahedra and incomplete fans has at least two private pairs. Otherwise some two repeated pairs meet at a vertex \(x\), giving an edge of the link at \(x\) whose endpoints each have degree at least two, hence a triangle component: that is a fan. Match two private pairs for each of those remaining triples. They cannot overlap another such choice or any fan selection, by privacy. Giving each matched pair of pairs its own colour and every unused pair a fresh colour is one two-bounded colouring. Every triple outside \(H_T\) is non-rainbow for it. In the rainbow-partition model, refine its countable rainbow partition by the ZFC colouring of \(H_T\). The result is a countable proper colouring of all of \(H\).

A full tetrahedron cannot be encoded by a single two-bounded colouring making all its triples non-rainbow: its six pairs allow at most three collisions, and two distinct pairs can witness a collision in at most one triple. This explains the exceptional subfamily in the single-matching theorem. The original 9/A and 9/E deductions need only the already published #1047 transfer.
