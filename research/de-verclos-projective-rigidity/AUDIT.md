# Proof, scope and prior-art review

[Written proof](PROOF.md). The argument was independently checked, including the transverse interpolation and both pinned-angular transfers. This is not external refereeing or Lean verification.

## Conventions and external dependencies

- [Conjecture 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.02236v1#Thmtheorem5) imposes a dimension threshold on the **support of one** line-null measure. The definition of `ℓ_μ` normalizes iid sampling; arbitrary total masses cannot be recovered. The order type is the orbit of the labeled **oriented** chirotope under relabeling, not under independent global sign reversal. Corollary 8 implies any realization of the same law is line-null. Exchangeability upgrades all equal unlabeled laws to equal labeled finite laws, hence equal infinite array laws. Theorem 4 explicitly states compact supports with nonempty interiors in its body; we do not use its hypotheses or assert it already implies the conjecture. §3.1's spherical transforms are orientation-preserving in the affine chart; ordinary projectivities crossing a pole line need not be.
- Real projectivity means an automorphism of `RP²`. Embedded measures are supported on the affine chart as measures, and a pole line always has zero measure. Orientation (not just projectivity) follows almost everywhere from the constructed relation. Requiring a finite image for **every** support point would change the problem and be false with unbounded target: take uniform μ on `[0,1]²`, apply `P(x,y)=(-1/x,y/x)` for `x>0`, and take ν as the pushforward. All order-type laws agree since the determinant and positive denominator preserve signs there, but ν is unbounded, whereas any projectivity finite at every point of the compact source support would have bounded target support.
- The Frostman witness is taken on the support, independent of both input probabilities. For example, a countable weighted mixture of normalized length measures of small circles dense in a square is line-null and carried by a set of dimension 1, but its closed support has dimension 2. At no point do we transfer absolute continuity or dimensional lower bounds from μ to ν or to a typical iid cloud.
- The [originating author's 2018 thesis, §§1.7 and 1.11](https://remi-de-verclos.github.io/manuscript.pdf#page=64) makes the distinction explicit. Theorem 1.37's dimension is `inf{dim_H A : μ(A)>0}`, and it applies the bound on **both** measures to ensure differentiability of a correspondence and its inverse on conull sets and then invertibility of the differential. Conjecture 1.38 on p. 71 asks precisely the one-sided support version used here. That prior method explains why mere differentiability is insufficient and deserves credit for the pencil differentiation strategy; the new proof does not invoke its Theorem 1.37 or require anything about the target dimension. The thesis has Cantor-dust digit-map Questions 1.1–1.2 and Proposition 1.33; the finite test here only concerns the threshold example as a counterexample to a three-direction intermediate claim.
- The only nonstandard external theorem in the new argument is [Orponen's Theorem 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.11053v3#S1.Thmtheorem11) (finite `s`-energy, outside the support, exceptional dimension at most `2−s` in the plane). Applying to restrictions rather than applying the theorem on the center's own support is essential. For each Borel angular null set, its pinned inverse image intersects each separated rational ball in σ-measure zero, hence their countable union and the center (atomless) are σ-null.
- [Goaoc–Padrol 2025, §1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14189v1#S1.SS2) is related prior art but treats unrestricted finite chirotope extensions; its finite extension points need not occur in a thin given support. Semantic Scholar citations of arXiv:1811.02236, the arXiv title query and bounded Crossref searches did not locate an exact published resolution. The published repository package and issue #853 are explicitly partial. This is not a comprehensive novelty review.

## Logical stress tests

1. **Weak signs and a multivalued graph.** Weak sign products suffice. A contrary strict sign inequality is open; zero source signs need reasoning from both sides separately. Neither projection of the closed relation is asserted to be an affine graph a priori. Upper-hemisphere representatives are retained until signs are no longer being used. For the selector's line-preimage bound, exclude antipodal lifts by arcs of length less than π; do not identify their signs in an orientation comparison. Two same-side target pairs are obtained with distinct source points using the nonatomic marginal, not by assuming an inverse selector.
2. **Two kinds of slice quantifier.** The angular/offset exceptional set alone is not sufficient to conclude that an actual pair of points lies in the good subset of its slice. Section 2 instead establishes full measure for `(anchor, angle)` under `σ×angular length`, with membership in `supp σ_{θ,t}` included. Section 4 transfers this separately for *both* endpoints via conditional radial absolute continuity. The same canonical `(θ,t)` and disintegration are used for both endpoints. No assertion that a fixed Lebesgue-generic line contains a prescribed σ-point is made. The strip estimate integrates over t before the angle bound: factor `≤(2δ)⁻¹ min(1,Cδ/|x−y|)≤C'/|x−y|`; with the `(s−1)` kernel this is integrable. Nonatomicity then gives arbitrarily close distinct same-slice anchors even if the slice has zero length.
3. **Transverse richness is not area density.** Good directions give AC for the projection of all σ and therefore every restriction. The density-one property of the positive-density set is verified for each ball containing the endpoint, not for area on the plane. For a finite list of desired height intervals of length proportional to r, the error is o(r) in each fixed O(r) window; a sequence r varying with the finite requests and shrinking balls suffices. Excluding the preimage of the *specific* target line is legitimate since σ, not the conditional slice probability, is used to source the off-line perturbations. No equidistribution of source heights, simultaneous bound over all rational heights before taking the diagonal, or target projection regularity is presumed.
4. **Transverse normalization.** The chosen image height at level 1 is strictly positive by the anchor signs and the target-line exclusion, however fast it tends to zero. At each finite stage all inequalities are homogeneous in image heights and may be divided by it. The two endpoint comparisons at positive heights and the two comparisons at `−d` prohibit infinity in the limiting functions. Comparisons within one cluster use a fixed *other* anchor, so only a fixed nonzero limiting horizontal offset is needed, not any rate relating horizontal convergence to r or to the target normalization. After division by the product of offsets, the determinant has the sign of the difference of the two ratios; with absolute offsets the ratio at a greater input height is ordered above the other. The negative range adjacent to 0 is indispensable: it rules out a collapsed positive-height limit after normalization, by continuity at 0.
5. **No assumed target differentiability in the blowup.** The weak order brackets imply the left/right interpolation identities for real arguments by rational sequences taken from the same side in *both* endpoint variables. An endpoint jump then produces uncountably many middle jumps, not just countably many rational ones. This supplies continuity and (4) before using any Cauchy equation. Normalization uses 0 and 1 strictly inside the domain. The elementary increment comparison yields affine **height** profiles; the varying slopes at abscissae are exactly what permits a nonaffine fractional-linear restriction on the original line. Equation (5) has `m_1/m_m`, not its inverse.
6. **Plane step and avoiding rank zero.** Monotone coordinate functions may be singular, so AC of source marginal by itself gives only finite derivatives, not positive derivatives. Nonzero slopes come from generic pencil slices with one anchor now at infinity: those slice projectivities are affine bijections in the new chart, and an actual same-slice sequence supplies difference quotients. The chart patch is relatively open, so a sequence converging to a point of the patch is eventually in it. For the derivative at x, normalized differences along the secant are nonzero in the limit because both derivatives are nonzero. Symmetry of the a.e. pair property then makes the diagonal ratio constant. The final plane map is a homothety after a potentially signed vertical scaling; no unrestricted sign preservation is asserted after the changes of charts. The prior strict-secant propagation handles both signs of pole lifts.

## Exact finite checks

Run `uv run --no-project --python 3.12 --offline python check_geometry.py` in this directory (uses only the standard library). These checks concern determinant/projective algebra and a counterexample to a proposed weaker lemma, not infinitary hypotheses or proof certification. The three-direction counterexample preserves each of the horizontal, vertical and unit-sum pencil relations exactly by no-carry base conversion, but its all-triples signs do not agree (the checker exhibits one witness). Thus the weak lemma is false, not the original conjecture.

Replay passed: 2,556 unordered off-axis pairs for the secant determinant; 110,160 triples of heights and ordered abscissae over the specified integer projective matrix range for the cross-ratio/profile identity and the sign. Depth three did not find a strict digit-map sign disagreement; depth four gives one on `((0,8/343),(36/343,268/2401),(758/2401,2/7))`, with exact source and target determinants `−1936/5764801` and `27/65536`. This tests the chosen pointwise map only, not equality or inequality of all finite laws for arbitrary choices of coupling.

## Independent replay and review

The determinant checks replayed with `python3 -B check_geometry.py`. In the analytic review, the left- and right-limit identities were reconstructed by bracketing the interpolated argument by two rational heights on the required side. A jump at either endpoint would then force uncountably many jumps at the middle anchor. The independent-secant step was checked by applying Orponen to restrictions with separated pinned centers, not on their own support. The final projective comparison tracks denominator signs on an auxiliary-positive patch and permits a null affine pole line.

A fresh read of issue #853 and merged PR #863 found only the positive-area and parallel-segment results. The cited finite-extension rigidity theorem and the thesis's two-sided lower-measure-dimension theorem do not settle this support-only formulation. Crossref title and author queries found no exact prior resolution. This bounded audit is not an absolute priority claim.

## Independent mathematical review, 27 November 2026 UTC

A separate Codex reviewer in the WadeDavis session audited Adam Benazouz’s [submitted proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/960/commits/630c2bee83d9b3a31f7608993378a50bcb33b55f). The SHA-256 of `PROOF.md` is `3b1c159be44d9b491325dc100b349a641f526a7accc11ecd28046bac09f59404`. The full implication holds for a line-null planar probability whose topological support has Hausdorff dimension greater than one. The sampling law on that support may be singular. Earlier secant, coupling and incidence arguments are due to Cinna Davis. [Conjecture 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1811.02236v1#Thmtheorem5) · [Original thesis, Conjecture 1.38](https://remi-de-verclos.github.io/manuscript.pdf#page=71)

1. On a good slice, for each of finitely many distinct anchors, positive projected density in every small neighborhood on a set of normal coordinates of density one gives the same sequence of transverse scales for countably many prescribed heights. At each stage only finitely many requests are imposed. The perturbations may avoid the null preimage of the target line. This gives the strict sign of the normalizing height even if its magnitude is arbitrarily small.
2. For two test points at the same anchor, comparison with a fixed different anchor compares their target heights divided by their horizontal offsets from the fixed anchor. Within each cluster those offsets tend to the same nonzero constant. Hence the normalized bounds and monotonicity require no comparison between rates of horizontal and vertical convergence. Both endpoint clusters are bounded using the interior observation at height one and one endpoint of zero height, followed by the comparisons with one negative height. This gives a finite diagonal limit on every rational height in the stated open interval.
3. Take rational endpoint inputs tending to real inputs from below. Bracket each interpolated input by two rational middle heights still below its limit. The weak inequalities give the equality of left limits. Approaching from above gives the equality of right limits. Subtracting these identities shows that any jump of one endpoint profile forces a jump at every point of an interval of the middle profile. Monotonicity excludes this. The resulting continuous Pexider identity has affine profiles, since opposite endpoint shifts keeping the middle argument fixed force constant increments. Height zero fixes each intercept, height one fixes a nonzero slope, and the two coefficient identities give precisely the factor in equation (5). Every four anchors therefore preserve cross-ratio.
4. Orponen’s Theorem 1.11 applies to compactly supported restrictions of the auxiliary Frostman measure to rational balls, using only centers outside each closed ball. The restrictions retain finite energy at an exponent strictly between one and the Frostman exponent. Their exceptional center sets have dimension at most two minus the energy exponent, and thus have zero Frostman mass. Countably many such restrictions cover every point different from each good center. Fubini transfers the slice conclusions to both anchors of an independent pair, including membership in the common conditional support. [Orponen, Theorem 1.11](https://arxiv.org/html/1710.11053v3#S1.Thmtheorem11)
5. Moving the two pinned anchors to infinity makes their good slice projectivities affine, with nonzero slopes. Their accumulation sequences give those same slopes for the finite one-variable derivatives on a positive-mass coordinate patch. A typical secant then compares the ratio of the two derivatives at both endpoints. This ratio is constant almost everywhere, forcing an affine homothety after a coordinate rescaling. The positive Frostman mass of the agreement patch makes its dimension greater than one. The strict secant argument therefore extends agreement to the full orientation coupling, including the possible null pole line. This establishes projective equality of the probability measures under the convention of the primary conjecture.
