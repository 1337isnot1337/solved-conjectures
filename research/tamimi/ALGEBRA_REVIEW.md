# Tamimi's conjecture: independent algebra review

Review date: 21 November 2026.

The integral proof in `PROOF.md` passes for the graph defined there, for every $`r,n\ge3`$. No parity restriction, coprimality assumption, or inference from the spanning-tree count is needed. Primary-source identification and the prior-status audit are separate gates; this review does not certify them.

## Graph presentation

Each private vertex belongs to one clique and has degree $`n-1`$. Each cycle vertex belongs to two cliques and has degree $`2(n-1)`$. This remains true for $`r=3`$: the two neighboring cycle vertices are distinct and no clique edge is counted twice. With the clique sum $`S_i`$, the full Laplacian relations are exactly

```math
n w_{i,j}-S_i=0,\qquad
2n v_i-S_{i-1}-S_i=0.
```

There are $`r(n-1)`$ vertices. The Laplacian is symmetric, so the row-versus-column convention for the cokernel presentation does not change the group.

## Integral changes

The changes from $`w_{i,j}`$ to $`a_i=w_{i,1}`$ and $`d_{i,j}=w_{i,j}-a_i`$, and then from $`a_i`$ to $`b_i=a_i-v_i`$, have explicit integral inverses. Subtraction of the distinguished private-vertex relation gives $`nd_{i,j}=0`$. The remaining private relation gives

```math
v_{i+1}-v_i=2b_i-D_i.
```

Subtracting both adjoining private relations from a cycle-vertex relation gives $`n(2v_i-a_{i-1}-a_i)=0`$. Substituting the preceding difference relation changes its left side to $`n(b_{i-1}-b_i-D_{i-1})`$. Since $`nD_{i-1}`$ is the sum of existing $`nd`$ relations, replacing this row by $`n(b_i-b_{i-1})=0`$ is reversible over the integers.

The first $`r-1`$ cycle-difference rows eliminate $`v_1,\ldots,v_{r-1}`$ with coefficient one. Their sum leaves the single closing relation

```math
2\sum_i b_i-\sum_i D_i=0.
```

After setting $`b=b_0`$ and $`c_i=b_i-b_0`$, the cyclic difference relation lattice equals the lattice generated by $`nc_i`$. The omitted cyclic row is their telescoping dependence; it carries no additional torsion. The free generator $`v_0`$ occurs in no remaining row.

Thus the finite part has $`r(n-2)`$ generators: one $`b`$, $`r-1`$ generators $`c_i`$, and $`r(n-3)`$ generators $`d_{i,j}`$. All but $`b`$ have their separate order-$`n`$ relations, together with

```math
2r b+2\sum_i c_i-\sum_{i,j}d_{i,j}=0.
```

## Smith factors and the exceptional presentation

For $`n\ge4`$, choose any $`d`$. Its coefficient in the last row is $`-1`$, so it can be eliminated integrally, without dividing by $`2`$, $`r`$, or $`n`$. Its old order-$`n`$ relation then becomes $`2rn b=0`$ after subtracting the other torsion rows. Exactly $`r(n-2)-2`$ independent order-$`n`$ generators remain.

For $`n=3`$, no $`d`$ exists. Replacing one $`c_i`$ by $`c=\sum_i c_i`$ is unimodular. In the two-generator block, the rows are $`3c=0`$ and $`2r b+2c=0`$. Subtracting the former from the latter gives $`2r b-c=0`$. Eliminating $`c`$ leaves $`6r b=0`$. The other $`r-2`$ order-three generators remain independent. This explicitly covers the cases where $`r`$ is even or divisible by three.

The final invariant factors are therefore

```math
(\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z)^{r(n-2)-2}
\oplus \mathbb Z/(2rn)\mathbb Z,
```

beside one free $`\mathbb Z`$ summand. Since $`n`$ divides $`2rn`$, these are already in invariant-factor order. The finite group is the torsion subgroup of the full Laplacian cokernel, as required.
