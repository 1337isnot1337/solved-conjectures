# Woodall's conjecture

**Partial research.** In the standard sink-regular reduction, pieces separated by a mixed two-edge cut can be solved independently and their full packings can be glued. Thus the search for a counterexample can be restricted to a class with 3-edge-connected underlying multigraph. This applies in the planar setting as well. A cubic example also identifies a connectivity hypothesis missing from a published sufficient-condition conjecture. The unrestricted problem remains open. [Issue #329](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/329)

## Exact target and literature

For a finite digraph $D$, a dicut is the set $\delta^+(W)$ of arcs leaving a nonempty proper set $W$ with no arc entering $W$. A dijoin meets every dicut. Woodall's conjecture asserts that if every dicut has at least $\tau$ arcs, then $D$ has $\tau$ pairwise arc-disjoint dijoins. For a graph with a dicut, this gives a min–max equality, since a smallest dicut can meet at most as many disjoint dijoins as it has arcs. This is the unweighted counterpart to the Lucchesi–Younger packing theorem, which exchanges the roles of dicuts and dijoins. [Woodall (1978)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BFb0070416) · [Abdi–Cornuéjols–Zlatin (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1137/22M1506511)

The source check on November 24, 2026 included current work on cube-ideal sets, weighted chordal graphs, and approximation via flows. The earlier weighted counterexample of Schrijver requires arcs of weight zero, and the chordal result and approximate packings do not resolve the unrestricted unweighted statement. Searches for mixed two-edge cuts and sink-regular gluing did not locate the reduction below. That search is bounded and priority is unestablished. [Abdi–Cornuéjols–Dadush–Dalirrooyfard (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms.70199) · [Liu–Ravi (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.70076) · [Cornuéjols–Liu–Ravi (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00493-025-00159-x)

## 1. Splitting and gluing across a mixed two-edge cut

**Proposition.** Let $D$ be an unweighted bipartite digraph whose arcs all go from sources to sinks. Suppose every sink has degree $\tau\ge3$, and every dicut has at least $\tau$ arcs. Suppose $U,V$ partition its vertices into nonempty shores joined by exactly two arcs. Then the arcs have the form $a\to b$ and $c\to d$ with $a,d\in U$ and $b,c\in V$. Form $D_U$ from $D[U]$ by adding one arc $a\to d$, and form $D_V$ from $D[V]$ by adding one arc $c\to b$. Each piece has the same degree at each retained vertex and every dicut has at least $\tau$ arcs. If each piece has $\tau$ disjoint dijoins, so does $D$.

Parallel arcs are treated as distinct. In particular, if a replacement arc is parallel to an existing arc, the two both contribute to degrees and cuts.

**Cuts in the pieces.** If the two original crossing arcs pointed the same way, they would form a dicut of size two. Thus they point in opposite directions. In the notation of the proposition, $a,c$ are sources and $b,d$ are sinks. The replacement arc in each piece restores the degree of each of its two ports.

Let $P$ be a nonempty proper in-shore in $D_U$, meaning no arc of $D_U$ enters $P$. If $a,d$ are both in $P$, extend it to $P\cup V$ in $D$. Otherwise keep $P$ as the in-shore in $D$. Indeed, if $a,d$ are both out there is no crossing through the replacement, and if $a$ is in and $d$ is out then the replacement arc leaving $P$ is replaced by $a\to b$. The case $a$ out and $d$ in is excluded by the local in-shore condition. The extension is a nonempty proper in-shore in $D$ whose dicut has the same size. Apply the symmetric argument to $D_V$.

**Gluing colors.** In a packing of $\tau$ dijoins in either piece, the $\tau$ arcs incident to each sink form a dicut. Every dijoin uses exactly one of these arcs, and together the dijoins use them all. Thus the packing colors every arc once. Permute the colors in the second piece so the two replacement arcs have a common color $r$. Keep the internal arc colors and assign color $r$ to both original crossing arcs.

Suppose for a contradiction that a proper nonempty in-shore $W$ of $D$ has no outgoing arc of color $i$. Write $P=W\cap U$ and $Q=W\cap V$. At least one restriction is nonempty and proper in its shore, since neither $U$ nor $V$ is an in-shore in $D$. No internal arc enters either restriction, and no internal arc of color $i$ leaves it.

- If $i\ne r$, consider a proper nonempty restriction, say $P$. Unless the new arc $a\to d$ enters $P$, the restriction is already an in-shore in $D_U$ with no outgoing arc of color $i$, which contradicts the local packing. If the new arc enters $P$, then $a\notin W$ and $d\in W$. The original in-shore condition implies $b\notin W$ and $c\in W$. Consequently $Q$ is nonempty and proper, and $c\to b$ leaves it. Thus $Q$ is an in-shore in $D_V$ with no outgoing arc of color $i$, again a contradiction. The case in which the chosen restriction is $Q$ is symmetric.
- If $i=r$, neither original crossing arc enters or leaves $W$. Thus $a,b$ have the same membership in $W$, and $c,d$ have the same membership in $W$. If all four agree, either proper nonempty restriction gives a missing-color dicut in its piece because the replacement arcs do not cross. Otherwise exactly one of the new arcs enters its restriction. Suppose this is $a\to d$. Remove $d$ from $P$. This creates no entering arc, since $d$ is a sink, and no newly outgoing arc of color $r$, since the only incident local arc of color $r$ at $d$ is $a\to d$, with $a$ outside. The new shore is proper because $a$ is outside. It is nonempty because $d$ has at least one original in-neighbor in $U$, and every such neighbor belongs to $P$ since $d\in W$. This again gives a missing-color dicut in $D_U$. The other case is symmetric.

All colors are therefore dijoins of $D$.

**Reduction to 3-edge connectivity.** The minimum dicut condition makes the underlying graph connected and bridgeless. Removing a two-edge cut therefore creates exactly two connected components. Each piece is strictly smaller in vertex count, lies in the same sink-regular $(\tau,\tau+1)$-bipartite class if $D$ does, and has no smaller dicut. If $D$ is planar, each replacement can be embedded by following the two crossing edges and a path through the opposite connected shore, then discarding that shore. Iteration shows that a counterexample of minimum order within the sink-regular class, planar or unrestricted, must be 3-edge-connected as an undirected multigraph.

Apply the unweighted reduction of Abdi, Cornuéjols and Zlatin to reduce the general problem to sink-regular $(\tau,\tau+1)$-bipartite instances. Their Theorem 2.9(2) applies for each discrepancy bound; for the unrestricted assertion the bound can be taken large enough for each input. Together with the proposition this gives a further reduction to 3-edge-connected instances, preserving planarity when the input is planar. The proposition makes no claim that a packing of the whole graph always projects to the two specified pieces. [Abdi–Cornuéjols–Zlatin, Definition 1.5 and Theorem 2.9](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/gc0v/webpub/dijoins-SIDMA.pdf)

## 2. The connected-complement route requires more than the dicut bound

Abdi, Cornuéjols and Zlatin's Conjecture 8.4 predicts that in a planar sink-regular $(3,4)$-bipartite digraph, a prescribed first base in a partition of the active vertices into three discrepancy-matroid bases can be realized by a rounded 1-factor with connected complement. In the cubic case there are no active vertices, all three bases are empty, and a rounded 1-factor is a perfect matching. The complement is connected exactly when it is a Hamiltonian cycle. Non-Hamiltonian 2-connected cubic bipartite planar graphs were already known through the work of Akiyama, Nishizeki and Saito. So the cubic special case supplies a prior obstruction to the literal stronger statement. Below is a direct example with its minimum dicut checked. [Abdi–Cornuéjols–Zlatin, Conjecture 8.4](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/gc0v/webpub/dijoins-SIDMA.pdf) · [Akiyama–Nishizeki–Saito (1980)](https://www.ecei.tohoku.ac.jp/alg/nishizeki/sub/j/DVD/PDF_J/J029.pdf)

Start with a cube graph and select one vertex $v$. For each of its three incident edges $vu$, take a new cube, delete one of its edges $xy$, and replace $vu$ by this cube-minus-edge with attachments $v x$ and $u y$. Choose $x$ in the opposite bipartition class from $v$, and hence $y$ opposite to $u$. The endpoints of a deleted plane edge are on a common face, so the attached cube can be drawn in a neighborhood of the edge it replaces. The result is simple, connected, planar, cubic, and bipartite. Each attached cube has precisely two edges to the rest. A spanning cycle would cross each of these cuts exactly twice. The three attachment edges at $v$ would then all belong to the cycle, contrary to degree two.

Orient from sources to sinks. For any proper nonempty in-shore $W$, its dicut has size $3(|W\cap S|-|W\cap T|)$ by summing signed degrees, where $S$ and $T$ are the two parts. Connectedness makes it positive, hence at least three. A singleton source has a dicut of size three. All hypotheses of the printed conjecture therefore hold, and no perfect matching has connected complement. An exact checker in this directory verifies the construction and enumerates its perfect matchings.

The graph still has three disjoint dijoins. Any cubic bipartite graph decomposes into three perfect matchings, and each matching uses exactly $|W\cap S|-|W\cap T|$ edges of every dicut by summing its signed degrees. The earlier 2-connectivity obstruction and the positive separator reduction are compatible. After removing the two-edge cuts in the packing problem, the recent proof of Barnette's conjecture gives connected complements in the simple cubic 3-connected planar subclass. The remaining difficulty of that route involves degree-four sources and realizing the required discrepancy bases. [OpenAI, Barnette proof, Theorem 1.1](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/preprints/Paired-states-and-Hamiltonian-cycles-in-cubic-bipartite-planar-graphs-September-24-2026/paper.pdf)
