# High-rank Gaussian cold spots of the root lattices of type A

Independent priority and scope screen, 27 November 2026 UTC. This records a bounded search and the public state at the commits below.

## Question and outcome

Write $`N=n+1`$, $`H=\mathbf1^\perp\subset\mathbb R^N`$, $`A_n=\mathbb Z^N\cap H`$ with the ambient Euclidean norm, and $`P_{\alpha,n}(x)=\sum_{a\in A_n}e^{-\alpha\|a-x\|^2}`$. The target is to identify the global minimizers on the entire torus $`H/A_n`$, for every $`\alpha`$ in a prescribed fixed compact subset of $`(0,\infty)`$ and every $`n`$ beyond a threshold depending only on that subset. We also searched for the weaker uniform asymptotic for the minimum value and for estimates close enough to compare the adjacent central cosets. The roots have squared length two. The heat kernel convention $`e^{-\|a-x\|^2/(4t)}`$ corresponds to $`\alpha=1/(4t)`$.

The bounded search found no checked source stating the high-rank exact optimizer theorem or establishing the corresponding sharp asymptotic for the global minimum over arbitrary translates. There is a clear antecedent conjectural question and there are known cases, techniques and ingredients. The compact-interval result was the first step toward the all-positive-scale statement in sufficiently high rank proved below. The elementary central-coset coefficient formula and scalar local limit asymptotic at a chosen coset alone merit no independent priority claim. [BMVZ, Theorem 1.3 and §§1.5–1.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08819v2#S1.SS5) · [published version](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2026.10169)

A second search for a rank-independent Gaussian stability threshold, including the terms “uniform threshold”, “root lattice type A”, “stable cold spots” and “large Gaussian parameter”, returned the fixed-lattice BMVZ result. Its proof chooses thresholds for each lattice and proves strict local uniqueness and separation away from the holes. The new half-line argument supplies one rank threshold for **all** $`\alpha\ge a`$, for each fixed positive $`a`$. With the fixed-rank theorem it gives one steep-scale cutoff across every rank. The search identified no prior source for either simultaneous conclusion. Bobkov, Marsiglietti and Melbourne already prove the discrete log-concavity closure used in this extension. [BMVZ, §§1.5–2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08819v2#S1.SS5) · [Bobkov–Marsiglietti–Melbourne, Corollary 4.3](https://doi.org/10.1017/S096354832100016X) · [Half-line theorem](HALF-LINE.md)

The final scope check searched for “universal Gaussian cold spots” with “sufficiently high rank”, “type A”, “root lattice”, dimension-independent small-parameter thresholds, and all-positive-scale minima of translated theta series on the flat determinant-one torus. The fixed-rank BMVZ proof addresses large parameters. Baernstein treats the triangular lattice. Kaiser’s trace-domain result controls the leading reciprocal shell at fixed rank. The Faulhuber honeycomb and planar polarization work studies two-dimensional configurations and shape optimization. The all-rank dual theorem concerns the projected integer lattice. None of the checked primary results supplies simultaneous control on the higher reciprocal shells and all ambient translates for every positive scale in unboundedly many primal ranks. The new proof leaves finitely many ranks at intermediate scales open. The bounds are existential. [BMVZ](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2026.10169) · [Kaiser](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0609082) · [Faulhuber](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00013-026-02300-8) · [Dual type-A theorem](../lattice-cold-spot-stability/dual-a-universal/PROOF.md) · [Small-scale proof](SMALL-SCALE.md)

## Definitions and antecedents

- BMVZ prove stability of the deep holes of every root lattice. For each fixed lattice the theorem supplies sufficiently large Gaussian parameters. Their §1.6 asks whether stable examples have universal cold spots and asks separately about stability for certain dual lattices. Appendix A.1 gives the root-lattice coordinates. For a central class indexed by $`k`$, every coordinate has common residue $`k/N`$ (up to the displayed sign convention) and its nearest representative has squared norm $`k(N-k)/N`$. For even $`N`$ there is one deepest class, and for odd $`N`$ there are two classes exchanged by negation. Because coordinate permutations preserve this residue, the two are distinct even under coordinate permutations and root translations. The one-orbit wording there is correct for the full isometry group. The last arXiv revision is v2, 11 February 2026. The publication is volume 14, e25. [Open questions](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08819v2#S1.SS6) · [Appendix A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.08819v2#A1.SS1) · [version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08819)
- The integer section gives the primal family. Its dual is the orthogonal projection $`\pi_H\mathbb Z^N`$. In ranks at least three these are geometrically different families. The already published all-rank dual theorem selects an equally spaced set of coordinate phases. On the primal torus the deepest classes have a single coordinate phase. The rank-one circle and Baernstein’s equilateral torus in rank two have classical all-scale solutions. In rank three $`A_3`$ is FCC, already treated in this repository together with the checkerboard lattices and their duals. [Baernstein](https://doi.org/10.1090/conm/201/02604) · [checkerboard proof and prior audit](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/tree/6c7cb524475d720742bdf41804b7b8927460702b/results/betermin-fcc-bcc-cold-spots) · [dual-type-A proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/6c7cb524475d720742bdf41804b7b8927460702b/docs/research-results/dual-a-universal-cold-spots/PROOF.md)
- The coefficient formula $`P_{\alpha,n}(c_k)=e^{\alpha k^2/N}[z^k](\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\alpha j^2}z^j)^N`$ follows by expanding the product and completing the square on $`\sum m_i=k`$. Centering the single-site law at $`1/2`$ gives a local limit estimate for each of the central cosets. Determining a sharp global value still requires controlling all phase tuples. The strict ordering of adjacent centered levels also involves a relative correction of order $`1/N`$. Conway and Sloane give the classical root and weight lattice geometry and theta-series context in Chapter 4 §6. [Conway–Sloane](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4757-2016-7) · [Petrov’s local expansions for independent lattice variables](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/tvp4732)
- Bétermin–Petrache’s Theorem 1.5 supplies the steep-Gaussian comparison for each fixed competing translate on a fixed lattice. Folkerts’ independently deposited November 2026 manuscript, Theorem 1.1, identifies universal cold spots for $`E_6,E_8,E_7^*,BW_{16},D_n^+`$ for even $`n\ge8`$. Proposition 6.1 imposes matching theta masses on the complementary projection fibers and is applied to $`E_7^*`$. It supplies no arbitrary-translate result for the integer section defining $`A_n`$. Both the paper and accompanying Lean sources were present in the author’s public repository at the pinned revision. [Bétermin–Petrache](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.4995401) · [Folkerts paper and Lean project](https://github.com/caiusdfolkerts/research/tree/487611b97e9c0b59ee583b451e92130a789b2caa/cold_spots)
