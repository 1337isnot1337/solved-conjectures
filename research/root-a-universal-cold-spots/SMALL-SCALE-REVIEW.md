## Suppression of the other dual shells

Let $`a=\pi^2/\alpha`$ and $`q=e^{-a}`$. I independently bounded the tail for $`N\le e^{a/100}`$, which includes $`N\le a^8`$ when $`a`$ is sufficiently large. Use all integer representatives with $`|\sum m_i|\le N/2`$, assigning half weight to boundary duplicates. The phase uses $`m`$ itself in the angular extension. This makes the all-minus point stationary in the extension in both parities and preserves the physical function on determinant-one tuples.

Writing $`Q=\sum m_i^2`$, $`D=\|\pi_H m\|^2`$, one has $`D\ge Q/2`$ and $`D\ge Q-Q^2/N`$. For $`N\ge16`$ and $`Q\ge2`$ these imply $`D-(1-1/N)\ge1/2+Q/8`$. Check $`Q=2,3`$ by the second inequality and $`Q\ge4`$ by the first. Consequently the noninitial shells, divided by the first-shell weight, have Hessian operator norm at most
```math
C e^{-a/2}\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-a j^2/16}\right)^N=O(e^{-a/2}).
```
Here the Hessian multiplier is at most $`Q`$, absorbed into $`e^{aQ/16}`$. The manuscript's alternative word-length bound also suffices. Integrating the Hessian error from all-minus and using $`1-\cos t\ge2t^2/\pi^2`$ localizes every constrained minimum in a ball of radius $`C/\sqrt N`$. Strict convexity holds there. A swap segment stays in the determinant-one constraint. The scalar restriction of the extension is even and strictly convex, selecting the nearest one or two permitted phases.

## The localized integral when the rank is large

I checked the alternative range $`N\ge a^8`$ directly in the normalized Fourier variable $`t\in[-1/2,1/2]`$ of the phase-integral proof.

1. Poisson summation gives the stated Gaussian factor, the phase $`e^{2\pi i t y}`$ and the normalized theta factor. On $`|t|\le1/a`$, bounds for four $`t`$-derivatives of its logarithm follow from the Gaussian series with factors $`e^{2|m|}`$. The first derivative of the logarithmic modulus at zero vanishes. On $`1/a\le|t|\le1/4`$ the error $`O(e^{-a/2})`$ is dominated by $`at^2\ge1/a`$. On the remaining interval the bounded theta factor is absorbed by $`at^2\ge a/16`$. The Gaussian envelope and its constants are uniform in the real phase.
2. In the probability integral with offset $`|b|\le1`$, expanding the centered logarithm gives quadratic variance, a purely imaginary cubic of size $`O(Nqa^3|t|^3)`$, and quartic remainder $`O(Nqa^4t^4)`$. The latter is absorbable in the modulus envelope. The real cubic contribution integrates with errors of size $`O(Nqa^3/(Na)^2+N^2q^2a^6/(Na)^3)`$ relative to the main Gaussian. Including the quartic term gives $`O(qa^2/N)`$. The exterior cost $`e^{-cN/a}`$ times powers of $`a,N`$ is negligible compared with this bound throughout the specified joint regime. Retaining the factor $`e^{-b^2/(2V)}`$ allows the odd-parity midpoint comparison to cancel its purely Gaussian shift cost exactly.
3. Differentiation of the one-site Jacobi series bounds $`v(y)\le v_*`$ and gives $`\alpha-1/(2v_*)\asymp q`$. The midpoint gap in $`\log\Theta`$ dominates $`cq`$ times squared wrapped distance. Comparing a minimizer with its deepest competitor therefore yields $`\sum_i\delta_i^2\le Ca^2/N`$ and $`\sum_i|\delta_i|\le Ca`$ for its original angular displacements. Both bounds persist along a swap.
4. The core integral remains complex, but its argument throughout each swap segment is bounded by $`Cqa^2|t|\le Cqa`$. This follows by summing the imaginary parts of the paired Fourier modes, using $`\sum|\delta_i|\le Ca`$ and the uniform lower bound on each core theta factor. Its modulus is between two Gaussian envelopes times $`B=\prod_i f(0,\pi+\delta_i)>0`$. Thus the weighted real part of each second logarithmic phase derivative is at least $`cq|G|`$ on the core. The square of the two first derivatives in the swap curvature costs $`O(q^2)|G|`$. In the tail one differentiates the original factors, leaving at least $`N-2`$ Gaussian-enveloped factors. The lower bounds on the removed real zero-frequency factors control normalization by $`B`$. The integrated error, even with $`N^2`$ derivative choices, is $`o(qB/\sqrt{Na})`$.
5. Along the diagonal $`|s|\le Ca/N`$, divide the second derivative by $`N`$. Relative to the core mass its adverse square term costs $`O(Nq^2(s^2+a/N))=O(q^2(a+a^2/N))=o(q)`$. Strict convexity therefore holds on the whole required interval. The diagonal is even by conjugation and $`t\mapsto -t`$. A constrained minimum must first be scalar by the swap argument and then have the smallest absolute permitted deviation. This gives both parities.

In the second derivation I checked the independent central-level comparison. If $`J_j`$ is the midpoint characteristic integral and $`G_j`$ its continuum Gaussian counterpart, the bound on $`(J_j-G_j)-(J_{j_0}-G_{j_0})`$ gains $`|j^2-j_0^2|t^2/2`$ from the subtracted cosines. Its relative size is $`O(|j^2-j_0^2|q/(N^2\alpha))`$. Localization gives $`|j|=O(1/\alpha)`$, so $`e^{\alpha j^2/N}`$ is uniformly bounded and its variation times the original error satisfies the same estimate. The main gap is of order $`q|j^2-j_0^2|/N`$, which dominates. The two proofs use different ways to resolve the last scalar comparison.

For each fixed $`N\ge3`$ the first-shell extremum and positive Hessian follow from the equal-sine equations and the scalar comparison. Cauchy bounds on the remaining Fourier shells on this fixed torus give convergence through second derivatives. Permutation symmetry fixes central points exactly at every parameter. Rank one follows by Jacobi's product. The finite remaining ranks therefore admit a common low-parameter interval. Classical trace-region results already describe the unperturbed extremal phases, while BMVZ prove large-parameter stability at each fixed rank.
