# Marcus's block-permanent inequality: Gaussian and operator routes

Research checkpoint, 20 November 2026 (America/Chicago). No proof or counterexample to the full conjecture is claimed.

## Exact target and current boundary

Let `A` be a positive semidefinite Hermitian matrix of order `mk`, split into `m x m` blocks of order `k`, and put `G_ij = per(A_ij)`. Minc Conjecture 9 asks whether

```math
\mathop{\mathrm{per}}A\ge\mathop{\mathrm{per}}G.
```

The current catalogue is https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/conjecture-9/index.html . Tubbenhauer's 17 November 2026 preprint, https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22058v1 , claims the inequality for at most four packets, for five packets of size at least three, and under an explicit logarithmic sufficient bound on packet size. The first unhandled quadratic packet count is five. These prior results must not be presented as new work. Its negative marked closure is explicitly not a negative full conjecture gap.

A notation correction matters in the older literature. Pate's 1981 real-matrix bound is `per(A) >= per(G)/m!`, where `m` is the number of blocks. The publisher abstract uses `k` for the number of blocks and `n` for block size: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/03081088108817400 . Tubbenhauer's Section 1C gives the translated factor `m!`. The catalogue's factor `k!` appears to use the older notation inside the newer statement.

## Exact Gaussian formulation

Write `A` as the Gram matrix of vectors `v_ia`, and let `ell_ia(z)` be the corresponding linear forms. In the Fischer inner product, monomials are orthogonal and

```math
\|z^\alpha\|_F^2=\alpha!.
```

Equivalently, this inner product is integration against a standard proper complex Gaussian vector. Put

```math
p_i(z)=\prod_{a=1}^k\ell_{ia}(z).
```

Wick's formula gives

```math
G_{ij}=\langle p_i,p_j\rangle_F,
\qquad
\mathop{\mathrm{per}}A=\left\|\prod_i p_i\right\|_F^2.
```

Thus the desired comparison is between the product moment of the packet polynomials and that of a Gaussian vector with their covariance matrix. The packet polynomials are homogeneous and factor completely into linear forms. Replacing them by arbitrary polynomials without proving that extension would change the problem.

The conjecture already contains Chollet's full inequality: take `A_ij = diag(B_ij,C_ij)` for arbitrary positive semidefinite `B,C`. A simultaneous permutation makes `A = B direct_sum C`, while `G = B Hadamard C`. Therefore a proof for every number of quadratic packets would also settle Chollet.

## Why P18's stability mechanism does not transfer directly

The nonnegative substitution used in the permanental-compound FPRAS is unavailable for a general complex Gram matrix. More directly, even a positive semidefinite matrix does not make its principal-permanent generating polynomial real stable. For the two-by-two all-ones matrix,

```math
\mathop{\mathrm{per}}(I+\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(z_1,z_2)A)
=1+z_1+z_2+2z_1z_2.
```

This vanishes at `z_1=i`, `z_2=(-3+i)/5`, both in the upper half-plane. Substituting determinant stability here is invalid.

## Two exact failures of stronger operator statements

### Substitution does not dominate every holomorphic polynomial test

Take `p_1=x^2`, `p_2=xy`, `p_3=y^2`. These are linearly independent in the Fischer space, so their covariance matrix is positive definite. But

```math
p_2^2-p_1p_3=0.
```

For a nondegenerate Gaussian vector `Y` with the same covariance, `Y_2^2-Y_1Y_3` is a nonzero polynomial and has positive squared norm. Therefore no covariance-preserving comparison can hold for all holomorphic polynomial tests. Marcus's test is the special completely factored polynomial `u_1 ... u_m`, so this is an obstruction to a stronger proof method, not a counterexample to Marcus.

### The natural commutator block matrix is not positive

Let `M_f` denote multiplication by `f` in the Fischer space. Its adjoint is `f*(partial)`, where the star conjugates coefficients. For `f_1=x^2`, `f_2=xy`, put

```math
C_{ij}=[M_{f_i}^*,M_{f_j}].
```

Direct differentiation gives

```math
C_{11}=4x\partial_x+2,
\quad C_{22}=x\partial_x+y\partial_y+1,
\quad C_{12}=2y\partial_x,
\quad C_{21}=2x\partial_y.
```

Compress to the two vectors `(y^2/sqrt(2),0)` and `(0,xy)`. The resulting matrix is

```math
\begin{pmatrix}2&2\sqrt2\\2\sqrt2&3\end{pmatrix},
\qquad \det=-2.
```

More simply, the vector `(y^2,-xy)` gives quadratic form `-1`. Thus a proof cannot discard normal-ordering remainders using positivity of this entire block operator. A usable statement must retain the constrained test vectors generated by products of the other packets.

## A concrete Gaussian replacement calculation

A natural sufficient route would be to show that replacing a packet vector by a Gaussian vector with the same covariance decreases its product moment. A still stronger route would declare all higher balanced cumulants nonnegative. The latter is false even in a simple valid packet family.

Let `X_1,X_2,X_3` be proper complex Gaussian variables with covariance `B_ii=1`, `B_ij=r` for `i!=j`, where `-1/2 <= r <= 1`, and put `p_i=X_i^2`. Write `G=2(B Hadamard B)`. Let `Y` be an independent proper Gaussian vector with covariance `G`, and set

```math
M(t)=\mathbb E\prod_{i=1}^3|\sqrt t\,p_i+\sqrt{1-t}\,Y_i|^2.
```

An exact Wick expansion gives

```math
M(t)=\mathop{\mathrm{per}}G+t^2S_4+t^3K_6,
```

where

```math
\mathop{\mathrm{per}}G=8+24r^4+16r^6,
\quad S_4=96r^2(1+2r^3),
\quad K_6=64r^3(r^3+3r+2).
```

At `r=-1/2`, these values are `39/4`, `18`, and `-3`. The sixth balanced cumulant is negative, so termwise cumulant positivity is unavailable. The full example still obeys the desired comparison. In fact

```math
M'(1)=192r^2(r^2+r+1)^2\ge0.
```

This calculation does not disprove Gaussian-replacement monotonicity. That monotonicity remains an unproved sufficient mechanism, with no complete argument currently known here.

## Why the simplest permanent-on-top lift fails

Tran's rank-two permanent-on-top counterexample belongs to the family

```math
H_{ij}=1+c\omega_i\overline{\omega_j},
\quad (\omega_1,\ldots,\omega_5)=(i,-1,-i,1,0),\quad c\ge0.
```

At `c=2`, `per(H)=504` and its Schur power has eigenvalue `512`. The primary paper is https://files.ele-math.com/articles/mia-25-01.pdf , Section 4. The displayed factor in its linear forms is `sqrt(2)`; some extracted PDF text drops the radical.

Duplicating each vector into one quadratic packet produces `A=H tensor J_2`, with `G=2(H Hadamard H)`. This is not a counterexample. Exact polynomial calculation gives

```math
\mathop{\mathrm{per}}(H\circ H)=8(3c^8+86c^4+15),
```

and

```math
\mathop{\mathrm{per}}A-\mathop{\mathrm{per}}G
=2048(39c^8+23c^4+1770)>0.
```

At `c=2` the two permanents are `25378560` and `552704`. Thus the known negative spectral direction is not preserved by this elementary wreath-product lift. A useful lift would have to select a Schur-power direction rather than average it away.

## Relevant recent Gaussian sources and their limits

- Ouimet and Greaves, *A proof of the strong Gaussian product inequality conjecture*, primary author-upload PDF: https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Frederic-Ouimet/publication/410720385_A_proof_of_the_strong_Gaussian_product_inequality_conjecture/links/6a67b848f475b23f400ec34e/A-proof-of-the-strong-Gaussian-product-inequality-conjecture.pdf . The proof uses inverse-square kernels, orthant branches of a nonlinear map, and a principal-minor orthogonality identity. Its comparator is independence, not a Gaussian vector with the covariance of nonlinear packets. Reading the central proof did not produce the required block comparison.
- Malicet, Nourdin, Peccati and Poly, *Squared chaotic random variables: New moment inequalities with applications*, JFA 270 (2016), 649–670, DOI https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jfa.2015.10.013 ; primary preprint https://orbilu.uni.lu/bitstream/10993/20405/1/GPC-arxiv.pdf . Its spectral and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck argument gives products of marginal moments. Applying that conclusion alone cannot recover the off-diagonal terms of `per(G)`.
