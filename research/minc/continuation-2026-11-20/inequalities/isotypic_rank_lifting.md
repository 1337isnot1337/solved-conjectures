# Lieb's permanental dominance conjecture

Date: 20 November 2026, America/Chicago.

Status: a structural rank-lifting lemma and an application to a subgroup
projection bound. Neither is a full MINC resolution. No novelty claim is made
for the Gaussian rank-lifting identity.

## 1. A Gaussian identity for one Fourier block

Let $`A=W^*W`$ be a PSD matrix of order $`n`$, with $`W`$ having $`r`$
rows. Let $`G`$ be an $`r\times s`$ matrix of independent standard complex
Gaussian entries, and put

```math
B=W^*GG^*W.
```

Then $`B\succeq0`$ and $`\operatorname{rank}B\leq s`$. For
$`a_\sigma(M)=\prod_i m_{i,\sigma(i)}`$, complex Wick contraction gives

```math
\mathbb E a_\sigma(B)
 =\sum_{\tau\in S_n}s^{\#\operatorname{cycles}(\tau)}
                         a_{\sigma\tau}(A).
\tag{1}
```

To see the coefficient, expand each entry of $`GG^*`$ and pair the
unconjugated Gaussian from factor $`i`$ with the conjugated Gaussian from
factor $`\tau(i)`$. The column labels must be constant around each cycle of
$`\tau`$, giving $`s^{\#\operatorname{cycles}(\tau)}`$. The row labels
then contract the Gram entries in the product $`a_{\sigma\tau}(A)`$.

For the Fourier convention
$`C_\lambda(M)=\sum_\sigma a_\sigma(M)\rho_\lambda(\sigma)`$, (1) yields

```math
\boxed{\mathbb E C_\lambda(B)=c_\lambda(s)C_\lambda(A),\qquad
c_\lambda(s)=\prod_{(i,j)\in\lambda}(s+j-i).}
\tag{2}
```

Indeed, the central group-algebra element
$`\sum_\tau s^{\#\operatorname{cycles}(\tau)}\tau^{-1}`$ acts on
$`\rho_\lambda`$ by the scalar in (2). This is the usual
Jucys--Murphy content product; alternatively it follows from the character
expansion of the permutation action on $`(\mathbb C^s)^{\otimes n}`$.

## 2. Pure-isotypic inequalities need only the minimal nonvanishing rank

Let $`\mathcal L`$ be any fixed real-linear map from Hermitian
$`d_\lambda\times d_\lambda`$ matrices to another Hermitian matrix space.
Suppose

```math
\mathcal L(C_\lambda(B))\succeq0
```

for every PSD $`B`$ of rank at most $`s`$, where $`s\geq\ell(\lambda)`$.
Then it holds for every PSD $`A`$, in arbitrary rank. In fact
$`c_\lambda(s)>0`$, and averaging the assumed inequality using (2) gives

```math
0\preceq\mathbb E\mathcal L(C_\lambda(B))
      =c_\lambda(s)\mathcal L(C_\lambda(A)).
```

This includes scalar inequalities, by taking a one-dimensional output space.
It also describes the closed convex cone generated by Fourier blocks: the
blocks of arbitrary PSD Gram matrices lie in the closure of the cone generated
by matrices of rank at most $`\ell(\lambda)`$.

The restriction to one isotypic block matters. If an inequality compares
different partitions, their factors $`c_\lambda(s)`$ are generally different.
In particular this argument does not lift a permanent-on-top inequality or
the rank-two inequality $`\operatorname{tr}C_{(3,3)}\leq2\operatorname{per}A-9h(A)`$
with its original coefficients.

## 3. The exceptional $`S_5`$ projection bound in every rank

Let $`H\cong S_5\leq S_6`$ be one of the six exceptional subgroups and
let $`v_H`$ be the unit sign-spherical vector in the $`(3,3)`$ Specht
component. The exact Segre-cubic argument in
`segre_alignment_checkpoint.md` proves, for rank at most two,

```math
\frac56\langle v_H,C_{(3,3)}(A)v_H\rangle
 \leq\frac12\operatorname{tr}C_{(3,3)}(A).
\tag{3}
```

Both sides involve only $`(3,3)`$. In (2),

```math
c_{(3,3)}(2)=(2\cdot3\cdot4)(1\cdot2\cdot3)=144.
```

Therefore (3) holds for **every PSD matrix of order six**, without a rank
restriction. Since
$`\operatorname{Ind}_H^{S_6}(\operatorname{sgn}|_H)=[1^6]+[3,3]`$, Fourier
inversion now gives the all-rank bound

```math
\boxed{f_{H,\operatorname{sgn}}(A)
 \leq\frac16\det A+\frac12\operatorname{tr}C_{(3,3)}(A).}
\tag{4}
```

This does not prove permanental dominance. For example, at $`A=I_6`$, the
right side of (4) is $`1/6+5/2=8/3`$, whereas the permanent and subgroup
function both equal one. The stronger comparison needed to replace that
right side by the permanent is still missing in higher rank.

## 4. Relation to the transfer research

The scalar coordinates in the Segre-cubic proof describe rank-two Gram
configurations. The positive projection inequality they imply is nevertheless
rank-stable because it is pure-isotypic. The refined permanent comparison in
the same note is not pure-isotypic and remains restricted to rank two.

The known counterexamples and conjecture definitions are documented in
[Wanless's survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.01867) and
[MINC Conjecture42](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/conjecture-42/index.html).
The Segre-cubic source is Howard--Millson--Snowden--Vakil,
[Section2.1 of the author PDF](https://math.stanford.edu/~vakil/files/sixjan2308.pdf).
The Wick derivation is included above to make the factors in (2) reviewable;
no external claim that generalized matrix functions are Schur-power
eigenvalues is used.
