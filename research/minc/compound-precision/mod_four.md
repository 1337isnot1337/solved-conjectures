# Characteristic polynomial of permanental compounds

Let $A$ be an integer matrix of order $n$, let $P=P_k(A)$ be its matrix of $k$-rowed subpermanents, and put $N=\binom nk$. Every coefficient of $\det(I-zP)$ modulo four can be computed deterministically in $\widetilde O(N\,\mathrm{poly}(n))$ bit operations after reducing the input modulo four. The algorithm works in all degrees and for singular matrices. Reversal gives the usual characteristic polynomial, with its full degree fixed at $N$ even when the reversed polynomial has smaller degree. In the middle degree the running time is within a polynomial in $n$ of the length of the modular output.

## The exterior correction

Use increasing orders on every row and column subset. Let $D=C_k(A)=\bigwedge^k A$ be the determinantal compound. For $i<h$ and $j>q$, set $E_{ij}=e_i e_j^T$. Define the integer operator
$$
F=\sum_{i<h,\ j>q} a_{ij}a_{hq}\,[uv]\,
 C_k(A+uE_{ij}+vE_{hq}).
$$
Then
$$
P\equiv D-2F\pmod4. \tag{1}
$$
Indeed, a bijection in a subdeterminant contributes its signed weight to $D$ and its signed weight times its inversion number to $F$. For an inversion number $d$, one has $(1-2)^d\equiv1-2d\bmod4$. Multiplying the sign $(-1)^d$ by that quantity restores the unsigned permutation weight. All formulas also allow zero entries.

Write $H(z)=\det(I-zD)$ and $R(z)=(I-zD)^{-1}$. Since a determinant perturbation by a multiple of two has only its constant and linear terms modulo four, (1) gives
$$
\det(I-zP)\equiv H(z)\left(1+2z\sum_{r=0}^{N-1} g_r z^r\right)
                 \pmod{4,z^{N+1}},\qquad
g_r=\operatorname{tr}(D^r F)\pmod2.                       \tag{2}
$$
Identities in this proof use formal power series with invertible constant term. They require no invertibility of $A$.

## Evaluating a correction trace in the input dimension

For a single crossing pair put $S=A^r$, $M=I+tA^{r+1}$ and $K=M^{-1}S$. Exterior multiplicativity and the identity $\operatorname{tr} C_k(T)=[t^k]\det(I+tT)$ give its contribution to $g_r$ as
$$
a_{ij}a_{hq}\,[t^{k-2}]\det(M)\,
       (K_{ji}K_{qh}-K_{jh}K_{qi}).                       \tag{3}
$$
In fact the required two-variable coefficient is the coefficient of $uv$ in $\det(M+tuS e_i e_j^T+tvS e_h e_q^T)$. Factoring $M$ and taking a two-by-two determinant gives (3). Degrees $k<2$ have zero correction. Compute $M^{-1}$ only through degree $k-2$ over the two-element field. Each $g_r$ takes a polynomial number of bit operations in $n$. Successive powers of $A$ are generated by ordinary matrix multiplication in the input dimension.

## Exterior baseline at controlled precision

For $1\le d\le N$, the power sum of $D$ is
$$
 s_d=\operatorname{tr}D^d=[t^k]\det(I+t A^d).
$$
Use any integer lift of the reduced input. Compute every $s_d$ modulo $2^B$ by division-free characteristic-polynomial arithmetic on matrices of order $n$. A working precision $B=2+\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil^2$ suffices to obtain $H$ modulo four. In particular $B$ is polynomial in $n$ even in the middle degree.

Here is a direct precision argument. Let $S(z)=-\sum_{d=1}^N s_d z^{d-1}$. It equals $H'/H$ through degree $N-1$. Start with the constant prefix $h=1$. At a step from length $m$ to length $L=\min(2m,N+1)$, form $S-h'/h$ through degree $L-2$, integrate its coefficients in degrees $m-1,\ldots,L-2$, and call the resulting terms of degrees $m,\ldots,L-1$ the series $v$. Replace $h$ by $h(1+v)$ truncated at length $L$.

With the exact prefix of $H$, this follows because $H/h=1+O(z^m)$, whose logarithm agrees with $H/h-1$ modulo $z^{2m}$. Those retained coefficients are integers. With an approximate prefix known modulo $2^b$, the numerator coefficients have the same precision. Division by the degree $j$ first removes $2^{v_2(j)}$ exactly and then inverts an odd unit. It loses at most $\lceil\log_2(N+1)\rceil$ bits of precision. At most that many doublings occur, which proves the stated bound. Fast polynomial multiplication, inversion of a series with constant term one, and integration cost $\widetilde O(N\,\mathrm{poly}(n))$ bits in total. Finally take the product in (2) at precision two bits. This derivation is a conservative instance of established fast recovery from Newton sums in small characteristic. [Bostan, González-Vega, Perdry and Schost](https://specfun.inria.fr/bostan/publications/BoGoPeSc05.pdf)

## Scope and prior work

This computes the low binary digits of the entire characteristic polynomial simultaneously. The characteristic-two identity with the exterior compound, algorithms for a single permanent modulo powers of two, and fast recovery from Newton sums are prior ingredients. The crossing-pair trace in (3) supplies the correction in the input dimension. The calculation does not recover arbitrary integer coefficients from two bits. The unrestricted characteristic-polynomial question remains open here. [Minc Problem 17](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-17/index.html), [Björklund, Husfeldt and Lyckberg](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0020019017300741)
