# Perron root of permanental compounds

Let $A$ be a rational $n$-square matrix in a given order. Suppose both off-diagonal rectangles across each prefix cut have rank at most $r$. Thus $A$ is quasiseparable of order at most $r$ in both directions. For **every** $0\le k\le n$, an exact application of the permanental compound $P_k(A)$ to a vector of length $N=\binom nk$ takes $N(n+1)^{O(r+1)}$ rational operations, including construction from $A$. The bound also covers the nearly full layers where $N$ is small. For $A\ge0$ this gives a deterministic exact representation of the Perron root in $N^2(n+1)^{O(r+1)}$ rational operations. Signed inputs admit the same action for characteristic-polynomial algorithms. These are field-operation bounds. [Minc P18](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-18/index.html) · [Minc P17](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-17/index.html)

Banded matrices, their simultaneous reorderings, and diagonal matrices with a global rank-$`r`$ correction are examples. The class also includes full-rank matrices with many distinct nested off-diagonal factors. Classical quasiseparable generators for the original matrix are known. The construction below tracks all matching minors in one ordered product of local maps. The fixed-weight contraction is stated for arbitrary local maps and may be used independently. [Pernet and Storjohann, generators for quasiseparable matrices](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747717117300767)

## Multiplication on a fixed Hamming layer

Suppose a binary-input, binary-output kernel has entries
```math
 K_{I,J}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\big(T_1(i_1,j_1)\cdots T_n(i_n,j_n)\big),
```
where $i_s,j_s$ are membership bits and the matrices have compatible dimensions bounded by $D$, including the wrap-around bond. Open boundary products are included by using dimension one on that bond. The principal block $|I|=|J|=k$ can be applied in $O(n^2 D^3 \sum_{a=0}^h\binom na)$ field operations up to polynomial indexing overhead, where $h=\min(k,n-k)$. At most $D^2\sum_{a=0}^h\binom na$ field elements of working array are needed apart from maps, input and output. In particular the sum of binomial coefficients is at most $(h+1)N$.

If $k>n/2$ complement all input and output bits and interchange their labels in the local matrices. Now both words have weight $h$. For a pair of such words let $s_u=\sum_{a=1}^u(i_a-j_a)$, with $s_0=s_n=0$; here $i_a,j_a$ denote the two membership bits at position $a$. Choose the **first** maximum on boundaries $0,\ldots,n-1$, say $b$. In the cyclic sweep of sites $b+1,\ldots,n,1,\ldots,b$, after reaching boundary $u$ the partially converted word has weight $h+s_u-s_b$. It has weight at most $h$ throughout, strictly below $h$ when $u<b$. Conversely these inequalities and final weight $h$ select precisely that first maximum. The boundary following site $n$ is numbered zero. This convention also handles ties and the identical-word case.

Sum $n$ contractions, one for each proposed $b$. Start with the arbitrary input vector on its weight layer. At each site apply its four local matrices, replacing the input bit by the output bit, contracting the bond index, and project out weights greater than $h$ (or greater than $h-1$ on a boundary $u<b$). At the end retain weight $h$ and take the trace over the initial bond. For any pair of input and output words the bit transitions on the path are prescribed. It survives exactly its unique contraction, with the original kernel value by cyclic invariance of trace. This proves the formula even for noncommuting maps of either sign. Dense multiplication on the bond costs $O(D^3)$ per word and step when retaining all starting indices. This is how the bound avoids a layer of size $\binom n{2h}$ when the first part of a word acquires many particles or holes before the second part loses them. For context, charge-preserving tensor representations for particle-number eigenstates and structured operators have an extensive literature. [Bachmayr, Götte and Pfeffer](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10092-022-00462-9)

## Constructing the local matrices

At prefix cut $t$, choose a basis $F_{t,l}$ for the rows from the prefix restricted to future columns. Choose another basis $G_{t,l}$ for the columns from the prefix restricted to future rows. Each basis has size at most $r$. Bases and all change-of-coordinate maps below are obtained by exact Gaussian elimination on the indicated slices, also for zero rank. Associate formal variables $x_l,y_l$ to the two bases. For each prefix row $i$ let $L_i(x)$ be its linear form of row coordinates. For each prefix column $j$ let $Q_j(y)$ give its column coordinates. A missing basis gives zero for its corresponding linear forms.

For the selected prefix rows and columns sum over all partial matchings $M$ between them. The summand is the product of their matched edge weights times one $L_i$ for each unmatched selected row and one $Q_j$ for each unmatched selected column. This polynomial records the prefix state. Before any site it is the constant one. At the final cut both bases are empty and only saturated matchings remain. The scalar is the desired subpermanent for equal-size sets.

For site $s=t+1$, define $D_x=\sum_l F_{t,l}(s)\partial_{x_l}$ and $D_y=\sum_l G_{t,l}(s)\partial_{y_l}$. They consume one unmatched prefix row or column with precisely its edge weight to the new site. The linear substitution $R$ restricts the old basis vectors to the next future index sets and expresses each restriction in the new basis. It is always defined because those restrictions lie in the spans for the larger prefix. Write $L,Q$ for the two new-site linear forms at the new cut. For site membership bits $u,v$ (row then column), the map on the old polynomial $p$ is
```math
 \sum_{a=0}^u\sum_{b=0}^v L^{u-a}Q^{v-b} R(D_y^a D_x^b p)
 \ +\ \mathbf1_{u=v=1}\,A_{ss} R(p).
```
Each new selected site either matches an old site or stays unmatched. When both are selected they can also match one another, giving the final term. The two derivatives act on separate variables and the newly introduced factors are inserted afterwards. This enumerates each extended matching once.

Truncate to degree at most $k$ separately in the two groups of variables at each step. Along every input-output pair of weight $k$, the numbers of unmatched selected rows and columns never exceed $k$, so truncation is exact. Arbitrary other pairs may have different entries after truncation and are never needed. The maximum bond dimension is $D=\binom{k+r}{r}^2$ (take the actual ranks at each cut). The local maps on all monomials can be assembled with $(n+1)^{O(r+1)}$ field operations: successive multiplication by linear forms realizes each substituted monomial using arrays of the displayed dimensions. In the orientation of the trace formula use the transition coefficient from the old monomial to the new as the entry in the old-index row and new-index column. Combining this construction with the first-maximum contraction proves the action bound.

For nonnegative $A$, form the first $2N$ scalar moments $\mathbf1^T P_k(A)^j\mathbf1$ by these products, starting at $j=0$. Exact rational reconstruction of their generating function has quadratic cost by the Euclidean algorithm or Berlekamp–Massey with the initial transient retained. The denominator after reduction is constant exactly for a nilpotent compound. Otherwise the reciprocal of its smallest positive real zero is the Perron root: the scalar series converges inside the reciprocal spectral radius and diverges on the positive boundary by a nonnegative Perron vector bounded above by $\mathbf1$. See the [exact reconstruction proof](diagonal_perturbation.md). For signed $A$ the action also supplies the hypotheses of the [arbitrary-multiplicity characteristic-polynomial bound](../nov20-independent/compound/blackbox_compound_bound.md). The cut-rank restriction leaves the general exact spectral questions open.
