# Ryser’s design conjecture

Fix $`\epsilon>0`$. For all sufficiently large $`n`$, if a symmetric $`(n,k,\lambda)`$ design exists and $`k\ge\epsilon n`$, **every minimum permanent among binary $`k`$-regular matrices is attained at a design**. More precisely every non-design has larger permanent than every design of its parameters. This includes the dense complements in the [matching-polynomial theorem](ryser_complementary_designs.md) and also fixed densities strictly inside $`(0,1)`$. It leaves [Ryser’s conjecture](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/conjecture-5/index.html) open for sparse design matrices and at unresolved finite orders. All design parameters here obey $`0<\lambda<k<n`$.

We give the comparison for the normalized stochastic matrices. For a support $`G`$ put $`B_G=G/k-J`$, where $`J`$ has entries $`1/n`$, and $`P(B)=\mathop{\mathrm{per}}(J+B)/d_n`$ with $`d_n=n!/n^n`$. Let $`H`$ be a design and put

```math
 a=(k-\lambda)/k^2,\quad U=I-J,\quad u=(n-k)/k,\quad
 D=GG^T-[(k-\lambda)I+\lambda\mathbf1\mathbf1^T],\quad E=\|D\|_F^2,\quad \delta=E/k^4.
```

Set $`C_G=B_GB_G^T=aU+X`$, $`X=D/k^2`$; for $`H`$, $`C_H=aU`$. The same facts and norms hold on transposing. Both $`X`$ and its transposed-side analogue have zero diagonal and margins. Always $`\|B_G\|_F^2=u`$, $`|B_{ij}|\le K/n`$, and each row and column of $`B`$ has squared norm $`u/n`$, with $`K`$ depending only on $`\epsilon`$. All constants below may depend on $`\epsilon`$. If $`k=n-1`$ every support is a design. Otherwise the degree $`r=n-k`$ of the complementary design satisfies $`r(r-1)/(n-1)\ge1`$ by integrality, so $`r\ge\sqrt{n-1}`$. Since $`k-\lambda=k(n-k)/(n-1)`$, the [all-parameter four-cycle gap](ryser_complementary_designs.md) implies $`E\ge c_\epsilon\sqrt n`$ for non-designs at all large orders. Thus $`\delta\ge c_\epsilon n^{-7/2}`$.

## Any competitive support has polynomially small centered norm

Suppose for contradiction along an unbounded sequence that $`E>0`$ and $`P(B_G)\le P(B_H)`$. The determinant asymptotic for diffuse centered matrices of [bounded Frobenius norm](../real-radius/hilbert_schmidt_limit.md) gives $`P(B_H)=e^{u/2}+o(1)`$ since $`\|B_H\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\sqrt a=O(n^{-1/2})`$. The [two-sided dense bound](../real-radius/dense_spectral_comparison.md) first forces $`\|B_G\|_{\mathrm{op}}`$ to stay away from one, since its regularized determinant would otherwise diverge. The signed determinant asymptotic then gives $`P(B_G)=\det(I-C_G)^{-1/2}+o(1)`$. The log determinant is at least $`u/2+\|B_G\|_{\mathrm{op}}^4/4`$, so $`\|B_G\|_{\mathrm{op}}\to0`$.

We need a rate. Write $`a_{n,l}=(n)_l/n^l`$ with falling factorial, and let $`\sigma_l(B)`$ be the sum of its order-$`l`$ subpermanents. Then $`P(B)=\sum_l\sigma_l(B)/a_{n,l}`$. The exact [partition expansion](../real-radius/hilbert_schmidt_limit.md) writes $`\sigma_l`$ as a sum over two partitions of $`l`$ labels with no singleton blocks. Form the bipartite multigraph with one vertex for each block and one edge per label. Its weight is the sum over all maps of its vertices into their index sets of the product of the $`B`$ entries on the edges. Its multiplier is $`\mu(\pi)\mu(\tau)/l!`$ for the partition Möbius functions from the discrete partition. This follows by distinct-index inclusion-exclusion, with singleton blocks vanishing by centering. The empty graph has weight one.

If the graph has $`v`$ vertices let $`d=l-v\ge0`$ be its excess. The total absolute multiplier at excess $`d`$ is at most $`(l+1)^{3d+1}`$. Indeed the weighted number of partitions with $`p`$ blocks of size at least two is the number of permutations having those cycles. Listing their lengths bounds it by $`l!\,l^{l-2p}/(2^p p!)`$ for $`l\ge2`$: distribute the $`l-2p`$ extra units among $`p`$ lengths and use that their product is at least $`2^p`$. For the two sides $`p+q=l-d`$ and $`l!/(p!q!)\le l^d2^{l-d}`$. Summing at most $`l+1`$ choices proves the bound. The absolute value of each graph weight is at most $`K^l n^{-d}`$ just by counting maps.

Take $`L=\lfloor b\log n\rfloor`$ for a fixed small $`b>0`$ such that $`\max(2,K)^L\le n^{1/20}`$. For $`l\le L`$, $`1\le a_{n,l}^{-1}\le1+O(L^2/n)`$. The entire contribution with $`d\ge j`$, $`l\le L`$, for fixed $`j\ge1`$, is $`O(n^{-j+1/10})`$: sum the geometric estimate with ratio $`(L+1)^3/n`$, absorbing remaining fixed powers of $`L`$. On the other hand the coefficient estimate (2) of the signed permanent theorem gives $`|\sigma_l(B)|/a_{n,l}\le C_R R^l`$ for any fixed $`R>0`$ once $`\|B\|_{\rm op}\le R/2`$, uniformly for bounded Frobenius norm. For the two supports under consideration the norms tend to zero. Choose $`R<e^{-12/b}`$; the tail beyond $`L`$ is $`O(n^{-10})`$ (the constant may depend on $`b,R`$).

The $`d=0`$ graphs have all degrees two. They give exactly the coefficients of $`\det(I-z^2BB^T)^{-1/2}`$ in the variable $`z`$, by two Gaussian pairings or the alternating cycles of two perfect matchings on the labels. These coefficients are nonnegative; their sum at any fixed positive radius stays bounded once the centered operator norm is small, by the logarithmic determinant series and the bounded trace. Thus they have the same negligible tail for a sufficiently large fixed radius. Removing their finite factorial corrections costs $`O(L^2/n)`$. We have shown for both supports that $`P(B)=\det(I-BB^T)^{-1/2}+O(n^{-9/10})`$.

The trace of each positive integer power of $`C_G`$ is at least the corresponding trace of $`C_H`$: on the centered space their eigenvalues have the same mean $`a`$, so apply scalar convexity. In degree two the difference is exactly $`\delta`$. Thus the log inverse square-root determinant difference is at least $`\delta/4`$. Since both determinants in this comparison mean inverse square roots and are at least one, competitiveness gives $`\delta=O(n^{-9/10})`$. In particular $`\|B_G\|_{\rm op}^4\le\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}C_G^2=(n-1)a^2+\delta=O(n^{-9/10})`$. For all sufficiently large orders **both** centered norms are at most $`n^{-1/8}`$.

## Only bounded graphs remain after removing isolated pairs

An isolated component consisting of two parallel edges contributes the same weight $`u`$ on both supports. If $`m_l(B)`$ is the signed sum with all such components forbidden (including the empty graph at zero), the labelled expansion gives exactly

```math
 \sigma_l(B)=\sum_{2j+s=l}\frac{(u/2)^j}{j!}\,m_s(B).
```

Choose which $`2j`$ labels form the components and pair them; their Möbius signs multiply to one. This identity is coefficientwise and does not require analytic continuation of a connected-graph logarithm. The sum of the displayed multipliers is bounded by $`e^{u/2}`$, uniformly in all degrees after summing over $`j`$.

In the range $`s\le L`$ the total absolute contribution of $`d\ge4`$ to these pair-free coefficients summed over $`s`$ is $`O(n^{-39/10})`$ by the same estimate (restriction can only reduce absolute sums). This is $`o(\delta)`$. For $`d\le3`$, all pair-free coefficients with $`s`$ beyond a sufficiently large absolute constant have total absolute contributions $`o(n^{-4})`$. To check this, any degree-two component is an even cycle with length $`j\ge4`$, whose weight is at most $`u\|B\|_{\rm op}^{j-2}`$. In the other components there are at most $`2d`$ vertices of degree at least three and at most $`3d`$ chains between them: suppress all internal degree-two vertices, retaining loops and parallel chains. A chain of length one has entry at most $`K/n`$; one of length $`j\ge2`$ has entry at most $`(u/n)\|B\|_{\rm op}^{j-2}`$ by multiplying its two endpoint Euclidean norms and the intervening operators, including for a returning chain. Summing the branching labels thus costs an overall factor $`n^{-d}`$ after the chain denominators. The product of all bounds is at most $`K_1^s n^{-d}\|B\|_{\rm op}^{s/2-6d}`$ for $`s`$ sufficiently large, since each pure cycle keeps at least half its length as exponent. Multiply by $`(s+1)^{3d+1}`$ and use $`\|B\|_{\rm op}\le n^{-1/8}`$; taking, for example, $`s>160`$ makes the geometric tail $`o(n^{-4})`$. These estimates include disconnected graphs. The factorial corrections for $`l\le L`$ are bounded by two, so convolution with the isolated-pair series preserves both error bounds.

## Comparison on each remaining connected graph

All graphs in this part have fixed bounded size. A cycle of length $`2j\ge4`$ contributes $`\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}C^j`$. At $`j=2`$ its difference, value on $`G`$ minus that on $`H`$, is $`\delta`$. At $`j>2`$ its difference is $`o(\delta)`$, by expansion of $`(aU+X)^j`$, $`\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}X=0`$, $`\|X\|_F^2=\delta`$, $`a=o(1)`$ and $`\|X\|_{\rm op}=o(1)`$. Each of these individual cycle values is $`o(1)`$. A positive-excess connected graph has value $`O(n^{-d})`$ by the entry bound. We prove its difference is $`o(\delta)`$.

**Excess at least two.** For a fixed connected simple test graph with $`v'`$ vertices and $`e'`$ edges, the difference of its ordinary homomorphism counts into the binary supports is

```math
 O_{e'}(E n^{\max(v'-3,0)}+n^{e'-4}),                           \tag{3}
```

and is zero for trees. Here is the detail adapting the [anchored graph induction](ryser_complementary_designs.md). For injective counts use precisely its leaf and non-cut degree-two recurrences, with codegree replacement error at most $`E n^{v'-3}`$ and all coefficients $`k,\lambda\le n`$. Each factor $`k`$ consumes one vertex and an edge, and $`\lambda`$ consumes one vertex and two edges. Patterns with at most two vertices have no difference. At a terminal graph of $`e'-v'\ge4`$ the absolute bound $`2n^{v'}\le2n^{e'-4}`$ suffices. The only remaining terminal graph is $`K_{3,3}`$, for which the triple-intersection argument there gives $`O(nk^4+k^2E)=O(n^5+n^2E)`$; proper identifications have fewer vertices. This proves (3) for injective counts. Summing over partitions to obtain ordinary counts does not increase $`v'`$ or $`e'`$. Tree homomorphism counts depend only on the margins.

For a connected multigraph of $`l`$ edges and $`v`$ vertices, expand its $`B`$-weight using $`B_{ij}=k^{-1}(G_{ij}-k/n)`$. A chosen subset of the binary factors gives a simple graph on the original sites after merging repeated edges, with isolated vertices allowed. When telescoping the products over its connected components, the other vertices have at most $`n^{v-v'}`$ assignments. Any component with $`v',e'`$ satisfies $`e'-v'\le l-v`$: since the original multigraph is connected, at least $`v-v'`$ other edges are needed to reach all remaining vertices. Using (3) on each non-tree component, and $`k\ge\epsilon n`$, proves that the difference of the centered graph weights is $`O_l(E n^{-3-d}+n^{-4})`$ with $`d=l-v`$. This is $`o(\delta)`$ for $`d\ge2`$, using $`E\ge c\sqrt n`$.

**Excess one.** There is either one vertex of degree four or two vertices of degree three. Suppressing degree-two chains yields two cycles meeting at one vertex, two disjoint cycles joined by a path, or three paths between the two degree-three vertices. This list also permits parallel edges and cycles of length two in the original bipartite multigraph.

For a cycle of length $`2j`$ with its attachment index retained, its vector of weights is the diagonal of $`C^j`$ on the corresponding side. Each entry is $`O(1/n)`$ by $`C_{ii}=u/n`$ and the contraction bound. Its difference from the constant design diagonal has $`\ell^1`$ norm $`O_j(\delta)`$: in the power expansion the first-order term is a multiple of $`X`$ with zero diagonal, and for $`h\ge2`$ the sum of absolute diagonal entries of $`X^h`$ is at most $`\|X\|_{\rm op}^{h-2}\delta`$ by the spectral theorem. For meeting cycles, telescope their scalar product to bound the difference by $`O(\delta/n)`$. For cycles joined by a nonempty path the constant diagonals are killed on both sides by the centered path matrix. Each remaining deviation has squared Euclidean norm $`O(\delta/n)`$ by its $`\ell^1`$ and $`\ell^\infty`$ bounds, and the path has operator norm at most one. This gives the same bound.

For three paths with endpoints on the same side the sum is $`\sum_{ij}(C^{p})_{ij}(C^{q})_{ij}(C^{s})_{ij}`$ with $`p,q,s\ge1`$. Split each power into $`a^p U`$ and its difference $`Y_p`$. We have $`\|Y_p\|_F=O_p(\sqrt\delta)`$ by the power expansion. Every full or base entry is $`O(1/n)`$. Thus terms with at least two differences sum to $`O(\delta/n)`$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. For one difference the other two factors give a multiple of $`U_{ij}^2`$. As $`Y_p`$ is centered, its pairing with this two-valued matrix reduces to a bounded multiple of $`\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}Y_p=O_p(\delta)`$. The prefactor contains at least $`a^2=O(n^{-2})`$. The no-difference term agrees exactly.

For opposite-side endpoints the three matrices are $`C^p B`$ with $`p\ge0`$ (transposition is immaterial after fixing the orientation). Write this as $`a^p B+W_p`$, with $`W_0=0`$ and $`W_p=(C^p-a^pU)B`$ otherwise. The $`W_p`$ vanish on the design, have Frobenius norm $`O_p(\sqrt\delta)`$, and every path entry is $`O(1/n)`$ by the two endpoint row norms or the entry bound in length one. Two or more differences again cost $`O(\delta/n)`$. The term with no differences is a fixed multiple of $`\sum B_{ij}^3`$, identical because the two entry values and their multiplicities are fixed. For a single difference, $`B_{ij}^2=(1/k-2/n)B_{ij}+\beta`$ for a scalar $`\beta`$, by the two possible values. The constant is killed by centering. The remaining inner product is $`\langle W_p,B\rangle=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(C^{p+1}-a^p C)=O_p(\delta)`$ by trace expansion (the same trace on the design is zero). The factor $`1/k-2/n=O(1/n)`$ proves the required bound. This treats all excess-one graphs.

## The sign of the leading difference

For fixed pair-free graphs, weights multiply over components. Every component has value $`o(1)`$, by the preceding cycle and positive-excess bounds. Thus any graph with two or more components has difference $`o(\delta)`$ by telescoping. Among connected graphs only the length-four cycle contributes at leading order. On four labels there are three pairings on the left and two different pairings for each on the right, with positive Möbius product. The coefficient is $`6/4!=1/4`$. We conclude that for each of the bounded core degrees the difference of $`m_s`$ is $`o(\delta)`$ except at $`s=4`$, where it is $`\delta/4+o(\delta)`$.

Inserting the isolated-pair convolution in the finite factorial expansion up to $`L`$ now gives

```math
 P(B_G)-P(B_H)=\tfrac14\big(e^{u/2}+o(1)\big)\,E/k^4.          \tag{4}
```

Indeed the sum of $`(u/2)^j/j!`$ for $`2j+4\le L`$ differs from $`e^{u/2}`$ by $`o(1)`$ uniformly since $`u`$ stays bounded; its factorial corrections are $`1+O(L^2/n)`$. All other core contributions and the tail past $`L`$ were bounded by $`o(\delta)`$. This is strictly positive for the assumed competing non-design, contradicting $`P(B_G)\le P(B_H)`$. The contradiction proves the uniform eventual assertion. Formula (4) itself holds for all non-design supports with $`\|B_G\|_{\rm op}\le n^{-1/8}`$ under the density assumption, independently of how that norm bound arose.

The source history for the exact Ryser statement and the fixed-order and short-cycle results is in the [prior-art audit](ryser_source_audit.md). The partition and Gaussian determinant expansions have classical antecedents in [McCullagh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5723). The contribution here is comparing errors at the scale of the individual integer codegree defect, including graphs with branching vertices; a leading determinant equivalent alone has insufficient precision. No assertion is made here about acceptance or external verification.

## A consequence for circulants

For all sufficiently large $`m`$, take $`n=4^m`$ and either $`k=(n+\sqrt n)/2`$ or $`k=(n-\sqrt n)/2`$. There is a matrix of degree $`k`$ strictly below every circulant of the same degree and order. This gives two positive-density families for [Minc's Problem 11](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-11/index.html).

Write $`V=\mathbb F_2^m\times\mathbb F_2^m`$ and $`f(x,y)=(-1)^{x\cdot y}`$. Summation over $`y`$ gives $`\sum f=2^m`$. For any nonzero shift $`(a,b)`$, the product $`f(x,y)f(x+a,y+b)`$ is $`(-1)^{a\cdot b}`$ times the nonconstant character $`(-1)^{a\cdot y+b\cdot x}`$ and thus sums to zero. The translates of $`f`$ are consequently the rows of an orthogonal sign matrix with constant row and column sums. Selecting either symbol gives a binary symmetric design: two rows of weight $`k`$ meet in $`\lambda=k-n/4`$ places, by orthogonality. Conversely replacing a binary design of either degree by its two signs gives a Hadamard matrix, since its design parameter has this value. In the circulant case this matrix would be a circulant Hadamard matrix of order $`4^m`$. Turyn's order restriction requires $`n=4t^2`$ with $`t`$ odd; here $`t=2^{m-1}`$ is even for $`m\ge2`$. Therefore no circulant is a design at these parameters, and the strict comparison above applies. [Leung and Schmidt, introduction](https://www3.ntu.edu.sg/home/Bernhard/Publications/pub/chdmII.pdf)

The [integer replay](verify_ryser_dense.py) checks the fourth and fifth subpermanent coefficients and the three-path contraction on 18 design switches, the partition multiplier bound through 64 labels, and sign autocorrelations for four orders. The strict comparison at unbounded orders follows from the estimates above.
