# Brualdi’s cohesive and barycentric matrix problems

Fix a fully indecomposable binary $h\times h$ support $D$. Replace each vertex in its bipartite graph by $l$ twins, joining both sets of clones completely for every original edge. Write $D^{(l)}$ for the resulting binary support. For $X\in\Omega(D)$ let $\tau(X)$ be the sum of products of edge weights over the spanning trees of the original $2h$-vertex bipartite graph, assigning weight $x_{ij}$ to each edge. Let $J_l$ have all entries $1/l$ and $d_N=N!/N^N$.

* For all sufficiently large $l$, the permanent has a **unique global minimizer** on $\Omega(D^{(l)})$. It has the form $X_l\otimes J_l$ and $`X_l\to X_*`$, the unique maximizer of $\tau$ on $\Omega(D)$. The minimum divided by $d_{hl}$ tends to $`(h\tau(X_*))^{-1/2}`$.
* If $`X_*`$ is positive on the original support, all sufficiently large clones are cohesive. If its optimization has strict complementarity, the support of $X_l$ is exactly that of $`X_*`$ for all sufficiently large $l$. Effective resistances on the original graph give the required first-order test.
* For each fixed $D$, **stationarity of the barycenter is necessary and sufficient for barycentricity** for all sufficiently large $l$. In particular every fixed edge-transitive support has barycentric clones for all sufficiently large multiplicities. When the doubly stochastic scaling of $D$ does not maximize its weighted tree sum, its clones are eventually nonbarycentric.

These asymptotic classifications allow any fixed base, including sparse bases with bottlenecks. No size bound for the cloning threshold is asserted. [Minc Problem 14](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-14/index.html), [Minc Problem 15](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-15/index.html)

## Reduction and the tree optimum

At a global minimizer, two rows with identical allowed supports can be averaged while retaining the minimum. This is Minc’s row-averaging lemma; it holds also for columns. Along the segment exchanging these two rows the permanent is a quadratic symmetric about the midpoint. Since it has the same value at the initial point and the midpoint, it is constant along that segment at a minimizer. Repeating a sweep of all within-group pair averages converges to identical rows in each group. This follows by orthogonal projection in Euclidean norm: on the complement of the common fixed space one sweep has norm strictly below one by compactness and the equality condition for an orthogonal projection. Do the same for columns. Every global minimizer thus belongs to a connected subset of global minimizers containing its fully averaged matrix $X\otimes J_l$. Indeed the averaging segments form a connected set, and adjoining their limiting endpoints preserves connectedness. [Averaging lemma and proof](brualdi_structure.md), [Pula, Song and Wanless](https://cs.du.edu/~mathfiles/preprints/nsm-math-preprint-1022.pdf)

Put $B=X-J_h$. The nonconstant singular values of $X$ are those of $B$ on the centered space. Kirchhoff’s matrix-tree theorem applied to the Laplacian with diagonal blocks $I$ and cross blocks $-X,-X^T$ gives
```math
 h\tau(X)=\det(I-B^TB).
```
For connected positive support the Laplacian spectrum consists of $0,2$ and $1\pm s_j$ for the other $h-1$ singular values; its nonzero eigenvalue product is $2h\tau(X)$. Disconnected support gives zero on both sides. A fully indecomposable support has a positive doubly stochastic point and its bipartite graph is connected, so the maximum tree weight is positive. Where the weight is positive, $`\|B\|_{\rm op}<1`$ and $F(B)=(h\tau(X))^{-1/2}$ is strictly convex. Indeed the restriction of $`-\tfrac12\log\det\left(\begin{smallmatrix}I&B\\B^T&I\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$ to any nonconstant affine line has positive second derivative. The set $`\|B\|_{\rm op}<1`$ is convex. Compactness gives a unique tree optimum $`X_*`$.

Set $Q_l(X)=\mathop{\mathrm{per}}(X\otimes J_l)/d_{hl}$. On the compact face all entries of the large matrix are bounded by $h/(hl)$. The [dense two-sided comparison](../real-radius/dense_spectral_comparison.md) bounds $Q_l(X)$ below by a fixed positive multiple depending only on $h$ of
```math
 \det\!\left(I-(1-1/(hl))B^TB\right)^{-1/2}.
```
Here the centered large matrix is $B\otimes J_l$, with the same nonzero singular values as $B$. Thus any sequence tending to disconnected support has $Q_l(X)\to\infty$. At every compact set with $`\|B\|_{\rm op}<1`$, the [determinant approximation](../real-radius/hilbert_schmidt_limit.md) gives $Q_l(X)-F(B)\to0$ uniformly, since maximum row and column norms of $B\otimes J_l$ are $O_h(l^{-1/2})$. This is the block form of [McCullagh's asymptotic](https://arxiv.org/abs/1205.5723). Comparison with the fixed point $`X_*`$ shows every averaged minimizer tends to $`X_*`$ and proves the minimum-value limit.

## Uniqueness and support selection

Choose a small convex neighborhood of $`X_*`$ in the affine margin space on which $`\|X-J_h\|_{\rm op}\le\rho<1`$. The [full Hessian estimate](../real-radius/diffuse_hessian.md) is uniform on its closure after lifting to $B\otimes J_l$. Since $`\|E\otimes J_l\|_F=\|E\|_F`$, it gives strict convexity of $Q_l$ there for all large $l$. All averaged minimizers lie there, so exactly one averaged minimizer exists. At that actual matrix the same estimate gives positive definite Hessian on the **entire** centered space of order $hl$. A global minimum on a convex polytope has nonnegative first variation on all feasible directions. Taylor’s formula and the positive Hessian therefore make this point isolated among global minimizers, including at a boundary. The connected averaging argument now implies that every global minimizer was already this point.

On positive tree weight, the partial derivative of $\log\tau$ in an edge weight is its effective resistance, including for a missing edge between existing vertices. Indeed differentiate the log determinant of a grounded Laplacian, whose edge derivative is the outer product of the grounded incidence vector with itself. Thus interior optimality means that on the allowed edges these resistances equal row plus column potentials. For a boundary optimum the potentials must majorize the resistances at excluded edges. This is the linear first-order condition on a transportation polytope, and concavity of $\log\tau$ on this face makes it sufficient.

For strict complementarity ask that all those majorizations off the positive support be strict. Minimize $Q_l$ on the face with those excluded edges set to zero. Its averaged solution tends to $`X_*`$ by the same argument and is positive on the smaller support for large $l$. On this support its affine gradient is a sum of row and column potentials. Normalize one potential to zero. The positive-support graph is connected, so following any fixed spanning tree recovers all potentials continuously from the gradient. The first derivative estimate gives convergence of these potentials when applied to $-2\log Q_l$, which approximates $\log(h\tau)$; one may choose convergent representatives of the affine gradients by orthogonal projection onto the margin tangent space. The strict gaps persist on all excluded edges. This gives the correct full-face first-order condition for $Q_l$ at the restricted optimizer. Strict convexity in the neighborhood containing all global minimizers identifies it with $X_l$. Interior $`X_*`$ needs only convergence for eventual cohesiveness.

## The barycenter

Let $S$ be the unique doubly stochastic diagonal scaling of $D$. The scaling of $D^{(l)}$ is $S\otimes J_l$. At a stationary barycenter the positive matching counts factor into row and column multipliers; equivalently the barycenter itself is the doubly stochastic scaling. Conversely when the barycenter equals that scaling its supported permutation products are constant, its matching marginals are its entries, and its allowed permanental cofactors equal its permanent. [Matching-count criterion](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/problem-15/index.html)

If $`S\ne X_*`$, the limiting objective has a nonzero feasible first derivative at $S$, since $S$ is interior and $F$ strictly convex. By gradient convergence, $S\otimes J_l$ cannot be stationary for large $l$. If $`S=X_*`$, a stationary barycenter is inside the strictly convex neighborhood containing the global minimizer and therefore coincides with it. This proves the claimed eventual finite test. Under edge transitivity all present edges have the same matching count at every multiplicity, including after independent permutations of the clones. Thus the stationarity condition always holds. For example, $(I_b+P_b)\otimes\mathbf1_{l\times l}$ is barycentric for every fixed cycle length $b$ and all sufficiently large $l$, even though clone multiplicity two is nonbarycentric for $b\ge4$. [Fischer and Hwang](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0024379596900117)

Prior work gives the averaging principle, stationary-barycenter test, and asymptotic permanent formula. Our searches covered clone and Kronecker faces, minimum permanents on cyclic block faces, spanning-tree maximization, and perfect matching scaling. Historical priority for the all-base uniqueness and classification implications has not been independently established. Individual small clone factors and unrestricted supports in the two catalogue problems remain open.
