# Integrality of the minimum permanent

For every fixed $\epsilon>0$, there exists $n_0(\epsilon)$ such that, whenever $n\ge n_0(\epsilon)$ and $\epsilon n\le k\le n$, **every minimizer over all nonnegative integer matrices of order $n$ with margins $k$ is binary**. There is no cap on the multiplicities. This gives exact equality of the two minima, uniformly over the specified degrees. Smaller orders and sequences of intermediate or sparse degrees remain outside this theorem. [Minc's statement and prior results](https://yair-lavi.com/mincs-list/conjecture-24/index.html)

The [diffuse integrality theorem](diffuse_integrality.md) already proves the assertion if the maximum normalized entry tends to zero. We prove here that minimizers must satisfy that hypothesis. A localized entry might produce a second singular value close to one even when the normalized permanent is bounded. We retain the permanent of the localized portion exactly and apply spectral estimates to the remaining portion.

## Splitting a minimizing sequence

Let $A=G/k$ for a sequence of unrestricted minimizers at positive density. Set $P_n(B)=\operatorname{per}(J_n+B)/d_n$ with $J_n=\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/n$, $d_n=n!/n^n$, and let $B=A-J_n$. The binary competitor gives $P_n(B)\le e^{u_0/2}+o(1)$, where $u_0=(n-k)/k$. Thus $\|A\|_F$ stays bounded by [uniform stability](../nov20-independent/inequalities/uniform_frobenius_stability.md). Assume for contradiction that the largest entry of $A$ stays at least some $\delta>0$ on a subsequence.

Sort rows and columns separately by decreasing Euclidean norm. After taking a subsequence, the entries converge coordinatewise to an infinite matrix $L$ with $\sum_{ij}L_{ij}^2<\infty$. Every normalized entry at least $\delta$ lies among a bounded number of initial rows and columns, so $\|L\|_F\ge\delta$. For each fixed row, convergence holds in $\ell_2$ after padding with zeros. Indeed the largest column norm past position $t$ is $O(1/\sqrt t)$ uniformly, and the row has sum one. Its squared tail norm is consequently $O(1/\sqrt t)$. The column version holds as well.

Choose $s=s_n\to\infty$ sufficiently slowly, with $s=o(n)$, so that the upper-left $s$-square part of $A$ converges in Frobenius norm to $L$ (with zero padding), and each of the two off-diagonal strips cut out by the first $s$ indices has Frobenius norm tending to zero. To verify this diagonal choice, first fix $s$. The squared norms of the two strips converge to those of the corresponding strips in $L$ by the strong convergence of each of the finitely many distinguished rows and columns. These limiting strip norms tend to zero as $s$ increases by square summability. The convergence on each fixed principal square and the bound $s=o(n)$ can be enforced in the same successive choice.

Let $U$ result from averaging all rows outside the first $s$ and all columns outside the first $s$, each within its own class. Let $V$ instead average the first $s$ rows and the first $s$ columns, leaving the bottom-right block of $A$ intact. Both are doubly stochastic. Put $H=U-J_n$, $D=V-J_n$. The following comparisons use Frobenius norm.

* $H$ differs by $o(1)$ from the upper-left block of $A$ padded with zeros. The two averaged strips of $U$ have squared norms at most $s/(n-s)$ each, since each distinguished margin sends at most one unit to the complement. The bottom-right rectangle of $U$ is constant, with value $(n-2s+\sum_{i,j\le s}A_{ij})/(n-s)^2$, within $O(s/n^2)$ of $1/n$. The norm of the part of $J_n$ incident to the first $s$ indices tends to zero.
* $D$ differs by $o(1)$ from the bottom-right block of $A-J_n$ padded with zeros. The two strip norms of $V$ are bounded by the original strip norms by contraction. The norm of its top square equals $s^{-1}\sum_{i,j\le s}A_{ij}=o(1)$. Indeed the unit flat vector on $[s]$ has pairing tending to zero against any fixed square-summable matrix evaluated on two such vectors: first truncate the matrix to a fixed square, then bound the tail in Frobenius norm. Apply this to $L$ and use its approximation by the top square of $A$.
* Consequently $\|B-H-D\|_F=o(1)$. Both $\|H\|_F,\|D\|_F$ are bounded and $\liminf\|H\|_F\ge\delta$. Every entry of $V$ tends uniformly to zero: an averaged row or column has entries at most $1/s$, and every remaining row has original norm $O(1/\sqrt s)$ on the untouched rectangle. Since $V$ is doubly stochastic, its maximum row and column Euclidean norms also tend to zero.

## The diffuse remainder has a spectral gap

Use the minimum total correlation $I$ of a permutation with given marginals. [Sra's pair-averaging theorem](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00118v1) implies $I(A)\le I(V)$, since each class average can be implemented by successive pairwise mixes. The variational bound gives $\log\operatorname{per}A\ge-I(A)\ge-I(V)$. Hence $-I(V)-\log d_n$ is bounded above.

If $\|D\|_{\rm op}$ tended to one on a subsequence, Cheeger's inequality for the symmetric bipartite matrix of $V$ would give a cut of size $h\le n$ with outgoing masses $y,z$ satisfying $y+z=o(h)$. The row and column parts both have size asymptotic to $h/2$. The largest entry of $V$ tends to zero, forcing $h\to\infty$ by the inside mass bound. The [two-class entropy calculation](../extremal-graphs/positive_density_minimum.md), with the [small-cut size estimate](diffuse_integrality.md), gives
$$-I(V)-\log d_n\ \ge\ \tfrac12\log\big(h/(1+\max(y,z))\big)-O(1).$$
For clarity, that proof averages the rectangles of $V$ to a matrix $C$ with $I(V)\le I(C)$, then constructs a permutation law of entropy $H_*$ with those marginals. Its calculated lower bound is already for $H_*-S(C)\le-I(C)\le-I(V)$. The last display diverges, a contradiction. Thus along the sequence $\|D\|_{\rm op}\le\rho<1$ eventually.

## Factoring the permanent through the localized portion

We claim
$$P_n(B)=P_n(H)P_n(D)+o(1). \tag{1}$$
First consider the coefficient of any fixed degree $j$ in the difference $P_n(zB)-P_n(zH)P_n(zD)$. In the [centered partition expansion](../real-radius/small_operator_perturbations.md) each pair of partitions specifies a bipartite graph with minimum degree at least two. Every contraction is bounded in absolute value by the product of the Frobenius norms of its edge matrices, by Hölder on the two successive sets of indices. Thus a Frobenius $o(1)$ change in any edge makes an $o(1)$ error in fixed degree. Expand the edges of $B$ into $H,D$ colors with this allowance. Replace each color by its approximating block from the first section *inside the contractions already having minimum degree two*. A connected component using both colors now vanishes, since adjacent edges of distinct colors would require an index in the disjoint index sets of both blocks. The monochromatic components factor. Choosing which labels belong to the first color cancels the binomial coefficient against the factorials of the two exponential generating functions. Equivalently, first choose the two label sets, then take arbitrary independent pairs of partitions on each. The centering signs and factors from each individual partition block are preserved. The finite-order normalizations $n^j/(n)_j$ tend to one in every fixed degree. This proves convergence of every fixed coefficient of the claimed difference to zero.

The passage to $z=1$ requires a bound beyond the unit circle; $\|H\|_{\rm op}$ itself may approach one. Approximate $H$ to any prescribed fixed Frobenius accuracy $\xi>0$ for all large $n$ by a *fixed centered matrix supported on finitely many rows and columns*. Indeed truncate $L$ to its first $r$ rows and columns, pad it to order $m$, then multiply on both sides by $I_m-J_m$ and pad further with zeros. The centering changes the Frobenius norm of the truncation by at most $2\sqrt{r/m}\|L\|_F$ in the sense of norm of the difference, by projecting the unit flat vector to its first $r$ entries. Take $r$, then $m$, sufficiently large and use $H\to L$ in Frobenius norm after padding. Call the finite centered matrix $K$.

Choose $\xi$ small compared with $1-\rho$. Both $B-K$ and $H-K$ then have operator norm bounded by $\rho+2\xi<1$ for all large $n$, and bounded Frobenius norms. Choose $R>1$ with $R(\rho+2\xi)<1$. For $F=B-K$ or $H-K$, the [uniform coefficient bound](../real-radius/hilbert_schmidt_limit.md) and [its complex form](../real-radius/small_operator_perturbations.md) bound $P_n(zF+wK)$ uniformly on $|z|\le R, |w|\le b$ for a sufficiently small fixed $b>0$. In fact the operator norms there stay strictly below one and the Frobenius norms stay bounded. The polynomial in $w$ has degree at most $m$ because only the first $m$ rows contain $K$. The Cauchy coefficient bounds on $|w|=b$ therefore give a uniform bound for $|w|\le R$ as well, with a possibly large constant depending only on $b,m,R$. Taking $w=z$ bounds $P_n(zB)$ and $P_n(zH)$ up to this radius. The same uniform bound for $P_n(zD)$ follows directly from the spectral gap, decreasing $R$ if necessary. Geometric control of the Taylor tails at one completes the proof of (1).

## Excluding a macroscopic entry

The [diffuse determinant approximation](../real-radius/hilbert_schmidt_limit.md) gives
$$P_n(D)=\det(I-D^TD)^{-1/2}+o(1)\ge\exp(\|D\|_F^2/2)+o(1).$$
In the untouched bottom-right block $W$ of $A$, the mass is at least $n-2s$. Each entry is an integer multiple of $1/k$, so $\sum W_{ij}^2\ge(n-2s)/k$. Subtracting $1/n$ from each entry gives
$$\|D\|_F^2\ge (n-2s)/k-2+(1-s/n)^2=u_0-o(1).$$
The actual localized matrix $U=J_n+H$ is doubly stochastic. Uniform stability gives $P_n(H)\ge\exp(\|H\|_F^2/32000000)$. Its limit inferior is at least $\exp(\delta^2/32000000)>1$. Using (1) contradicts the binary competitor $P_n(B)\le e^{u_0/2}+o(1)$. Thus the maximum normalized entry of every minimizing sequence tends to zero. The cap-preserving switch in [the diffuse theorem](diffuse_integrality.md) now removes any repeated entry and proves the assertion.

## An explicit check of the factor

Consider a single exceptional row and column with intersection $a$, all cross entries $c=(1-a)/m$, and outside block $(1-c)T$ of order $m$. Here $T$ is doubly stochastic. The permanent is exactly
$$a(1-c)^m\operatorname{per}T+c^2(1-c)^{m-1}\sum_{i,j}\operatorname{per}T(i\mid j).$$
This follows by separating permutations that use the exceptional cell from those crossing both ways. For the baseline $T=J_m$ the ratio to $d_{m+1}$ tends to $e^a(1-a+a^2)$, which remains finite even at the isolated vertex $a=1$. For even $m$, the [exact check](verify_localized_factor.py) uses the two-class matrix $T_{ij}=(1+b h_i h_j)/m$ with equally many $h_i=\pm1$. It checks 48 border formulas directly. For $a=2/3,b=1/2$ the ratio of the actual permanent to the baseline border permanent times $\operatorname{per}(T)/d_m$ is $0.992218847$ at $m=8$ and $0.999623187$ at $m=128$, computed as rational numbers before rounding. This checks the factor normalization on a family with both a localized entry and a diffuse singular value.
