# Tutte’s 3-flow conjecture

**Partial research.** Every balanced orientation of four boundary edges extends across a shore with at most two odd-degree vertices. In a smallest counterexample, a nontrivial four-edge cut would force a smaller single-type shore with an internal three-edge separation and a constrained five-edge boundary. For the equivalent 5-regular problem, a spanning orientation has an exact description by a pseudoforest bisection and a matching between its tree components. The universal existence step remains open. [Issue #375](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/375)

## Target and sources

Tutte’s conjecture asks whether every finite 4-edge-connected graph admits a nowhere-zero 3-flow. The equivalent modulo-three form is an orientation with outdegree minus indegree divisible by three at every vertex. Parallel edges are allowed, with no effect from deleting loops. The planar dual background is in Tutte’s chromatic-polynomial paper. Prałat and Wormald state the unrestricted conjecture and its known equivalence to the restriction to 5-regular 4-edge-connected graphs, and prove an asymptotic result for that restriction. [Tutte](https://doi.org/10.4153/cjm-1954-010-9) · [Prałat–Wormald](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.03572)

The November 24, 2026 source check found the 2013 six-edge-connectivity theorem, separate work on almost-even graphs, and 2026 results for forbidden cuts and for modular spanning-tree packing. Those sources leave the unrestricted 4-edge-connected statement open. The cut extension below uses prescribed divergence on a shore, which is stronger than finding some flow when no boundary directions are prescribed. Priority for the exact boundary conditions, the descent, and the matching description is provisional. [Lovász–Thomassen–Wu–Zhang](https://www.math.wvu.edu/~cqzhang/Publication-files/my-paper/JCTB-2013-3-flow-LTWZ.pdf) · [Almost-even graphs](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1877050921021748) · [Li et al., forbidden cuts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08377) · [Modular Nash–Williams theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07034)

## Prescribed orientation at a boundary vertex

For an undirected graph $H$, an integer vector $d$ with $\sum_v d(v)=0$ is the outdegree-minus-indegree vector of an orientation exactly when

```math
d(v)\equiv\deg_H(v)\pmod2,
\qquad
|d(S)|\le |\delta_H(S)|\quad\text{for every }S\subseteq V(H),
```

where $d(S)=\sum_{v\in S}d(v)$. Necessity follows from counting the oriented cut. For sufficiency, give each vertex capacity $(\deg_H(v)+d(v))/2$ and assign each edge to one endpoint as its tail. The bipartite assignment criterion says that the capacity in each set $S$ must cover the edges with both ends in $S$. To see it, any subset of edge nodes has all its endpoints in some $S$ and has size at most $|E(H[S])|$. The cut inequality gives the needed capacity bound and the total capacity is the number of edges.

**Four ports.** Let $J$ be 4-edge-connected, $w$ a vertex of degree four, and suppose $J-w$ contains at most two vertices of odd degree as measured in $J$. Prescribe an arbitrary two-in, two-out orientation at $w$. Write $H=J-w$. For $S\subseteq V(H)$ put $`k=|E_J(S,\{w\})|`$, $c=|\delta_H(S)|$, and let $b$ be the prescribed net divergence at the $S$ ends of its ports. For a nonempty proper $S$, using the cuts on either side of $w$ gives

```math
c\ge\max(4-k,k),\qquad |b|\le\min(k,4-k),\qquad
c+k\equiv |S\cap T|\pmod2,
```

where $T$ is the odd-degree set. It has size zero or two since $w$ is even. Seek total divergence zero at the even vertices and opposite divergences $+3,-3$ at the two odd vertices if present. The required internal divergence at each vertex is its sought total divergence minus its prescribed port divergence. This internal vector has the right parity and sums to zero.

When $|S\cap T|$ is even the desired total divergence on $S$ is zero, and $|b|\le c$. For a cut separating the two odd vertices, let its desired divergence be $3\varepsilon$, with $`\varepsilon\in\{1,-1\}`$. For $k=0,4$ one has $b=0$ and $c\ge4$. For $k=1,3$ one has $|b|=1$ and $c\ge4$ by parity. For $k=2$, $c\ge3$ and $c$ is odd. The only possible constraint on the sign is when $c=3$ and $b=2$ or $-2$, in which case $\varepsilon$ must have the sign of $b$.

Choose the shores of such constraining cuts to contain the first odd vertex and exclude the second. If one constraining set $S$ carries the two positive ports and another $S'$ the two negative ports, their intersection carries no port and their union all four. Both still separate the odd vertices. Their internal cut sizes are at least four and odd, so at least five. This contradicts submodularity since $c(S)+c(S')=6$. Thus all sign constraints are consistent, and the orientation criterion completes the extension.

Consequently any four-edge-cut shore with at most two odd vertices can be replaced by a single degree-four vertex. A flow on the resulting graph extends to the original graph, because contracting the opposite shore also gives a 4-edge-connected graph and a boundary with two edges of each sign. This can be iterated, allowing arbitrarily many odd vertices across multiple such shores.

**Five ports.** If $J$ has exactly two odd vertices and $w$ is one of them with degree five, every prescribed modulo-three orientation at $w$ extends. Its net divergence is $+3$ or $-3$, so prescribe the opposite divergence at the other odd vertex and zero elsewhere. For $S$ containing no odd vertex of $J-w$, the same cut argument gives $c\ge k-1$ and $c\equiv k\pmod2$. Thus $c\ge k\ge|b|$. A cut containing the other odd vertex is the complement of one just treated. The internal divergence vector again has the correct parity and zero sum.

The exact controls in [verify.py](verify.py) exercise all six four-port and all ten five-port prescriptions on two multigraphs. In the four-port control the two odd vertices are separated by three internal edges, and the port prescription forces the choice of the vertex receiving divergence $+3$.

## Descent in a smallest counterexample

At a four-edge-cut shore, contract the exterior to a vertex $w$. Up to reversing the whole orientation of this contracted graph, a feasible boundary type is the unordered partition of the four ports into the two sign pairs. Two shores can be joined if and only if their feasible type sets intersect. The following observations refer to types of modulo-three orientations.

First, in any 4-edge-connected $J$ at a vertex $w$ of degree four, at most one of the three port pairings fails to preserve 4-edge connectivity when $w$ is removed and the two pairs are joined by new edges. For any cut of $J-w$ with $k$ ports on one side, splitting preserves its size at least four automatically if $k=0,1,3,4$. When $k=2$, the cut gains two edges if both pairs cross it. A bad pairing thus separates its own two pairs by an internal cut of size at most three. Suppose two different pairings are bad, witnessed by cuts $S,S'$. The four quadrants have one port each, with internal cuts of size at least three. Cut submodularity for both $S,S'$ and $S,V(H)\setminus S'$ gives equality throughout, each quadrant has internal cut size three, and the two pairs of opposite quadrants have no connecting edges. The numbers of edges between consecutive quadrants then alternate $a,3-a,a,3-a$. The sizes of the two witnessed cuts are even, contradicting their required sizes of three. This also treats coincident port endpoints, since two witnesses for different pairings would have to put the four ports into distinct quadrants.

Now suppose $G$ is a vertex-minimal 4-edge-connected graph with no modulo-three orientation and has a four-edge cut whose shores each have at least two vertices. Each contracted shore has a flow by minimality. Their feasible type sets are disjoint, so one is a singleton. Choose an inclusion-minimal proper shore $A$ of a four-edge cut with a singleton type $P$. A one-vertex shore has all three types, and a shore missing only one vertex would contradict the choice of $G$, so $A$ and its exterior each have at least two vertices. If splitting the pairing $P$ in the contracted shore preserved 4-edge connectivity, minimality would give a flow after splitting. Lifting across each split pair gives opposite signs within that pair, hence a type different from $P$. Thus $P$ fails the splitting test.

Let $S$ and $A\setminus S$ separate the two pairs of external ports of $A$, with at most three edges between them. There are at least two such edges by 4-edge connectivity. If there are two, each subshore has four ports. A flow of type $P$ uses the grouped type at each subshore, with its two external ports on one side of the sign partition and its two internal ports on the other. Indeed, when the two external signs agree, the two internal signs must both be the opposite to attain zero divergence modulo three. If each subshore also admits a type splitting the external signs, each splits the two internal signs. Reverse one whole orientation if needed to join the two internal edges consistently. This gives a second feasible type on $A$. Hence at least one smaller subshore has only the grouped type, contrary to the choice of $A$.

There must therefore be exactly three edges between $S$ and $A\setminus S$. Both are five-edge-cut shores of $G$. In any modulo-three orientation of one of these shores, its boundary has a unique minority sign. If each shore can put this minority on one of its two external ports, its three internal ports point uniformly, and reversing one orientation if needed gives a consistent join. This would split the two external signs on each subshore and give a second type on $A$. Thus at least one of the five-edge shores can put its minority only on the three internal edges. A single vertex would permit all five positions, so this gives a nontrivial five-edge cut with constrained orientations. The argument localizes a possible obstruction without excluding it.

## Matching criterion for the 5-regular formulation

Every modulo-three orientation of a 5-regular graph has divergence $+3$ or $-3$ at each vertex. Their sum is zero, so the signs give a bisection $(A,B)$. Within $A$ every vertex has indegree at most one, and within $B$ every vertex has outdegree at most one. Each induced component therefore has at most as many edges as vertices, so is a tree or unicyclic. For a tree in $A$, exactly one vertex has internal indegree zero, and for a tree in $B$, exactly one has internal outdegree zero. Every unicyclic component uses up the corresponding quota at every vertex. The edges directed from $B$ to $A$ must therefore form a matching between tree components, meeting every tree component in exactly one edge.

Conversely, take a bisection in which both induced subgraphs are pseudoforests. Form the bipartite graph whose vertices are the tree components of the two sides, joined when there is an edge between them in the original graph. If it has a perfect matching, choose one edge realizing each match, directed from $B$ to $A$, and direct the remaining crossing edges from $A$ to $B$. Orient the $A$ trees away from their chosen endpoints and the $B$ trees toward theirs. On $A$ unicyclic components, orient the cycle consistently and the off-cycle trees away from it. On $B$ unicyclic components use the reverse rule. Then each $A$ vertex has indegree one and each $B$ vertex has outdegree one. This proves the equivalence. The number of tree components is the same on both sides since the bisection and 5-regularity give $|E(G[A])|=|E(G[B])|$. In particular the matching is guaranteed if all monochromatic components are trees of one common order, since the contact multigraph is then regular bipartite.

The matching condition can fail on a particular pseudoforest bisection even in a simple 5-edge-connected graph. The exact control in [verify.py](verify.py) uses two blocks of 13 vertices, each with five vertices on one bisection side and eight on the other. In a block, each of the five vertices $i=0,\ldots,4$ has neighbors $5+(5i+k)\bmod8$ for $k=0,\ldots,4$, and the eight vertices are paired consecutively by four edges. The second block uses the opposite bisection labels, and its seven degree-four vertices are joined to their respective copies in the first block. Exact enumeration verifies that the only cuts of size four inside either block isolate a single degree-four vertex. Hence a cut splitting both blocks has size at least eight, and a cut splitting just one block has size at least five after the interblock edges are included. The cut between the intact blocks has size seven. In the prescribed bisection the five isolated tree components of the first block are adjacent to just its four opposing two-vertex trees. This violates Hall’s condition. It rules out treating an arbitrary pseudoforest bisection as a flow certificate. The existence of a bisection with the matching property in every 5-regular 4-edge-connected graph remains open.
