# Independent review of the countable rainbow-star girth theorem

The reviewed statement is Problem 7 of Erdős, Galvin and Hajnal. A single vertex must see all the edge labels on edges to neighbors of its own vertex color. The host is countable, the edge palette is countably infinite, and the vertex coloring uses any finite number of colors. The authors' positive triangle-free example establishes the lower side of the sharp girth threshold. [Definition 6.3](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=24) · [Theorem 8.1](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=30) · [Problem 7](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=42)

## A coloring chosen before the edge labels

Suppose every set of $`k`$ vertices of a countable graph has finitely many common neighbors. Enumerate the vertices in a finite sequence or a sequence indexed by the nonnegative integers. When coloring a vertex, avoid the colors of its first $`k-1`$ earlier neighbors, using all its earlier neighbors if there are fewer. Any list of $`k`$ distinct colors permits this choice.

Fix a vertex $`u`$. Its earlier neighbors are finite in number. If a later neighbor $`v`$ has the same color as $`u`$, then $`u`$ was outside the first $`k-1`$ earlier neighbors of $`v`$. Thus $`v`$ is adjacent to $`u`$ and to $`k-1`$ distinct predecessors of $`u`$. There are only finitely many choices for those predecessors. Each resulting $`k`$-set has finitely many common neighbors. Consequently $`u`$ has finite degree in its own color class. For $`k=1`$ the condition says that each vertex already has finite degree.

This gives a second proof of the countable case of the research team’s [locally finite color-class lemma](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/1e6fae23ca04dbd44bc086739c383ce5a7cd8f42/research/erdos-galvin-hajnal-rainbow-star-girth/FINITE-PALETTES.md). The same coloring works for every edge labelling simultaneously. Only finitely many labels occur at any center on edges to its color class. In a graph with no four-cycle, two different vertices have at most one common neighbor. Taking $`k=2`$ proves the required obstruction. The published literature screened in the [audit](AUDIT.md) includes the unstarred simultaneous chromatic number. Additional checks for partitions into locally finite induced graphs, monochromatic infinite stars and almost-disjoint neighborhoods found results on different coloring requirements. In particular, a conflict-free coloring of a set system singles out a color inside each set without prescribing the color of a center. A majority coloring allows infinitely many same-color neighbors. [Hajnal, Juhász, Soukup and Szentmiklóssy](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.0181) · [*Majority choosability of countable graphs*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0195669823001476)

## Positive construction and checks

For the induction step in the research proof, $`k(k+1)+1`$ roots ensure $`k+1`$ with one color in every coloring with at most $`k+1`$ colors. Choosing one missing label at each of these roots selects an actual attached copy. That copy loses their common color, and the previous induction stage supplies a rainbow star. Each vertex of a copy is adjacent to one root. Thus a complete bipartite subgraph with at least two vertices in each part contains at most one root, and vertices on the same side in different copies would have at most one common neighbor. Removing a possible root from a putative $`K_{k+2,k+2}`$ leaves a forbidden biclique in one copy. Taking the disjoint union over finite $`k`$ preserves exclusion of an infinite balanced biclique. The avoiding-coloring theorem then forces the triangle-free union to contain a four-cycle.

The existing finite replay exercises the original list-coloring stage as well as the related label-profile transfer construction. Its finite tests supplement the infinite proof.
