# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s rainbow-star girth problem

## Sharp two-versus-three threshold at the first successor

For every finite \(L\ge4\), there is a graph on \(\aleph_1\) vertices of girth eight and odd girth \(2L+1\), with edges labelled by \(\omega\), such that every two-colouring of its vertices gives \(\aleph_1\) roots each having infinitely many neighbours of its own colour at every label. But in every \(C_4\)-free graph on at most \(\aleph_1\) vertices, the [companion list-colouring theorem](SUCCESSOR-HOSTS.md) produces a three-colouring whose monochromatic neighbourhoods are finite. Thus the palette-sensitive obstruction at \(\aleph_1\) is exactly three, even though the original countable Problem 7 needs only two. This is a structural successor extension of the original result, not a second resolution of it.

We prove the construction in a general form. Let \(\kappa\) be uncountable and regular. Let \(I\) be an infinite set of labels, \(\mu\) an infinite cardinal, and \(|I|,\mu<\kappa\). Suppose there is a map \(d:\kappa\times\kappa\to I^2\) taking every value on every \(R\times C\) with \(|R|=|C|=\kappa\). At \(\kappa=\aleph_1\), [Moore, *A solution to the L space problem*, Theorem 1.1](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~justin/Ftp/Lspace.pdf#page=2) gives this in ZFC: compose his map on increasing pairs with a surjection from \(\omega_1\) onto \(I^2\), assigning arbitrary values for the other order of the coordinates. It is the rectangular form of his result that is used, not just its restriction to a single uncountable square.

**Grid constraint.** Given two independent pools \(U=\{u_\alpha:\alpha<\kappa\}\) and \(W=\{w_\beta:\beta<\kappa\}\), add one private point \(p_{\alpha\beta}\) for each pair, adjacent to \(u_\alpha,w_\beta\), with those two edges labelled respectively by the coordinates of \(d(\alpha,\beta)\). Suppose a two-colouring of a larger host is deficient at every vertex of both pools: at each vertex there is some label with fewer than \(\mu\) same-colour neighbours. Then one cannot have \(\kappa\) vertices of one colour in \(U\) and \(\kappa\) of the opposite colour in \(W\).

Indeed, fix such opposite-colour sets, and thin each to cardinality \(\kappa\) with respective constant deficient labels \(i,j\); this uses regularity and \(|I|<\kappa\). Index the thinned pools without repetitions as \((u'_\xi,w'_\xi)\) for \(\xi<\kappa\), and let \(\varphi(\xi)\) consist of all \(\eta\ne\xi\) such that either the point for \((u'_\xi,w'_\eta)\) is of \(u'_\xi\)'s colour at its edge label \(i\), or the point for \((u'_\eta,w'_\xi)\) is of \(w'_\xi\)'s colour at its edge label \(j\). The dependency set has size \(<\mu\). [Hajnal’s free-set theorem](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1973-03.pdf) gives a free index set of size \(\kappa\). Split it into disjoint row and column index sets of size \(\kappa\), excluding the diagonal. By the defining rectangle property, some pair across these indices has value \((i,j)\). Its point has one of the two opposite endpoint colours, contradicting freeness at that endpoint. Consequently, provided every vertex is deficient, each pool has a unique colour of size \(\kappa\), and adjacent pools have the same such colour. For uniqueness, the adjacent pool has at least one colour of size \(\kappa\) and the opposite one must be small in the given pool.

**Host.** Use a root pool and one leaf pool for each index in \(I\times\mu\). Join each leaf pool to the root pool by a private path of \(L\) grid adjacencies using distinct intermediate pools (no actual direct edges between consecutive pools). All vertices introduced by grids are distinct. Add an ordinary star from the \(\xi\)th root-pool vertex to the \(\xi\)th vertex of every leaf pool; the edge to leaf pool \((i,\eta)\) has label \(i\). These are \(\kappa\) vertex-disjoint stars, and their edges are the only edges not contributed by grids.

If no vertex has the required neighbour counts, every pool is deficient. Along the connected pool tree all large colours coincide. Fewer than \(\kappa\) vertices in each pool are of the other colour, and there are fewer than \(\kappa\) pools. Thus fewer than \(\kappa\) stars meet an exceptional vertex. A whole common-colour star contradicts deficiency at its root. The construction survives deleting fewer than \(\kappa\) vertices: discard from the grid arguments the pool endpoints of any deleted grid points, which are fewer than \(\kappa\), and discard all stars affected by deletions or minority endpoints. If fewer than \(\kappa\) good vertices existed in the whole graph, delete all of them. No remaining vertex can gain neighbours by deletion, so every remaining pool vertex is deficient, contradicting the same argument. Thus there are \(\kappa\) good vertices overall; they need not be in the root pool.

**Girth.** The grid-only graph is the one-subdivision of a simple bipartite graph (the pool tree is bipartite), so it has girth at least eight; any one grid has an eight-cycle. Call the root pool and leaf pools active. Any two distinct active pools have tree-distance at least \(L\). A maximal nonempty grid-edge run in a mixed simple cycle starts and ends in active pools. In a cycle of length less than eight its endpoints must be in the same active pool, since crossing between distinct active pools needs at least \(2L\) grid edges. In that case a grid run has length at least four: it goes between distinct vertices of one pool, whereas a two-edge grid path joins distinct adjacent pools. So a short mixed cycle has only one such run. The remaining segment is a path in a single added star and joins distinct active pools, a contradiction. Hence total girth is exactly eight. Collapsing each pool to one vertex and each grid point to the subdivision vertex of its pool-tree edge gives a homomorphism to a bouquet of cycles of length \(2L+1\). This rules out shorter odd cycles, since an odd closed walk contains an odd cycle. One added star edge and the grid path through its corresponding pools realize length \(2L+1\).

At \(\kappa=\aleph_1,I=\omega,\mu=\aleph_0\), the claimed conclusion follows. The same construction works whenever the displayed rectangle principle holds for the chosen cardinal and palette. No upper bound of eight is claimed for all uncountable two-colour witnesses. The separate construction of a single high-girth host handling every finite vertex palette is not settled here.
