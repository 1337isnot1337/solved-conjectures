# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s rainbow-star girth problem

## Defective list colourings above the palette cardinality

The [countable girth result](../../results/erdos-galvin-hajnal-rainbow-star-girth/PROOF.md) and the [same-cardinality supplement](FINITE-PALETTES.md) use list colourings whose monochromatic neighbourhoods are smaller than the edge palette. This note extends that obstruction when the host is larger than the palette by any finite number of cardinal successors. The original Problem 7 remains the countable problem; this is a separate strengthening of the obstruction, not a new claim to have resolved that problem.

**Theorem.** Let $`\lambda`$ be an infinite cardinal, let $`s\ge2`$ and $`n\ge0`$ be integers, and let $`|V(G)|\le\lambda^{+n}`$. Suppose every $`s`$-element vertex set has fewer than $`\lambda`$ common neighbours. Either assume $`\lambda`$ is regular, or assume these common-neighbour cardinalities have a common upper bound $`\rho<\lambda`$. Given arbitrary lists of size at least
```math
s+n(s-1),
```
one can colour the vertices from their lists so that every vertex has fewer than $`\lambda`$ neighbours of its own colour.

In particular, take $`\lambda=\aleph_0,s=2`$. For every finite $`n`$, every $`K_{2,t}`$-free graph (fixed finite $`t\ge2`$) on at most $`\aleph_n`$ vertices is the union of $`n+2`$ vertex classes inducing locally finite graphs, and the same bound holds for lists. Finiteness of every pair's common neighbourhood is enough, without a uniform finite bound. Under any countably infinite edge palette no root in this vertex colouring sees every label among its own-colour neighbours. In particular, a $`C_4`$-free graph of size at most $`\aleph_1`$ cannot force a rainbow star against three colours, even when its edge palette is countably infinite rather than $`\aleph_1`$.

**Proof.** For $`n=0`$, order the vertices by an initial segment of $`\lambda`$. From lists of size $`s`$, colour in order, and on assigning $`v`$ its available colour forbid that colour at each later neighbour unless this would exhaust its list. At limit stages an unprocessed vertex has a colour left: a finite list cannot have lost all colours for the first time at a limit. Any same-colour later neighbour $`w`$ of $`v`$ occurred among the exceptions when processing $`v`$; the other $`s-1`$ entries of $`w`$'s list were already blocked by $`s-1`$ distinct earlier neighbours of $`w`$. Thus these $`w`$'s lie in a union of fewer than $`\lambda`$ of the specified common neighbourhoods, since $`v`$ has fewer than $`\lambda`$ earlier vertices and $`s`$ is finite. The union has size less than $`\lambda`$ by regularity or the uniform bound. The earlier same-colour neighbours of $`v`$ also number less than $`\lambda`$.

For the successor step, put $`\kappa=\lambda^{+(n+1)}`$. Close subsets of $`V(G)`$ under taking common neighbours of every $`s`$-tuple: repeatedly adjoin those common neighbourhoods and take the union after $`\omega`$ rounds. This closure has size at most $`\max(\lambda,|X|)`$ when applied to $`X`$, and a union of an increasing chain of closed sets is closed (each tuple is finite). Form an increasing continuous chain $`(A_\alpha)_{\alpha<\kappa}`$ of closed sets of size $`<\kappa`$, starting empty and covering all vertices, by closing after adjoining the next enumerated vertex. A vertex outside $`A_\alpha`$ has at most $`s-1`$ neighbours in $`A_\alpha`$.

Process the blocks $`A_{\alpha+1}\setminus A_\alpha`$ in order. In each vertex's list delete the colours on its already-coloured neighbours, losing at most $`s-1`$ colours. Each block has size at most the predecessor cardinal $`\lambda^{+n}`$, and the same common-neighbour bounds hold inside it. The inductive theorem colours the block from the remaining lists. Every edge between distinct blocks is bichromatic by construction, so the only same-colour neighbours of a vertex are inside its own block. This completes the induction.

**Scope.** At arbitrary cardinals the proof only gives one deletion allowance per layer in the closure decomposition. It gives no fixed finite bound when the number of cardinal-successor layers is unbounded; an $`\aleph_\omega`$-sized graph is not covered by the countable-palette specialization. It also does not establish that $`n+2`$ colours are necessary. The closure step is the finite-common-neighbour device underlying the classical Erdős–Hajnal colouring-number argument, also used in the [finite-to-countable edge Ramsey research](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1085). The added conclusion here is its combination with defective list colouring at a smaller palette cardinality.
