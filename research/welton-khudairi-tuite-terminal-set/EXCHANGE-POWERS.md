# Terminal cliques in powers of exchange graphs

**Partial result for the [Welton–Khudairi–Tuite terminal-set conjecture](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/869).** Every positive graph power of a Hamming graph, Johnson graph, Grassmann graph, or finite classical dual polar graph has a terminal clique. The assertions hold for unrestricted radii and diameters. These supplement the [rank-threshold forms graphs](RANK-THRESHOLDS.md).

For a connected graph \(G\), the power \(G^s\) joins distinct vertices with \(G\)-distance at most the positive integer \(s\). Its distance is \(\lceil d_G/s\rceil\): split a \(G\)-geodesic into segments of length at most \(s\) for the upper bound and use the triangle inequality in \(G\) for the reverse. Thus it suffices to find a set \(C\) of \(G\)-diameter at most \(s\) such that for every \(x\notin C\) its two extreme distances to \(C\) differ by \(s\). Then \(C\) is a clique in \(G^s\), these extremes differ by one in \(G^s\), and the corresponding clique edge witnesses terminality. Graph powers at or above the whole diameter are complete and trivial.

## Hamming and Johnson

For the Hamming graph, whose distances count differing coordinates, take all words with a fixed pattern off a set of \(s\) coordinates. In each free coordinate a word can match \(x\) or differ from \(x\); hence the extremes differ by exactly \(s\). This also works with different finite alphabets of size at least two at different coordinates.

For \(J(v,k)\), assume \(2k\le v\) by complementation. Distances are \(k-|X\cap Y|\), by exchanging one element at a time. Fix a \((k-s)\)-set \(A\) and take all \(k\)-sets containing \(A\). They have pairwise distances at most \(s\). Every \(k\)-set \(X\) has at least \(s\) elements outside \(A\), and at least \(s\) elements of the ground set lie outside \(X\cup A\), by \(v\ge2k\). Complete \(A\) with \(s\) elements from the first or second set; the intersection sizes with \(X\) differ by \(s\).

## Grassmann and dual polar

In the Grassmann graph on \(k\)-spaces of \(V\) assume \(2k\le\dim V\) by duality. Distances are \(k-\dim(X\cap Y)\); this follows by exchanging one dimension at a time. Fix a \((k-s)\)-space \(A\) and take all \(k\)-spaces containing \(A\). In \(V/A\), the image of \(X\) has dimension between \(s\) and \(k\) and codimension at least \(s\). An extending \(s\)-space in \(V/A\) can thus lie inside this image or be disjoint from it. The two extension spaces intersect \(X\) in dimensions differing by \(s\).

For a finite classical dual polar graph, vertices are maximal totally singular or isotropic \(k\)-spaces and the base distance is again \(k-\dim(X\cap Y)\), as in the [rank-one argument](DISTANCE-REGULAR.md). Fix a totally singular \((k-s)\)-space \(A\). All its maximal extensions have pairwise distance at most \(s\). Put \(w=\dim(X\cap A)\). Since \(A\cap X^\perp=A\cap X\) by maximality of \(X\), the pairing of \(A\) and \(X\) has rank \(k-s-w\), and \(X\cap A^\perp\) has dimension \(s+w\). Its image \(B\) in \(A^\perp/A\) is a maximal totally singular \(s\)-space. Maximal extensions of \(A\) correspond to such maximal spaces in the rank-\(s\) residue, and their intersection dimension with \(X\) is \(w\) plus their quotient-space intersection dimension with \(B\). One can take the extension corresponding to \(B\), or the extension corresponding to a disjoint maximal space in the residue. The latter exists by a Witt basis extending \(B\): the other vector in each hyperbolic pair spans a disjoint maximal space. This changes the intersection dimension by \(s\).

## A matroid variant and its limitation

Let \(M\) have rank \(k\) and suppose no basis contains a cocircuit, equivalently the complement of every basis spans. In its basis graph, where neighbors differ by one exchange, \(d(X,Y)=k-|X\cap Y|\) by basis exchange. Fix any independent \((k-s)\)-set \(A\) and use the clique of all bases containing \(A\) in the \(s\)-th power. Any basis \(X\) spans, so one can extend \(A\) entirely by elements of \(X\setminus A\). The complement of \(X\) also spans, so one can extend \(A\) entirely by elements outside \(X\). Therefore \(X\)'s two distances differ by \(s\) and the clique is terminal. In rank one for the graph power, the weaker hypothesis that there exists just one dependent cocircuit suffices: choose \(A\) a basis of its complementary hyperplane. The bases in the clique are exactly \(A+e\) for elements \(e\) of that cocircuit, and every other basis contains some but not all such \(e\). The dual case follows by complementing all bases.

The last matroid statements do not assert that all matroids have a dependent cocircuit or that all basis graph powers are covered. The standard Hamming, Grassmann, polar and matroid metric facts are discussed in [Brouwer–Cohen–Neumaier](https://aeb.win.tue.nl/drg/index.html) and [Oxley](https://www.math.ens.psl.eu/~benoist/refs/Oxley.pdf). The [terminal-set source](https://oro.open.ac.uk/98050/9/98050final.pdf) and [survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.19385) do not state these power results. Targeted source searches did not find another matching proof.
