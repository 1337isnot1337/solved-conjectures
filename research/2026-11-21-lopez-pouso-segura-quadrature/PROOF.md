# López Pouso–Segura's node and partial-moment conjectures

Research note, 21 November 2026. Status: complete proposed disproof of the exact upper inequalities in published Conjectures 3 and 7; independent review and the prior-resolution audit are pending. The separate Bessel-limit assertions, Conjectures 2, 5, and 8, are not contradicted.

## 1. Exact source and scope

Óscar López Pouso and Javier Segura, *Uniform relations between the Gauss–Legendre nodes and weights*, Journal of Inequalities and Applications (2025), article 40, [published full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13660-025-03283-w), [arXiv:2305.19128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19128).

Let $`x_1<\cdots<x_n`$ and $`w_1,\ldots,w_n`$ be the ordinary Gauss–Legendre nodes and weights on $`[-1,1]`$, and let $`\kappa=n+1/2`$. The source defines
```math
\bar z_0=-1,\qquad
\bar z_i=-1+\sum_{j=1}^i w_j,\qquad
z_i=\frac{\bar z_{i-1}+\bar z_i}{2},
\qquad
\alpha_i=-2\sum_{j=1}^i x_jw_j.
```
Published Conjecture 3 asserts, for the defined nonzero ratios,
```math
0<\frac{x_i}{z_i}-1<\frac{\pi^2}{12\kappa^2}.
\tag{1}
```
Published Conjecture 7 asserts, for the interior intermediate nodes,
```math
0<\frac{\alpha_i}{1-\bar z_i^2}-1
<\frac{\pi^2}{12\kappa^2}.
\tag{2}
```
The source also discusses asymptotic evidence and an optimal asymptotic constant. An asymptotic expansion with a remainder of unspecified sign does not imply either strict upper inequality. We disprove the displayed inequalities. In addition, we prove that (1) fails at the smallest positive node for every sufficiently large even degree. Thus the failure of (1) persists if its exact upper inequality is required only eventually in the degree.

## 2. Exact common counterexample

Take $`n=2`$. Then
```math
x_1=-\frac1{\sqrt3},\quad x_2=\frac1{\sqrt3},
\quad w_1=w_2=1,\quad \kappa=\frac52,
```
and consequently
```math
\bar z_1=0,\qquad z_2=\frac12,\qquad
\alpha_1=\frac2{\sqrt3}.
```
Both left-hand expressions in the proposed upper inequalities are therefore
```math
\frac{x_2}{z_2}-1
=\frac{\alpha_1}{1-\bar z_1^2}-1
=\frac2{\sqrt3}-1.
```

## 3. Failure at arbitrarily large degrees

Here we establish the stronger asymptotic statement
```math
\frac{x_{m+1}}{z_{m+1}}
=1+\frac{\pi^2}{12\kappa^2}
+\frac{\pi^4}{120\kappa^4}
+O(\kappa^{-6}),
\qquad n=2m,\quad \kappa=2m+\frac12.
\tag{4}
```
The positive fourth-order term proves failure of (1) for all sufficiently large even $`n`$.

### 3.1 The central node and its exact weight

By symmetry, the total weight of the negative nodes is 1. Thus
```math
\bar z_m=0,\qquad z_{m+1}=\frac{w_{m+1}}2.
\tag{5}
```
Write $`x=x_{m+1}>0`$ and $`w=w_{m+1}`$. For the usual normalization $`P_n(1)=1`$, the exact Gauss–Legendre weight formula is
```math
w=\frac{2}{(1-x^2)P_n'(x)^2}.
\tag{6}
```
Set $`t=\arcsin x`$ and
```math
Y(t)=\sqrt{\cos t}\,P_n(\sin t).
```
The Legendre differential equation gives
```math
Y''(t)+\left(\kappa^2+\frac{1}{4\cos^2t}\right)Y(t)=0.
\tag{7}
```
At a zero $`t`$, one has
```math
Y'(t)=\cos^{3/2}t\,P_n'(x),
\qquad
\frac{2x}{w}=\tan t\,Y'(t)^2.
\tag{8}
```

```math
f_\varepsilon(u)
=\frac{\sqrt{\cos(u/\kappa)}\,P_n(\sin(u/\kappa))}
       {P_n(0)}.
```
Then
```math
f_\varepsilon''(u)+
\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
       +\frac{\varepsilon^2u^2}{4}
       +O(\varepsilon^3)\right)f_\varepsilon(u)=0,
\qquad
f_\varepsilon(0)=1,\quad f_\varepsilon'(0)=0.
\tag{9}
```

$`1+(\varepsilon/4)\sec^2(\sqrt\varepsilon u)`$; it is analytic in $`\varepsilon`$ near zero on such an interval. Standard analytic dependence for the initial-value problem, or the corresponding Volterra integral equation, therefore justifies the expansion and its uniform remainder below.


```math

\qquad

\tag{10}
```


Put $`\varepsilon=\kappa^{-2}`$ and $`u=\kappa t`$. For even $`n`$ define
```math
f_\varepsilon(u)
=\frac{\sqrt{\cos(u/\kappa)}\,P_n(\sin(u/\kappa))}
       {P_n(0)}.
```
Then
```math
f_\varepsilon''(u)+
\left(1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
       +\frac{\varepsilon^2u^2}{4}
       +O(\varepsilon^3)\right)f_\varepsilon(u)=0,
\qquad
f_\varepsilon(0)=1,\quad f_\varepsilon'(0)=0.
\tag{9}
```

$`1+(\varepsilon/4)\sec^2(\sqrt\varepsilon u)`$; it is analytic in $`\varepsilon`$ near zero on such an interval. Standard analytic dependence for the initial-value problem, or the corresponding Volterra integral equation, therefore justifies the expansion and its uniform remainder below.

```math
f_\varepsilon(u)=\cos u+\varepsilon f_1(u)
+\varepsilon^2f_2(u)+O(\varepsilon^3).
```

```math
f_1(u)=-\frac{u\sin u}{8},
\qquad
f_2(u)=\int_0^u\sin(u-v)
\left(\frac{v\sin v}{32}-\frac{v^2\cos v}{4}\right)\,dv.
\tag{10}
```
a=\pi/2`$. Elementary integration yields
```math
f_1(a)=-\frac a8,\qquad f_1'(a)=-\frac18,
\qquad f_1''(a)=\frac a8,
```
```math
f_2(a)=\frac{9a}{128}-\frac{a^3}{24},
\qquad
f_2'(a)=\frac9{128}-\frac{7a^2}{128}.
\tag{11}
```
Since $`\cos u`$ has a simple first positive zero at $`a`$, the implicit-function theorem gives a simple zero $`u_\varepsilon`$ near $`a`$. Uniform convergence before that zero shows that it is the first positive zero. Substituting (10)–(11) gives
```math
u_\varepsilon
=a-\frac a8\varepsilon+
\left(\frac{11a}{128}-\frac{a^3}{24}\right)\varepsilon^2
+O(\varepsilon^3),
\tag{12}
```
and
```math
f_\varepsilon'(u_\varepsilon)^2
=1+\frac{\varepsilon}{4}
+\frac{a^2-1}{8}\varepsilon^2+O(\varepsilon^3).
\tag{13}
```

### 3.3 The central normalization

The exact central value is
```math
P_{2m}(0)=(-1)^m\frac{\binom{2m}{m}}{4^m}
=(-1)^m\frac{\Gamma(m+1/2)}
                 {\sqrt\pi\,\Gamma(m+1)}.
```
The classical gamma-ratio expansion ([DLMF 5.11.8 and 5.11.13](https://dlmf.nist.gov/5.11)), with
$`m+1/2=\kappa/2+1/4`$ and $`m+1=\kappa/2+3/4`$, gives
```math
P_{2m}(0)^2
=\frac{2}{\pi\kappa}
\left(1-\frac{\varepsilon}{8}
+\frac{11\varepsilon^2}{128}+O(\varepsilon^3)\right).
\tag{14}
```
For clarity, the logarithm of the parenthesized factor is
$`-\varepsilon/8+5\varepsilon^2/64+O(\varepsilon^3)`$, so its exponential gives exactly the coefficient $`11/128`$ in (14). These expansions have ordinary remainder estimates along the positive real axis.

### 3.4 The resulting ratio

Equations (5), (8), and the definition of $`f_\varepsilon`$ imply
```math
\frac{x_{m+1}}{z_{m+1}}
=\frac{2x}{w}
=\tan(u_\varepsilon/\kappa)\,
 \kappa^2P_{2m}(0)^2 f_\varepsilon'(u_\varepsilon)^2.
\tag{15}
```
Multiplying (13) and (14), the relevant factor is
```math
1+\frac{\varepsilon}{8}
+\left(\frac{a^2}{8}-\frac9{128}\right)\varepsilon^2
+O(\varepsilon^3).
```
Also,
```math
\kappa\tan(u_\varepsilon/\kappa)
=a+\left(-\frac a8+\frac{a^3}{3}\right)\varepsilon
+\left(\frac{11a}{128}-\frac{a^3}{24}
       -\frac{a^3}{8}+\frac{2a^5}{15}\right)\varepsilon^2
+O(\varepsilon^3).
```
Using $`2a/\pi=1`$, the constant and quadratic-in-$`a`$ fourth-order correction terms cancel. Equation (15) becomes
```math
\frac{x_{m+1}}{z_{m+1}}
=1+\frac{a^2}{3}\varepsilon
+\frac{2a^4}{15}\varepsilon^2
+O(\varepsilon^3),
```
which is exactly (4).

In particular,
```math
\kappa^4\left(
\frac{x_{m+1}}{z_{m+1}}-1-\frac{\pi^2}{12\kappa^2}
\right)\longrightarrow\frac{\pi^4}{120}>0.
```
The exact upper bound in Conjecture 3 therefore fails for every sufficiently large even degree, at a specifically identified node.

## 4. What this does and does not settle

The exact inequalities in the published Conjectures 3 and 7 are disproved by (3). For Conjecture 3, (4) also rules out an eventual version of its exact upper inequality. We do not use a finite computation as evidence for an unrestricted theorem: the negative answer is the exact certificate (3), and the infinite family is justified by the differential equation and its controlled asymptotic expansion.

The leading asymptotic constant $`\pi^2/12`$ remains compatible with (4). Neither an $`O(\kappa^{-2})`$ estimate nor an expansion with an $`O(\kappa^{-4})`$ remainder is disproved. The source's Bessel-limit Conjectures 2, 5, and 8 concern different limits and can all be true while the strict finite-degree upper bounds fail.

The counterexample is one joint obstruction to the two related assertions, not two unrelated named results. The prior-resolution audit must be completed before this package is counted as new.


## 5. Existing asymptotic ingredients
