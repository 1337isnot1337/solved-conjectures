# Steinerberger's circular triangle-area question: finite-rank configurations

**Status:** sharp-order partial result for positive finite rank, not a solution for arbitrary real angles. The torsion-only results overlap with, and can also be deduced from, Felix Huber's October 2026 regular-polygon classification, credited below. The independent torsion proof here is included to illustrate the short-relation method.

For a set \(A\subset S^1\) and a pin \(p\in A\), write \(\mathcal A_p(A)\) for the set of unsigned areas of triangles with one vertex at \(p\) and the other two distinct vertices in \(A\setminus\{p\}\).

**Theorem.** Suppose the phases of \(A\) lie in a coset \(z_0\Gamma\) of a multiplicative subgroup \(\Gamma\le S^1\) of finite rank \(r\). Then, for every \(p\in A\) and \(n=|A|\),
\[
  |\mathcal A_p(A)|\ \ge\ \frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{12}-C_r(n-1),
\]
where \(C_r\) depends only on \(r\). In fact, apart from \(O_r(n)\) exceptional pairs at each pin, any equal-area triangle at that pin is congruent to the given triangle. In particular, \(|\mathcal A_p(A)|\gg_r n^2\) for \(n\ge3\). This applies to an arbitrary subset of any regular polygon, uniformly in the polygon's order; it also applies to all sets drawn from a group generated by boundedly many irrational rotations and arbitrary roots of unity.

The coefficient \(1/12\) is optimal: for the full regular \(N\)-gon, the number of possible unordered triples of positive integer circular gaps summing to \(N\) is \(N^2/12+O(N)\), giving an upper bound even without pinning. The proof also rederives a consequence of Huber's stronger complete classification: for \(N\ge3\) coprime to \(2310\), triangles in a regular \(N\)-gon have equal area only if they are congruent.

## 1. Short relations in a multiplicative group

For a fixed \(\Gamma\), let \(R\) include \(1,-1\) and every ratio \(z_j/z_i\) from a minimal zero relation
\[
  c_1z_1+\cdots+c_tz_t=0,\qquad
  2\le t\le 12,\quad c_i\in\{+1,-1\},\quad z_i\in\Gamma.
\]
Minimal means that no nonempty proper subset of the labelled terms has zero sum. For \(t=2\) the ratio is \(\pm1\). For \(t\ge3\), divide by \(-c_1z_1\) and apply Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt, Theorem 1.1, to the normalized tuple in \(\Gamma^{t-1}\), of rank \((t-1)r\). All its nonempty subsums are nonzero by minimality of the original relation. For each coefficient tuple there are at most
\[
 \exp\!\left((6(t-1))^{3(t-1)}((t-1)r+1)\right)
\]
solutions, uniformly in \(\Gamma\). All pairwise ratios are determined by the normalized tuple. Consequently \(|R|\le B_r\) for a constant depending only on \(r\).

The displayed bound gives \(B_r\le \exp(C(r+1))\) for an absolute constant \(C\). Thus one still gets at least \((1/12-o(1))n^2\) pinned areas along families whose ranks grow as \(o(\log n)\).

If \(\Gamma\) consists of roots of unity, Mann's theorem gives the explicit choice \(R=\mu_{2310}\), where \(2310=2\cdot3\cdot5\cdot7\cdot11\), since the ratios in a minimal relation of length \(t\) have order dividing the product of primes at most \(t\).

## 2. The area formula and the exceptional set

Rotate so \(p=1\), after which all phases lie in \(\Gamma\). Write the other vertices as \(x,y\). For an ordered pair \((x,y)\) of distinct points other than 1, put
\[
 (u,v,w)=(x,y/x,1/y).
\]
These are the directed circular gap multipliers and \(uvw=1\). If \(\Delta_{\mathrm{or}}\) is signed area, the determinant identity is
\[
 4i\Delta_{\mathrm{or}}(1,x,y)=
 u+v+w-u^{-1}-v^{-1}-w^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
Indeed, the right side is \((x-x^{-1})+(y/x-x/y)-(y-y^{-1})\), which is twice \(i\) times the imaginary part of \(\overline{x-1}(y-1)\). For each unordered pair choose the ordering \((x,y)\) giving positive signed area.

Call the pair good if no ratio of two different slots in
\[
  (u,v,w,u^{-1},v^{-1},w^{-1})
\]
belongs to \(R\). For each of the \(15\) slot pairs and each \(h\in R\), the corresponding ratio equation has the form \(x^a y^b=h\) with \((a,b)\ne(0,0)\) and \(|a|,|b|\le 2\). If \(b\ne0\), at most two \(y\)'s work for each fixed \(x\); if \(b=0\), use the same bound by fixing \(y\). Thus at most \(30|R|(n-1)\) ordered pairs, and no more unordered pairs, are excluded. This includes both orientations since the underlying set of six slots is preserved by reversing orientation and \(R\) is inverse-closed.

## 3. Good triangles have rigid area collisions

Suppose (1) for a good pair equals (1) for another pair, each ordered with positive area. Subtract the equations and partition the 12 labelled terms into minimal zero blocks, which is possible by repeatedly removing a nonempty zero subset minimal by inclusion. A block cannot contain two slots from the first six, since their ratio would belong to \(R\). Every block containing one first slot must contain at least one second slot. There are six first and six second slots, so there are exactly six blocks, each with one from each pair.

Write \((u',v',w')\) for the second positive triple of gap multipliers. A cancellation match identifies each entry of \((u',v',w')\) either with one of \((u,v,w)\), or with the negative of its inverse. Once the slot containing an entry of \((u',v',w')\) is matched, the inverse slot must be matched to the inverse of the same original entry: its matched original term is forced because all original six monomial values are distinct even up to sign (goodness excludes \(\pm1\) ratios). Thus each of the three original entry labels occurs exactly once. After permuting, the second triple is obtained by replacing \(p_j\) by \(-p_j^{-1}\) on a set \(J\subseteq\{1,2,3\}\), where \((p_1,p_2,p_3)=(u,v,w)\).

Because both products equal 1, the replacements would require \((-1)^{|J|}\prod_{j\in J}p_j^{-2}=1\). For \(|J|=1\) this gives \(p_j^2=-1\), impossible by goodness. For \(|J|=2\) it gives \(p_\ell^2=1\) for the remaining entry, also impossible. For \(|J|=3\) the product changes to \(-1\). Hence \(J\) is empty. The circular gap multiset, and therefore the triangle congruence class, is the same. At the given pin, at most six positively oriented ordered pairs can realize it: a permutation of \((u,v,w)\) determines \(x=u\) and \(y=uv\). Dividing the number of good pairs by six proves the theorem, for example with \(C_r=5B_r\). A uniform positive \(c_r\) for the remaining bounded values \(3\le n\le 120B_r+6\) follows from the existence of one triangle.

## 4. Roots of unity, and exact regular polygons away from small primes

**Prior work.** Felix Huber, *Equal Areas of Vertex Triangles in a Regular Polygon* (1 October 2026), linked from [OEIS A400213](https://oeis.org/A400213), gives a full regular-polygon classification and exact counts for all \(N\). It also gives the sharp pinned lower bound for arbitrary torsion subsets: embed the subset in a common regular \(N\)-gon. In Huber's list, exceptional gap multisets comprise finitely many isolated patterns and two one-parameter families whose three gap entries all have nonzero slope in the parameter. Fixing a first gap at the pin determines the parameter up to a bounded number of choices, even when the order \(N\) is much larger than the subset. So there are only \(O(n)\) exceptional pinned pairs, with absolute constant, and all nonexceptional pairs yield the same \(1/12\) coefficient by division by at most six. The arguments below are independent but are not stronger than this prior rank-zero classification.

For arbitrary torsion configurations, \(B_0=2310\) suffices. For a regular \(N\)-gon this proves \(N^2/12+O(N)\) distinct areas at every pin, with absolute implicit constant. By rotational symmetry the pinned and unpinned area sets coincide for the full polygon.

If \(\gcd(N,2310)=1\), each minimal cancellation block consists entirely of copies of the same root by Mann's theorem. In the six slots of a triangle, no positive slot can equal a negative slot. Indeed a gap multiplier \(p_j\) cannot equal its own inverse, because it is not 1 and \(-1\notin\mu_N\). Nor can \(p_j=p_\ell^{-1}\) for \(j\ne\ell\), since the remaining multiplier would have to equal 1. Thus no inverse cancellation can occur even for isosceles triangles. Gathering equal roots in the area identity forces the multisets of positive gap multipliers to be equal. This proves the exact congruence criterion.

For the full polygon with \(\gcd(N,2310)=1\), the number of areas is exactly \((N^2-1)/12\). To count unordered integer gap triples, apply Burnside's lemma to the \(\binom{N-1}{2}\) ordered positive triples summing to \(N\): each of the three transpositions fixes \((N-1)/2\) triples, while the two 3-cycles fix none since \(3\nmid N\). Every gap triple can be realized at every pin. For a subset of a regular \(N\)-gon satisfying this coprimality, area coincidences likewise occur exactly between congruent triangles.

## 5. The remaining gap and sources

- If \(|AA|\le K|A|\), Green and Ruzsa's [Freiman theorem for arbitrary abelian groups](https://doi.org/10.1112/JLMS/JDL021) puts \(A\) in a translate of a bounded-rank coset progression in \(S^1\). The finite subgroup in the progression contributes no rank. Thus the theorem gives the same leading coefficient and \(O_K(n)\) error for all bounded-doubling configurations, without an independently stated rank hypothesis.
- Stefan Steinerberger poses both the unpinned and stronger every-pin quadratic questions in [*Some Open Problems*, §14](https://faculty.washington.edu/steinerb/openproblems.pdf), last update 31 March 2026; see also his [original MathOverflow question](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/446704/areas-of-triangles-induced-by-n-points-on-mathbbs1). His displayed half-angle product is the **squared** area; (1) is the signed area formula.
- Claudiu Valculescu and Frank de Zeeuw, [*Distinct pinned triangle areas on algebraic curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3867v1), later titled [*Distinct values of bilinear forms on algebraic curves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.3867v3), gives \(\Omega(n^{4/3})\) on an uncentered circle relative to the pin (translate the pin to the origin). Steinerberger's March 2026 update also credits Mixon with this exponent.
- Jan-Hendrik Evertse, Hans Peter Schlickewei and Wolfgang M. Schmidt, [*Linear equations in variables which lie in a multiplicative group*](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p04), Ann. of Math. 155 (2002), 807–836, Theorem 1.1. It applies over \(\mathbb C\), not merely to algebraic phases.
- H. B. Mann, [*On linear relations between roots of unity*](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300005210), Mathematika 12 (1965), 107–117, Theorem 1.
- Searches combining the pinned-area question with finite-rank multiplicative groups and the Evertse–Schlickewei–Schmidt theorem did not identify a prior result for positive rank. The rank-zero overlap with Huber is credited above. This is not exhaustive priority clearance.
- The argument does not control arbitrary rank growth. It bounds the exceptional ratios by rank, rather than showing their rarity for every real configuration.
