# Divisors with repeated cyclotomic factors

The [ultimate-periodicity theorem](cyclotomic-divisors.md) holds for every fixed nonconstant monic polynomial $`P\in\mathbb Z[X]`$ dividing $`(X^h-1)^e`$, with arbitrary fixed $`h,e\ge1`$. In particular it holds for every fixed power $`(k^m-1)^e`$. The multiplicative order modulo the entire divisor can grow exponentially in $`m`$. A loop in the initial numerator digits allows all the extra congruences to be imposed after a bounded number of rows have been checked.

Fix the input automaton and use the common-zero saturation from the previous proof. In choosing an original witness, first remove its common leading zeros. Put $`X=k^m`$, $`B=P(X)`$, and let $`N`$ be the number of states. The suffix beginning with the first nonzero digit of $`B`$ has length $`O_P(m)`$.

### A loop with a fixed nonzero coefficient

If the zero-divisor prefix has fewer than $`N`$ digits, the whole witness already has length $`O_{P,N}(m)`$. Otherwise its first $`N`$ transitions include a repeated state. Choose paths $`u,v`$ in this prefix with $`|uv|\le N`$, $`v`$ nonempty and returning to the state after $`u`$. Only finitely many such marked prefixes and loops need to be tried. Write $`x=[u]_k`$, $`a=[v]_k`$, $`d=|v|`$, $`D=k^d-1`$, and $`C=Dx+a`$. The first numerator digit in the zero-divisor prefix is nonzero, so $`C>0`$, including when $`u`$ is empty.

Choose $`L`$ a common multiple of $`2,h,d`$. Put $`W=X^L-1`$, so $`D\mid W`$, $`B\mid W^e`$, and $`\gcd(B,k)=1`$. Any accepted candidate whose prefix starts with $`u`$ can be changed by inserting $`(Lm/d)z`$ copies of $`v`$ at its marked state. Here $`z\ge0`$. If its original numerator is $`A`$ and there are $`\ell`$ digits after the mark, the new numerator is

```math
 A+k^\ell \frac C D\big((1+W)^z-1\big).
```

The denominator and accepting state remain the same. This formula includes the unmodified word at $`z=0`$.

### The residue classes obtained by insertion

As $`z`$ varies, $`(1+W)^z`$ takes every value $`1+Wt`$ modulo $`W^{e+1}`$. Indeed for every prime $`p\mid W`$,

```math
 v_p((1+W)^z-1)=v_p(W)+v_p(z)\qquad(z>0).
```

For odd $`p`$ this follows from the binomial expansion, first for exponents coprime to $`p`$, then by raising to $`p`$ one step at a time. For $`p=2`$, $`k`$ must be odd and $`Lm`$ is even, so $`8\mid W`$. Squaring likewise increases the valuation by exactly one. The order modulo $`W^{e+1}`$ is therefore $`W^e`$, the number of residue classes congruent to one modulo $`W`$.

Since $`DB\mid W^{e+1}`$, the displayed insertion changes $`A`$ modulo $`B`$ by precisely the subgroup generated by $`CW/D`$. The factor $`k^\ell`$ is a unit and does not change this subgroup. Thus the mark allows a genuine witness precisely when

```math
 G=\gcd(B,CW/D)\quad\text{divides }A.
```

Although $`G`$ depends on $`m`$, its base-$`k`$ order is $`O_{k,u,v,L}(m)`$. To see this let $`C_0`$ be the part of $`C`$ coprime to $`k`$. Then $`G\mid C_0 W`$. Write $`t=\varphi(C_0)`$, taking $`t=1`$ if $`C_0=1`$. The number $`k^{Lmt}-1`$ is divisible by $`W`$, and $`k^{Lmt}\equiv1\pmod{C_0}`$. Raising $`k^{Lmt}`$ to the power $`C_0`$ supplies a geometric sum divisible by $`C_0`$, so $`C_0W\mid k^{Lmt C_0}-1`$.

### The finite-automaton test

Given an original full witness with this prefix, shorten its zero-divisor prefix modulo $`G`$ by the [short representative theorem](residue-length.md). In the prefix graph require the word to start with $`u`$ by adjoining a finite state check, and keep the original endpoint. Append the original suffix. This gives a candidate of length $`O(m)`$, still marked after $`u`$, with numerator divisible by $`G`$. Conversely any such candidate lifts to a full witness by the insertion argument.

To recognize a short candidate, avoid computing $`G`$ from its digits. Its test is equivalent to the existence of integers $`s,q\ge0`$ satisfying

```math
 DA+sC(X^L-1)=D P(X)q.
```

Indeed this is the congruence $`A+sCW/D\equiv0\pmod B`$. If soluble one can take $`0\le s\lt B`$, after which $`q\le A+CW`$. Both new variables have $`O(m)`$ digits with effective constants. For the short-prefix case without a mark use simply $`A=P(X)q`$, with $`q\le A`$.

In both cases also require that the original denominator equals $`P(X)`$. Read all tracks in boundedly many rows of width $`m`$, as in the previous proof. Multiplication by $`P(X)`$ on the $`q`$ track and multiplication by $`X^L-1`$ on the $`s`$ track are sums of fixed integer multiples of shifted rows. Every coefficient of the resulting polynomial identity is a fixed linear expression in the row values, bounded in magnitude by a fixed multiple of $`X`$. The bounded signed carries in the [block construction](cyclotomic-divisors.md) test these identities. The mark requirement is regular. For the row padding, apply common-zero saturation *after* restricting to the regular candidate language with its required initial $`u`$, or with short prefix, so the padding does not affect the mark.

All constants are computable from $`P,k`$, the input graph and finitely many choices of $`u,v`$. Intersect and project the column automata to the unary length as before. This proves effective ultimate periodicity, even though a full witness obtained by lifting a short candidate may itself have far more than $`O(m)`$ digits. The unrestricted bounty permits divisors outside these families. [Original question](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/Fields/shallit.pdf)


### A family requiring exponentially long witnesses

In base two, let the numerator consist of an arbitrary positive number of one digits. At divisor $`B=(2^m-1)^2`$ with $`m\ge 2`$, the shortest successful numerator has exactly $`m(2^m-1)`$ digits. Indeed, put $`U=2^m-1`$. The order of two modulo $`U`$ is exactly $`m`$, since every smaller positive exponent gives a smaller positive number $`2^j-1`$. Hence any successful length has the form $`mt`$. Modulo $`U^2`$, the numerator has residue $`(1+U)^t-1\equiv tU`$. Thus $`U\mid t`$, and the stated minimum is attained. This example explains why bounding the length of the *candidate before insertion* is necessary.

The [arithmetic replay](check_cyclotomic_divisors.py) compared the subgroup prediction against direct finite orbit enumeration in 864 marked cases, including 220 where a multiple was attained and 644 where the congruence obstructed it.
