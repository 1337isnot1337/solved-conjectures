# Limits of shortening a witness by its multiplicative order

Let $`k\ge2`$ and let $`P\in\mathbb Z[Y]`$ be a fixed nonconstant monic polynomial with $`\gcd(P(0),k)=1`$. Put $`B_m=|P(k^m)|`$ for all sufficiently large $`m`$, so $`B_m>1`$ and $`\gcd(B_m,k)=1`$.

**The order of $`k`$ modulo $`B_m`$ is $`O(m)`$ exactly when $`P`$ is a product of distinct cyclotomic polynomials.** A noncyclotomic irreducible factor gives a lower bound $`\Omega_{k,P}(m^2)`$. A repeated cyclotomic factor gives a lower bound exponential in $`m`$. Both lower bounds concern the length of an actual accepted numerator in one fixed regular relation. The decision method for repeated factors avoids this length requirement by lifting a shorter candidate. [Short representatives](residue-length.md) · [Repeated-factor decision theorem](repeated-factors.md)

For a product of distinct cyclotomic polynomials, take $`h`$ such that $`P(Y)\mid Y^h-1`$; then $`B_m\mid k^{hm}-1`$.

For a noncyclotomic monic irreducible factor $`Q`$, put $`d=\deg Q`$ and take a root $`\alpha`$. The elements $`\alpha,k`$ are multiplicatively independent. Indeed, if $`\alpha^u=k^v`$ with $`u>0`$ and $`v`$ an integer, taking absolute field norms gives $`|Q(0)|^u=k^{vd}`$. Since $`Q(0)`$ is nonzero and coprime to $`k`$, this forces $`v=0`$. Then $`\alpha`$ would be a root of unity. Relations with $`u=0`$ are trivial.

Set $`X=k^m`$. Consider the residues of $`k^aX^b`$ modulo $`|Q(X)|`$ for $`0\le a,b\le T`$, where $`T=\lfloor\epsilon m\rfloor`$. A collision from two distinct pairs gives the nonzero resultant

```math
 R=\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}_Y\bigl(Q(Y), k^aY^b-k^{a'}Y^{b'}\bigr).
```

It is nonzero by independence, and divisible by $`Q(X)`$: the integral polynomial Bézout identity for the resultant, evaluated at $`X`$, expresses $`R`$ as a multiple of $`Q(X)`$ plus a multiple of the colliding difference. On the other hand, the product formula over the roots of the monic $`Q`$ gives

```math
 |R|\le 2^d\left(k^d\prod_{Q(\beta)=0}\max(1,|\beta|)\right)^T.
```

Choose $`\epsilon>0`$ small enough that this is less than $`|Q(k^m)|\sim k^{dm}`$ for large $`m`$. The contradiction proves injectivity. All $`(T+1)^2`$ residues belong to the subgroup generated by $`k`$, giving the quadratic lower bound first modulo $`|Q(X)|`$, then modulo $`B_m`$.

Now suppose $`\Phi_t(Y)^2\mid P(Y)`$. Put $`F=\Phi_t(X)`$, $`H=(X^t-1)/F`$, and $`r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{B_m}(k)`$. Since $`F^2\mid k^r-1`$,

```math
 1\equiv k^{mtr}=(1+FH)^r\equiv1+rFH\pmod {F^2}.
```

Thus $`F\mid rH`$. The polynomials $`\Phi_t(Y)`$ and $`(Y^t-1)/\Phi_t(Y)`$ are coprime and monic. Their fixed nonzero resultant bounds $`\gcd(F,H)`$, using the same Bézout identity. Consequently $`r\gg_t F\asymp k^{m\deg\Phi_t}`$.

To see these as actual witness-length bounds, let the numerator language consist of all strings of the digit $`k-1`$. Its length-$`j`$ value is $`k^j-1`$, hence its least successful positive length for modulus $`B_m`$ is exactly $`\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{B_m}(k)`$. This rules out a direct $`O(m)`$ bound for genuine witnesses as soon as the polynomial leaves the distinct-cyclotomic case. It leaves open whether a different decision construction works for general divisors. [Shallit’s original question](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/Fields/shallit.pdf)
