# Short representatives of attainable residues

Fix a finite deterministic base-$`k`$ digit automaton. There is an effective constant $`D`$, depending only on its graph and $`k`$, with the following property. Fix any starting and ending states and any modulus $`M>1`$ coprime to $`k`$. Every numerical residue modulo $`M`$ attained on a path between these states is attained on such a path of length at most $`D\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_M(k)`$. Leading zeros on paths are allowed only where the original transitions allow them.

Write $`r=\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_M(k)`$. Then $`M\mid k^r-1`$, in particular $`k^r>M`$. We prove the bound for each of two types of paths. The decomposition of a finite automaton into cycles and branching components is also used in the automatic additive-basis literature. [Bell, Hare and Shallit, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.08353)

**A path through a branching state.** At the state $`s`$, take two return words of the same length $`d`$ with numerical values $`a\lt b`$, as in the [branching construction](branching-prefix.md). Put $`K=k^d`$ and $`C=b-a`$. Replace the original path by a shortest path with the same endpoints and numerical residue modulo $`C`$ that still visits $`s`$. Such a path has length at most $`2NC`$ for an $`N`$-state graph, by recording the current residue and one visit bit. Arrange the mark at any visit to $`s`$ on the shorter path.

At the mark insert $`K-1`$ passes, each consisting of $`r`$ of the two blocks. If all blocks have value $`a`$, the whole insertion acts as the identity modulo $`M`$. One pass has slope $`K^r\equiv1\pmod M`$ and adds $`a(1+K+\cdots+K^{r-1})`$. Repeating this translation $`K-1`$ times adds $`a(K^r-1)\equiv0`$.

Changing a block from $`a`$ to $`b`$ adds $`CK^i`$ modulo $`M`$, where $`i`$ is its position counted from the right within a pass. The powers align across all passes because $`K^r\equiv1`$. For each position independently we may change between zero and $`K-1`$ of its blocks. Hence the attainable changes include $`Cw`$ for every $`0\le w\lt K^r`$, and therefore every multiple of $`C`$ modulo $`M`$. The remaining suffix multiplies changes by a unit. The old and shortened path values agree modulo $`C`$, so one such choice restores the original residue modulo $`M`$. This uses $`dr(K-1)`$ inserted digits.

**A path avoiding branching states.** Delete all branching states. The surviving strongly connected components are singletons or directed cycles. In any visited cyclic component the path is a power of one full cycle followed by fewer than one cycle’s worth of edges. For a cycle of length $`d`$ with value $`a`$, its map is $`x\mapsto Kx+a`$, $`K=k^d`$. Exactly $`r(K-1)`$ copies act as the identity modulo $`M`$, by the same geometric-sum calculation. Reduce the exponent modulo $`r(K-1)`$ in every component. There are at most $`N`$ components, each $`d\le N`$, and at most $`N-1`$ edges between components.

Both bounds have the required form and are effective. For modulus one, ordinary graph reachability gives a path of length less than $`N`$. The order term can matter even for a fixed automaton. In the language of words consisting of one followed by zeros, the shortest word of value $`k^{-1}`$ modulo $`M`$ has length $`r`$.

For an arbitrary modulus write $`M=M_0M_1`$, where $`M_0`$ is coprime to $`k`$ and every prime divisor of $`M_1`$ divides $`k`$. Choose the least $`h\ge0`$ with $`M_1\mid k^h`$. Keep the last $`h`$ digits of the original path if it has at least that many and shorten the earlier part modulo $`M_0`$. The replacement preserves both residues. Thus every attainable residue has a representative of length $`O_{k,N,\mathrm{graph}}(1+\log M+\mathop{\mathrm{ord}}_{M_0}(k))`$, with order one by convention when $`M_0=1`$.
