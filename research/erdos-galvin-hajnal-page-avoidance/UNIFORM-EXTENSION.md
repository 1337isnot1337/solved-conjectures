# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s repeated-pair page avoidance problem

## Uniform extension by words of pair blocks

The four-tuple construction extends to every finite uniformity \(r\ge3\). On exactly \(\kappa\) vertices, with chromatic number exactly \(\kappa\), it has the following property: no edge contains two distinct completions of any common \((r-1)\)-set. Here \(\kappa\) is any infinite cardinal, and a completion is a vertex which extends the set to an edge. This excludes in particular any three distinct \(r\)-edges on \(r+1\) vertices. For \(r=3\) it is the property in [the original-scope proof](PROOF.md).

### Disjoint-coordinate words force the full chromatic number

Fix a positive finite tuple length \(m\), and a word containing each of \(r\) role symbols exactly \(m\) times. On \([\kappa]^m\), take as edges all disjoint-coordinate tuples of that word type. Any such hypergraph has chromatic number \(\kappa\).

For a regular \(\kappa\) and a colouring by \(\theta<\kappa\) colours, define for each colour \(i\) a tree of allowed prefixes backwards. The allowed \(m\)-tuples are exactly those of colour \(i\). A prefix of length \(j<m\) is allowed if cofinally many larger coordinates extend it to an allowed prefix of length \(j+1\). Inducting downward, every prefix belongs to the allowed-prefix family for at least one \(i\): its set of future coordinates is the union of fewer than \(\kappa\) candidate extension sets, so some is unbounded. Thus some colour has the empty prefix allowed. Read the prescribed word from left to right. At each occurrence of a role choose a coordinate above *all* those chosen so far, extending the current prefix of that role in the same colour's tree. This is possible by cofinality, and at the end all tuples have that colour. At an infinite singular cardinal, restrict to cofinally many smaller regular-cardinal initial segments. This word lemma clarifies why only the forbidden-configuration analysis requires a specially chosen word.

### The uniform word

Number roles \(0,\ldots,r-1\), and for each pair \(i<j\) put a block \(\texttt{ijij}\), using lexicographic order of \((i,j)\). Let the edge word be the concatenation of all blocks. Every role occurs \(2(r-1)\) times. The first occurrences order the roles numerically. No two consecutive letters are equal, including at block boundaries: the block ends in the larger role and the next block begins in the same smaller role or its successor; at a row change the larger ending role is \(r-1\), beyond the next smaller role.

Project the word onto the roles \(i<j\), naming them \(X,Y\). Regard the resulting word as a sequence of successive two-letter blocks. The single shared block \(\texttt{ijij}\) contributes exactly two blocks \(\texttt{XY},\texttt{XY}\); every other surviving original block contributes one \(\texttt{XX}\) or \(\texttt{YY}\). Before the two mixed blocks there are exactly \(i\) \(\texttt{YY}\) blocks (pairs \((h,j)\) with \(h<i\)) and \(j-1\) \(\texttt{XX}\) blocks (pairs \((h,i)\) with \(h<i\), and \((i,h)\) with \(i<h<j\)). Therefore the projected type identifies both original roles. Also, since \(r\ge3\), it contains at least one same-letter two-block and is not a sequence of mixed blocks alone.

Fix any \((r-1)\)-set in an edge. Its pair projections identify the role of every member and hence the missing role. Each coordinate of a completion must lie in its prescribed gap among the coordinates of the fixed tuples. The gaps for the missing role are distinct because no two equal letters were consecutive in the full edge word. If two completions have disjoint coordinates, their projection is therefore made only of mixed two-blocks, one block per gap. No permitted pair projection has that form. And if the two completions share a coordinate, they cannot be in an edge together by definition. This proves the desired property and the uniform extension. Rigidity and the chromatic argument are independent of any cardinal arithmetic beyond regularity at a cofinal collection of cardinals.
