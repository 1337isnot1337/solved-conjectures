# Small-height rational points carry little mass of a slow vector

At level $`M+1`$, write $`N=p^M`$ and normalize the slope as $`x=p y`$. Regard $`y\bmod N`$ as an affine point of $`\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Z/N)`$. The actions in this coordinate are given by integral determinant-one matrices, by conjugating the original matrices with the change of slope. A chart below can be any additional element of $`\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Z_p)`$, independently at each level. The height of a rational projective point is $`\max(|a|,|b|)`$ in coprime integer coordinates $`[a:b]`$. Let $`B_N(H)`$ consist of the reductions of all such points of height at most $`H`$ in the chosen chart.

**Theorem.** Fix an odd prime. Along any sequence $`M\to\infty`$, let the generating triple vary arbitrarily and let the unit vectors $`f_M`$ on the ordinary projective ball have energy tending to zero. For an absolute constant $`C`$,

```math
\limsup_M \|\mathbf1_{B_N(H)}f_M\|_2
\ \le\ C\limsup_M\frac{\log(2H)}{\log N}.
```

This holds for every choice of charts. In particular a set of rational points of height $`N^{o(1)}`$, or a cyclic interval of that length translated to start at zero, has vanishing mass. This estimates mass in position. The [Fourier estimates](CYCLIC-FOURIER.md) concern the separate distribution of cycle frequencies. The theorem allows a slow vector spread over the projective ball, including one whose modulus is nearly constant.

We use counting $`\ell^2`$ norm throughout, converting from probability norm by a constant factor. Replace the vector by its modulus, which preserves the mass and can only decrease energy. Extend by zero to the full finite projective line after the chart change. The image of the original ball is invariant. Its maximum point mass tends to zero. Indeed a word of length at most $`3(p-1)/2`$ gives a full cycle on the ball, since its first digit before the change of slope is $`E_{21}`$, as proved in [the cycle calculation](CYCLIC-FOURIER.md). For each fixed $`J`$, the first $`J`$ translates of a point of maximum amplitude have amplitudes differing from that maximum by $`o(1)`$. Their squares sum to at most one. Now let $`J`$ grow.

## Recovering a rational transformation from three returns

Suppose an integral projective transformation $`g`$ modulo $`N`$ takes three distinct points of $`B_N(H)`$ back into this set. Use primitive representatives of height at most $`H`$ for both triples. Over $`\mathbb Q`$ there is a unique projective matrix $`A`$ taking the three source representatives to the three target representatives. It has a primitive integral representative of height at most $`6H^6`$. Indeed the three matching equations are linear rows on the four matrix entries, each coefficient of size at most $`H^2`$. The signed maximal minors supply the kernel vector. Uniqueness follows from the action on triples of distinct projective points.

Let $`l`$ be the minimum $`p`$-valuation of a nonzero maximal minor. It is at most $`\log_p(6H^6)`$. If $`l\lt M`$, the matrix $`A`$ has good reduction at $`p`$ and agrees projectively with $`g`$ modulo $`p^{M-l}`$. To see this, pick a minor of valuation $`l`$ and use the adjugate of that three-column submatrix on the matching equations for $`g`$. For its four coefficients $`g_i`$ and signed minors $`D_i`$, this gives $`D_jg_i-D_i g_j=0\bmod N`$, where $`v_p(D_j)=l`$. After division by $`p^l`$ the $`j`$th coefficient of the minor vector is a unit. The proportionality scalar is a unit since $`g`$ is invertible modulo $`p`$. In particular the primitive matrix has determinant prime to $`p`$.

## Obtaining nonamenable return words

Pass to a subsequence attaining the upper limit of the norm in the theorem, and suppose its limit is $`\sqrt\alpha>0`$. We may assume $`\log(2H)/\log N`$ has a limit $`\theta`$. It suffices to consider $`\theta<1/20`$. By compactness, the original generating triples and integral charts converge along a subsequence. The limit triple still generates $`K`$, by its basis of first digits. Let $`\Gamma`$ be the countable group generated by the limiting transformed matrices. It is nonamenable. Its closure is conjugate to an open subgroup of $`\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb Q_p)`$, hence it is Zariski dense in the projective group, and the Tits alternative applies.

Put $`h_M=\mathbf1_{B_N(H)}|f_M|`$ and let $`P_M`$ denote the six-step averaging operator. For every fixed $`k`$,

```math
\langle P_M^{2k} h_M,h_M\rangle
=\|P_M^k h_M\|_2^2\ge (\alpha-o(1))^2 .
```

Indeed $`P_M^k|f_M|`$ is within $`o(1)`$ of $`|f_M|`$. For each word of length $`2k`$, its term in this return average is $`o(1)`$ if it maps at most two points of $`B_N(H)`$ back into the set, by the point-mass estimate. All terms are at most one.

The symmetric walk on $`\ell^2(\Gamma)`$ has norm $`\rho<1`$ by nonamenability. Choose $`k`$ with $`\rho^{2k}<\alpha^2/2`$. After a subsequence the list $`D`$ of words of this length returning at least three points is fixed. The word law gives $`D`$ probability at least $`\alpha^2`$. Its values at the limiting matrices therefore generate a nonamenable subgroup. Otherwise the walk on the cosets of that subgroup has norm at most $`\rho`$, by weak containment for amenable stabilizers, although the chance of returning to the identity coset at time $`2k`$ is at least $`\alpha^2`$. [Quasi-regular representations and amenable stabilizers](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15364v1)

For each $`w\in D`$ use the rational matrix $`A_{w,M}`$ reconstructed above. It agrees with the original word modulo $`p^{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}`$ for all sufficiently large $`M`$. Thus as a $`p`$-adic projective matrix it converges to the limiting word. The groups generated by these rational matrices are eventually nonamenable. Here is the algebraic detail. Virtually solvable subgroups of $`\mathrm{PGL}_2`$ in characteristic zero all satisfy the same law

```math
[[u^m,v^m],[z^m,t^m]]=1,\qquad m=60!.
```

Over an algebraic closure an infinite virtually solvable subgroup preserves a point or an unordered pair, by the fixed points of its nontrivial connected solvable Zariski closure. In the first case its commutator subgroup is abelian, and in the second even powers are diagonal in a common basis. The finite subgroups are cyclic, dihedral, or of order at most 60 by the characteristic-zero classification of finite projective linear groups in dimension two. The limiting nonamenable linear group contains a nonabelian free group, so some fixed four words violate the displayed nontrivial law there. Their evaluations still violate it for all sufficiently large $`M`$. [Tits alternative](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.1395) · [Finite subgroups of $`\mathrm{PGL}_2`$, Beauville](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.3942)

## A height cutoff on the rational projective line

The strong Tits alternative applied to the adjoint embedding $`\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb Q)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb Q)`$ supplies two free generators among words of length at most an absolute $`B`$ in the $`A_{w,M}`$ and their inverses. Call them $`A_1,A_2`$. Their primitive matrix heights are at most $`(12H^6)^B`$, by multiplying integral representatives. Their corresponding original words have length at most $`2kB`$. On $`\ell^2(\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q))`$ the average over these two generators and their inverses has norm at most $`\sqrt{3}/2`$. Every point stabilizer in the free group is amenable, being a subgroup of a conjugate of the triangular group. The regular norm of the simple walk on the rank-two free group is $`\sqrt3/2`$. [Strong Tits alternative](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.1395) · [Quasi-regular norm comparison](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.15364v1)

Put $`N'=p^{\lfloor M/2\rfloor}`$ and let $`a_M`$ be the square root of the pushforward of $`|f_M|^2`$ by reduction modulo $`N'`$. It has unit norm. Taking component norms on each fiber is a contraction, so the displacements of $`a_M`$ under both $`A_i\bmod N'`$ tend to zero.

For $`z\in\mathbb P^1(\mathbb Q)`$ write $`H(z)`$ for its height and $`\bar z`$ for its reduction modulo $`N'`$. Take $`\tau(z)`$ equal to one up to height $`(N')^{1/8}`$, zero from height $`(N')^{1/4}`$, and linear as a function of $`\log H(z)`$ in between. Define $`F_M(z)=\tau(z)a_M(\bar z)`$. For $`\theta<1/20`$ one has $`H\le (N')^{1/8}`$ eventually and hence $`\|F_M\|_2\ge\sqrt\alpha-o(1)`$. Indeed the pushforward mass of the original small-height set is included. Reduction is injective on rational points of height at most $`(N')^{1/4}`$ for large $`N'`$, since equality modulo $`N'`$ forces the integer cross determinant to vanish.

For either $`A=A_i`$, its inverse has the same matrix height. Thus $`|\log H(Az)-\log H(z)|\le\log(2H(A))`$, and $`|\tau(Az)-\tau(z)|\le8\log(2H(A))/\log N'`$. In the difference $`F_M(Az)-F_M(z)`$, first take the change of $`a_M`$ with coefficient $`\tau(Az)`$, then the change of $`\tau`$ with coefficient $`a_M(\bar z)`$. The first term has norm at most $`\|A a_M-a_M\|_2`$ by injectivity on the preimage of the cutoff support. For the second term, the union of that preimage and the cutoff support has at most two points over any residue. Its norm is at most $`8\sqrt2\log(2H(A))/\log N'`$. Equivariance of reduction follows from good reduction of $`A`$. Consequently both displacements of $`F_M`$ are at most $`o(1)+C_0 B\log(2H)/\log N`$ with an absolute $`C_0`$. The free-group norm bound proves $`\sqrt\alpha\le C\theta`$. Enlarging the absolute constant covers $`\theta\ge1/20`$ as well.

The exact checker reconstructs 450 matrices from three matches over prime powers at $`p=3,5,7`$. Its examples include points at infinity and triples agreeing modulo smaller powers of $`p`$, where the modular matches lose digits of matrix precision. Run `python3 -B research/padic-sl2-uniform-gap/check_rational_points.py`. [Verification script](check_rational_points.py)

The argument uses the returned points to recover two rational transformations and compares them on a smaller modulus. It supplies a restriction on the position distribution of a candidate slow vector. A depth-independent gap for vectors distributed outside all such sets remains open.
