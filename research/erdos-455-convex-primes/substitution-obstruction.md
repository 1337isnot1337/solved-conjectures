# Substitution rules force composite terms

Suppose $x_0<x_1<\cdots$ are positive integers. Their second differences cannot eventually form a morphic word if the $x_n$ are eventually prime. This includes every coding of a fixed point of a finite substitution, including nonuniform substitutions and ones whose consecutive blocks have different first and second moments. More generally, the conclusion holds for second differences regular in any integer base. Convexity and a growth bound are unnecessary here.

The prior [October account](https://satoru.net/math/455/) excludes periodic and equal-block-moment curvature patterns, including a Thue–Morse example. Below the obstruction is an ordinary integer linear recurrence on a subsequence. We also get terms having arbitrarily many prime factors counted with multiplicity, on whole arithmetic progressions of substitution depths.

## Recurrence lemma

Let $v_t$ be a positive integer sequence tending to infinity that eventually satisfies a monic linear recurrence with constant integer coefficients. For each positive integer $K$ there is an infinite arithmetic progression of indices on which $\Omega(v_t)\ge K$, where $\Omega$ counts prime factors with multiplicity.

Discard any zero characteristic roots by shifting the start of the recurrence. Its last coefficient is now nonzero, say with absolute value $D$. The pure-zero-root case would give an eventually zero sequence. Modulo any integer coprime to $D$ the companion matrix is invertible, so its state on the finite residue space is periodic from the start of the shifted recurrence.

Let $S$ be the prime divisors of $D$. The recurrence state is eventually periodic modulo the product of $p^K$ over $p\in S$ (modulus one if $S$ is empty). Fix an infinite arithmetic progression in that periodic tail on which the residues are constant. If any $p^K$ divides the values, we are done. Otherwise the part $H$ supported on $S$ is constant along the progression. Every $v_t/H$ there is coprime to $D$.

Pick one such cofactor $c>1$. Periodicity modulo $c$ lets us thin the progression, starting at its chosen index, so all its subsequent values are divisible by $c$. Since $H$ is coprime to $c$ and $v_t/H\to\infty$, a later cofactor $c'$ is a strictly larger multiple of $c$. Repeat with $c'$ on the thinned progression. Each step increases $\Omega(c)$ by at least one. After finitely many steps a whole infinite arithmetic progression has the required divisibility.

## Prefix lengths of a substitution

After discarding finitely many terms, let the second differences be the letter weights on a coding of the infinite fixed word generated by iterating a substitution $\sigma$ on an initial letter $a$. Thus $w_t=\sigma^t(a)$ is a prefix whose length $N_t$ tends to infinity. Assigning the output weight of each letter before coding is equivalent to coding it first.

For a finite word $w$ count its letters $C_z(w)$ and its ordered scattered pairs $C_{zr}(w)$, with the two positions strictly increasing. Under substitution the vector of counts transforms by a fixed integer matrix. Indeed a letter count transforms by the ordinary substitution matrix, and

$$
C_{xy}(\sigma w)=
\sum_z C_z(w)C_{xy}(\sigma z)
+\sum_{z,r}C_{zr}(w)C_x(\sigma z)C_y(\sigma r).
$$

The two terms distinguish pairs coming from the same input letter and from two different input letters. Adjoin a constant coordinate. Write $g=x_1-x_0$ and let $h(z)$ denote the integer second difference coded by the letter $z$. Summing the differences twice gives the value after this prefix as

$$
x_{N_t+1}=x_0+(N_t+1)g+
\sum_z h(z)C_z(w_t)+\sum_{z,r}h(z)C_{zr}(w_t).
$$

The contribution of the letter at position $i$, numbered from zero, is its weight times $N_t-i$. Thus $x_{N_t+1}$ is a fixed integer linear functional of powers of one integer matrix. Cayley–Hamilton gives a monic integer recurrence. The values tend to infinity, so the lemma applies.

## Unbounded regular second differences

In the sense of [Allouche and Shallit](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(92)90001-V), an integer sequence is $k$-regular if the module generated by its subsequences on residue classes modulo powers of $k$ is finitely generated. Partial sums preserve regularity. Explicitly, choose a finite generating column $F(n)$ with $F(kn+r)=M_r F(n)$ for integer matrices $M_r$. For $G(n)=\sum_{i<n}F(i)$ one has

$$
G(kn+r)=\left(\sum_{s=0}^{k-1}M_s\right)G(n)
+\left(\sum_{s=0}^{r-1}M_s\right)F(n).
$$

Thus $(F,G)$ has a finite integer matrix representation as well. Applying this twice and adjoining the initial constant and linear terms gives a representation for $x_n$. In particular $x_{k^t}$ is a fixed linear functional of powers of its zero-digit matrix applied to the state at index one. The recurrence lemma applies again. If regularity starts on a tail, reindex that tail before taking sums.

This gives a structural restriction on every candidate for [Erdős #455](https://www.erdosproblems.com/455). It does not force a general sequence of second differences into either of these classes.
