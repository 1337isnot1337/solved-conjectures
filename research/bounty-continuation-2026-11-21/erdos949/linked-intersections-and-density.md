# Linked translates and upper Banach density in Erdős problem 949

**Theorem.** Let $`G`$ be a rational vector space of infinite cardinality $`\kappa`$, and let $`S\subseteq G`$ satisfy $`(S+S)\cap S=\varnothing`$. Suppose $`S`$ has positive upper Banach density in a coset of some subgroup of cardinality $`\kappa`$. Then there exists $`A\subseteq G\setminus S`$ of cardinality $`\kappa`$ with $`(A+A)\cap S=\varnothing`$, including repeated summands. The subgroup may be any additive subgroup. The result applies at a singular continuum and leaves the case of arbitrary sum-free sets open. [Erdős's question, section 6](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1977-27.pdf#page=15) · [Erdős problem 949](https://www.erdosproblems.com/949)

## Three translated sets

Write $`N(c)=S-c`$. Suppose some $`b\in G`$ and some $`C\subseteq G`$ of cardinality $`\kappa`$ satisfy

```math
\bigl|N(b)\cap N(c)\cap N(d)\bigr|=\kappa
\qquad(c,d\in C),
```

including $`c=d`$. Then the conclusion of the theorem holds.

For each $`t\in G`$, the set $`B_t=\{z\notin S:z-t,z+t\in S\}`$ is admissible. Indeed $`z+w=(z-t)+(w+t)\in S+S`$ for $`z,w\in B_t`$. If some $`B_t`$ has size $`\kappa`$ we have finished. Otherwise all these sets have size below $`\kappa`$. [Midpoint exception lemma](midpoint-profiles-and-rational-fibers.md#midpoint-exceptions)

For $`c,d\in C`$, choose $`x`$ in the displayed triple intersection such that $`x+(c+d)/2`$ is outside $`B_{(c-d)/2}`$. This removes fewer than $`\kappa`$ possibilities for $`x`$. Its red endpoints $`x+c,x+d\in S`$ force $`x+(c+d)/2\in S`$. Subtracting $`x+b\in S`$ gives

```math
\frac{c-b}{2}+\frac{d-b}{2}\in S-S\subseteq G\setminus S.
```

For unary avoidance, take $`x\in N(b)\cap N(c)`$ outside the translated midpoint exception for endpoints $`x+b,x+c`$. The intersection has cardinality $`\kappa`$ by the case $`c=d`$. Subtracting $`x+b`$ from the red midpoint gives $`(c-b)/2\in S-S`$. Thus $`A=(C-b)/2`$ works.

## Finitely additive measures

Let $`\mu`$ be any finitely additive probability on all subsets of $`G`$ which vanishes on sets of size below $`\kappa`$. Suppose $`\mu(N(c))\geq\varepsilon>0`$ for every $`c`$ in a set $`D`$ of cardinality $`\kappa`$. These hypotheses imply the three-translate condition.

Join distinct $`c,d\in D`$ if $`\mu(N(c)\cap N(d))\leq\varepsilon^2/2`$. This graph has bounded clique size. For a clique of size $`n`$, let $`f`$ be the sum of the indicators of its $`n`$ translates. Cauchy–Schwarz for the finite partition generated by these sets gives

```math
n^2\varepsilon^2
\leq \left(\int f\,d\mu\right)^2
\leq \int f^2\,d\mu
\leq n+n(n-1)\varepsilon^2/2.
```

The Erdős–Dushnik–Miller theorem supplies an independent set $`D_1\subseteq D`$ of cardinality $`\kappa`$. Fix $`b\in D_1`$. For $`c\in D_1\setminus\{b\}`$ the set $`E_c=N(b)\cap N(c)`$ has measure greater than $`\varepsilon^2/2`$. Join $`c,d`$ in this new index set if $`\mu(E_c\cap E_d)=0`$. Each finite clique has size at most $`2/\varepsilon^2`$, since its members are pairwise disjoint modulo null sets and have total measure at most one. Apply the same partition theorem to obtain a set $`C`$ of cardinality $`\kappa`$ with $`\mu(E_c\cap E_d)>0`$ for all $`c,d\in C`$, including $`c=d`$. Every such intersection has cardinality $`\kappa`$. [Dushnik and Miller, Theorem 5.22](https://doi.org/10.2307/2371374)

In particular, when $`\mathop{\mathrm{cf}}(\kappa)>\aleph_0`$, a counterexample must have fewer than $`\kappa`$ shifts $`c`$ with $`\mu(S-c)>0`$ for every such $`\mu`$. Otherwise countably many thresholds $`1/n`$ give one positive lower bound on $`\kappa`$ shifts.

## Positive density on a coset

For a subgroup $`H\leq G`$ of size $`\kappa`$ and $`r\in G`$, positive upper Banach density on $`r+H`$ means that some translation-invariant finitely additive probability $`m`$ on $`H`$ gives positive mass to $`(S-r)\cap H`$. The measure $`\mu(X)=m((X-r)\cap H)`$ then assigns this same positive mass to $`S-c`$ for every $`c\in H`$. It vanishes on sets of size below $`\kappa`$. Indeed, for each $`T\subseteq H`$ smaller than $`\kappa`$, its difference set $`T-T`$ is also smaller than $`\kappa`$. Greedy selection gives arbitrarily many disjoint translates of $`T`$ in $`H`$, so $`m(T)=0`$. The measure criterion proves the theorem. [Griesmer, section 1.2, on invariant means and upper Banach density](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2026.10205)

The density hypothesis includes the [piecewise-syndetic case](midpoint-profiles-and-rational-fibers.md#piecewise-syndetic-forbidden-sets). It is strictly weaker even among sum-free subsets of the discrete additive reals. Take a compact nowhere dense set $`K\subset(1/3,2/3)`$ of positive Lebesgue measure and put $`S=K+\mathbb Z`$. Reduction modulo one proves sum-freeness. Griesmer's periodic construction gives this $`S`$ positive upper Banach density and proves that it is not piecewise syndetic. [Griesmer, Lemma 12.11](https://doi.org/10.1017/fms.2026.10205)

## Finite averages in the remaining case

Suppose no admissible set has size $`\kappa`$. For every $`D\subseteq G`$ of size $`\kappa`$ and every $`\varepsilon>0`$, there is a probability distribution $`w`$ of finite support in $`D`$ such that

```math
\left|\left\{x\in G:\sum_{c}w(c)1_S(x+c)\geq\varepsilon\right\}\right|\lt\kappa.
```

If the conclusion fails, fix any finite nonempty $`F\subseteq D`$ and partition $`G`$ according to the incidence vector $`(1_S(x+c))_{c\in F}`$. Retain the vectors whose cell has cardinality $`\kappa`$. For every probability vector $`w`$ on $`F`$, at least one retained vector has inner product at least $`\varepsilon`$ with $`w`$, because the finitely many other cells together have size below $`\kappa`$. The finite minimax theorem gives a convex combination of retained vectors with every coordinate at least $`\varepsilon`$. On each retained cell used, choose an ultrafilter containing the cell and the complements of all sets smaller than $`\kappa`$. Weighting the resulting zero-one measures by the convex coefficients gives a finitely additive probability annihilating small sets and having $`\mu(N(c))\geq\varepsilon`$ for $`c\in F`$.

The probabilities annihilating small sets form a compact space in the topology of pointwise convergence on all subsets of $`G`$, since normalization, finite additivity, and vanishing on small sets are closed conditions in the product $`[0,1]^{\mathcal P(G)}`$. The additional constraints $`\mu(N(c))\geq\varepsilon`$, one for each $`c\in D`$, are also closed. The finite construction and compactness give a single probability satisfying them all. This contradicts the measure criterion.
