# Erdős 128: a failed compression and the precise spectral rounding gap

21 November 2026. These are bounded route checks for the [full sparse-half target](target-and-prior-audit.md). There is no proof or counterexample to Erdős 128 here. Established weighted-half and spectral results are credited below; no priority is claimed for their elementary consequences.

## 1. Weighted formulation and proposed bridge

For a finite triangle-free graph H with vertex weights wᵢ≥0 summing to one, define

```math
\beta(H,w)=\min\left\{\sum_{ij\in E(H)}x_i x_j:
0\le x_i\le w_i,\quad \sum_i x_i=\tfrac12\right\}.
```

This weighted framework is prior work; see [Norin–Yepremyan, Section 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1311.5818). For rational weights it represents blow-ups. The minimum can be taken with at most one coordinate strictly between its endpoints: transferring mass between two such coordinates makes the objective affine if they are nonadjacent and concave if they are adjacent, so one endpoint of the transfer interval does not increase the objective. In a uniform graph of even order this yields an actual half-set.

A tempting bridge is to merge opposite vertices of an induced C₄. In the unweighted description, replace u by a false twin of the nonadjacent vertex v: erase u's old incident edges and give it exactly v's neighborhood. Triangle-freeness is preserved. Equivalently, delete u in the quotient and transfer its weight to v.

The proposed unconditional monotonicity would require some such move not to decrease β. The following exact example defeats that assertion, because all nonadjacent pairs of the example are equivalent by automorphisms. It does **not** defeat the conditional assertion restricted to β>1/50. That conditional assertion remains open in this work.

There is a separate terminal-case issue: the known girth-five theorem is for unweighted graphs, while a quotient obtained by mass merging has arbitrary vertex weights. Razborov explicitly notes in Section 4.6 that girth does not survive blow-up. His theorem therefore cannot be applied to the weighted quotient without an additional argument. The [conditional attempt](conditional-compression-gap.md) records this distinction.

## 2. Clebsch witness, without a numerical lower-bound assumption

Use the vertex set F₂⁴, encoded by integers 0 through 15, and join x to y when

```math
x+y\in D=\{1,2,4,8,15\}.
```

Here addition is bitwise XOR. No sum of two distinct members of D lies in D, so the graph is triangle-free. It is 5-regular.

For a Walsh character indexed by a bit vector a of Hamming weight j, the adjacency eigenvalue is

```math
\lambda_a=4-2j+(-1)^j.
```

Consequently the spectrum consists of 5, 1, and −3. For any set S of eight vertices, write its indicator as 1/2+z, where z is orthogonal to the constant vector and has squared norm four. Then

```math
2e(S)=\boldsymbol{1}_S^T A\boldsymbol{1}_S
 =20+z^T A z\ge20-12=8.
```

Thus every half has at least four edges. Equality holds for

```math
S=\{0,3,5,6,8,11,13,14\},
```

whose four edges are (0,8), (3,11), (5,13), and (6,14). Therefore β=4/256=1/64.

Now replace vertex 0 by a twin of vertex 3. Their common neighbors include 1 and 2, so they are opposite vertices of an induced C₄. The new neighborhood of 0 is

```math
N(3)=\{1,2,7,11,12\}.
```

In the modified graph,

```math
T=\{0,3,4,5,8,9,14,15\}
```

induces precisely the three edges (4,5), (8,9), and (14,15). Its minimum-half density is therefore at most 3/256, strictly below the original 4/256. No lower bound for the modified graph is needed for this conclusion.

Both displayed comparisons first concern graphs on the same 16 vertices, all with weight 1/16. For these graphs the fractional-half minimum equals the cardinal-eight-set minimum by the rounding argument in Section 1. In the 15-vertex quotient after merging the new twins, vertex 3 has weight 2/16 and every other vertex has weight 1/16. Aggregating the two twin coordinates preserves the objective and maps the entire feasible half polytope onto the quotient's feasible polytope. Thus that **weighted fractional** quotient has exactly the same minimum as the modified 16-vertex graph. Its unweighted seven-vertex-half objective is a different quantity and is not used.

To see that no other opposite-pair choice avoids this loss, represent the same graph on F₂⁵ modulo the one-dimensional subspace generated by (1,1,1,1,1). The five coordinate vectors give D. Each nonzero coset has a representative of weight one or two; the nonneighbors of zero are exactly the ten weight-two cosets. Coordinate permutations act transitively on these, and translations act transitively on vertices. Hence every ordered nonadjacent pair is equivalent to (0,3). The reverse cloning direction is equivalent as well. Every opposite-pair merge strictly decreases β.

This is a counterexample to the proposed compression rule, not to sparse halves: 1/64 is already below 1/50. No extension of a positive finite screen is being used.

## 3. A uniform counterexample family already excluded by a published theorem

[Balogh, Clemen, Lidický, Norin, and Volec, Theorem 1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2204.00093) prove that the smallest eigenvalue q_min of the signless Laplacian of a triangle-free n-vertex graph satisfies

```math
q_{\min}\le\frac{15n}{94}.
```

Consider any triangle-free Cayley graph on F₂ᵈ. If it has no edges, the claim is immediate. Otherwise its degree is a positive integer k, and its adjacency matrix has a negative eigenvalue because its trace is zero and the constant eigenvalue is k>0. Every eigenvalue has a Walsh character eigenvector. Choose a character attaining λ_min. It is nonconstant, takes the values ±1 equally often, and satisfies q_min=k+λ_min.

Its positive level set S is an exact half. Orthogonality to constants gives

```math
e(S)=\frac{n(k+\lambda_{\min})}{8}
 =\frac{nq_{\min}}8
 \le\frac{15n^2}{752}
 \lt\frac{n^2}{50}.
```

Therefore this whole unrestricted-in-dimension class cannot supply a counterexample to Erdős 128. This is a direct consequence of the published spectral theorem, not a new mathematical resolution or a new bounty. No assumption of connectivity is needed.

## 4. Why the spectral theorem does not settle arbitrary graphs

The least eigenvector of a general regular graph need not be a balanced sign vector. The missing rounding implication cannot be silently assumed. The Petersen graph already gives an exact obstruction.

View its vertices as the two-element subsets of a five-element set, adjacent when disjoint. It is 3-regular with least adjacency eigenvalue −2, so q_min=1. Every independent four-set is a star: an intersecting family of four two-subsets must have a common element, because a pairwise-intersecting family without a common element is contained in a triangle and has at most three members. Every vertex outside such a star has exactly two neighbors in it.

If a five-set induced at most one edge, deleting an endpoint of that edge, or any vertex if there were no edges, would leave an independent four-set. The fifth vertex would then have two neighbors in that star, a contradiction. Conversely, a star plus any outside vertex induces exactly two edges. Thus its minimum five-set edge count is two, whereas

```math
\frac{nq_{\min}}8=\frac{10}{8}<2.
```

The asserted universal rounding rule e(S)≤nq_min/8 is therefore false. The Petersen graph meets the sparse-half threshold exactly, so this failure also is not a counterexample to the target.

## 5. Remaining mathematical obligation

The controlled-compression route could use a nondecreasing replacement theorem conditional on β>1/50, or a quantitative theorem in which any decrease is absorbed by proved slack. Neither has been obtained. A reduction ending at a weighted graph of girth at least five also needs a valid weighted terminal theorem. The spectral route would need an additional structural argument ensuring a half-set at the required threshold despite nonflat eigenvectors. The cited theorem alone does not provide it.

The calculations above were first cross-checked by a short exhaustive offline calculation on the 16-vertex witness. The written proof replaces that numerical lower bound with the exact Walsh spectrum; the displayed edge lists suffice to check the upper bounds. No Lean verification or full-target resolution is claimed.
