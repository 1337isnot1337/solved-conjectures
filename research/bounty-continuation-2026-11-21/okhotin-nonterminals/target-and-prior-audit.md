# Okhotin Problem 9: target and prior-work audit

21 November 2026. This is a new research reservation, not a solution. The count of complete qualifying paid targets remains zero.

## Exact full target

Is there an integer $`k>0`$ such that every language generated by a Boolean grammar can be generated by a Boolean grammar with at most $`k`$ nonterminal symbols, over the same terminal alphabet?

The quantifier over grammars is unrestricted within the sponsor's Boolean-grammar language family. A negative answer must establish unbounded nonterminal complexity. Separating one small level, restricting to unambiguous grammars, or proving a result only for linear grammars does not resolve this statement. The intended semantics must be preserved; arbitrary unresolved language equations or three-valued grammars with undefined memberships are not interchangeable with the sponsor's standard language family.

The statement is Problem 9 in §1.9 of [Okhotin's 30 March 2010 update](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=d0e62d472c289c617866fde28ff6dd32758a73c7&repid=rep1&type=pdf), printed page 6. The [2013 survey](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cosrev.2013.06.001) repeats the question in its list of theoretical problems.

## Relevant prior theorems

1. [Okhotin, 2004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2004.03.002) proves effective compression of **linear conjunctive grammars** to two nonterminals. This already covers the regular and linear-conjunctive subcases; they are not new targets here.
2. [Jeż and Okhotin, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-31623-4_14) separate the first small levels for **unambiguous conjunctive grammars** over a unary alphabet. The restriction on ambiguity is essential to the reported theorem and cannot be imposed on all competing Boolean grammars.
3. [Okhotin, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2018.11.001) constructs a fixed hardest Boolean language under inverse homomorphisms, with the usual separate handling of the empty word. This gives the starting point for the present attempted compression; it does not state constant nonterminal cost for the inverse-image operation.
4. [Lehtinen and Okhotin, 2010](https://doi.org/10.1142/S0129054110007568) establish closure under inverse GSM mappings. The construction is reproduced in [Lehtinen's 2013 dissertation](https://www.utupub.fi/server/api/core/bitstreams/0d998135-ef70-41a7-b708-f29301509d08/content), §§4.1–4.3, printed pages 61–72. It tracks transducer states and does not by itself give the required uniform compression.

The exact statements and relevant construction were read; the full proofs of the 2004 compression and 2019 hardest-language theorem were not independently rebuilt. A search finding no newer theorem is not a novelty proof.

## Proposed complete route

Fix the hardest Boolean language from the 2019 theorem. Try to bound the number of nonterminals needed for all of its inverse homomorphic images, uniformly over the alphabet and morphism. Such a bound would answer Problem 9 positively. Mere effective closure or a bound that depends on codeword lengths would not.

The companion mathematical note tests the exact location where the usual inverse-image construction loses uniformity. No restricted numerical search is planned.
