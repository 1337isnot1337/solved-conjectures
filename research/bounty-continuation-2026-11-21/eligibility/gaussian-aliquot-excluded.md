# Pegg's first Gaussian aliquot-cycle offer: excluded

Checked on 21 November 2026. This is the fourth target in the current bounded screen. No search or new-solution claim follows.

## Exact offer and divisor convention

[Ed Pegg's primary page, *The Neglected Gaussian Integers*](https://www.mathpuzzle.com/Gaussians.html), still prints a $50 offer for the first proper Gaussian aliquot cycle. The same page explicitly includes perfect numbers as cycles of length one. Its divisor convention chooses the unique associate $`a+bi`$ with $`a>0`$ and $`b\ge0`$ from every Gaussian-divisor associate class, and sums the chosen proper divisors.

This must be distinguished from Spira's multiplicative Gaussian divisor sum. The [2013 Clark thesis record](https://encompass.eku.edu/etd/158/) and the [author's research page](https://www.ranthonyclark.com/research-in-algebra.html) report Gaussian amicable pairs, but their displayed research convention is Spira's. Those records alone do not resolve Pegg's exact question.

## Existing exact resolution already recorded in this repository

The live issue search found the earlier [campaign comment](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/223#issuecomment-7068239869) and the [pinned Gaussian source ledger](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/9a05a8b00061cb46b8b0da748785544ea32dd262/research/bounty-session-2026-11-21/jsp-gaussian/README.md), section “Other Pegg offers excluded.” That ledger already checked the example

```math
q=430089+665198i,
\qquad
-q=(1+2i)^3(5+2i)(38+17i)(316+5i).
```

It reports exact enumeration of all 32 normalized divisors, whose sum is

```math
860178+1330396i=2q.
```

Consequently the selected proper-divisor sum is $`q`$, giving a period-one cycle under the sponsor's convention. This earlier calculation was read, not rerun as a new discovery.

The current [MathWorld entry](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/PerfectGaussianInteger.html), equations (4)–(5), independently distinguishes the two divisor conventions and credits this example to A. Sanchez Solovieva, *Piecewise-Affine Slope-Chamber Search for a Proper Gaussian Perfect Number: Method, Prior-Art Search, and Historical Feasibility Model (2003–2026)*, an unpublished manuscript dated 17 August 2026. The entry supplies the explicit factorization and divisor-sum identity. The unpublished manuscript itself was not obtained in this screen.

The $50 first-cycle offer therefore cannot be treated as an unclaimed new target. Whether that prior solver submitted a claim or received payment was not checked. The visible old offer is not evidence that the underlying first-example question remains open.
