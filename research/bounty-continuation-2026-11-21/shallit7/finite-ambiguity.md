# Finite ambiguity of grammars for primitive words

**Proposition.** If an ordinary context-free grammar generates exactly the nonempty primitive binary words, its numbers of parse trees per word have no finite uniform upper bound. This is a partial result on [Shallit's £200 problem 7](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~shallit/Talks/bc4.pdf). The sponsor asks whether any context-free grammar exists. An explicit context-free language at the end shows why the distinction matters.

## The complement of a matched diagonal

Write $`H=\{(u,u):u\in\mathbb R^m\}`$ and $`E=\mathbb N_0^{2m}\setminus H`$ for $`m\ge1`$. In a linear set $`b+\mathbb N_0P`$, the last term denotes all nonnegative integer sums from a finite set of period vectors. Periods are *stratified* if each has at most two nonzero coordinates and there are no two periods with respective supports $`\{i,k\},\{j,l\}`$ where $`i<j<k<l`$. All bases and periods are nonnegative integer vectors.

**Theorem.** In every finite cover of $`E`$ by stratified linear sets contained in $`E`$, some point belongs to at least $`m`$ members. There is a cover with maximum overlap exactly $`m`$ and linearly independent periods in each member.

For the upper bound, use two members for each index $`i\le m`$. For $`x_i>x_{m+i}`$, use base $`e_i`$ and periods $`e_i,e_i+e_{m+i}`$ together with the unit vectors outside the pair. Reverse the roles for the other strict inequality. These periods are independent and stratified. Each point belongs to one member for every differing pair.

**Span lemma.** If $`A`$ is a subset of a stratified family and $`V=\mathop{\mathrm{span}}_{\mathbb R}A`$ contains $`H`$, then $`V`$ has codimension at most one. This is the support-graph argument in the proof of Brough's Proposition 3.8. [Brough, `3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.1806)

Place the coordinates on a circle in order. Each two-coordinate period connects its endpoints by a chord. On a connected component, a vector orthogonal to the periods has all its coordinates determined by any one coordinate, because both coefficients on every edge are nonzero. A singleton period forces its coordinate to vanish. The equations split by component, so each component contributes at most one dimension, and a nonzero component solution has support equal to the entire component. Extend it by zero to all coordinates. Since $`V^\perp\subset H^\perp`$, its support contains both $`i`$ and $`m+i`$ whenever it contains either. Two different nonzero components would connect two alternating pairs. Paths between alternating boundary vertices in a disk must meet, whereas stratification makes the drawing crossing-free and different components have disjoint vertices. This proves the lemma, including isolated vertices.

**Lower bound.** Fix a finite cover. Choose a strictly positive integer $`z\in H`$ that belongs to no span of any subset of any member's periods unless that span contains $`H`$. There are finitely many forbidden proper subspaces within $`H`$. For example, take $`(1,T,\ldots,T^{m-1})`$ twice with a sufficiently large integer $`T`$.

Choose an integer $`D>0`$ such that for every one of these subsets $`A`$,

```math
D\bigl(\mathbb Z^{2m}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{span}}_{\mathbb R}A\bigr)
   \subseteq \mathbb ZA .
```

Such an integer exists since each integer span has finite index in the integer lattice of its real span. For every $`e\in\mathbb Z^{2m}\setminus H`$, the points $`x_N=N^2z+Ne`$ belong to $`E`$ for sufficiently large positive multiples $`N`$ of $`D`$. Call a member active for $`e`$ if it contains infinitely many of those points. At least one is active for each $`e`$.

Consider an active member $`b+S`$, with $`S=\mathbb N_0P`$ and $`C=\mathop{\mathrm{cone}}(P)`$. The limits of $`(x_N-b)/N^2`$ show $`z\in C`$, since a finitely generated cone is closed. Also $`e\in\mathop{\mathrm{span}}P`$, by applying each linear functional vanishing on $`P`$ to $`N^2z+Ne-b`$ for infinitely many $`N`$. The minimal face $`F`$ of $`C`$ containing $`z`$ is generated by $`A=P\cap F`$. By the choice of $`z`$, its span $`V`$ contains $`H`$. The span lemma applies.

If $`e\in V`$, choose one of the covered $`x_N`$. Then $`Ne\in\mathbb ZA`$. Some positive integer multiple $`hz`$ is an integer sum of the nonzero periods in $`A`$ with every coefficient strictly positive. Indeed, $`z`$ is in the relative interior of $`F`$, so subtracting a sufficiently small positive rational multiple of the sum of those generators leaves a rational vector in their rational cone. Clear denominators in a rational nonnegative representation. For large integer $`J`$ it follows that $`Jhz-Ne\in\mathbb N_0A`$. Adding it to $`x_N`$ puts $`(N^2+Jh)z`$ in the same member, contradicting its disjointness from $`H`$.

Hence $`e\notin V`$. The span lemma now forces $`V`$ to be a hyperplane. Since $`e\in\mathop{\mathrm{span}}P`$, the cone $`C`$ is full dimensional, and $`F`$ is its unique facet containing $`z`$. Choose a rational inward normal $`\phi`$ for this facet. It vanishes on $`H`$ and $`\phi(e)>0`$, since $`\phi(e)\ne0`$ and $`N\phi(e)-\phi(b)\ge0`$ along the covered subsequence. The facet and normal depend only on the member and the common choice of $`z`$.

The normals of all members active for some direction span $`H^\perp`$. Otherwise their common rational kernel would contain an integer vector outside $`H`$. A member active for that vector would have a strictly positive normal value on it. Select $`m`$ members with independent normals and choose an integer $`e_*`$ on which all are positive: solve the independent rational equations with right-hand sides one and clear denominators.

It remains to obtain common **integer points** in the selected members. For each, the lattice $`L=\mathbb ZP`$ contains $`D\mathbb Z^{2m}`$. Its base lies in $`L`$, since it differs by an element of $`S`$ from a covered $`x_N\in D\mathbb Z^{2m}`$. There is $`g\in S`$ satisfying $`g+(C\cap L)\subseteq S`$. To see this, round down the coefficients in a nonnegative real period representation of a point of $`C\cap L`$. The possible residuals lie in a bounded set in $`L`$ and hence form a finite set. Express each as $`s_r-t_r`$ with $`s_r,t_r\in S`$, and take $`g=\sum_r t_r`$.

For every sufficiently large multiple $`N`$ of $`D`$, the point $`N^2z+Ne_*-b-g`$ lies in $`L`$ and in $`C`$. The unique facet through $`z`$ has a strictly positive linear term in $`N`$, and every other facet has a strictly positive quadratic term. The property of $`g`$ therefore puts $`N^2z+Ne_*`$ in the member. The same point belongs to all $`m`$ selected members. This proves the lower bound.

## From covers to parses

For distinct letters $`c_i`$, the language $`\{c_1^{x_1}\cdots c_{2m}^{x_{2m}}:x\in E\}`$ has minimum possible uniform ambiguity exactly $`m`$. The quantitative bounded-language theorem says that a letter-bounded language has a grammar with at most $`k`$ parses per word exactly when its exponent set has a finite cover by stratified linear sets with linearly independent periods in each and maximum overlap at most $`k`$. The upper and lower bounds above give the claim. [Koechlin, p. 41:16, citing Ginsburg–Ullian and Rosenberg](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.FSTTCS.2022.41)

Take $`2m`$ to be any power of two with $`2m\ge2`$. For $`x\in\mathbb N_0^{2m}`$ put

```math
w(x)=ab^{x_1}ab^{x_2}\cdots ab^{x_{2m}} .
```

Every proper power representation has exponent dividing the number $`2m`$ of occurrences of $`a`$, so every such word that is a proper power is a square. If $`w(x)`$ is a square, the second half starts at the $`(m+1)`$-st occurrence of $`a`$. Thus $`w(x)`$ is a square exactly when $`x\in H`$, also when some runs are empty.

Suppose the primitive words have a grammar with ambiguity at most $`k`$. Introduce distinct letters $`d_1,c_1,\ldots,d_{2m},c_{2m}`$. Lift the grammar along the length-preserving morphism $`d_i\mapsto a,\ c_i\mapsto b`$, then intersect with the regular language $`d_1c_1^*\cdots d_{2m}c_{2m}^*`$. For each fixed annotated word, each original parse has a unique lift, since its terminal leaves are fixed. The deterministic automaton for the restriction uniquely specifies the states at both ends of every node's yield. Consequently the resulting grammar still has at most $`k`$ parses per word.

Apply the bounded-language theorem in these $`4m`$ coordinates. All separator exponents are identically one, so every linear member has base one and all periods zero in the separator coordinates. Deleting those coordinates preserves overlaps, stratification and period independence. The result covers $`E`$, forcing $`k\ge m`$. The allowable powers of two give arbitrarily large $`m`$.

## Why the unrestricted problem survives

The language of binary words that are not squares is itself context-free and has exactly the same slices above. To see context-freeness directly, include all odd-length words. An even-length word is a nonsquare exactly when it factors as $`uavwbt`$ or $`ubvwat`$, where $`|u|=|v|`$ and $`|w|=|t|`$. The two displayed central letters are in corresponding positions in the two halves. Conversely, any mismatching pair at those positions yields this factorization. For each possible central letter, the language of words with equally long strings on its two sides has a grammar that adds one arbitrary terminal on each end per step. Concatenation of two such languages and finite union give the asserted grammar. The example shows that the unbounded ambiguity established here is compatible with context-freeness. Settling Shallit's problem requires an additional argument.

Petersen proved that primitive words admit no unambiguous context-free grammar. Koechlin gives another proof as Proposition 17. Neither result as stated supplies the bound for every finite degree. This note makes no priority claim for the bound and supplies no formal or independent verification.
