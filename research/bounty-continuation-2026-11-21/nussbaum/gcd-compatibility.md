# GCD-dependent compatibility in Nussbaum’s period problem

**Theorem.** Suppose a ternary formula orbit has length $`N`$ equal to the product of three distinct primes. If whether two states are compatible depends only on the gcd of their phase difference with $`N`$, then $`N\le2^n`$. Equivalently its circulant compatibility graph has integral adjacency spectrum, by [So's characterization](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2005.11.006). This includes every such period for which all maximal cliques are subgroup cosets. An arbitrary formula orbit need not have gcd-dependent compatibility.

## A witness for trivial clique stabilizer at any period

Let $`D\subseteq\mathbb Z/N\mathbb Z`$ contain 0 and the differences between adjacent phases. A set $`C`$ of phases is a clique exactly when $`C-C\subseteq D`$. For every prime $`\ell\mid N`$ let $`H_\ell`$ be the unique subgroup of order $`\ell`$. If a clique $`W`$ has the property that $`W+H_\ell`$ fails to be a clique for every $`\ell`$, then every maximal clique containing $`W`$ has trivial stabilizer. A nontrivial stabilizer would contain some $`H_\ell`$ and hence force $`W+H_\ell`$ inside the clique. Its $`N`$ distinct translates have disjoint nonempty common Boolean intersections. Thus the union contains at least $`N`$ vertices.

Conversely if a maximal clique $`C`$ has trivial stabilizer, $`C+H_\ell`$ strictly contains $`C`$ and cannot be a clique. Two nonadjacent elements of this sum use at most two representatives from $`C`$. Choose such representatives for every distinct prime divisor. Their union is a witness $`W`$ of size at most twice the number of distinct primes in $`N`$. This equivalence makes no assumption about gcd-dependence or prime multiplicities.

## Three prime axes

Write $`N=abc`$ for primes $`a<b<c`$. Under the Chinese remainder isomorphism, phases are triples. For two triples, their difference support is the subset of axes on which their entries differ. This determines the gcd with $`N`$ and is determined by it. Write $`\mathcal D`$ for the allowed supports, including the empty support. Thus there are $`2^7`$ possibilities. If all maximal cliques are subgroup cosets, $`D`$ is the union of the subgroups that are maximal cliques through zero, since every edge from zero extends to a maximal clique. Membership in a subgroup of this squarefree cyclic group depends only on the difference support. This proves the asserted scope for coset cliques.

The [certificate](check_three_prime_supports.py) enumerates support patterns, independently of the three prime sizes. The categories below account for all 128. In the first four cases it supplies a positive check for the condition stated. A leftover case must equal one of the two explicitly displayed patterns.

1. **Common subgroup (19 patterns).** Suppose toggling one fixed axis preserves membership for every allowed support. Every face label on that axis then has the same closed neighborhood as every other label with the other two coordinates fixed. Any maximal clique containing one such label contains the entire axis fiber, by maximality. The common clique stabilizer therefore contains that prime subgroup. Its index divides the product of the other two primes. If the index is one or prime, the stabilizers are linearly ordered above their common intersection, and the [least-stabilizer count](prime-power-periods.md) applies. If the index is the product of the two primes, the [weighted-grid theorem and quotient classification](all-weighted-grids.md) apply. These give the ambient bound.
2. **Hamming components (8 further patterns).** The inclusion-maximal supports are disjoint and $`\mathcal D`$ consists of all their subsets. Freeze the unused coordinates. In each component two tuples are adjacent precisely if they differ within one of these blocks. Treating each block as a single coordinate identifies a Hamming graph. The [matched-row theorem](matched-cliques.md) bounds the size of each component by the number of Boolean vertices in its own union. Different components have disjoint unions. The case with no nonempty support consists of singletons.
3. **Least stabilizer (57 further patterns).** At most one of the singleton supports is allowed. Every prime divisor of a clique stabilizer forces its singleton support to be allowed, since a clique containing an orbit of that prime subgroup contains two points differing only on that axis. As $`N`$ is squarefree, all stabilizers are trivial or equal to one fixed prime subgroup. The least-stabilizer count applies.
4. **Trivial stabilizer witness (42 further patterns).** In each case the checker finds at most six triples on alphabets of sizes $`2,3,5`$, with every pair's support allowed. For each of the three axes some pair, possibly equal, has a support whose toggle on that axis is forbidden. The triples embed in the actual axes of sizes $`a,b,c`$, preserving all equality patterns. By translating the two points independently in the indicated prime axis, their entries on that axis can be made equal or unequal while all other entries are preserved. The forbidden toggle gives two incompatible elements of $`W+H_\ell`$. Thus the witness above works for all prime sizes. The script additionally checks the translates themselves on two concrete triples of moduli.

## Two remaining patterns

Use $`X`$ for the smallest prime axis. After possibly interchanging the other two axes, write them $`Y,Z`$. The two patterns both take the form
```math
 \mathcal D=\{\varnothing,\{X\},\{Z\},\{X,Y\},\{X,Y,Z\}\}.
```
Let the axis cardinalities be $`A,B,C`$ in this order, so $`B>A`$. For fixed $`x,y`$, all $`C`$ faces with those two entries form a clique. For fixed $`x`$, the $`B`$ groups as $`y`$ varies have disjoint unions since a difference in $`Y`$ without a difference in $`X`$ is forbidden.

Fix $`x'\ne x`$. For every $`y'\ne y`$, the whole group $`(x,y,*)`$ and the whole group $`(x',y',*)`$ together form a clique, since supports containing both $`X,Y`$ are allowed for either choice on $`Z`$. Choose a Boolean vertex common to this joint clique. For different $`y'`$ the chosen vertices are distinct by the disjointness at $`x'`$. All $`C`$ faces of $`(x,y,*)`$ therefore have a common intersection with at least $`B-1`$ vertices.

If $`C`$ distinct cube faces have common intersection $`J`$, their union contains at least $`C|J|`$ vertices. Relative to $`J`$, each face has its own set of additional free coordinates among the fixed coordinates of $`J`$. Flip precisely those coordinates of every point of $`J`$. The resulting copies of $`J`$ are disjoint, because the differing additional free sets can be read off their fixed coordinates. Hence the $`B`$ disjoint groups with the fixed value $`x`$ already cover at least $`BC(B-1)\ge ABC=N`$ vertices. This finishes the final cases and the theorem.

The unrestricted [sponsor question](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~zeilberg/math640_10.html) permits arbitrary cyclic difference sets and arbitrary periods. The witness test can be used on those difference sets, but the three-axis support classification alone gives no universal reduction to this theorem.
