# Short factors and residue parity do not close the CNF gap

This is a uniform obstruction to one proposed separator construction. It does not resolve [Shallit's paid problem](target-and-prior-audit.md), improve its general lower bound, or establish novelty. The residue-parity family itself is due to Robson; the calculations below audit what that family can and cannot supply.

## 1. The attempted construction

A binary pattern of length $`\ell`$ has a straight-line CNF grammar with $`O(\ell/\log\ell)`$ productions. To see the relevant size accounting, take blocks of length

```math
b=\left\lfloor\log_2\ell-2\log_2\log_2\ell\right\rfloor
```

for sufficiently large $`\ell`$. Build the binary dictionary of all words of length at most $`b`$, using one binary production per nontrivial dictionary word. Its size is $`O(2^b)`$. Concatenate the $`O(\ell/b)`$ chosen blocks and the final shorter block. This costs $`O(\ell/\log\ell)`$ productions in total. Adding arbitrary binary prefixes and suffixes uses only constantly many extra productions: a nonterminal with rules $`D\to0\mid1\mid DD`$ generates all nonempty binary strings, and the four possibilities for empty or nonempty exterior strings are handled at the start rule. The start production of a straight-line grammar can be copied, so no unit production is required.

Therefore a distinguishing factor of length $`O(\log n)`$ would already meet the desired $`O(\log n/\log\log n)`$ cost. Such a factor need not exist. The proposed repair was to use a compressed modular-position test when short factors fail.

For $`p\ge1`$ and $`0\le i\lt p`$, define

```math
L_{p,i}=\left\{w\in\{0,1\}^*:
\sum_{\substack{0\le j\lt |w|\\j\equiv i\pmod p}}w_j
\equiv1\pmod2\right\}.
```

Robson's 1996 paper, Sections 2.2–2.3, defines this family and proves that some such test with $`p=O(\sqrt n)`$ separates every distinct equal-length pair. It does not imply a sublogarithmic CNF production bound.

## 2. Exact-language production cost of a parity test

The language $`L_{p,i}`$ has a CNF grammar with $`O(1+\log p)`$ productions. Here is a construction that keeps the production measure explicit.

Exact-length languages $`\{0,1\}^a`$ use binary addition chains. Positive bounded-length languages $`\{0,1\}^{[1,a]}`$ also have strict CNF grammars of size $`O(1+\log a)`$: use variables $`T_a`$ with

```math
T_1\to0\mid1,
\qquad
T_a\to T_{\lfloor a/2\rfloor}T_{\lceil a/2\rceil}\mid0\mid1
\quad(a\ge2).
```

At each halving depth only two neighboring positive indices are required. Induction shows that $`T_a`$ generates every binary word of each length from 1 to $`a`$.

Build full-block languages

```math
C_b=\{0,1\}^{i}\,b\,\{0,1\}^{p-i-1},
\qquad b\in\{0,1\}.
```

Zero-length factors are omitted. Each $`C_b`$ has a single binary or terminal root production after adding at most one concatenation variable. For nonempty sequences of full blocks, parity is generated by

```math
E\to C_0\mid C_0E\mid C_1O,
\qquad
O\to C_1\mid C_0O\mid C_1E.
```

The displayed unit alternatives are replaced by the single root production of $`C_0`$ or $`C_1`$. Thus this stage adds constantly many strict CNF productions.

A final incomplete block has length less than $`p`$. Its parity-zero part is the union of lengths 1 through $`i`$ and words in

```math
\{0,1\}^{i}0\{0,1\}^{[0,p-i-2]},
```

while its parity-one part is

```math
\{0,1\}^{i}1\{0,1\}^{[0,p-i-2]}.
```

The latter two parts are omitted when $`i=p-1`$. Empty tail factors are handled separately at the root, rather than by nullable grammar variables. The bounded-length construction above and finitely many root alternatives handle these tails. Combine an odd full-block sequence with a parity-zero tail, an even full-block sequence with a parity-one tail, or a parity-one tail alone. This proves the upper bound without a general unit-elimination blowup.

There is also a lower bound $`\Omega(\log p)`$ for any CNF grammar generating exactly $`L_{p,i}`$, regardless of the construction. Suppose such a grammar has $`k`$ productions. After deleting useless variables it has at most $`k`$ variables. Intersect it with the three-state regular language $`0^*1`$. The usual CNF product construction has at most $`9k`$ variables and generates precisely

```math
\{0^{i+tp}1:t\ge0\}.
```

Its CNF pumping bound is at most $`2^{9k}`$. Pump a sufficiently long accepted word. Both pumped substrings must contain only zeros: pumping a substring containing the unique 1 would create a word with a different number of ones, which is outside this intersected language. Their total positive length $`d`$ is at most $`2^{9k}`$, and acceptance after pumping forces $`p\mid d`$. Therefore

```math
p\le2^{9k},\qquad k\ge\frac{\log_2p}{9}.
```

This concerns grammars generating the entire predicate language. A grammar separating one specified pair is free to generate a different language.

## 3. One family defeats both proposed tests

Fix integers $`P,L\ge1`$. Work over $`\mathbb F_2`$ and set

```math
F_P(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{P}(1+z^j),\qquad
D=\frac{P(P+1)}2,
\qquad C(z)=z^LF_P(z^L),
\qquad n=L(D+2)+1.
```

The polynomial $`F_P`$ is nonzero, has constant and leading coefficient 1, and satisfies $`F_P(1)=0`$. Its support therefore has a positive even cardinality $`2t`$. Split the support of $`C`$ into two sets of size $`t`$, and let the length-$`n`$ words $`w,x`$ have 1s at the respective sets and 0s elsewhere. For a definite split, assign the first $`t`$ support positions to $`w`$ and the last $`t`$ to $`x`$.

The words are distinct and have the same number of ones. All their 1-positions are multiples of $`L`$, their distinct 1s are at distance at least $`L`$, and they have at least $`L`$ zeros at each end.

### Identical short-factor counts

Let $`1\le\ell\le L`$. A length-$`\ell`$ factor cannot contain two 1s. Every factor of the form

```math
0^a10^{\ell-a-1},\qquad0\le a\lt\ell,
```

occurs exactly $`t`$ times in each word: every 1 has enough zeros on both sides, and every occurrence contains exactly one 1. The factor $`0^\ell`$ consequently occurs

```math
n-\ell+1-t\ell
```

times in each. Every other length-$`\ell`$ factor occurs zero times. Thus all factor counts through length $`L`$ coincide. Both prefixes and both suffixes of length $`L`$ are also $`0^L`$.

This is stronger than agreement of short-factor presence. It also blocks any Boolean combination of statistics determined solely by these short-factor counts and these endpoint strings.

### Identical small-modulus position parity

For every $`1\le p\le P`$, $`C(z)`$ contains the factor $`1+z^{Lp}`$. Since

```math
1+z^{Lp}=(1+z^p)\sum_{a=0}^{L-1}z^{ap}
\quad\text{in }\mathbb F_2[z],
```

we have $`C(z)\equiv0\pmod{z^p-1}`$. Reduction modulo $`z^p-1`$ adds coefficients in each residue class modulo $`p`$. The coefficient vector of $`C`$ is the bitwise difference of $`w`$ and $`x`$. Hence they have the same position parity in every residue class for every $`p\le P`$.

In particular, no $`L_{p,i}`$ with $`p\le P`$ separates them. Any separating member of the residue-parity family has $`p>P`$, and any grammar implementing that entire member needs at least $`(\log_2P)/9`$ productions by Section 2.

### The asymptotic scale relevant to the attempt

For any fixed positive constant $`A`$, choose $`L=\lceil A\log_2P\rceil`$ and let $`P\to\infty`$. Then

```math
n=\Theta(P^2\log P),\qquad
\log_2n=2\log_2P+O(\log\log P),\qquad
\frac{L}{\log_2n}\longrightarrow\frac A2.
```

Thus, for any fixed desired short-factor cutoff $`c\log_2n`$, choosing $`A>2c`$ blocks that entire cutoff for sufficiently large $`P`$. At the same time, every successful whole-language residue-parity implementation costs $`\Omega(\log n)`$ productions. The proposed “short factor or a cheaper residue-parity predicate” dichotomy is false.

## 4. Exact scope and stopping point

These pairs are not evidence for an unrestricted $`\Omega(\log n)`$ grammar lower bound. With the ordered support split above, their initial runs of zeros have different lengths. The old unequal-length unary separation theorem provides an $`O(\log\log n)`$-production grammar distinguishing those run lengths. Appending a 1 and an arbitrary suffix adds only constantly many productions and separates the two original words. A small grammar can inspect a structural feature that the proposed tests miss.

Nor does this obstruction cover long factors that happen to have short straight-line grammars, arbitrary regular transductions of the words, or nonlinear context-free tests. No claim that any of those classes has a uniform small separator has been proved here.

The full unresolved obligation remains a construction with $`O(\log n/\log\log n)`$ productions for every pair, or a matching stronger general lower bound. This attempt stops because its proposed exhaustive dichotomy has the explicit counterfamily above. No finite-word experiment is being promoted to a full resolution.
