# Erdős 835: exact cubic moments and a uniform feasibility barrier

Date: 2026-11-21. Status: research deduction, independently reviewed by the coordinator and a second worker. This note neither constructs the coloring in Erdős 835 nor proves its
nonexistence. It does not complete a bounty. No novelty or priority claim is made.

## Scope and sources

The exact target asks whether there exists an integer $`k>2`$ for which

```math
\chi(J(2k,k))=k+1.
```

Equivalently, the $`k`$-subsets of a $`2k`$-set admit $`k+1`$ colors, with every
$`(k+1)`$-set seeing every color among its $`k`$-subsets. The live target is
[Conjectures.io, Erdős 835](https://conjectures.io/problems/erdos835-erdos-835).
The eligible statement is existential in $`k`$, not a prescribed finite case.

The following are prior work, not results claimed here:

* [Ma and Tang's primary note](https://github.com/QuanyuTang/erdos-problem-835/blob/7f131832bab5abfc903014ddb61efff21d56ae01/On_Problem_835.pdf)
  excludes composite $`k+1`$ using Steiner-system divisibility. Prime $`k+1`$
  passes all those divisibility tests.
* The [prior E835 research page](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/research/erdos_835)
  records the top-eigenspace formulation, exact pair counts, complement closure,
  and feasible pair moments. Its partition analysis focuses on primes
  $`k+1\ge17`$. This note does not infer that the smaller primes have all been
  eliminated merely from that focus.
* Laurent Manivel, [*A note on certain Kronecker coefficients*, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0809.3710),
  proves that $`[k,k]\otimes[k,k]`$ is multiplicity free, with the stated even-
  partition and four-part odd-partition decomposition. In particular, for even
  $`k`$, the invariant subspace of $`[k,k]^{\otimes3}`$ has dimension one.
* Bremner, Bickis and Soltanifar,
  [*Cayley's hyperdeterminant: a combinatorial approach via representation theory*, Theorem 36](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.5068),
  prove that the invariant algebra for a $`2\times2\times2`$ tensor under
  $`SL_2^3`$ is generated by Cayley's quartic hyperdeterminant.

The contribution of this work session is an explicit calculation and limitation
of a proposed third-moment obstruction. Three statements are kept separate:

1. The ground-set-symmetrized third moments are uniquely forced. Their exact
   coefficient formula is given below.
2. Their one-vertex-conditioned degree-one covariance matrices are positive
   semidefinite for **every even $`k\ge2`$**.
3. The five possible equality-pattern probabilities on three vertices are
   nonnegative for **every even $`k\ge14`$**. Consequently these tests cannot
   exclude any prime $`k+1\ge17`$.

The third statement is a uniform proof, not an extrapolation from computations.
It does not prove feasibility of every higher-order semidefinite hierarchy, nor
the existence of a joint distribution on colorings.

## 1. The projector and forced cubic tensor

Fix even $`k\ge2`$, and put $`p=k+1`$. Let
$`\mathcal V=\binom{[2k]}k`$, $`N=|\mathcal V|`$. All vector inner products
below are unnormalized sums over $`\mathcal V`$.

Let $`D`$ be the inclusion operator from functions on $`k`$-sets to functions on
$`(k-1)`$-sets. Write $`V_k=\ker D`$, and let $`E`$ be its orthogonal projector.
For $`A,B\in\mathcal V`$, with $`s=|A\cap B|`$,

```math
E_{AB}=\frac{(-1)^s}{p\binom ks},\qquad
 e_{AB}=pE_{AB}=\frac{(-1)^s}{\binom ks}.                 \tag{1}
```

The same expression uses Johnson distance $`k-s`$, because $`k`$ is even.
The projector has rank $`N/p`$, $`E_{AA}=1/p`$, and $`E\mathbf1=0`$.
In particular $`E_{A^c,B}=E_{AB}`$.

Suppose temporarily that a proper $`p`$-coloring exists, and average it under
all permutations of the $`2k`$ points and all permutations of its colors. For a
fixed color $`i`$, put

```math
X_i(A)=\mathbf1_{\{c(A)=i\}},\qquad h_i=X_i-1/p.
```

Each star is rainbow, so $`h_i\in V_k`$. The representation $`V_k`$ is the
Specht module $`[k,k]`$. Its irreducibility, or the known pair-level equality
calculation, gives

```math
\mathbb E[h_i(A)h_i(B)]=\frac{k}{p}E_{AB}.             \tag{2}
```

The scalar is fixed by the diagonal variance $`k/p^2`$. Thus the same-color
pair probability is

```math
K_{AB}=\Pr(c(A)=c(B))=\frac1p+kE_{AB}
       =\frac{1+ke_{AB}}p.                             \tag{3}
```

Define an invariant cubic tensor

```math
T_{ABC}=\sum_{D\in\mathcal V}E_{AD}E_{BD}E_{CD}.
                                                               \tag{4}
```

It lies in $`V_k`$ in each argument. Its diagonal is

```math
\theta:=T_{AAA}
 =\frac1{p^3}\sum_{s=0}^k\frac{(-1)^s}{\binom ks}
 =\frac{2}{p^2(k+2)}.                                  \tag{5}
```

For completeness, the alternating inverse-binomial identity follows from
$`\binom{k}{s}^{-1}=(k+1)\int_0^1t^s(1-t)^{k-s}\,dt`$ and the finite geometric
sum. Hence $`T\ne0`$. Manivel's multiplicity-one theorem now implies that the
averaged tensor $`\mathbb E[h_i^{\otimes3}]`$ is a scalar multiple of $`T`$.
Its diagonal is $`k(k-1)/p^3`$, fixing that scalar.

Put

```math
R_{ABC}=T_{ABC}/\theta,\qquad
 \kappa=\frac{k(k-1)(k+2)}2.
```

The forced probability that all three colors agree is

```math
t_{ABC}=\frac1{p^2}+\frac{k}{p}(E_{AB}+E_{AC}+E_{BC})
            +\kappa T_{ABC},                           \tag{6}
```

or, equivalently,

```math
p^2t_{ABC}=1+k(e_{AB}+e_{AC}+e_{BC})+k(k-1)R_{ABC}.     \tag{7}
```

The other four aggregate equality-pattern probabilities are

```math
\begin{split}
 q_{AB|C}&=K_{AB}-t_{ABC},\\
 q_{AC|B}&=K_{AC}-t_{ABC},\\
 q_{BC|A}&=K_{BC}-t_{ABC},\\
 u_{ABC}&=1-K_{AB}-K_{AC}-K_{BC}+2t_{ABC}\\
 &=\frac{k(k-1)}{p^2}
       (1-e_{AB}-e_{AC}-e_{BC}+2R_{ABC}).                \tag{8}
 \end{split}
```

Here $`u`$ means that all three colors are distinct. These quantities sum to
one, with the correct pair marginals, by construction. Nonnegativity remains a
substantive condition; it is proved in Section 5.

## 2. Exact hyperdeterminant coefficient formula

Let $`n_{abc}`$ be the eight Venn-cell sizes of $`A,B,C`$, where

```math
n_{abc}=|\{v:\mathbf1_A(v)=a,\mathbf1_B(v)=b,
                     \mathbf1_C(v)=c\}|.
```

Index the variables $`x_0,\ldots,x_7`$ by $`4a+2b+c`$. Cayley's polynomial is

```math
\begin{split}
\Delta={}&x_0^2x_7^2+x_1^2x_6^2+x_2^2x_5^2+x_3^2x_4^2\\
 &-2(x_0x_1x_6x_7+x_0x_2x_5x_7+x_0x_3x_4x_7
       +x_1x_2x_5x_6+x_1x_3x_4x_6+x_2x_3x_4x_5)\\
 &+4(x_0x_3x_5x_6+x_1x_2x_4x_7).
\end{split}                                                   \tag{9}
```

Then the normalized tensor has the exact formula

```math
\boxed{\quad
 R_{ABC}=[x^n]\Delta^{k/2}\,
           \frac{\prod_{a,b,c}n_{abc}!}{(k!)^2}.
 \quad}                                                       \tag{10}
```

To check the factorial normalization, encode the invariant tensor by

```math
F(x)=\sum_{A,B,C\in\mathcal V}R_{ABC}
          \prod_{v=1}^{2k}x_{\mathbf1_A(v),\mathbf1_B(v),\mathbf1_C(v)}.
```

The kernel condition in each argument identifies $`F`$ with an
$`SL_2^3`$-invariant polynomial. One may see this directly from the raising
and lowering operators on the middle layer: weight zero and the vanishing down
operator imply the trivial $`SL_2`$ representation in that factor. Equivalently,
this is the Schur-Weyl correspondence used in Manivel's paper. The invariant
algebra theorem therefore gives $`F=c\Delta^{k/2}`$.

The coefficient of $`x_0^kx_7^k`$ in $`F`$ is $`N`$, since it comes precisely
from $`A=B=C`$, and $`R_{AAA}=1`$. That coefficient in $`\Delta^{k/2}`$ is
one, so $`c=N`$. A fixed Venn profile has $`(2k)!/\prod n_{abc}!`$ ordered
triples. Comparing its coefficient proves (10).

This also proves directly that the formula is not based on a choice of labels
within any Venn cell. At $`A=B`$, it reduces to $`R_{AAC}=e_{AC}`$, as required
by $`t_{AAC}=K_{AC}`$.

## 3. An elementary diagonalization after fixing one vertex

Fix $`A\in\mathcal V`$, and view $`A,A^c`$ as two $`k`$-point sets. Let
$`\mathcal H_j`$ be the kernel of the down operator on their $`j`$-subsets,
for $`0\le j\le k/2`$. Its dimension is

```math
d_j=\binom kj-\binom k{j-1}.
```

The usual up/down commutator gives this dimension and the orthogonal harmonic
decomposition of functions on subsets. The particular facts needed here can be
obtained directly. If $`g\in\mathcal H_j`$, put

```math
G_s(S)=\sum_{J\in\binom Sj}g(J),\quad |S|=s.
```

For $`j\le s\le k-j`$,

```math
D G_{s+1}=(k-s-j)G_s,\qquad
 U G_s=(s+1-j)G_{s+1},\qquad
 \|G_s\|^2=\binom{k-2j}{s-j}\|g\|^2.                  \tag{11}
```

The norm formula follows by taking inner products in the first two identities.
Lift $`g\in\mathcal H_j(A)`$, $`h\in\mathcal H_j(A^c)`$ to the $`k`$-subsets
by assigning on layer $`s=|A\cap B|`$ the function

```math
f(B)=\frac{(-1)^{s-j}}{\binom{k-2j}{s-j}}
       G_s(B\cap A)H_{k-s}(B\cap A^c).                  \tag{12}
```

The down equation is

```math
\alpha_{s+1}(k-s-j)+\alpha_s(s+1-j)=0,
```

which is exactly satisfied by (12), so $`f\in V_k`$. By (11), its squared
norm is the same on each of the $`k-2j+1`$ permitted layers. These spaces,
denoted $`W_j`$, are orthogonal and have dimension $`d_j^2`$. They fill $`V_k`$,
because

```math
\sum_{j=0}^{k/2}d_j^2
 =\binom{2k}k-\binom{2k}{k-1}=N/p.
```

The identity follows by extending the binomial differences through
$`0\le j\le k+1`$, pairing opposite differences, and applying Vandermonde's
identity.

Let $`P_j`$ be the orthogonal projector onto $`W_j`$. Transitivity within each
layer, together with its equal norm mass, gives the useful diagonal formula

```math
(P_j)_{BB}=\frac{d_j^2}
  {(k-2j+1)\binom ks^2}\quad(j\le s\le k-j).           \tag{13}
```

It is zero outside that range.

Define $`e_A=E(A,\cdot)`$ and the operator

```math
F_A=E\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}(e_A)E.
```

Its $`BC`$-entry is $`T_{ABC}`$. Orthogonality of the harmonic lifts on each
layer shows that $`F_A`$ acts on $`W_j`$ by the scalar

```math
\begin{split}
 \lambda_j
 &=\frac1{k-2j+1}\sum_{s=j}^{k-j}
                       \frac{(-1)^s}{p\binom ks}\\
 &=\frac{2(-1)^j(k-j+1)}
   {p(k+2)(k-2j+1)\binom kj}.                           \tag{14}
\end{split}
```

For the last equality, integrate the finite geometric sum as in (5). More
explicitly, the two end terms give equal beta integrals because $`k-2j`$ is
even, producing

```math
\sum_{s=j}^{k-j}\frac{(-1)^s}{\binom ks}
 =\frac{2(-1)^j(k-j+1)}{(k+2)\binom kj}.
```

## 4. Conditional positive semidefiniteness for every even k

Condition on $`c(A)=0`$. The mean of $`X_0(B)`$ is $`K_{AB}`$, and its forced
second moment with $`X_0(C)`$ is $`t_{ABC}`$. Its covariance operator is

```math
C_A=\frac{k}{p}E+\kappa F_A-k^2 e_Ae_A^{\mathsf T}.     \tag{15}
```

The $`j=0`$ space is spanned by $`e_A`$, on which this is zero. On $`W_j`$,

```math
C_A|_{W_j}=\left(\frac{k}{p}+\kappa\lambda_j\right)I
       \quad(j\ge1).                                   \tag{16}
```

For a color contrast $`z=(0,z_1,\ldots,z_k)`$ with $`\sum_i z_i=0`$, the
conditional mean of $`z_{c(B)}`$ is zero. Its covariance, divided by
$`\sum_i z_i^2`$, is

```math
Q_A=E-\frac{p(k+2)}2F_A.                               \tag{17}
```

Indeed, its entries are
$`(K_{BC}-t)/k-u/[k(k-1)]`$. The remaining color direction is determined by
the identity that all color indicators sum to one. Color-permutation symmetry
annihilates the cross blocks. Thus (15) and (17) account for the full covariance
matrix of degree-one color indicators conditioned on one color at one vertex.

The required scalar bounds are

```math
-\frac{2}{p(k-1)(k+2)}=\lambda_1
 \ \le\lambda_j\le\lambda_0=\frac2{p(k+2)}.             \tag{18}
```

For even $`j\ge2`$,

```math
\frac{\lambda_j}{\lambda_0}
 =\frac{k-j+1}{(k-2j+1)\binom kj}
 \le\frac{k+2}{k(k-1)}\le1.
```

Here the ratio before the binomial is at most $`(k+2)/2`$, and
$`\binom kj\ge\binom k2`$. For odd $`j\ge3`$, necessarily $`k\ge6`$, and

```math
\frac{|\lambda_j|}{|\lambda_1|}
 \le\frac{(k-1)(k+2)}{2\binom k3}
 =\frac{3(k+2)}{k(k-2)}\le1.
```

The last inequality is equivalent to $`(k-6)(k+1)\ge0`$. The indices $`j=0,1`$
are explicit. This covers $`k=2,4`$ as well, where no further negative index
occurs. Equations (16)--(18) prove both covariance operators are positive
semidefinite. In particular the $`j=1`$ eigenvalue in (16) is also zero.

This proof did not assume an existing coloring after defining the forced
moments: the formulas themselves supply the positive semidefinite operators.

## 5. Entrywise probability feasibility for every even k at least 14

Write the folded distance as

```math
\delta(A,B)=\min(|A\cap B|,k-|A\cap B|).
```

Complementing any of $`A,B,C`$ leaves all formulas unchanged. This follows
directly from (1) and (4), without assuming a coloring.

### 5.1 Equal, complementary and adjacent pairs

If $`\delta(A,B)=0`$, then $`K_{AB}=1`$ and $`t_{ABC}=K_{AC}=K_{BC}`$.
All five probabilities are therefore nonnegative, since $`0\le K\le1`$.

If $`\delta(A,B)=1`$, then $`K_{AB}=0`$. The diagonal of $`C_A`$ at $`B`$
is $`K_{AB}(1-K_{AB})=0`$. A zero diagonal entry in a positive semidefinite
matrix has zero row, so (15) forces $`t_{ABC}=0`$ for every $`C`$. Thus the
three pair-only probabilities are their respective nonnegative $`K`$'s. If

$`\delta(A,C),\delta(B,C)\ge2,`$

then $`K_{AC},K_{BC}\le1/(k-1)`$, hence
$`u=1-K_{AC}-K_{BC}\ge0`$ for $`k\ge4`$. If another folded distance is zero
or one, the same conclusion follows immediately from the preceding cases.

It remains to consider triples whose three folded distances are at least two.

### 5.2 A uniform estimate away from two short pairs

Suppose some vertex, renamed $`A`$, has folded distance at least three from
both $`B,C`$. Set $`r=|A\cap B|`$, $`s=|A\cap C|`$. Then

```math
\binom kr,\binom ks\ge\binom k3.
```

For $`k\ge14`$,

```math
\max_{3\le j\le k/2}|\lambda_j|=|\lambda_3|
   =\frac{12}{p(k+2)k(k-1)(k-5)}.                       \tag{19}
```

Here is an elementary check valid for the entire range. The successive
absolute-value ratio is

```math
\frac{|\lambda_{j+1}|}{|\lambda_j|}
 =\frac{(j+1)(k-2j+1)}{(k-j+1)(k-2j-1)}.
```

It is at most one exactly when $`(k-2j)^2\ge k+2`$. Consequently the maximum
on $`3\le j\le k/2`$ is at an endpoint. Comparing the middle endpoint to

$`j=3`$ amounts to

```math
\binom k{k/2}\ge\frac{k(k-1)(k-5)(k/2+1)}6.
```

For $`k=14`$ this is $`3432\ge2184`$. For even $`k\ge16`$, use
$`\binom k{k/2}\ge\binom k5`$. The sufficient polynomial inequality is

```math
(k-2)(k-3)(k-4)-10(k-5)(k+2)
 =x^3+29x^2+216x+204>0,\qquad x=k-16.
```

Now split $`F_A`$ into $`j=0,1,2`$ and the remaining spaces. For the first three,
Cauchy-Schwarz and (13)--(14) give

```math
\left|\frac{\lambda_j}{\theta}(P_j)_{BC}\right|
 \le\frac{p\binom kj}{(k-j+1)\binom kr\binom ks}.
```

Their summed numerator is $`1+p+pk/2=(k^2+3k+4)/2`$. For the remaining
spaces, the operator norm bound (19) and
$`\|E(B,\cdot)\|=\|E(C,\cdot)\|=p^{-1/2}`$ give

```math
|R_{ABC}|\le B_k:=
 \frac{k^2+3k+4}{2\binom k3^2}
 +\frac6{k(k-1)(k-5)}.                                 \tag{20}
```

The useful strict estimate is

```math
k(k-1)B_k<1-\frac{18}{(k-1)(k-2)}<1.                  \tag{21}
```

Subtract the left side from the middle expression. Its denominator is
$`k(k-5)(k-2)^2(k-1)>0`$. Its numerator, on writing $`x=k-14`$, is

```math
x^5+54x^4+1091x^3+9828x^2+34362x+12204>0.
```

For every nondegenerate pair in the current case,

```math
-\binom k3^{-1}\le e_{UV}\le\binom k2^{-1}.
```

Equations (7), (20) and (21) imply $`t>0`$. For each of the three pair-only
probabilities,

```math
\frac{p^2}{k}q_{AB|C}
 =1+ke_{AB}-e_{AC}-e_{BC}-(k-1)R
 >1-\frac6{(k-1)(k-2)}-\frac4{k(k-1)}-\frac1k>0.
```

The last expression is positive at $`k=14`$, and each subtracted term decreases
thereafter. Similarly the bracket in (8) is greater than
$`1-8/[k(k-1)]>0`$. This proves all five signs in this geometry.

### 5.3 The four remaining geometries

If no vertex has folded distance at least three from the other two, at least
two of the three pairs have folded distance two. Rename their common vertex

$`A`$, and complement $`B,C`$ as necessary so both have ordinary Johnson
distance two from $`A`$. Write

```math
B=A\setminus X\cup Y,\qquad C=A\setminus Z\cup W,
```

where $`X,Z\subset A`$, $`Y,W\subset A^c`$, and all four sets have size two.
Put $`a=|X\cap Z|`$, $`b=|Y\cap W|`$. The distance between $`B,C`$ is
$`4-a-b`$. Values $`a+b=3,4`$ are already covered by adjacent or equal pairs.
Exchanging the halves interchanges $`a,b`$. Thus only

```math
(a,b)=(0,0),(0,1),(0,2),(1,1)
```

remain. Their Venn profile is

```math
(n_0,\ldots,n_7)
 =(k-4+b,2-b,2-b,b,a,2-a,2-a,k-4+a).
```

Formula (10) gives the following exact $`R`$'s:

| $`(a,b)`$ | $`R`$ |
|---|---|
| $`(0,0)`$ | $`4(k-7)(k-5)/[k(k-3)^2(k-2)(k-1)^2]`$ |
| $`(0,1)`$ | $`8(k-5)/[k(k-3)(k-2)(k-1)^2]`$ |
| $`(0,2)`$ | $`16/[k(k-3)(k-1)^2]`$ |
| $`(1,1)`$ | $`-(k-11)/[k(k-2)(k-1)^2]`$ |

For a short independently checkable coefficient calculation, write $`m=k/2`$.
The four coefficients of $`\Delta^m`$, before the factorial factor in (10),
are respectively

```math
\begin{split}
 &m(m-1)(2m-7)(2m-5),\\
 &8m(m-1)(2m-5),\\
 &8m(m-1),\\
 &-4m(m-1)(2m-11).
\end{split}
```

These follow by expanding the twelve monomials in (9): the six non-end cell
exponents have total at most eight, so only finitely many non-
$`x_0^2x_7^2`$ factors contribute. The accompanying symbolic verifier lists
and sums those contributions as polynomials in $`m`$.

Substitute in (7)--(8). The following table gives the five probabilities, with
each row divided by the listed common denominator. The first two pair-only
probabilities are equal.

| $`(a,b)`$ | Denominator | $`t`$ numerator | $`q_{AB\mid C}=q_{AC\mid B}`$ numerator | $`q_{BC\mid A}`$ numerator | $`u`$ numerator |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $`(0,0)`$ | $`(k-3)^2(k-2)(k-1)`$ | $`k^2-7k+14`$ | $`(k-4)(k^2-5k+8)`$ | $`k^3-11k^2+46k-68`$ | $`(k-4)(k^3-8k^2+25k-34)`$ |
| $`(0,1)`$ | $`(k-3)(k-2)(k-1)`$ | $`k-4`$ | $`k^2-6k+10`$ | $`(k-4)^2`$ | $`k^3-9k^2+30k-38`$ |
| $`(0,2)`$ | $`(k-3)(k-1)`$ | 1 | $`k-4`$ | $`k-4`$ | $`k^2-7k+14`$ |
| $`(1,1)`$ | $`(k-2)(k-1)`$ | 1 | $`k-3`$ | $`k-3`$ | $`k^2-6k+10`$ |

All entries are nonnegative already for $`k\ge4`$. The quadratic numerators
have negative discriminant and positive leading coefficient. The three cubic
numerators are positive at four and have positive derivative on
$`[4,\infty)`$: respectively the derivative discriminants are negative,
negative, and the derivative is $`3((k-3)^2+1)`$. The displayed linear factors
are nonnegative there. This finishes the uniform proof for even $`k\ge14`$.

## 6. What the calculation does and does not settle

For every even $`k\ge14`$, equations (3), (6) and (8) define nonnegative,
consistent distributions on the color-equality patterns of any three vertices.
Splitting each equality pattern uniformly among its permitted color labels
gives full color-symmetric three-vertex distributions. The covariance matrices
of linear color indicators conditioned on one specified color are positive
semidefinite, by Section 4. Adjacencies have zero same-color probability, and
all first and pair marginals agree.

These are necessary conditions for a coloring, not sufficient conditions.
There is no constructed coloring, no verified obstruction to every possible
coloring, and no compatibility proof for four or more vertices. In particular,
the existence question quantified over $`k>2`$ remains unresolved by this note.
Any next spectral attack must use a condition beyond this particular forced
third-moment system, such as additional higher moments or unsymmetrized
compatibility constraints.

## Verification record

The proof of Sections 3--5 is symbolic and uniform. Computations were used to
check normalizations and the four short-distance coefficient extractions:

* `check_erdos835_cubic.py` computes the projector-sum tensor directly with exact
  rational arithmetic on small parameter values.
* `check_erdos835_hyperdet.py` computes sparse powers of the twelve-term
  hyperdeterminant and the five pattern probabilities. It never searches for
  colorings.
* `verify_erdos835_cubic_barrier.py` compares the two tensor definitions on all
  245 triple geometries at $`k=2,4,6`$, and verifies the symbolic identities used
  in the uniform proof. Its output is saved separately.

Earlier exploratory coefficient checks at $`k=2,4,6,8,16,20,30`$ found no
negative equality-pattern probability. Those checks are not the justification
for the uniform theorem and are not counted as solved cases or bounty results.

The polynomial invariance and multiplicity results used here are established
primary-source theorems. Targeted searches for E835/hyperdeterminant and
third-moment Johnson-coloring obstructions did not locate this exact writeup;
that limited search is not a priority claim. The relation to existing
three-point coding/semidefinite bounds needs a separate literature comparison
before any novelty assertion.
