# Named conjecture research

Date: 21 November 2026. Coordination: [issue #156](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/156).

## Scope

The target is complete resolution of named open conjectures. No Lean work is part of this run. A finite screen, a numerical special case, an improvement to a known bound, a result previously present in this repository, or a prior published resolution does not count as a new resolution.

A result will be counted only after the exact original statement is transcribed, a fresh prior-art search is recorded, and its complete proof or exact refutation receives a separate review. Search failures alone do not establish priority.

- Algebra: fresh matrix and algebra conjectures outside the active Minc campaign.
- Analysis: full analytic and special-function conjectures, with current primary-source screening.
- Root: additional graph-theoretic targets, cross-checks, and integration.

## Further work on established conjectures

These research checkpoints do not increase the resolution count.

- **Standard Simplex.** The [reviewed low-correlation theorem](../standard-simplex/SMALL_CORRELATION.md) proves that for every number of cells at least three, there is a positive correlation threshold depending only on that number such that the regular simplex uniquely maximizes noise stability among all equal-mass measurable partitions, in every permitted ambient dimension, throughout that interval. The proof combines [sharp first-moment stability](../standard-simplex/FIRST_MOMENT_STABILITY.md), a dimension-independent Hermite remainder estimate, and separate core and tail bounds. The [quantitative noise-stability corollary](../standard-simplex/NOISE_STABILITY.md) bounds the deficit below by a positive constant times the correlation and the squared distance from the family of regular simplex partitions; a balanced strip exchange proves that power is sharp. It uses the explicitly credited prior all-cell first-moment theorem; that prior manuscript and the new deduction remain unrefereed. [Separate mathematical reviews](../standard-simplex/) and the completed [bounded primary-source and archive audit](../standard-simplex/LOW_CORRELATION_PRIOR_AUDIT.md) are included. Larger positive correlations and the negative-correlation clause remain unresolved here. This is a partial theorem, with no worldwide first-discovery claim.
- **Audenaert.** The [norm-compression checkpoint](../audenaert-norm-compression/) derives the necessary entropy inequality at the Hilbert–Schmidt exponent, including singular and zero blocks. An exact Holevo-information decomposition identifies the missing joint entropy tradeoff. Separate lower bounds on its two terms cannot be added. Exact examples exclude two proposed applications of existing theorems. A claimed-resolution catalogue entry was checked against its primary paper and found to concern a different Audenaert inequality. The full two-row norm-compression conjecture remains unresolved here.
- **Marcus–de Oliveira.** A [reviewed spectral-jet reduction](../marcus-de-oliveira-spectral-jet/) shows how an exact strict separator at a real spectral base would produce an analytic counterexample family by imaginary perturbation. No separator was found. A separate Walsh-matrix argument gives an exact obstruction to common nonnegative permutation weights matching all compound-minor probabilities. That stronger coupling is already known to fail; the checkpoint credits the prior disproof and does not infer a Marcus–de Oliveira counterexample from it. Floating-point linear-programming screens remain diagnostic.
- **Merino–Welsh.** The [joint orientation/Tutte-factor research](../merino-welsh-joint-constraints/) isolates an unproved coupling inequality and records exact block decompositions and minimum-counterexample constraints. Infinite graph families disprove a proposed shortcut that discarded the Tutte factor. A deletion/contraction calculation locates the mixed term missing from an attempted induction; the available scalar constraints do not close it. None of these statements disproves or proves Merino–Welsh.
- **Bourin.** The [crossed-Heinz checkpoint](../bourin-crossed-heinz/) proves the full unitarily invariant norm inequality in a neighborhood of each endpoint for every fixed positive semidefinite pair. The neighborhood may depend on the pair. For each Ky Fan sum, either equality holds at every interior parameter or the endpoint inequalities are strict. The proof handles singular summands and repeated cutoff eigenvalues, and credits Tian–Xu's global operator-norm theorem in its equality case. Exact examples exclude two stronger contraction routes; the positive-block route is attributed to its earlier literature. This gives no common interval for all pairs and no full resolution or priority claim for the partial lemmas.
