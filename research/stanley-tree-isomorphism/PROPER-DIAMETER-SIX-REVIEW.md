# Review and sources for proper trees of diameter six

The [theorem](PROPER-DIAMETER-SIX.md) determines every proper unweighted tree of diameter six among all finite trees using its ordinary chromatic symmetric function. It also determines the extended generalized degree polynomial of the weighted core of every proper tree, in arbitrary diameter. The original Stanley question and the diameter-six case with an arbitrary distribution of nonleaf vertices lacking leaf neighbors stay open. This is a written proof, independently reviewed within the WadeDavis Codex research session. It has no Lean formalization or external referee report. [Original question](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1995.1020) · [Tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/315)

## Internal mathematical review

One mathematical contributor in the WadeDavis session derived both the birational two-colour extraction and the central factorization. Two other internal reviewers independently read the frozen manuscript at SHA-256 `5fe82da2ab45e58acb86fd9b2597f02717b9ced5d3f3018d9f052738bc9548d2`. The first verified that every transformed core block contributes one nonsingleton variable, the two independent rational coordinates retain cardinality and mass, and the two binomial inversions recover both monochromatic internal edge counts. The same reviewer checked properness against arbitrary competitors. There is also a direct test: the leaf count gives the core order, and this order equals the largest number of parts of a singleton-free forest precisely for proper trees. Attaining equality requires one core vertex in each component and at least one adjacent leaf for each.

The second reviewer independently checked the entire proof, including the leading degree when the central degree and sum of child exponents are tied, recognition of the central term by its order in the cardinality variable, and irreducibility of every child factor over the rational function field. The factorization allows repeated child profiles, identical parent weights and a center with no core leaf neighbors.

The first reviewer independently enumerated small weighted cores. Two trees on eleven vertices have a five-vertex path as their core and all weights two except one three. Putting the three at the center or at an adjacent nonleaf yields identical singleton weight/degree pairs and identical weight-pairs on the edges. Independent core vertex pairs of total weight four and degree-excess sum one occur three times versus twice. A pair with three nonleaf child branches gives seven versus six occurrences at order seventeen. These checks exhibit the information gained from looking at whole subsets.

## Verification

The [exact checker](verify_proper_diameter_six.py) reconstructs 279 weighted cores with arbitrary repeated weights, using all three possible comparisons of the top exponents. The center term leads in 42 examples, the child product leads in 201, and they tie in 36. The checker compares every recovered branch, including its multiset of leaf weights, with the actual tree. The specialization uses a prime of valuation one to preserve the Eisenstein property, and checks for coincidences among candidate rooted factors.

In 87 examples the checker evaluates the ordinary cut polynomial on the actual leaf-expanded unit tree at three exact specializations, compares it with direct core-subset enumeration, and verifies the complete two-dimensional binomial inversion from all coloured connected partitions. The unrestricted weight and order quantifiers follow from the written proof.

## Source and identity checks

Gonzalez, Orellana and Tomba reconstruct every tree of diameter at most five. Zeng's August 2026 preprint gives a canonical branch model for all diameter-six trees, explicitly leaves this case open and handles proper trees whose repeated core weights occur only at terminal vertices of the core. Awan's first-derivative result gives the same multiplicity restriction in every diameter using a smaller invariant. The theorem here covers arbitrary repetitions, including the common case when every core weight is equal, along with nonuniform multiplicities and unbounded genuine branching. [Gonzalez, Orellana and Tomba](https://www.mat.univie.ac.at/~slc/wpapers/FPSAC2025/78.pdf) · [Zeng, Sections 4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18179) · [Awan, revised manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13464)

Aliste-Prieto, de Mier, Orellana and Zamora introduced the marked and undotted graph polynomials used in the extraction. Their Lemma 7.10 separates the block count in a strictly weighted tree; Lemma 7.13 and Corollary 7.14 recover marked two-block cuts. Their reconstruction covers strictly weighted stars and double stars, and proper original trees through diameter five. The proof here recovers the two-colour aggregate for every block count and separately records both sizes and both internal edge counts. Liu and Tang’s earlier work on the leaf adjacency sequence already recovers the degree and number of leaf neighbors of individual vertices. [Marked graphs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11787) · [Leaf adjacency sequences](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.18972)

The extended generalized degree polynomial is an established invariant. The chromatic MacMahon theorem recovers it from a weighted chromatic function in two alphabets; the ordinary chromatic function of a weighted core alone can fail to determine even its weighted generalized degree polynomial. Here the input is the ordinary unweighted chromatic symmetric function of the *leaf-expanded tree*. The core extraction retains cardinality and weight independently from that input. Earlier work on the unweighted generalized degree polynomial retains the ordinary subset cardinality and edge counts. [Chromatic MacMahon functions, Theorem 1.1 and Figure 1](https://jeremymartinmath.github.io/papers/ChromaticMacMahon.pdf) · [Unweighted generalized degree polynomial](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10333)

Orellana and Tomba show that a particular uniform leaf extension gives a subclass of proper trees equivalent to the full Stanley question. Its diameter grows by two. The reduction leaves the stated diameter-six classification open. The earlier [independent subdivision theorem](DECORATED-SUBDIVISION-DENSITY.md) identifies a density-one family on each fixed skeleton. The present theorem covers every permitted tree, including short edges and any number of branches. On 27 November 2026 the bounded source check covered these primary papers, diameter-six and radius-three reconstruction, weighted radius-two trees, degree-marked independence polynomials, both permanent repository branches and the current issue and PR queue. No exact earlier resolution of all proper trees of diameter six was found. The supplement remains in the existing unranked Stanley campaign. [Orellana and Tomba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18940)
