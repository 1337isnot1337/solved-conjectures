# Recovering a marked comparison from an unmarked tree distance histogram

Fix a positive reduced metric tree $`T`$ with rationally independent edge lengths and $`m`$ leaves. Let $`R`$ be any positive reduced metric tree with the same signed measure of reduced pair distances,

```math
B=\sum_{\{u,v\}}w_u w_v\delta_{d(u,v)},\qquad w_u=\deg(u)-2.
```

The [signed second difference](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md) gives this measure from the ordinary vertex-distance histogram of an integer subdivision. It is also equivalent to the [distribution of distances between two arclength samples](METRIC-TREE-DISTRIBUTION.md). Write $`J=m+c`$ for the number of rival leaves. We treat $`c\ge1`$. A path target with two leaves admits no such rival since the squared-defect moment is two. In the proof below both trees have a hub.

**Theorem.** If $`m\ge97c`$, the two trees have leaves with identical full signed distance profiles. Mark their respective parents. The original trees then have identical marked profiles as well as the original unmarked pair measure. Their labelled subtrees spanned by the marks and all but at most $`4c`$ target leaves are isometric. All nonzero target vertex heights from the mark are rationally independent. This conclusion allows arbitrary vertex degrees and makes no variability assumption on a rival edge.

**Finite reduction.** For each positive integer $`c`$, the existence anywhere of a generic unmarked ambiguity with exactly $`c`$ extra leaves is decidable by testing all reduced target topologies with fewer than $`97c`$ leaves. Rivals in this test have fewer than $`98c`$ leaves. Both vertex bounds are linear in $`c`$. Moreover, any ambiguity at or above the threshold supplies a marked ambiguity with the same surplus on at most $`4c+1`$ target leaves. This is a reduction for each surplus. A uniform affirmative outcome of the finite tests, and Stanley's original chromatic symmetric function question, remain open.

## Matching signs before choosing an origin

Every target reduced vertex pair has its own positive distance, since its nonempty edge set determines the path and the target lengths are rationally independent. Expand the weight of every pair, on both trees, into integer units of its own sign. At each distance match every target unit with a rival unit of the same sign. This can be done because the target atom has a single sign and the signed totals agree. At a distance absent from the target the positive and negative rival totals agree. The total number of unmatched units of each sign is

```math
\Omega=J(J-2)-m(m-2)=c(2J-c-2).
```

Indeed the negative units are all leaf–hub pairs and the total hub defect for a tree with $`L`$ leaves is $`L-2`$. The unmatched positive and negative counts agree even separately at each distance.

For a rival leaf $`o`$, call a different rival leaf bad if its pair with $`o`$ was unmatched. Call a rival hub bad if none of its negative units paired with $`o`$ was matched. Summing bad leaf counts plus bad hub counts over all choices of $`o`$ gives at most $`3\Omega`$. Each unmatched leaf pair is counted twice and every bad hub incidence uses at least one different unmatched negative unit. Fix an origin $`o`$ for which the number $`d`$ of bad vertices satisfies $`d\le3\Omega/J\lt6c`$.

Take the rational span of all edge lengths in both trees. Quotient by the span of the target internal edge lengths and of the $`d`$ actual distances from $`o`$ to bad vertices. Write $`e_i`$ for the formal unit corresponding to target pendant $`i`$ in $`\mathbb Q^m`$, and $`g_i`$ for the image of its length in the quotient. The target pendant lengths are independent modulo its internal lengths before adding the bad heights. Hence $`\dim_{\mathbb Q}\langle g_i\rangle\ge m-d`$.

Root the target at any hub. The height of every target vertex now has tag zero or $`g_i`$ according as it is a hub or leaf $`i`$. Root the rival at $`o`$. The origin and all its bad vertices have tag zero. Each good hub can be assigned the single formal label $`e_i`$ from its matched target leaf–hub distance. A good rival leaf is assigned zero for a matched target hub pair, or $`e_i+e_j`$ for a matched target leaf pair with $`i\ne j`$. At most one rival leaf is assigned any particular formal pair because each target leaf pair has just one positive unit. These assignments represent the respective actual heights in the quotient, including when formal tags coincide there.

## Signed Fourier characters on the pendant labels

Let $`\Gamma=\sum_i\mathbb Zg_i`$. It is a free abelian group of rank at least $`m-d`$ and is generated by the displayed elements. The map $`\mathbb F_2^m\to\Gamma/2\Gamma`$ taking $`e_i`$ to the class of $`g_i`$ is onto, with kernel $`D`$ of dimension at most $`d`$. This use of the intrinsic lattice allows arbitrary rational or real relations involving rival lengths. For a sign character of $`\Gamma/2\Gamma`$ write $`z_i`$ for its value on $`g_i`$. The resulting points of the sign cube are exactly $`D^\perp`$ under the usual binary identification.

For either rooted tree let $`F(z)`$ be the signed weighted sum of its endpoint monomials in the formal tags above, using weight $`w_v`$ at vertex $`v`$. On $`D^\perp`$ each actual path has character equal to the product of its two endpoint characters. The lowest common ancestor contributes twice its height, and that height lies in $`\Gamma`$ on both trees. Consequently

```math
F(z)^2=\sum_v w_v^2+2\sum_{\{u,v\}}w_uw_v\,\chi(d(u,v)).
```

The signed pair measures agree, and their common mass determines $`\sum_v w_v^2=4-2B(\mathbb R)`$. Thus $`F_R^2=F_T^2`$ on $`D^\perp`$. The target polynomial is $`F_T=m-2-\sum_i z_i`$. The rival polynomial has constant terms, positive terms $`b_i z_i`$ from good hubs of label $`i`$, and negative terms $`-z_i z_j`$ from a simple graph of tagged good leaves. Let $`d_i`$ be the degree of vertex $`i`$ in this graph. Both polynomials have value minus two at the all-positive signs.

Take a basis of the span of words of $`D`$ of Hamming weight at most eight, chosen from those words. Let $`E`$ be the union of their supports. Then $`|E|\le8d`$, and every word of $`D`$ of weight at most eight is supported on $`E`$. Each Fourier monomial of $`F_R^2-F_T^2`$ after reducing $`z_i^2=1`$ has support size at most four. Its coefficients sum to zero in each coset of $`D`$, by linear independence of distinct characters restricted to $`D^\perp`$. Two occurring monomials in the same coset differ on at most eight coordinates. They therefore agree outside $`E`$. Setting $`z_i=1`$ on $`E`$ preserves every cancellation and gives squared equality on the entire remaining sign cube.

## The graph on the remaining labels

For a set $`S`$ of remaining labels put its signs equal to minus one and all other signs equal to one. Let $`e(S)`$ count graph edges inside $`S`$. Squared equality gives

```math
\left|-2+2\sum_{i\in S}(d_i-b_i)-4e(S)\right|=\left|2|S|-2\right|.
```

A singleton gives $`d_i-b_i=1`$. Every triple therefore contains zero or two edges. This forces the induced graph to be complete bipartite, allowing the empty graph. Indeed fix one vertex and partition the others by adjacency to it. Two distinct others are adjacent exactly when their adjacency to the fixed vertex differs. This also describes graphs on at most two vertices. If both parts have at least two vertices, take two from each. Four edges would give absolute value ten on the left and six on the right. The remaining graph is consequently empty or a spanning star.

In the empty case discard no center. In the star case discard its center. Each remaining label $`i`$ has at least one assigned leaf edge in the full graph by the singleton identity. Reserve one incident edge separately for each such $`i`$, using its star edge if applicable or an edge to $`E`$ in the empty case. No edge meets two of the remaining noncenters. The sum of $`b_i=d_i-1`$ over them is at most the number of unreserved edges. There are at most $`J-1`$ graph edges, one per good nonzero-tagged leaf different from $`o`$. If $`m>|E|+1`$, at least $`m-|E|-1`$ edges were reserved, leaving at most $`c+|E|`$. We have therefore found at least

```math
m-c-2|E|-1\ \ge\ m-c-16(6c-1)-1=m-97c+15
```

labels $`i`$ which occur at exactly one rival leaf and at no rival hub. These leaves are distinct across such labels. The hypothesis $`m\ge97c`$ ensures that there are remaining labels to which the argument applies.

## Real distances, common marks and the finite test

At a label $`i`$ just found a rival pair's formal endpoint sum modulo two contains $`i`$ precisely when that pair meets its unique rival leaf. The same is true of target pairs and leaf $`i`$. Any two pairs, on either tree, with the same *actual distance* have the same parity class in $`\Gamma/2\Gamma`$. Their endpoint parity supports have at most four labels apiece, so their difference belongs to $`D`$, has weight at most eight and vanishes at $`i\notin E`$. Membership at that label is therefore constant among all occurrences of the distance, even at a fully canceled rival distance.

Restrict the original real signed measure to distances with this membership. It yields exactly the contribution of pairs touching the indicated leaf in each tree. Negating that restriction and adjoining $`-\delta_0`$ gives their identical full signed distance profiles. In particular the nearest other reduced vertex is the parent $`p`$, alone at its pendant length $`a`$. Its defect is positive. All other paths from the leaf pass through $`p`$. Translating the positive-distance part of the profile by $`-a`$ yields $`\sum_{x\ne i}w_x\delta_{d(p,x)}`$. Subtracting $`\delta_a`$ adds back the leaf and gives the full marked profile $`U_p`$ on the original tree. The two parents thus supply the required marks. Every edge of the target is a difference of successive heights from the parent mark. The nonzero heights number exactly as many as the independent edge lengths and are themselves rationally independent. The [marked comparison theorem](ROBUST-HUB-DISTANCES.md) now gives the common labelled metric subtree on all but at most $`4c`$ target leaves and the mark.

To reduce a marked ambiguity, use the signed-profile deletion identities in [the marked two-surplus proof](MARKED-TWO-SURPLUS.md#peeling-a-leaf-while-keeping-the-mark). The marked comparison theorem leaves at most $`4c`$ exceptional labels for any $`c`$. At most one further target leaf has the mark inside its open pendant. For a clean leaf this happens precisely when its root distance is strictly less than its pendant length. Both quantities agree in the two trees, so the same leaf is protected on each side whenever it is clean. Whenever more than $`4c+1`$ target leaves remain, remove a nonexceptional, unprotected leaf simultaneously and retain the mark. The original identities for the parent defect, marked profile and pair measure hold for arbitrary $`c`$. They preserve the surplus and independence of the surviving nonzero heights. Restoring the ordinary positive reduced trees by forgetting the mark gives a genuine unmarked comparison with the same surplus. Its target reduced edge lengths remain independent: the individual edge lengths with the mark retained are differences of independent rooted heights in a triangular invertible change of basis, and suppressing a bivalent mark replaces two of them by their sum.

If an unmarked ambiguity exists on $`m\ge97c`$ leaves it thus occurs also on at most $`4c+1\lt97c`$ leaves. Finite reduced trees with $`L`$ leaves have at most $`2L-2`$ vertices. Testing every smaller topology and the prescribed surplus using the [rational matching-cell algorithm](PATH-HISTOGRAM-DECISION.md) decides existence. A matching cell whose projection is full dimensional contains rationally independent target vectors. Conversely a comparison with an independent target lies in such a cell, and the cell supplies a positive-density family of integer subdivisions. A uniform affirmative test gives the usual exceptional estimate $`O_T(t^{k-2})`$ for a target on $`k`$ reduced vertices. None of these finite tests is assumed to have returned an affirmative outcome.
