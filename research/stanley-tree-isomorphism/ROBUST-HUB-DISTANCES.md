# Recovering arbitrary branching from a tree's distance histogram

For a finite reduced tree with at least one edge, all of positive length, let the defect of a vertex $`v`$ be $`w_v=\deg(v)-2`$. Leaves have defect minus one. A hub of degree at least four is called a high-degree vertex below. Write $`L`$ for the number of leaves and put

```math
M=\sum_v w_v^2,\qquad
\Delta=M-\lvert V\rvert,\qquad
K=\sum_{v\text{ a hub}}\binom{w_v}{2}.
```

A consequence for arbitrary valences is the following. Fix a reduced topology $`T`$. Suppose that **each component at every vertex of degree at least four contains more than $`4K`$ leaves of $`T`$**. Then all but $`O_T(t^{k-2})`$ of the $`t^{k-1}`$ labelled integer subdivisions with lengths in $`\{1,\ldots,t\}`$ are determined among all finite trees by the ordinary histogram of unordered vertex-pair distances. Here $`k`$ is the number of reduced vertices. The same generic real lengths are determined by the distribution of the distance between two independent arclength samples, against every finite metric tree with positive edge lengths. There is no bound on the number or degrees of the high-degree vertices. The condition only concerns the fixed reduced topology.

For an arbitrary topology, even without the branch hypothesis, a generic subdivision is always the **unique minimum-leaf representative** of its histogram. Its leaf count can be read from negative variation, so the canonical minimum representative can be recovered without supplying a degree sequence. Other representatives, if present, have strictly more leaves.

## The minimum-leaf representative

Use the signed measure $`B=\sum_{u\lt v}w_uw_v\delta_{d(u,v)}`$ over unordered reduced vertex pairs, obtained from the ordinary histogram by the [signed second difference](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md). Its first moment is total length, since the sum of defects on either side of each edge is minus one. The total weight of leaf–hub pairs before cancellation is $`-L(L-2)`$, by the degree sum. For generic lengths every pair distance is distinct, so the observed negative variation is $`L(L-2)`$. Every competing tree has at least this many leaves in the sense $`L_R(L_R-2)\ge L(L-2)`$, hence $`L_R\ge L`$.

Equality of the leaf counts permits no mixed-sign cancellations in the competitor. Every nonempty reduced path in it then has a length occurring in the support of $`B`$. Each target edge length is indecomposable as the sum of two positive support values, by the generic path certificate. A competitor path realizing such a length must be a single edge, since otherwise it splits at an interior reduced vertex. The distinct target edge lengths already consume the entire common total length. Thus the two reduced trees have the same order and every competitor pair has a different distance. The magnitudes of the negative weights give the same degree sequence: for each positive $`w`$ exactly $`L`$ times the number of hubs of defect $`w`$ have coefficient $`-w`$. The [path certificate](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md) now gives isometry. Its three types of excluded path equalities suffice for this statement.

## A marked comparison that retains almost all leaf labels

Let both trees have an interior mark $`o`$. Besides $`B`$, suppose they share $`U_o=\sum_v w_v\delta_{h_v}`$, with $`h_v=d(o,v)`$. Assume that the heights of the target reduced vertices are rationally independent. The extra marked point has height zero and is omitted from this list. Every target leaf has a distinct negative atom and hence at least one representing rival leaf at its own height. Write $`c=L_R-L_T\ge0`$. A rival height that contains both a hub and a leaf will be called mixed. Each mixed height uses at least one leaf in addition to the single representative at each target leaf height: at a negative unit atom a positive contribution forces at least two leaves, and elsewhere any leaf is extra. There are at most $`c`$ distinct mixed heights.

Tag target hub heights by zero and tag the heights of the target leaves by distinct rational basis vectors $`e_i`$. This defines a rational-linear map by independence. Extend it linearly to the span of all heights in both trees, then pass to the quotient by the span $`W`$ of the images of the mixed heights. Its dimension is at most $`c`$. Every rival hub now has tag zero. Indeed a pure positive atom occurs only at a target hub height, and all mixed heights were killed. A rival leaf is tagged zero at a mixed height. At a pure negative height it is the unique leaf corresponding to a target leaf. Thus the tag of any pair distance is the sum of its two endpoint tags: its lowest common ancestor is a hub or the mark, both tagged zero.

Inside $`W`$ span all vectors supported on at most four leaf coordinates. Choose a basis from these spanning vectors. Their combined supports give a set $`S`$ of at most $`4c`$ target labels; every at-most-four-sparse vector in $`W`$ is supported on $`S`$. For a label $`j\notin S`$, its height is a pure leaf height in both trees since $`e_j\notin W`$. If $`i,j\notin S`$ are distinct, the only pair with quotient tag $`e_i+e_j+W`$ in either tree has these precise two leaf labels. Otherwise subtract the two endpoint sums before quotienting: the result is a nonzero vector in $`W`$ supported on at most four labels and including at least one of $`i,j`$. This is impossible. Equality of the actual signed measures restricted to the inverse image of that tag yields the **real labelled distances**. The marked distances to these leaves agree too, by the original choice of representatives. Consequently the two trees contain isometric labelled metric subtrees spanned by all these leaves and the mark. Here we use [Buneman’s tree-metric theorem](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/the-recovery-of-trees-from-measures-of-dissimilarity/): a finite tree spanned by a labelled set of points, with bivalent points suppressed except at labels, is determined by their pairwise distances. Interior marks are allowed, for example by attaching a common extra unit pendant edge there before applying the leaf version.

There is an additional conclusion for each $`j\notin S`$. Every quotient tag of a pair incident to $`j`$ can arise only from a pair incident to $`j`$ on either side. The same four-coordinate subtraction proves this, including when the other endpoint has tag zero. Restriction of $`B`$ to the union of these tag fibers therefore gives exactly the signed distance profile seen from that leaf, by removing its common factor minus one and adjoining its atom at zero. In particular its pendant edge length and the defect of its parent agree in the two trees: the parent is its unique nearest other reduced vertex.

## Excluding a single extra leaf at every degree

In the marked comparison suppose also that the two trees have equal $`M`$ and that $`c=1`$. Then they cannot match, for any target topology. The degree identity below gives $`K_T-K_R=1`$. There is at most one mixed height. If its original tag is zero (including the case of no mixed height), the preceding projection has $`W=0`$. The target subtree spanned by all leaves is the whole target, so the rival contains it and $`K_R\ge K_T`$, a contradiction.

Otherwise the mixed height is distinct from every target hub height. All positive atoms of the target are now accounted for by pure positive rival vertices at their own heights. Each target hub defect is partitioned into the defects of those representatives. The single extra leaf forces the mixed height to contain exactly one trivalent hub $`q`$ of defect one and a canceling leaf; if it is a target leaf height, one further leaf represents that target negative atom. The extra trivalent hub contributes zero to $`K_R`$. The difference $`K_T-K_R`$ is therefore the sum over all split target atoms of the pairwise products of their positive parts. Its value one forces exactly one split: a target defect two at $`b`$ splits into two unit defects $`b_1,b_2`$. All other target hubs have a single representative of exactly the same defect.

The [one-quartic marked argument](MARKED-QUARTIC-RIGIDITY.md) applies to this one split even when other, unchanged hub defects are arbitrary. Here are the points where their weights enter. Write $`Z=X^v`$ for the nonzero mixed tag and $`A_1,A_2`$ for the signed child-subtree polynomials of $`q`$. In the difference of the squared endpoint profiles all unchanged diagonal weights cancel. The diagonal correction for the split and the neutral pair is still $`1-Z^2`$, and only $`q`$ has a nonzero ancestor tag. The factorization is unchanged and yields $`(A_1+Z)(A_2+Z)=0`$. Consequently $`-Z`$ is one of the child sums. Its hub coefficients are positive and its leaf monomials distinct, so that subtree is a single selected leaf and $`v=e_i`$ for its label $`i`$. The two incomparable trivalent representatives $`b_1,b_2`$ have at least four descendant leaves in total. Choose a selected one $`j\ne i`$, excluding the extra leaf. The actual distance $`h_j-h_b`$ has tag $`e_j`$. The ancestor cannot be $`q`$, since that would require three basis units in the endpoint sum. Rational independence then forces the other endpoint to be an ancestor hub at height $`h_b`$. On the rival this contributes exactly minus one. On the target it contributes zero or minus two. Every unchanged hub, whatever its defect, is at a different independent height and cannot supply that atom. The contradiction proves the assertion. In particular a full-dimensional competing cell admitting free-pendant trimming must have at least two more leaves than the target.

## Applying the marked comparison to unmarked subdivisions

If generic integer identification fails, the [finite matching theorem](PATH-HISTOGRAM-DECISION.md) gives a full-dimensional nonisomorphic matching cell. Choose positive real lengths in it with rationally independent target lengths. The competitor has $`k'\ge k`$ by the number of distinct visible target pairs. If $`k'=k`$, every competitor pair distance is distinct and equality of negative variation forces the same leaf count. The minimum-leaf argument rules it out. Thus $`k'>k`$, the common $`M`$ gives $`\Delta_R\lt \Delta_T`$, and the minimum-leaf theorem gives $`c=L_R-L_T\ge1`$. The degree sum and $`M\! =\!k+\Delta`$ give

```math
2L=M+2-2K,\qquad c=K_T-K_R\le K_T.
```

Whenever $`k>12\Delta`$, some rival pendant edge at a trivalent parent can be varied independently in the full matching nullspace. In detail, the rival has at least $`(k'+2)/2>6\Delta`$ leaves and the [free-edge argument](FIXED-EXCESS-REDUCTION.md) excludes at most $`6\Delta`$ leaves. Its zero-length comparison matches a target pendant at a trivalent parent with the very same length. Delete this pair of leaves and mark their suppressed attachments. The two trimmed trees now share $`B`$ and $`U_o`$. Their leaf difference remains $`c`$. The new target heights are rationally independent: with the mark retained as a bivalent vertex, the edges are precisely the surviving original edges, and a height minus its parent's height recovers each of their lengths. Apply the marked comparison and restore the two equal removed edges at the corresponding labelled marks. We have proved that the original rival contains an isometric copy of the target subtree spanned by **all but at most $`4c`$ of its leaves**, with the appropriate rival leaves as labels.

If every component at every target high-degree vertex contains more than $`4K`$ leaves, no such vertex loses any incident direction in this common subtree. Each of them maps to a distinct rival vertex of at least its own degree. It follows that $`\Delta_R\ge\Delta_T`$, since $`\Delta`$ sums $`w_v^2-1`$ over high-degree vertices only. This is the required contradiction.

The branch-size hypothesis implies $`k>12\Delta`$ when $`\Delta>0`$. Write $`E=\sum_{w_v\ge2}(w_v-1)\le K`$. Then $`\Delta=2K+E\le3K`$ and $`k=2L-2-E`$. If there is just one high-degree vertex of defect two, $`K=1`$ and each of its four directions has at least five leaves, so $`k\ge37>36`$. For one higher vertex there are at least five disjoint such components. For at least two high-degree vertices, take their minimal connecting subtree. The sum of its degrees over the high-degree vertices is at most twice their number minus two, by the degree sum after suppressing bivalent vertices outside that set. Hence at least $`2+\sum_{w_v\ge2} w_v\ge6`$ incident components contain no other high-degree vertex. Those components are pairwise disjoint. In all these remaining cases $`L\ge5(4K+1)`$ and $`k\ge39K+8>12\Delta`$. For trivalent targets use the existing histogram-only theorem. As every full-dimensional ambiguity was excluded, the rational-cone theorem gives the stated exceptional bound. The [arclength identity](METRIC-TREE-DISTRIBUTION.md) gives the metric assertion.

The common-subtree statement supplies a more general test when $`k>12\Delta`$. For each integer $`1\le c\le K_T`$, require that deleting any at most $`4c`$ leaves and taking their complementary spanning subtree (suppressing degree-two vertices) lowers $`K`$ by strictly less than $`c`$. By the single-extra-leaf argument the test need only examine $`c\ge2`$. Then that subtree would have $`K>K_T-c=K_R`$, which cannot occur in a rival containing it. The ample-branch criterion is one sufficient condition. This advances the unrestricted identification problem for distance histograms but does not settle arbitrary sparse branching, all individual length assignments, or Stanley's tree conjecture.
