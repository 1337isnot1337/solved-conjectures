# Independent mathematical review of the linear leaf-surplus reduction

A Codex mathematical reviewer in the WadeDavis session independently reviewed the [complete proof](LINEAR-LEAF-SURPLUS.md) before its final explanatory sentence on the shared protected leaf, including its dependencies on the earlier marked comparison, signed deletion and finite-cell theorems. The unmarked-to-marked transfer, linear bounds, and finite decidability conclusion follow with the stated hypotheses.

At a target atom the sign is unique; matching all its units leaves equal unmatched positive and negative counts at that same real distance, including atoms absent entirely from the target. The total for each sign is the difference between negative leaf–hub units. While summing over all rival leaf origins, an unmatched leaf–leaf pair can contribute twice, and a bad hub at an origin consumes a separate negative unit at least once. Division by the number of origins gives strictly fewer than six times the surplus bad vertices. Distinct nonzero good-leaf pair labels use distinct target leaf pairs, which have unit weight. Multiple compatible matches of a good hub cause no problem since all its chosen formal labels represent its one actual quotient height.

The target pendants begin independent modulo internal edges. Removing the bad heights costs at most their number of dimensions. The group generated over the integers by those surviving pendant images lies in a rational vector space and is finitely generated and torsion-free; its own quotient by twice itself has its genuine rank. Every vertex height was represented in this very group. Computing a path parity by removing twice the lowest common ancestor’s height therefore needs no assumption about divisibility in the ambient real length group. The common signed pair measure fixes the diagonal adjustment by its total mass, so the squared Fourier polynomials agree on the restricted cube. A basis drawn from the kernel words of weight at most eight covers the supports of all such words. Two monomials of degree at most four in one character coset have that bounded-weight difference, making substitution on the eliminated coordinates valid on the entire remaining cube.

The singleton identity fixes degree minus hub weight at one. Triple parity implies a complete bipartite induced graph, including the empty graph, by testing adjacency from any chosen vertex; two vertices in each part contradict the four-coordinate equation. Outside the center, reserved leaf edges are distinct, and all residual hub weights fit the unreserved edge budget. The manuscript’s lower bound on the number of unique leaf labels and its sufficient large-leaf threshold follow.

Two actual paths of the same real length have the same parity class even if their net coefficient vanishes. Their supports differ on at most eight coordinates, so the membership of each surviving unique leaf is fixed by that length. Restricting the original signed measure and recovering the negative mass at distance zero gives identical genuine leaf profiles. In a positive reduced tree with a hub, the parent is the sole nearest reduced vertex of a leaf and has strictly positive defect. Every other path passes through it. Translation by the common pendant length and restoration of the leaf contribution indeed marks the two parents on the unchanged trees.

The parent mark gives independent nonzero target heights by the invertible triangular relation to edges. The previous marked comparison and deletion identities apply at every surplus. At most one target leaf has the retained mark inside its open pendant. If more than four times the surplus plus one leaves remain, an unprotected nonexceptional leaf can be deleted. Heights of surviving vertices persist, including when a bivalent mark must be kept. When the mark is forgotten, replacing its two incident edges by their sum preserves independence of the reduced target edges. Iteration gives a genuine smaller unmarked ambiguity with the same surplus. Rational full-dimensional comparison cells imply positive-density integer witnesses, while a rationally independent target forces full dimension. The finite decision bound does not assert that every such test has positive outcome.

The final text explains that the matched leaf’s root distance and pendant length determine whether its mark lies in the open pendant. A second internal review checked this shared protection criterion and the unchanged-tree construction.

The [finite checker](verify_parity_root_transfer.py) tested all 33,867 simple graphs through six vertices, with 25 empty or spanning-star cases. It checked 61,671 colliding monomial comparisons across 75 binary quotient codes and recovered 17,774 full leaf profiles across 110 reduced trees and 1,370 choices of a leaf origin. These checks exercise the derivation. The all-order proof is analytic.

## Attribution, prior work and scope

This is an original supplement to the repository’s [Stanley tree investigation](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/315). Two Codex researchers in the WadeDavis session developed the sign allocation and rooted profile transfer, and the signed character identity and sparse parity argument, respectively. Both checked the combined derivation. The separate mathematical referee reviewed the complete statement, including its descent to smaller trees. The original question asks whether the chromatic symmetric function distinguishes every finite tree, posed by Richard P. Stanley. [Stanley, *A symmetric function generalization of the chromatic polynomial of a graph*](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1995.1020)

Gutman’s endpoint identity, Buneman’s labelled tree-metric theorem and the repository’s [earlier finite matching-cell argument](PATH-HISTOGRAM-DECISION.md) supply the signed measure, the interpretation of recovered labelled distances and the finite decision procedure. The [earlier surplus reduction](LEAF-SURPLUS-KERNEL.md) bounded the comparison order quadratically in the number of extra leaves, and obtained common profiles under additional free-edge hypotheses. Here the unmarked input itself matches a parent on the original trees for every surplus below the stated linear threshold. The resulting finite test permits arbitrary degrees in both trees.

The bounded source check on 2026-11-27 covered generic tree reconstruction from path-length multisets, homometric trees, unlabelled distance distributions, marked distance profiles and tree reconstruction with extra leaves. Bartha, Burcsi and Lipták study weighted path and subtree decks, including collisions and reconstruction in specified classes. Evans and Lanoue use the joint distribution of successively spanned subtree lengths under leaf sampling. Mémoli and Needham develop global and local distance-distribution invariants, including a local injectivity result for metric graphs. The reviewed statements do not supply this surplus-uniform transfer from one global distance distribution or the resulting linear order bound.
