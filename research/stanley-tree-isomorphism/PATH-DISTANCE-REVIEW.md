# Review of the path-distance certificate

On 27 November 2026 UTC the WadeDavis research session obtained a sufficient certificate from the degree sequence and ordinary path counts, a uniform probability bound for independent subdivisions, and the path-count-only corollary for reduced trivalent trees. A separate Codex reviewer read the complete proof, rebuilt the finite checks independently and examined the closest primary sources. These are internally reviewed written partial results with no Lean formalization. Stanley's question for unrestricted trees remains open. [Theorem and proof](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md) · [Original tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/315)

## Independent mathematical review

Shortening both ends of an unordered three-edge path leaves a unique edge, so the endpoint identity starts at distance one without a factor of two. An observed support of size equal to the full count of reduced vertex pairs rules out unobserved pairs, cancellations and repeated distances in every competitor sharing the degree data. A distance which cannot split as a sum of two observed positive distances must be a true edge. Distinctness of the true lengths and completeness of the candidate line graph follow from the other two cardinality equalities. The edge count used there includes pairs at every reduced nonleaf vertex and omits all suppressed vertices. The clique inversion works for a single reduced edge too.

The three exceptional equations have nonzero integer coefficient vectors. Disconnected support rules out a path vector for two nonadjacent edges and total coefficient sum rules out a single edge as the sum of two nonempty path vectors. Conditioning on any nonzero coordinate therefore gives the stated uniform estimate for integer lengths. For histogram-only inputs the known sum of squared degree defects bounds the competitor's reduced vertex count from above; the support count gives the opposite inequality on a certified trivalent target. This forces trivalence in its competitors, including the path case. The reviewer confirmed the distinction between a density over the edge lengths of a specified topology and a density over unlabelled trees of fixed order.

## Comparison with earlier reconstruction results

Martin, Morin and Wagner already recover path counts and degree counts from the chromatic symmetric function. Gutman's Theorems 3 and 4 express the degree-sum and degree-product distance polynomials of a tree through its Hosoya polynomial. Their positive-distance coefficients yield the signed second difference used here. These known identities are dependencies. The line graph of a tree also determines it, by the elementary clique argument in the proof. [Martin, Morin and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339) · [Gutman](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/Article.aspx?id=0561-73320530001G)

Bartha, Burcsi and Lipták study unlabelled multisets of weighted path lengths of a tree. Their Proposition 11 gives infinite families of nonisomorphic unweighted trees with identical path histograms. Proposition 15 recovers the weighting of a specified star, and Theorem 20 enumerates weightings compatible with a specified topology. The observable certificate additionally identifies an unpromised reduced topology from the much longer subdivided tree's ordinary, unweighted histogram and degree data, or from the histogram alone for the trivalent conclusion. The probability estimate supplies a uniform density-one family across all reduced topologies of the indicated sizes. We make no separate priority claim for the existence of isolated reconstructible edge weightings. [Bartha, Burcsi and Lipták, §§4 and 6](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2016.10)

Evans and Lanoue's Theorem 1.5 uses the joint distribution of lengths of successively spanned subtrees of a random ordering of leaves; Remark 1.8 specifies the prior general-position promise. Mémoli and Needham's metric-tree reconstruction uses local shape measures, retaining profiles of balls at points of a metric-measure space. Gutkin and Smilansky recover connectivity and incommensurate bond lengths from a quantum graph's Schrödinger spectrum. These inputs differ from the global integer unweighted pair-distance counts of a subdivided tree. Searching by weighted path decks, Large Unjumble, homometric trees, unlabelled tree metrics, distance distributions, Hosoya or Wiener polynomials and arbitrary subdivisions produced no prior statement of the three observable tests and the quantitative all-competitor result. This was a bounded primary-source review. [Evans and Lanoue](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01091) · [Mémoli and Needham](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09646) · [Gutkin and Smilansky](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0105020)

The earlier Stanley packages on the two repository branches use end attachments, rooted forest factors, boundary layers or cut partitions. They do not use this global path-distance certificate. This extends the existing unranked partial result without changing the status of Stanley's general question.

## Reproducible checks

Run `python3 verify_path_distance_certificate.py` from this directory. The [checker](verify_path_distance_certificate.py) enumerates the 2,287 nontrivial unlabelled trees through order 13. Fourteen pass each certificate, and 149 ordinary distance histograms have multiple nonisomorphic realizations in the census. The signed difference agrees with direct endpoint weighting in every case. On 816 literal subdivisions, 28 pass the reconstruction test. A further exact check recovers all 108 nontrivial reduced topologies through 13 vertices after assigning ternary lengths, including eight path or trivalent cores. These numerical observations do not enter the proof.

The independent review used a separate canonical-form implementation and checked against every other tree of each size in the same census, finding the same 14 certified cases and no false positives. It also tested 36 further independently generated subdivisions on four topologies and identified 23 certified inputs.
