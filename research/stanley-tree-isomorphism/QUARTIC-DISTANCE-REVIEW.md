# Review of reconstruction with one four-valent vertex

The WadeDavis research session derived the marked signed-profile comparison and the three-row collision argument on 27 November 2026 UTC. Together they establish generic identification from the bare distance histogram throughout the first positive degree-excess class. The proof concerns independent subdivisions of a fixed topology, has no Lean formalization and leaves [Stanley’s general question](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/315) open. [Theorem](QUARTIC-DISTANCE-RIGIDITY.md) · [Marked lemma](MARKED-QUARTIC-RIGIDITY.md)

## Sources and scope

The underlying CSF problem belongs to Stanley. Martin, Morin and Wagner proved that the chromatic symmetric function of a tree determines its path counts, and distinguished all spiders using the CSF. Gutman’s degree-weighted distance polynomial identities give the signed path difference used in the earlier unmarked certificate. These results supply the observation and its link to the CSF. [Stanley](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1995.1020) · [Martin, Morin and Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339) · [Gutman](https://doiserbia.nb.rs/Article.aspx?id=0561-73320530001G)

Bartha, Burcsi and Lipták constructed nonisomorphic trees with identical unweighted path histograms and studied reconstruction for a specified weighted topology, including stars. Evans and Lanoue use joint subtree-length distributions obtained from a random ordering of leaves. Mémoli and Needham study global and local distance distributions, including reconstruction results from local data on metric trees. The quartet step here uses the classical four-point property of a labelled tree metric. The new argument obtains the required labelled quartet distances from two unlabelled signed measures and then rules out every competing topology using the single unmarked histogram. [Bartha, Burcsi and Lipták](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2016.10) · [Evans and Lanoue](https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.01091) · [Mémoli and Needham](https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.09646) · [Rhodes, quartet metrics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7650847/)

A bounded search checked weighted path and distance decks, Wiener and Hosoya polynomials, unlabelled tree metrics, arclength distance distributions, degree-four and trivalent cores, metric-tree reconstruction and formal theorem-library matches. No earlier result located in that search gave the stated generic conclusion against unrestricted competitors. The [previous path certificate](PATH-DISTANCE-CERTIFICATE.md) used degree counts for nontrivalent cores. The [finite-excess reduction](FIXED-EXCESS-REDUCTION.md) gave decidability, and the [research four-star calculation](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/9462f12fa72abaf25a878cb1869be9a0832a9686/research/stanley-tree-isomorphism/FOUR-ARM-FLATS.md) treated the core with no additional trivalent vertices. Both permanent branches and the existing Stanley issue and publication PRs were checked against the new full excess-three statement. This supplements the existing unranked entry. ## Independent mathematical review

A separate Codex reviewer checked both proofs. The following is the reviewer’s receipt.

I checked the analytic general theorem in QUARTIC-DISTANCE-RIGIDITY.md. The reviewed final mathematical text has SHA-256 7bd05a99b3bdbc6ba5328eadb948fd4d7a733844a393ff3cd86331ab5cf4b5ac. My independent review of the underlying marked theorem covers the source with SHA-256 91a9c02f7f01eecc57c09f9af4338ee673d478344a48d956de00751208136383. I did not derive either theorem.

The comparison permits arbitrary positive competitor lengths, a mark anywhere strictly inside an edge and arbitrarily many coincidences of competitor heights. Target independence gives distinct heights. Each height of the prescribed sign has sufficient representatives. After selecting them, the two leftover vertices give total profile zero, hence are oppositely signed at one height. The two representatives of the quartic height are incomparable. A hub cannot have the extra leaf as a descendant because they have equal heights.

I checked the Laurent-polynomial calculation independently. Finite sums use only a finitely generated subgroup of the target rational tagging space, which is free abelian. The difference of the uncorrected weighted pair polynomials is one minus the square of the residual-height monomial. The only possible ancestor correction is at the extra hub. Counting cross-child pairs and pairs containing the hub itself gives the displayed product identity. When that monomial has nonzero exponent, a child sum equal to its negative must have zero constant coefficient and exactly one negative leaf monomial. This forces a single selected leaf with that tag. These deductions still apply when the extra height equals a target height.

At a selected descendant leaf chosen under either quartic representative and distinct from the exceptional leaf label, the specified real distance has a single basis-vector tag. An extra-hub lowest ancestor would require endpoint tags with three units of total degree. A zero-tag ancestor therefore forces the endpoints to be that leaf and a selected hub. Equality of real distances forces the selected hub to have the quartic height and to be the ancestor, by independence and strict edge positivity. On the competitor there is exactly one such vertex on the chosen path, giving signed weight minus one. The target contribution is zero or minus two. The alternative zero residual tag separates every pair of distinct selected leaves, even in the presence of an unmatched competitor leaf. Equality of the entire real measures on each tag's inverse image recovers each labelled distance. Selecting one target leaf in each component at the four-valent vertex gives an unresolved quartet. The quartet induced by four distinct leaves in a positive-length trivalent tree is resolved, so its three four-point sums cannot all agree.

For a target with L leaves, the only problematic competitor has L+1 leaves and L−1 trivalent hubs. Each of the L distinct target quartic-to-leaf distances has signed coefficient −2. At least two chosen differences between negative competitor paths at these distances are independent. If every difference were proportional with nonzero pendant restriction, the same fixed leaf would occur at all L distances but could supply only L−1 leaf-to-hub paths. With zero pendant restriction, the two endpoints at hubs are fixed by the common support; the two outward branches at each of them exclude at least four distinct leaves by strict positivity. Each remaining leaf supplies at most one of the required distances. Both cases contradict the count.

I checked the cancellation count separately as excess of the total number of negative competitor paths over the negative variation of the target signed measure. It is exactly 2L−1, and the matched positive paths can be taken distinct. Orienting a mixed row to have total pendant coefficient one correctly gives either a positive single-leaf unit vector or a positive leaf-pair vector minus one leaf vector. These rows are independent of the two selected negative differences, and hence together exhaust the at-most-three-dimensional matching row space.

Identifying the leaf coordinates appearing in the two negative differences kills precisely the differences needed in the quotient calculation, regardless of whether either projects to zero. A projected mixed vector other than a singleton forces every positive path to be a leaf pair supported on the same two groups. Two identifications permit at most four distinct such paths. The common vector must therefore be a singleton, and the whole matching row space vanishes off at most five pendant coordinates. An unconstrained pendant permits deletion and application of the separately reviewed marked theorem.

If five rival leaves are all constrained, the quotient must have two pairs and a singleton. The two projected differences and the mixed vector are then independent on pendant coordinates. Each canceled positive leaf pair includes the singleton. Four such pairs and three hub pairs exhaust the seven required positive paths, forcing the hub-pair distances to be precisely the singleton-to-hub distance multiset, on the full matching space. The singleton cannot attach to an end of the three-hub path. At the middle, comparison of minima and sums forces the longer internal edge to be twice the shorter one. This internal-edge-only homogeneous linear relation holds on a relatively open neighborhood, so it belongs to the matching row space and contradicts injectivity on pendant coordinates. The proof covers the four-star without an enumerative assumption.

I independently replayed the author's checker: 720 equal-profile rooted pairs, including 180 labelled-quartet cases, 122 single-ancestor cases and 418 tagged-pair discrepancies. My independent projection exercise enumerated all 1,073 labelled group partitions reachable by at most two identifications on five through nine rival leaves. These checks supplement the analytic proof.

The finite union of proper rational matching hyperplanes gives the announced codimension-one exceptional count for labelled integer subdivisions and a null exceptional set for real lengths. The global arclength distance law determines the finite signed endpoint distribution and total length by the earlier metric identity. Its application here is valid against arbitrary finite metric-tree competitors with positive edge lengths. The proof resolves generic identifiability for the entire first positive excess class. Neither every integer subdivision nor the unrestricted Stanley conjecture is claimed. Both the mathematical argument and this scope pass independent internal review.

## Reproducible checks

The [marked-profile verifier](verify_marked_quartic.py) builds 720 pairs of rooted trees having exactly the same signed root profile, including coinciding hidden and visible heights. Direct pair distances distinguish all 720. The three tests respectively reach a projected discrepancy in 418 cases, the single-ancestor discrepancy in 122 cases and the labelled-quartet argument in 180 cases. Run `python3 verify_marked_quartic.py` in this directory.

The [pendant-projection verifier](verify_quartic_rows.py) checks 36,134 possible projected mixed vectors over 56 quotient types through eighteen leaves. For three hubs it tests 24,000 length and attachment choices and finds exactly twenty equal multisets, all with the central attachment and forced two-to-one internal lengths. Run `python3 verify_quartic_rows.py` in this directory. The finite routines are regression checks. The proofs apply to every stated size and real positive competitor without an exhaustive search over lengths.
