# Independent mathematical and source review of the single bare vertex argument

Assume one core vertex has weight one and every other core weight is divisible by a common integer d at least three. The leading leaf-component partition or full leaf-adjacency sequence supplies the promised weights for a competing ordinary tree. I checked the ordinary, signless forest formulation of the argument. In a forest without isolated ordinary vertices all original pendant edges remain. Its unique component with mass one modulo d marks the root. This component contains another core vertex, since isolated components are excluded. No hypotheses on branching, repeated weights, or diameter enter.

A forest with exactly one singleton and all its other component masses divisible by d either retains all pendant edges and isolates the bare vertex, or cuts a single pendant from the root component of a uniquely associated singleton-free forest. The parent in the latter case is obtained by restoring that specific pendant edge. The damaged rooted block has size at least d and still contributes an ordinary component. Cutting a nonroot pendant in a fixed singleton-free parent instead leaves residues one and minus one; these differ for d at least three and all damaged parts are at least two. Its partition determines the original partition by incrementing the unique minus-one part. Repeated parts with the same original mass automatically contribute their combined multiplicity. In each singleton-free parent there are n minus the number of core vertices many cuttable original pendant edges. Subtracting the nonroot-cut counts yields the root-cut multiplicity, partition by partition. Subtracting those counts from the all-zero residue singleton sector then recovers every missing root-isolated record. Together the two sectors determine the rooted weighted core forest polynomial.

The rooted product factorization follows by deciding whether each child edge is retained. Every child factor is monic linear in the variable for its total branch mass, which appears solely for the unrooted fully connected branch. The other term is independent of that variable, proving irreducibility in the full polynomial ring. The root-variable-free part of the factor equals its unrooted summand; extracting positive root degree gives its rooted summand. This argument works for positive integer vertex weights by recursion, including one-vertex branches. The rooted unweighted identifiability procedure is prior; the recovery of the full rooted weighted forest polynomial from unrooted ordinary input is the new step.

The same one-dot identification by residues breaks for d=2, since the original root and the damaged nonroot can both have odd mass. The stronger hypothesis that a subset sum of nonroot weights determines its summand count permits a separate direct recovery in that case: a rooted component of mass h then has h−1−ℓ(h−1) pendant edges, where ℓ gives the subset cardinality. This observation does not establish the general d=2 case.

A bounded search of primary sources using weighted core, deep vertex, first derivative, modular weights and rooted U-polynomial found adjacent prior work, without a match for this congruence theorem. Gonzalez–Orellana–Tomba read certain deep-vertex adjacencies and prove all diameter below six. Zeng and the first-derivative criteria concern proper trees; their weights can repeat under specified nonleaf restrictions. Orellana–Tomba's November reduction attaches the same number of leaves everywhere, a different operation. Aliste-Prieto–de Mier–Zamora obtain rooted identifiability when rooted input is already supplied. These delimit the priority assessment, which is bounded.
