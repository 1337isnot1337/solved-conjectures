# Review of generic reconstruction from unlabelled leaf distances

The original target here is the leaf-distance multiset of a nontrivial positive reduced metric tree with generic lengths. It forgets which leaves realize each distance. The conclusions determine total length and characterize all competing weighted trees. In a binary topology outside the explicit exceptional hyperplanes, the unweighted shape is determined and the number of possible metrics can reach the factorial of the number of cherries. The argument addresses a distinct source of ambiguity in the existing Stanley tree-reconstruction campaign. The general chromatic symmetric function question stays open. **Finite replay.** The standard-library check uses one explicitly separated length vector on each of the twelve reduced shapes with four, five, or six leaves. It tests strict triangles and the additive four-point condition against every reassignment of the observed distances to labelled pairs. Failing prefixes discard all their extensions, accounting for 9,153,731,463,840 assignments across the twelve cases. Exactly 18,936 assignments passed. In every case they were exactly the labelled versions of the possible cherry shifts on the binary refinements. The minimum length of a tour of the leaves also equalled twice the original tree length in every realization. A separate quartet has two nonisometric positive metrics with the same multiset. These finite examples support the written proof. The results have no Lean formalization or external peer review. [Reproducible check](verify_leaf_deck_length.py)

**Primary sources and bounded priority search.** Pauplin's formula computes tree length from the distances and externally specified pair weights. The new argument identifies these weights through unlabelled observations. Evans and Lanoue give nongeneric unweighted examples with equal leaf-distance distributions, and use the richer joint distribution of random subtree lengths in their generic reconstruction theorem. Huber and Steel work with distances for specified leaf pairs. Searches for pair-distance multisets, homometric metric trees, phylogenetic tree metrics, generic reconstruction, tree-cone symmetries and cherry permutations found no matching general classification in the sources examined. These checks establish the scope of the comparison and cannot certify absolute priority. **Application to every positive leaf surplus.** In the ordinary vertex-distance histogram problem the rational functional counting target pendant coefficients must be nonzero on the intersection of the target length span with the rival internal-edge span whenever a rival has more leaves. A separate mathematical reviewer checked the signed Laurent identity after denominators were cleared. Inversion symmetry would center its unique possible negative coefficient at zero and force the derivative to vanish, contrary to the common total length. Restricting the original signed measure to the surviving tag for two target pendants gives an actual equal leaf-distance multiset. Trimming every extra leaf then contradicts the equal-total-length theorem. This necessary condition applies to arbitrary surplus and degrees, including the four-leaf boundary. Competitors satisfying the rational condition remain open in general.
