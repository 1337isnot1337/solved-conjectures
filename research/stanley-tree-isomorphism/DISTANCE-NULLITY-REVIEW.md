# Review and sources for the arithmetic bounds on tree distance distributions

Two Codex mathematical contributors in the WadeDavis session developed this supplement to the existing Stanley campaign. The first derived the marked height-relation count, protected-pendant re-marking, cancellation-rank inequality and exceptional-set count. The second independently audited those arguments, supplied the two-relation uniqueness proof and identified the four-pendant matching bound for the finite unmarked decision. The first checked those deductions. A third internal reviewer checked the resulting bridge between the unmarked and marked proofs. This is a written result with exact regression checks and no Lean formalization or external referee report.

## Mathematical review

The independent internal review of the [marked theorem](HEIGHT-RELATION-KERNEL.md) follows.

I reviewed the manuscript with SHA-256 `8997a07d1792036a2fdab617b39b39bf6c8d1fed3ed56108811c12c691519511` and its signed-profile deletion and rational-cell dependencies. The claim is a finite decision for each prescribed upper bound on the rational nullity of all distinct positive height values in a **marked** comparison. It allows arbitrary topology, degree and positive leaf surplus. The full unmarked tree problem remains open.

The independent target heights and the marked profile ensure that every positive target height is present on the rival. At a rival-only level the weights sum to zero, so that level uses at least one leaf beyond the leaves reserved for all true leaf levels. Specializing one positive height to one sends a pair to two exactly when both endpoints lie at that height. They have a strictly lower common ancestor. The resulting signed mass is negative at every rival-only level and at every repeated true leaf level. Splitting by actual real distance therefore produces at least one equal-distance row comparing a pair of just this form against a pair of a different specialized value. It has at most five labels and includes the chosen label. This step works for distances with total signed weight zero on both trees.

A basis of the selected rows must cover the coordinate of every special level. If its dimension is $`p`$, its combined support can meet at most $`5p-s`$ true leaf labels after reserving the $`s`$ rival-only levels. Each remaining true leaf label is represented by a singleton leaf in both trees. In any other equal-path row an uncovered true leaf can appear only at one of the four endpoints. Extending the basis therefore costs at most four new true leaf labels per row. Its total rank is at most $`\delta`$ under actual evaluation, giving at most $`5\delta-s`$ exceptional target leaves. For every other leaf, equality of actual distances preserves whether a pair meets it. Restricting the signed pair measure to that incidence recovers the whole leaf profile even in the presence of signed cancellations.

I independently derived both deletion formulas. The nearest nonzero term of the signed leaf profile is its positive parent defect at its pendant length. A bivalent mark on the pendant has zero weight. Once the common pendant is deleted, the parent defect falls by one. If the mark stays in place the only marked changes are the loss of the negative leaf atom and the unit decrease at the parent. If the old mark was strictly inside the pendant, every surviving vertex is on the parent side. Mark the parent on both trees. All surviving nonzero heights then shift by the same negative old parent height, and the new rooted profile is the common old-parent profile with the removed leaf omitted and the parent weight reduced. The formal differences from the old parent coordinate are independent before evaluation. Consequently the nullity cannot grow under the translation, while all rival-only levels survive. These statements also cover a parent becoming bivalent. They justify the bound on the number of leaves in the final comparison without a protected-leaf exception.

The total pair mass determines the square-defect sum, so at $`m`$ target leaves the surplus is at most $`\binom{m-2}{2}`$. This excludes positive surplus below four leaves and supplies the finite upper bound on rival order after descent. For each pair of bounded topologies and marks, weak orderings of finitely many path and height forms yield rational cells. Feasible cells are relatively open in the space defined by their equalities. Avoiding the countable union of rational rank-drop hyperplanes attains the maximal ranks for the target and all union heights simultaneously. Feasibility and both ranks are decidable using rational arithmetic. The test asserts existence and does not presume a practical bound on runtime or an affirmative answer at every nullity.

As a separate regression I generated all 25 unrooted reduced topologies with four through seven leaves and tested all vertex-hub and interior-edge midpoint marks with two length assignments. Direct recomputation of both signed measures checked 3,508 single-leaf deletions, including 312 with the old mark strictly inside the removed pendant, 312 with the mark at the parent, 1,950 whose parent became bivalent and 3,051 involving a repeated height. No discrepancy was found. These checks illustrate the geometric identities. The written argument proves the unrestricted bounds. This is independent internal review and there is no Lean formalization in this package.

The reviewer separately checked the [unmarked cancellation and codimension arguments](DISTANCE-CANCELLATION-CODIMENSION.md), then collaborated on the final two-relation and finite-decision consequences.

I checked the signed cancellation and codimension arguments and the cited ordinary-distance matching results. The conclusion about the difference in leaf counts applies to every pair of positive metric trees with the same global distance data. The uniqueness theorem and the finite search across all topologies assume rationally independent target edge lengths. This supplement has no Lean formalization.

**Cancellation rank.** At each actual distance, cancel opposite signed units until one sign remains. The negative sum over the unmatched units is the negative variation of the signed measure. Root heights of individual vertices are an invertible integral change of variables from the edge lengths. After setting hub coordinates to zero, a mixed cancellation row is either a zero constraint on one leaf or the equality of one leaf variable with the sum of two other distinct ones. Projection cannot increase rank. In each connected incidence component of the chosen row basis the number of labels is at most twice the number of rows plus one. When equality holds, the incidence graph is a tree and every row has three labels. Giving one label the value one can then be extended away from the starting label. This works whichever side of an equation contains the prescribed variable. All other components can use the zero solution. Consequently a binary solution retains all but at most twice the projected row rank in leaf labels. A rank-one system needs at most one zero.

Every cancellation at a selected negative leaf consumes one positive unit from a pair of leaves of opposite binary labels. No such positive pair supplies more than one unit. Writing $`h`$ for the number of selected leaves, this gives the lower bound $`h(h-2)`$ on the negative variation. An arbitrary target with $`m`$ leaves contributes at most $`m(m-2)`$ unbalanced negative units. These facts give the universal leaf-deficit inequality even if the target itself has mixed distances. For the counting result there are only finitely many signed matchings on a fixed topology. Each sufficiently large loss of negative variation forces the requisite rank in the original equations, and a rational subspace of codimension $`k`$ has at most $`t^{e-k}`$ integer points in the coordinate box of side $`t`$. This uses no genericity of an integer edge assignment.

**Connecting the two reductions.** In a marked comparison the positive heights in the union are precisely the distinct positive heights on the rival. Its segmented edge nullity equals the union-height nullity plus the sum of the multiplicities beyond one at these heights. This sum is at least the whole leaf surplus: reserve one leaf for each target leaf height, and one hub at each target hub or rival-only level. All additional leaves are among the remaining representatives. The target's positive heights are independent. Deleting a common free pendant and retaining its parent as a mark even when bivalent leaves a subset of the original rival segment lengths; further suppressions and finally forgetting the mark take disjoint nonempty sums. The map from the new formal coordinate space into the old is injective, so edge nullity never grows.

The sign-matching and telescoping equations in the published one-extra-leaf proof apply at every surplus. Each row uses at most four rival pendants, and each mixed cancellation row uses at most three. These equations are actual rational relations on the rival edges. A rival pendant outside a basis support can vary alone with every other rival edge held fixed. The published common-pendant argument then gives a marked comparison of unchanged surplus. This shows that every bounded-nullity unmarked ambiguity has a witness on at most four times the original nullity in rival leaves; the bounded marked witness has at most four times the nullity minus four times the surplus in target leaves. The finite matching-cell rank test can attain target independence and the required edge rank on the same point by avoiding countably many proper rational hyperplanes. This is an existence test; it does not report the outcome at every nullity.

For completeness the positive result at rival nullity at most two follows without enumerating trees. A nonisometric rival of an independent target would need at least two extra leaves by the published one-extra-leaf theorem. The projected cancellation rank would then be two, since rank one admits a binary solution with at most one omitted leaf. Two mixed cancellation rows span the whole rival numerical relation space and use at most six leaf coordinates altogether. The defect-square moment forces at least five target leaves for a surplus of at least two, hence at least seven rival leaves. Some rival pendant is therefore free. Transfer to a marked comparison produces a rival segment nullity of at most two. Its repeated positive height multiplicities already account for both extra leaves, leaving no positive union-height nullity. The marked theorem rules this out. More generally a marked ambiguity must have surplus at least three by the separate existing one- and two-surplus marked theorems, and union-height nullity at least two by the present marked theorem. It therefore needs at least five units of segmented edge nullity.

I separately enumerated 63,383 binary row systems, including 125 with dependent rows and 7,942 with a positive predicted number of retained labels. Exact integer row elimination and enumeration of binary assignments confirmed the rank bound in every case. A full binary tree of ternary equations illustrates why an arbitrary solution need not retain one label for every row: its ones form one root-to-leaf path. The finite replay tests the combinatorics; the proof treats arbitrarily many labels and all actual cancellation patterns.

The third internal reviewer read the unmarked manuscript at SHA-256 `d10b58efa28d66b4e6cdd64df42a15be78bd990e560786f0a4cbfed591e24c83`. The two negative and one positive representatives in a path equation use at most four pendants. ## Finite checks

The [height and deletion checker](verify_height_relation_kernel.py) directly evaluates 2,990 doubled-height fibres and 3,152 signed deletions on 482 marked metrics, including 316 changes of mark to the surviving parent. It also tests 2,000 abstract row systems and 280 rational height translations. The [unmarked checker](verify_distance_cancellation.py) computes the signed measure from vertex pairs on 2,023 metrics, including 1,948 with mixed signs. It tests 4,046 unit-pairing and projected-rank comparisons by enumerating their binary assignments. The finite systems exercise the proofs; they do not claim to exhibit a positive-surplus generic ambiguity.

## Scope, sources and priority

The input here is the distance between two independent samples from normalized arclength on a finite metric tree, or the corresponding ordinary histogram for a subdivision. The arithmetic parameter is the number of independent rational relations among the rival edge lengths. The marked result uses the two-point law together with one signed profile, measured from one specified point. The original Stanley question asks whether the chromatic symmetric function determines every finite unweighted tree. The present bounds sit in the existing unranked campaign; that question and the unbounded generic unmarked case remain open. [Stanley, original paper](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1995.1020) · [Existing campaign](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/315)

The bounded source and identity check on 27 November 2026 covered rational dependence and codimension in tree-distance reconstruction, sparse equal-length paths, marked profiles, global metric-tree distance distributions, homometric weighted trees, and leaf-count discrepancy. Mémoli–Needham examine distance distributions with separate global and local invariants; their generic metric-tree result uses a distribution of local profiles. Bartha–Burcsi–Lipták study weighted path and subtree decks. Evans–Lanoue use the joint law of successively spanned subtree lengths. Those results do not give the twice-nullity bound on leaf discrepancy from one global law, its integer exceptional-set exponent, or the marked rational-height reduction. [Mémoli–Needham](https://doi.org/10.1111/sapm.12526) · [Bartha–Burcsi–Lipták](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.CPM.2016.10) · [Evans–Lanoue](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aam.2018.01.001)
