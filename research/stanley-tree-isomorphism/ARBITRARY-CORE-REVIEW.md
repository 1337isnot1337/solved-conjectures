## Mathematical checks

- The two disjoint small-block saturations and normalized rooted factorization in the earlier proof already apply on a tree of unspecified skeleton. A vertex outside the contracted blocks keeps its degree, and all old leaves were covered. The number of hubs and the cap multiset transfer before imposing any restriction on the competing topology. Every terminal hub in the reduced internal tree supports at least two arms.
- The two-smallest-arm sum bounds the size on either side of every internal cut. Below that sum, differencing the two-part coefficients finds the short arms; the scan through the second-smallest arm is valid for every competitor. The bound on the largest arm gives complete length recovery. A component covering all but this cutoff in vertices retains the core. The existing logarithmic derivative proof therefore matches lengths to arbitrary caps through exactly the available cutoff. [Rooted factors and division](TWO-HUB-SUBDIVISIONS.md)
- In the alternative proof, every omitted connected component of the core after a connected choice meeting a hub has one incident boundary edge and contains a terminal hub. It loses the leaves of at least two exterior arms. Choices avoiding every hub lie in a single exterior arm or in a path interior. These bounds justify isolating the same high boundary coefficients used in the two-hub proof. Unit caps and repeated rooted caps cause no ambiguity. [High-degree calculation](TWO-HUB-FREE-LENGTHS.md)
- Every known rooted arm contributes its oriented cuts without reference to its attachment vertex. Once those contributions are removed, each remaining side contains between one and $`H-1`$ core vertices. Separation decodes one label subset. Selecting the orientation away from a fixed leaf counts each internal edge once. The multiplicity of each split counts the edges along the corresponding subdivided path between two hubs. The cluster containment hierarchy recovers every hub, including a hub with no attached exterior arm.
- The density bounds fix the core and the caps before taking the limit. For each pair of distinct subsets, after conditioning on all but one differing coordinate, fewer than $`2H`$ integer choices cause a collision. The arbitrary-cap interval enforces the short-cut condition for large $`t`$. In the leaf-count case only a bounded number of infeasible or too-small orders need be excluded in each coordinate.

## Prior results and scope

The novelty check compared the exact unmarked conclusion and both length regimes with the existing materials on the permanent branches and the ongoing issue. It is dated and bounded. Stanley’s power-sum expansion supplies the spanning-forest data. Martin–Morin–Wagner recover degree information, and Wang–Yu–Zhang recover the connected-subset boundary distribution and distinguish all trees with exactly two branch vertices. Their two-vertex result already covers the corresponding overlap. Our core and the internal structures of the attached caps may both have arbitrarily many branch vertices. [Stanley](https://doi.org/10.1006/aima.1995.1020) · [Martin–Morin–Wagner](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0609339) · [Wang–Yu–Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.03980v3)

Crew recovers the order of the ordinary trunk and the lengths of its pendant paths. A rooted cap can contain many branch vertices, so its stem can itself lie inside Crew’s trunk. Even for point caps, twig lengths alone do not give the internal incidence relations or their path lengths. The final recovery from an already *labeled* family of splits is classical. The present step obtains those splits from unmarked cut data. Loebl–Sereni distinguish trees in a good weighted family and reconstruct caterpillars. The present hypotheses allow arbitrary topology and compare against all trees. [Crew](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.04417) · [Loebl–Sereni, Theorems 1–2](https://kam.mff.cuni.cz/~loebl/clanky/LoSe17AIHP.pdf) · [Buneman’s original split reconstruction](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/the-recovery-of-trees-from-measures-of-dissimilarity/)

The star-basis work of Gonzalez–Orellana–Tomba, Zeng’s multiplicity-isolated core criterion and Awan’s first-derivative criterion study leaf components and proper-tree conditions. Long unary stems here generally have internal vertices without leaf neighbors. Orellana–Tomba’s equivalence for a special class of proper trees concerns the general conjecture. The repository’s earlier one-edge, one-hub and two-hub subdivision results provide specific arm-recovery ingredients; none of those conclusions reconstructs an arbitrary branching core by decoding its internal splits. This extension belongs with the existing unranked Stanley partial theorem. [Gonzalez–Orellana–Tomba, star basis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.06002) · [Leaf components and reconstruction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15986) · [Zeng](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18179) · [Awan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13464) · [Orellana–Tomba](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18940) · [Earlier two-hub publication](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1024)
