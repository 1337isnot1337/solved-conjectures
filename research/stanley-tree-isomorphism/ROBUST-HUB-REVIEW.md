I reviewed `ROBUST-HUB-DISTANCES.md` at SHA-256 `d55977157a58297e78b5337467a8f76907e05ca9929629e5dcd2989d860b91ad` against the path-distance certificate, finite matching cells, fixed-excess free-edge lemma and continuous metric-tree identity. The mathematical argument passes. The natural domain throughout is a tree with at least one positive-length edge. In the marked lemma, rational independence of heights means the heights of the reduced vertices. The auxiliary marked point has height zero and is omitted from that list.

**Leaf minimum.** Negative variation is bounded by the total mass of all leaf–hub pairs before cancellations, which is L(L−2). Equality of the competitor’s and original leaf counts attains this maximum, so every distance class has at most one sign. Every nonempty reduced path therefore has an observed length. Each distinct indecomposable target edge value in the support is then realized by a single competing edge. Their sum is the full common edge length from the signed first moment, so there are no remaining competing edges. Both reduced orders now agree. The number of observed distances is the number of available pairs, giving distinctness and recovering each hub multiplicity from the negative coefficients. This also handles a nontrivial path, where the negative variation is zero.

**Marked comparison.** A mixed height at a negative target atom uses one more leaf than its obligatory representative. A mixed height at any other position uses an extra leaf as well. Thus at most c heights must be killed, and the quotient removes a subspace of dimension at most c. All pair-distance tags add the endpoint tags because the least common ancestor is a hub or the chosen root, with tag zero. For any aliased pair tags their difference is a vector of this subspace on at most four coordinates. A basis chosen from the span of all such sparse vectors uses at most 4c coordinates. A coordinate outside this union must have the same incidence in every pair belonging to one fiber. Two clean coordinates identify exactly one leaf pair. One clean coordinate detects precisely the events involving that leaf, including when the other endpoint is a hub or a leaf collapsed to zero. At a clean height the only possible rival contribution is one leaf. Restricting the signed measures therefore recovers both the individual distances and the full signed profile claimed. The nearest other reduced vertex to a leaf is its own parent.

**Unmarked transfer.** The freely variable pendant from the fixed-excess proof gives a marked bivalent attachment after pruning on both sides. Rooted height differences invert the remaining edge coordinates integrally when the mark with zero height is used as origin. The labelled metric subtree of the clean leaves together with this point can be recovered using the ordinary labelled distance criterion, even when the mark ends up at a leaf of the restricted subtree. Attaching the common pendant at the mark restores the distinguished leaf, so at most 4c *original* leaves are missing. A high-degree vertex with a surviving leaf in each incident component gives a distinct rival hub of at least that degree. Summing its nonnegative excess contradicts the strict drop in the rival's excess. The variant using the loss in the sum of binomial defects follows by degree monotonicity under isometric subtree inclusion.

**Size and genericity.** The identities Δ=2K+E and k=2L−2−E follow from the degree sum. With multiple high-degree vertices, their minimal connector has total degree on these vertices at most twice their count minus two because all remaining connector vertices have degree at least two. Therefore there are at least 2 plus the sum of the high-vertex defects disjoint directions with no other high-degree vertex. The single quartic case gives k ≥ 37, the other positive-excess cases give k ≥ 39K+8, and both exceed 12Δ. This justifies the use of the free-edge reduction. Excluding every full-target-dimensional nonisomorphic cell yields the integer exceptional bound on each fixed topology and the corresponding generic real statement, using the previously checked global arclength identity.
