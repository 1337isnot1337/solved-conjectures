The statement concerns finite positive metric trees marked at a hub or inside an edge. Under rational independence of the target's nonzero reduced vertex heights, equality of its signed marked height profile and signed unmarked reduced-pair distance measure precludes a rival with exactly two additional leaves. For ordinary subdivisions the consequence requires a freely variable rival pendant in a full-dimensional matching. The unmarked no-free-pendant case remains open.

* At a clean leaf, isolating the pair tags gives the entire signed profile based there. The parent is strictly the closest other reduced vertex. The pendant length and its parent's defect are therefore intrinsic even if the mark lies on that pendant. Deletion away from the open marked pendant changes the old marked measure by adding back the leaf atom and subtracting one parent atom. For the pair measure, decreasing the parent's old weight by one subtracts exactly the signed parent profile off its atom at zero. This works as well when a trivalent marked parent becomes bivalent. The condition that the leaf height is less than its pendant length detects the mark exception simultaneously in the two trees. There is at most one such target pendant. Deletion preserves the excess of two leaves and independence of the remaining nonzero heights.
* Two independent mixed tags require two distinct heights exhausting the two extra leaves. Each neutral cancellation therefore has one positive unit, a trivalent hub. All other positive mass is at true target hub heights. In the quotient retaining the first tag, the difference of diagonal squared masses is twice its squared monomial minus twice the constant monomial. The constant coefficient follows from equality of the total squared mass. The factor one half for unordered pairs gives precisely the manuscript's correction. Only pairs with that one trivalent hub as lowest common ancestor need a different distance monomial. This yields the stated product factorization in a domain. Its nonzero tag must have a negatively weighted descendant with that tag. The coheight extra rival leaf cannot be below the hub, and the other extra leaf maps to zero. Interchanging the two tags gives the second relation. Distinct selected basis units span the two-dimensional space. Equal selected units force their common value to be the sum of the independent tags.
* In the remaining case every relation on at most three coordinates is a multiple of the one selected unit. A split positive target defect exists by the moment discrepancy. Split representatives are incomparable, their descendants include at least four leaves, and at most three of those leaves are excluded. The height of this split is positive since a mark at zero is unique. In the quotient the distinguishing distance forces one endpoint to be the selected leaf with the surviving label. It is unique: a mixed tag equal to that label would yield another one-coordinate relation. Full endpoint and ancestor tags now show both of the other tags must vanish. At actual heights this gives the arithmetic mean identity between three true hub heights, allowing the mark at zero. Independence forces all three to be the same positive height. A strictly increasing rooted path thereby picks exactly one reduced hub, even when a true height has multiple incomparable representatives on the rival. Its smaller positive defect supplies a strictly intermediate negative mass, whereas the target gives either zero or the entire negative defect.
* In every remaining rank/support case at most four leaf labels must be reserved. Minimality under deletion leaves at most five target leaves. The sum of its positive defects is at most three, whereas its sum of defect binomial coefficients is at least two. Only the five-leaf star with defect three qualifies. The rival then has one defect two, with all others one. Reading a clean leaf's unique nearest parent already contradicts this, including when that leaf cannot be removed.

The mathematical claims with the stated finite, positive, marked and full-dimensional restrictions pass this review. This is an internal written-proof review.
