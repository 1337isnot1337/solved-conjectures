# Bipartite targets and all forest targets

A second classification for [Erdős #596](https://www.erdosproblems.com/latex/596), the edge-color version, issue #1085. This is not a classification of all graph pairs.

Write “star forest” to include isolated vertices. A “nontrivial component” has at least one edge.

## Countable escape for bipartite targets other than star forests

**Theorem.** For a finite bipartite $`H`$ that is not a star forest, every $`F`$-free graph admits a countable edge coloring avoiding $`H`$ if and only if $`F`$ is bipartite.

If $`F`$ is nonbipartite, a large complete bipartite graph is $`F`$-free and arrows $`H`$ for countable edge palettes. For a direct argument, take left part of size $`\aleph_1`$ and right part of size $`\aleph_2`$. For each right vertex choose $`t`$ left neighbors whose edges to it have one common color. There are only $`\aleph_1`$ choices of the $`t`$-set and color together, so at least $`t`$ right vertices make the same choice. This gives a monochromatic $`K_{t,t}`$, for any fixed $`t`$ large enough to contain $`H`$.

Suppose instead that $`F`$ is bipartite, and choose $`s,t\ge1`$ with $`F\subseteq K_{s,t}`$. We show directly that every $`K_{s,t}`$-free graph admits a well-order with finitely many earlier neighbors per vertex. For $`s=1`$ the degree bound suffices. In general, any $`s`$ vertices have fewer than $`t`$ common neighbors. Define the closure of a set of vertices by repeatedly adjoining all common neighbors of each $`s`$-subset, for countably many steps. An infinite set's closure has the same cardinality; the closure of a finite set is at most countable. Any vertex outside a closed set has fewer than $`s`$ neighbors inside it.

Proceed by induction on the infinite size $`\lambda`$ of the graph, the countable case being immediate by enumeration. Enumerate all vertices as $`(v_\xi:\xi<\lambda)`$. Form a continuous increasing chain $`(X_\xi:\xi<\lambda)`$ of closed sets by starting empty, adjoining $`v_\xi`$ and closing at successor steps, and taking unions at limits. Its sizes are bounded by $`|\xi|+\aleph_0<\lambda`$, including when $`\lambda`$ is singular. Well-order each layer $`X_{\xi+1}\setminus X_\xi`$ by the induction hypothesis. Concatenate the layer orders. A vertex has finitely many earlier neighbors within its layer and fewer than $`s`$ in prior layers.

For each vertex assign distinct natural numbers to its edges to its earlier neighbors. Color the edges by those numbers at their later endpoints. No color contains a cycle: its largest vertex would have two earlier edges with the same number. Each color is therefore a forest. Root its components and split its edges by parity of the parent's depth; each part is a star forest. This gives a countable decomposition into star forests, so no class contains $`H`$.

## Classification for every forest target

**Theorem.** If $`H`$ is a finite forest, $`(F,H)`$ is exceptional in #596 exactly when **$`F`$ is bipartite and contains a cycle, and $`H`$ is not a star forest**.

First suppose $`H`$ is not a star forest. The countable side is the preceding theorem. For the finite side, a forest of copies of $`H`$ glued along vertices or shared edges is a forest. If $`F`$ contains a cycle, the girth Ramsey theorem therefore gives finite $`F`$-free edge Ramsey witnesses at every finite palette (the cycle alone already cannot embed into a forest of copies). Conversely, if $`F`$ is a forest, extend it into a finite tree $`T`$ by adding edges or vertices. Every finite $`F`$-free graph has degeneracy at most $`|V(T)|-2`$ when $`|V(T)|\ge2`$, since a nonempty subgraph of minimum degree at least $`|V(T)|-1`$ permits greedy embedding of $`T`$. The degenerate order gives finitely many forests by the same outgoing-number coloring, and splitting each by depth parity gives finitely many star forests. Thus one uniformly bounded finite palette already avoids $`H`$. The vacuous forbidden graphs with at most one vertex have no nontrivial hosts.

Now suppose $`H`$ is a star forest. **No pair is exceptional.** The following cases show that either some finite palette works for every finite $`F`$-free graph, or there is an $`F`$-free host that arrows $`H`$ even under countably many colors.

1. **$`H`$ has at least two nontrivial components, including a star with at least two edges.** If $`F`$ is not a star forest, the disjoint union of $`\aleph_1`$ many stars, each with $`\aleph_1`$ leaves, is $`F`$-free. In each star some color occurs on uncountably many edges; one common color does so in uncountably many components. This supplies $`H`$. If $`F`$ is a star forest with $`q`$ nontrivial components, an $`F`$-free graph has fewer than $`q`$ vertices of degree at least $`|V(F)|`$, except that an edgeless $`F`$ just bounds the whole order. To see the degree claim, use $`q`$ such vertices as centers and greedily choose distinct leaves, avoiding all centers; those vertices and, if needed, additional unused neighbors of a center give the required isolated vertices as a **noninduced** subgraph. Use one color for edges incident with each of the finitely many high-degree vertices, assigning each affected edge to only one such vertex. Properly edge-color the bounded-degree remainder with a separate finite palette (greedy coloring of the line graph suffices on finite hosts). Each monochromatic subgraph is a single star or a matching, so none contains $`H`$.
2. **$`H`$ has one nontrivial component, a star of at least two edges.** If an uncountable single star avoids $`F`$, it already arrows $`H`$ for countable colors. Otherwise $`F`$ embeds in a single sufficiently large finite star, so all $`F`$-free graphs have bounded maximum degree. A bounded finite proper edge coloring avoids $`H`$.
3. **$`H`$ is a matching of at least two edges, with possible isolated vertices.** If $`F`$ does not embed in an uncountable matching, that matching witnesses failure of countable escape. Otherwise $`F`$ embeds in a large finite matching, so $`F`$-free graphs have bounded matching number. The endpoints of a maximal matching cover all edges; color each edge by a selected endpoint. Each color forms a single star, and avoids $`H`$.
4. **$`H`$ has at most one edge.** If any $`F`$-free graph contains $`H`$, all palettes already give a monochromatic $`H`$ in that graph. Otherwise the finite clause fails at one color.

## Additional exact families

For any **bipartite cycle target or balanced complete bipartite target with a cycle**, the countable side above is equivalent to $`F`$ bipartite. By Theorems 1.2 and 1.3 of [Reiher–Rödl–Schacht](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09830), the finite side holds precisely when $`F`$ does not embed into a forest of copies of $`H`$. One may discard isolated vertices of $`F`$ for this test: any forest embedding its nonisolated part extends by adding enough disjoint copies of $`H`$ to accommodate the isolated vertices. In the absence of an embedding the local-girth witnesses avoid the nonisolated part, as required.

For **nonbipartite cycles as target**, the same forest criterion decides the finite side, and the countable side is known in particular for all bipartite $`F`$ by the stronger star-forest decomposition above. The case of nonbipartite $`F,H`$ in general is not settled.

## Sources and scope

The published forest inputs are [Reiher–Rödl's girth Ramsey theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15589) and its local form and partial converse in [Reiher–Rödl–Schacht, Theorems 1.2–1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.09830). The forbidden $`C_4`$ countable decomposition is classical; the closure argument above treats every finite bipartite forbidden graph uniformly. [Erdős's 1987 paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-28.pdf) gives the original finite-versus-countable problem. The [current #596 working report](https://erdosproblemaday.com/report/596) treats the $`C_4`$ row, whose forest part agrees with this classification. The classification is by structural families across all sizes, not by a finite census. No priority is claimed for the component lemmas.
