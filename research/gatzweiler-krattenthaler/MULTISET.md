# Symmetric first-coefficient search

This is a failed construction search for the separate symmetric conjecture, not a proof of that conjecture. The arbitrary-exponent Conjecture 4 is already disproved in [the CoPEER archive](https://github.com/qqliu/CoPEER/blob/105523fdaf018b62eaf2883347724dfe79935611/mathematics/discoveries/entries/number-theory/fake-gaussian-positivity-counterexample.md).

## Construction being tested

Let the nonnegative exponents satisfy $`a_i=a_{n+1-i}`$, and let $`j`$ be the least index with $`a_j>0`$. Put $`t=m+j`$. If the product is a polynomial and $`t`$ is not in the additive semigroup generated by the denominator support, then its coefficient of $`q^t`$ is $`-a_j<0`$. This follows by expanding the numerator through its first nonconstant term and the denominator as geometric series.

The earlier search excluded representations of $`t`$ by at most two supported indices. The new script [symmetric_multiset_probe.py](symmetric_multiset_probe.py) excludes every representation by at most four indices. It restricts $`5j>t`$, so longer representations are impossible. Repeated indices are allowed in these representations. Each forbidden representation is converted to a constraint on the distinct reflection orbits that it uses; thus repeated coins and paired indices do not accidentally weaken the support exclusion.

Polynomiality is imposed by nonnegative cyclotomic valuations. Exponents are integer variables between 0 and 64, with binary support indicators. The least support orbit is forced to be present. A returned witness would be rechecked using exact integer valuations and unrestricted integer semigroup reachability, independently of the floating-point solver result.

## Completed runs

Both runs used SciPy's MILP interface, with a 0.15-second limit for each optimization call.

| Range | Total parameter triples | Excluded because the forced orbit already represents the target | Solver returned infeasible | Witnesses or timeouts |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $`3\le n\le100`$, $`n\le m\le n+11`$ | 14,269 | 2,611 | 11,658 | 0 |
| $`3\le n\le80`$, $`1\le m<n`$ | 65,959 | 6,642 | 59,317 | 0 |

For each $`(m,n)`$, the loop uses $`\lfloor m/4\rfloor<j\le\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor`$. This is precisely the four-coin restriction and the necessary symmetry bound.

Reproduction commands:

```sh
python symmetric_multiset_probe.py 100 3 large_shift
python symmetric_multiset_probe.py 80 3 small_shift
```

## Limits

The negative search result covers only a first-numerator-coefficient construction, only these parameter ranges, and only exponents at most 64. Solver infeasibility reports were not converted to formal certificates. A negative coefficient could occur after the first numerator exponent, or the same mechanism could require parameters outside the search. No mathematical resolution or improved verified range of the symmetric conjecture is claimed.

The lack of a witness suggests that polynomiality plus reflection symmetry may obstruct this particular semigroup gap. That suggestion is unproved and should be tested structurally before expanding the search further.
