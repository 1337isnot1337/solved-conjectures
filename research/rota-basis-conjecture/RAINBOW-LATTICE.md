# Rota’s basis conjecture

**Partial research.** The integer lattice of rainbow bases is saturated for arbitrary matroids when every color spans. For the original rank-$n$ input of $n$ bases, this gives a signed decomposition and a genuine rainbow decomposition for every sufficiently large repetition number. The one-copy nonnegative decomposition remains open. [Tracking issue #298](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/298)

## Target and attribution

Rota asks whether the labeled disjoint union of $n$ bases of a rank-$n$ matroid can be partitioned into $n$ bases that each use one label of every color. The November 24, 2026 search found no unrestricted proof or counterexample. Montgomery and Sauermann give asymptotically tight packing and covering. The November 23 *Lattice of rainbow bases* preprint proves the lattice and sufficiently large repetition statements for representable matroids. Its Remark 2.3 bounds the possible obstruction for arbitrary matroids by a finite 2-group, using Hörsch, Imolay, Mizutani, Oki, and Schwarcz. The proof below removes the representability hypothesis by direct single-color exchanges. Priority for the extension is provisional. [Huang–Rota, Conjecture 4](https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(94)90114-7) · [Montgomery–Sauermann](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05601) · [*The lattice of rainbow bases*, v1](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00941v1) · [Hörsch et al., Theorems 3.9 and 3.14](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.16259v1)

## 1. A separation lemma for one color

Let $M$ have rank $r\ge1$ and ground set partitioned into $r$ spanning sets $E_1,\ldots,E_r$. A completion of an independent transversal of $r-1$ colors is an element of the missing color that extends it to a basis. Fix one color $E_i$ and partition it into $A$ and $B$. Assume that no transversal of the other $r-1$ colors admits completions from both $A$ and $B$.

**Lemma.** Under this assumption, $r_M(A)+r_M(B)=r$ and every element lies in $\operatorname{cl}_M(A)$ or $\operatorname{cl}_M(B)$.

**Proof.** Write $a=r_M(A)$ and $b=r_M(B)$. If one side has rank zero, the other side spans and the conclusion follows. If both sides have positive rank, then $a,b\lt r$. To prove this for $a$, start with a nonloop of $B$. Greedily augment with one element from each other color, which is possible because each color spans. The resulting missing-color transversal admits this completion from $B$. It spans all of $A$ by assumption. Thus $a\le r-1$, and the reverse argument gives the bound for $b$. In rank one the two positive ranks are therefore impossible.

Induct on $r$. Take a nonloop $x$ of any other color $E_j$. Contract $x$ and remove the remaining elements of $E_j$. Each surviving color is still spanning and the rank is $r-1$. The no-mixed-completion condition persists because a transversal and either completion in the contracted matroid lift by adding $x$. Let $\alpha$ be zero or one according as $x$ is in or out of $\operatorname{cl}_M(A)$, and similarly let $\beta$ correspond to $B$. The contracted ranks of the two sides are $a+\alpha-1$ and $b+\beta-1$ by the contraction rank formula. The induction hypothesis gives

```math
 a+b-r=1-\alpha-\beta .
```

Because $A\cup B$ spans, $a+b\ge r$. It follows that $\alpha+\beta\le1$. Thus every nonloop of every other color belongs to the claimed union of closures. Loops and the missing color itself belong to that union as well.

Since $b\lt r$ and every other color spans, there is some nonloop $x$ in another color outside $\operatorname{cl}_M(B)$. The covering just proved puts it in $\operatorname{cl}_M(A)$. For this $x$, $\alpha=0$ and $\beta=1$, so the same equation shows $a+b=r$. This completes the induction. ∎

The equality of the ranks and the covering give a direct-sum separation of the nonloops by the two closures. Their intersection has rank zero by submodularity. A basis of either closure joined to a basis of the other spans the ground set with exactly $r$ elements. In particular every matroid component of positive rank lies entirely on one side.

## 2. Single-color exchanges generate the lattice

In color $i$, join two labels when some two rainbow bases differ exactly by exchanging those labels. The connected components of this exchange graph are exactly the nonempty intersections of $E_i$ with matroid components. A single-color exchange cannot cross components, since all bases have the fixed number of elements $r(C)$ on every matroid component $C$. Conversely, if a split of the exchange graph separates two labels from the same matroid component, no missing-color transversal has completions across the split. The separation lemma would split their matroid component, a contradiction. Loops are isolated in both graphs.

Write $C_1,\ldots,C_c$ for the positive-rank components with ranks $r_1,\ldots,r_c$. For $z\in\mathbb Z^E$ and $X\subseteq E$, write $z(X)=\sum_{e\in X}z_e$. The lattice generated by all rainbow-basis incidences is exactly

```math
\left\{z\in\mathbb Z^E:\ z(E_i)=d\text{ for all }i,\quad
z(C_a)=d r_a\text{ for all }a,\quad z_e=0\text{ on loops},\quad d\in\mathbb Z\right\}.
```

Each basis incidence satisfies the equations. For sufficiency subtract $d$ times a fixed rainbow basis incidence. Such a basis exists by selecting greedily one element from each successive spanning color. The residual has zero sums on rows and components.

Next project to the table whose $i,a$ entry is the sum on $E_i\cap C_a$. Every cell is nonempty because every color spans the whole matroid and therefore spans each of its components separately. Every assignment of colors to components with $r_a$ colors assigned to $C_a$ is feasible. Within each component, greedily pick one independent element of each assigned color because that color spans the component.

For two rows and two components, assign those rows to the two components and then exchange their assignments. These two rainbow bases can be taken identical in all other colors. For each of the two components select independent elements first from the $r_a-1$ other assigned colors, then complete independently from either of the two exchanged colors. The difference of the two base incidences gives the table rectangle with opposite signs on rows and columns. The rectangles generate every integer table with zero margins. For example, fix one anchor row and one anchor column and use the rectangle involving them to clear each other entry. Subtract the lifted rectangle differences. When there is only one row or one component the projected zero-margin table is already zero.

The remaining vector has zero sum in each color-component cell. The connected exchange graph in that cell expresses any such integer vector as a sum of elementary differences along single-color exchanges. This completes the lattice proof. The displayed equalities define a saturated sublattice since $d$ is the first row sum. The proof also identifies generators using differences of rainbow bases that change at most two colors.

## 3. Fixed quotas and the role of spanning

More generally let there be $m$ spanning colors with positive quotas $q_i$ summing to $r$. A quota basis has exactly $q_i$ elements in color $i$. Its incidence lattice has the same description with color equations $z(E_i)=d q_i$ and component equations $z(C_a)=d r_a$.

Indeed, replace each color by $q_i$ labeled copies, giving each original element a parallel clone in every copy of its color. The rank remains $r$ and the copies are spanning. A rainbow basis on the copies projects to a quota basis because no independent set contains two parallel clones of one label. Conversely a quota basis lifts by allocating its distinct elements to the copies. For any proposed integer vector on the original labels, split the coordinates among the copies so that the sum on each copy is $d$. This is possible because the original color sum is $d q_i$. One can put all coordinates in the first copy initially and then shift signed amounts at one nonloop label between copies. Loop coordinates stay zero. The component sums are unchanged because each clone of a nonloop belongs to its original positive-rank component. The lattice theorem on the copies and projection prove the assertion. For $q_i=1$ this is the saturated rainbow lattice above. For general quotas the quotient of its saturation by the lattice is cyclic of order $\gcd(q_1,\ldots,q_m,r_1,\ldots,r_c)$. To see this, a vector in the saturation has rational common multiplier $d$, with all margins integral exactly when $d$ is a multiple of the reciprocal of that gcd. Every such choice of margins can be realized by an integer table on the full grid and then by an integer vector on labels. The lattice corresponds exactly to integer $d$.

The spanning hypothesis matters. The three perfect matchings of $K_4$ as colors yield four rainbow spanning trees, namely the stars. Their half-sum is the all-one vector and it is no integer combination of them, as the equation at edge $uv$ forces the coefficients of the stars centered at $u,v$ to sum to one. These colors have rank two in a rank-three matroid. This is the earlier common-base lattice obstruction reproduced in Example 4.4 of the November preprint. [Harvey–Király–Lau, Appendix A](https://doi.org/10.1137/100815232) · [*The lattice of rainbow bases*, Example 4.4](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00941v1)

## 4. All sufficiently large repetitions for arbitrary matroids

Return to the original input of $n$ bases of a rank-$n$ matroid and write $w$ for the all-one vector. Let $x=w/n$ and let $F$ be the smallest face of the base polytope containing $x$. The point $x$ is the average of the input basis incidences, so all those bases lie in $F$. A face of a matroid base polytope is the base polytope of a matroid on the same ground set. For a weight exposing the face, take its decreasing-weight prefix sets. A basis maximizes the weight exactly when it meets each prefix in a basis of that restriction. The possible choices in consecutive layers are the bases of the corresponding restriction–contraction minors, and their direct sum is the face matroid $N$. Thus the original colors are still bases in $N$.

By matroid intersection, the rainbow-basis polytope of $N$ is its base polytope intersected with the equations requiring one element of each color. Since $x$ is in the relative interior of $F$ and satisfies those equations, it is in the relative interior of this intersection. All its rainbow bases are original rainbow bases. The lattice theorem applies to $N$, so $w$ is an integer combination of their incidences. The relative-interior property also gives a strictly positive rational combination for $w$. For the latter statement, subtract a sufficiently small positive rational multiple of the sum of the generators from $w$, stay in their cone, and choose a rational nonnegative representation. Such a representation exists by using a linearly independent minimal support of integer generators.

Clear denominators to write $Dw=\sum_R b_R\chi_R$ with every $b_R$ a positive integer. Write the signed decomposition as $w=\sum_R a_R\chi_R$. For $k=qD+s$ with $0\le s\lt D$,

```math
kw=\sum_R(q b_R+s a_R)\chi_R.
```

Every coefficient is nonnegative for all sufficiently large $q$. Taking the sum on one color gives exactly $kn$ bases. This is the face and arithmetic step from the November preprint applied with the arbitrary-matroid lattice theorem. The argument gives no guarantee at $k=1$. [*The lattice of rainbow bases*, §§3–4](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00941v1)

### Two consecutive repetition numbers with an explicit bound

The exchange proof gives a short signed expression for $w$ in the face matroid. Begin with $n\chi_{R_0}$ for one rainbow basis, leaving a residual of $\ell_1$ norm $2n(n-1)$. Clearing the nonanchor table entries uses at most $2n(n-1)$ lifted rectangle differences counted with multiplicity. These differences have norm four. The remaining norm is at most $10n(n-1)$. Within each cell, pair positive units with negative units and transport along the single-color exchange graph. There are at most $n$ labels in a cell, so each unit takes at most $n-1$ swaps. The resulting signed expression $w=w^+-w^-$ uses at most $2n(n-1)+5n(n-1)^2\le7n^3$ negative basis occurrences. Write their number as $h$.

Put $t=nh+1$ and $u=tw-w^-$. Every coordinate of $w^-$ is at most $h$, so $u$ is nonnegative. Its color sums are all $tn-h$. For each subset $X$ of the face matroid $N$, the slack of its rank bound for $u$ is

```math
(tn-h)r_N(X)-u(X)=t\bigl(nr_N(X)-|X|\bigr)-\bigl(hr_N(X)-w^-(X)\bigr).
```

If the integer $nr_N(X)-|X|$ is zero, the rank inequality is tight at the relative-interior point $w/n$ of the base polytope of $N$. It is then tight on all its bases, so the slack is zero. Otherwise the first term is at least $t$, and the subtracted term is at most $hn$. Thus $u/(tn-h)$ is in the rainbow-basis polytope. Pick a nonnegative conic expression for $u$ using linearly independent rainbow-basis incidences. There are at most $n^2$ of them and each has Euclidean norm $\sqrt n$. An invertible square row minor and Hadamard's inequality give a common integer denominator $D\le n^{n^2/2}$ for the coefficients.

Now $Du$ is a nonnegative integer combination. With $K=tD$, the following are both nonnegative integer combinations as well.

```math
Kw=Du+D w^-,\qquad (K+1)w=Du+(D-1)w^-+w^+.
```

Thus there are two consecutive valid repetition numbers with $K\le(7n^4+1)n^{n^2/2}$. Every $k\ge K(K-1)$ is a nonnegative combination of $K$ and $K+1$. Indeed, write $k=qK+s$ with $0\le s\lt K$. Then $q\ge s$, and $k=(q-s)K+s(K+1)$. This is an explicit, coarse bound.

## 5. A semigroup boundary inside full-color inputs

The $K_4$ obstruction in §3 can be padded to have three full spanning colors. Add a labeled copy of edge $13$ to the color $\{12,34\}$ and a labeled copy of edge $12$ to each of the other two colors. Each color is now a spanning tree in the multigraph. Let $z$ assign one to the six old labels and zero to the three new ones. The four original stars still give a nonnegative rational expression for $z$ as their half-sum. The lattice theorem gives an integer signed expression. An unsigned expression would have to use only the six old labels because the new coordinates are zero. The previous star obstruction rules this out. Consequently the semigroup can fail to contain every lattice point of its cone even with full spanning colors. The all-one vector of the padded input has positive coordinates on the new labels and is a different point.

## Verification

The proof above is general. The companion checker enumerates every rainbow basis of each of 192 sampled inputs on Vámos, non-Pappus, a free extension, and a direct sum with a rank-one component. It compares the single-color components with fundamental-circuit components and replays the rank over four prime fields. These checks exercise the combinatorial identities on finite examples.
