# Source and priority check

- Publisher final text: Z. Lángi and S. Wang, IMRN 2026 (1), rnaf372,
  https://doi.org/10.1093/imrn/rnaf372. The final title locator is **Question 5.1**. It asks about the upper and lower averages under an inradius lower bound and a circ/inradius upper bound, and about existence of a tile of at most six sides if the averages fail. Definition 2.1 and preceding §2 use tiles wholly contained in concentric disks, not tiles selected by centroids. Its subsequent Example 5.1 is expressly for **square** windows.
- Open arXiv version: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.10622v4. Here the same question is **Question 5.6** and the square example is **Example 5.7**, pp. 18–20; Definition 2.3, p.5 gives the averages and §2 defines the disk window. The standard normal tiling result with uniformly bounded sizes is Lemma 2.4 in that version.
- Original Croft–Falconer–Guy p.96 C15, https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-0963-8: the first formulation is for congruent unit-area tiles (covered by Hales), and the following one concerns varying tile sizes with diameters bounded below. Only the convex case of the second formulation is addressed in this note.
- Related existing results checked: Akopyan's *On the number of non-hexagons in a planar tiling* (2018), https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.crma.2018.03.005.pdf, imposes a common diameter upper bound for its finite defect theorem; Yang–Wang's monohedral tiling theorem (November 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13664, concerns the common number of neighbors of congruent copies; Oh's vertex-parabolicity statement (2010) applies to graph representations by fat sets but is not a result about the precise circular liminf/limsup.
- Targeted searches by article DOI, title, both question numberings, author names, “inradius circumradius lower average number sides circular disk tiling,” “fat convex tiles at most six sides,” “upper circular average unbounded convex tiling,” and “Steinhaus minimum diameter convex tilings” found no earlier resolution of the circular liminf/limsup problems. This is a dated search result, not a priority guarantee.
