# Attainment and strictness in the clique-isodiametric problem

This is a companion to [the packing-density bounds](PROOF.md). It concerns the fixed-$`k`$ vertex optimum, not a new claim about the conjecture's earlier best-packing disproof.

**Proposition.** For each $`d\ge 1`$, $`k\ge2`$, the supremum $`V_{d,k}`$ is attained by a compact set. In dimension $`2`$, it satisfies the strict inequality
```math
V_{2,k}<2\sqrt3\,(k-1)\qquad(k\ge 2).
```
The second claim does not give a uniform quantitative gap from the upper bound.

### Attainment

Any nonempty $`K_k`$-free set $`A`$ is bounded. Indeed, choose a maximum-cardinality subset with pairwise distances greater than $`2`$. Its size $`m`$ is an attained integer between $`1`$ and $`k-1`$. Maximality implies that the closed radius-$`2`$ balls around those $`m`$ points cover $`A`$. Its closure is still $`K_k`$-free: any $`k`$ points in the closure with all pairwise distances strictly greater than $`2`$ can be approximated by points of $`A`$ retaining these finitely many strict inequalities. Thus it is enough to maximize over nonempty compact sets.

For such a set $`A`$, join two points by an auxiliary edge when their distance is at most $`2`$. The (finite-chain) components number at most $`k-1`$, since any two points in distinct components have distance greater than $`2`$. Each component is compact: a limit point in $`A`$ of a sequence in the component belongs to it by a single auxiliary edge from a sufficiently close sequence term. For points $`x,y`$ in the same component, a shortest finite auxiliary path of length $`\ell`$ has every nonconsecutive pair at distance greater than $`2`$; a shorter path would otherwise exist. Its even-indexed vertices therefore form a clique in $`G_A`$. The count $`\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor+1\le k-1`$ implies $`\ell\le2k-3`$, so each component has diameter at most $`4k-6`$.

Set $`D=4k-6`$. Center each component at any one of its points and translate the components so that the chosen points occupy consecutive positions spaced $`2D+3`$ along the first coordinate axis. Every cross-component distance in the new arrangement is at least $`3`$, hence strictly greater than $`2`$, as it was before. Within-component distances are unchanged. The rearrangement preserves volume and $`K_k`$-freeness, and the resulting union lies in a single compact cube depending only on $`d,k`$.

Take a maximizing sequence of these rearranged compact sets. A subsequence converges in Hausdorff distance to a nonempty compact set $`A_*`$ in the fixed cube, by compactness of the nonempty compact subsets of a compact metric space. The strict edge threshold implies that $`A_*`$ is $`K_k`$-free by the same finite approximation argument. For each $`\varepsilon>0`$, all sufficiently late sets lie in the closed $`\varepsilon`$-neighborhood of $`A_*`$. The volumes of these neighborhoods converge to $`|A_*|`$ as $`\varepsilon\downarrow0`$, by continuity from above inside a bounded cube. Thus $`|A_*|`$ is at least the limit superior of the sequence's volumes, and attains $`V_{d,k}`$.

### Why the planar upper bound is never attained

Suppose a compact admissible $`A\subset\mathbb R^2`$ has $`|A|=2\sqrt3(k-1)`$. Let $`\Lambda`$ be any rotation of the triangular lattice generated by $`(2,0),(1,\sqrt3)`$. Its fundamental parallelogram $`T`$ has area $`2\sqrt3`$. For $`s>1`$, define on $`T`$
```math
q_s(t)=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mathbf1_A(s(t+\lambda)).
```
The points $`s(t+\lambda)`$ are pairwise separated by strictly more than $`2`$, giving $`q_s\le k-1`$ pointwise. Since $`A,T`$ are bounded, only finitely many terms are nonzero for $`t\in T`$ and $`1\le s\le2`$, uniformly in $`t,s`$. Dilations of an $`L^1`$ function are continuous in $`L^1`$ at $`s=1`$; this follows by approximating the function by compactly supported continuous functions and using the change-of-variables identity for their $`L^1`$ norms. Consequently $`q_s\to q_1`$ in $`L^1(T)`$, so $`q_1\le k-1`$ almost everywhere. Its integral is $`|A|=(k-1)|T|`$, by tiling with $`T+\lambda`$. Thus $`q_1=k-1`$ almost everywhere.

Let $`\Lambda^*=\{\xi:\xi\cdot\lambda\in\mathbb Z\ \text{for all }\lambda\in\Lambda\}`$ denote the dual lattice. Every $`\xi\in\Lambda^*\setminus\{0\}`$ is a zero of the Fourier transform $`\widehat{\mathbf1_A}`$, since
```math
\widehat{\mathbf1_A}(\xi)
=\int_T q_1(t)e^{-2\pi i\xi\cdot t}\,dt=0.
```
The equality holds because lattice translates have phase $`1`$; the vanishing is orthogonality of a nontrivial character to constants on a fundamental cell. The conclusion holds for every rotation of the lattice. Hence $`\widehat{\mathbf1_A}`$ vanishes on the full circle with radius equal to the length of any nonzero vector of the original dual lattice.

The squared dual lengths include
```math
\frac{a^2-ab+b^2}{3}\quad(a,b\in\mathbb Z).
```
With $`b=2c`$, $`a=v+c`$, the numerator becomes $`v^2+3c^2`$. For each integer $`v\ge1`$, take $`0\le c\le\lfloor\sqrt{v/3}\rfloor`$. The resulting numerators are distinct within that row, and lie in $`[v^2,(v+1)^2)`$, so they are distinct across rows as well. The first $`N`$ rows yield $`\Omega(N^{3/2})`$ distinct positive radii at most $`N`$, since $`\sqrt{(N^2+N)/3}\le N`$.

Consider the entire function
```math
F(z)=\int_A e^{-2\pi i z x_1}\,dx.
```
Compact support gives $`|F(z)|\le |A|\exp(2\pi R|z|)`$ for some finite $`R`$, and $`F(0)=|A|>0`$. Jensen's formula yields $`O_A(N)`$ zeros in the disk $`|z|\le N`$: on a radius $`r\in[2N,3N]`$ avoiding the zeros, each such zero contributes at least $`\log 2`$, while the average logarithmic boundary growth above $`\log|F(0)|`$ is at most $`6\pi RN`$. But each dual radius above is a distinct positive zero of $`F`$, giving $`\Omega(N^{3/2})`$ zeros, a contradiction. Therefore no compact admissible set attains the packing-density upper bound. The attainment part gives the asserted strict inequality for the supremum.

### Scope

Attainment and strictness do not determine the exact optimum. The result is consistent with $`V_{2,k}/(k-1)\to2\sqrt3`$. Tight finite-$`k`$ bounds and extremizer geometry remain open here. No external review or priority claim is made.
