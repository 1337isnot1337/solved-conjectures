# A residue-class obstruction for two fixed affine forms

Fix an integer $Q\ge1$ and two units $a_R,a_B$ modulo $Q$. Partition the primes up to $X$ not dividing $Q$ into two colours. Count integers up to $X$ having only red prime factors and congruent to $a_R$, and integers having only blue prime factors and congruent to $a_B$. **Uniformly over all partitions, the sum of these two counts divided by $X/\log X$ tends to infinity.** Each partition may change with $X$. For $Q=1$ the [monochromatic factor theorem](monochromatic-factors.md) gives an explicit sharp rate.

## Proof

Write $G=(\mathbb Z/Q\mathbb Z)^\times$. Suppose a sequence of partitions keeps the normalized sum bounded. Take a diagonal subsequence on which every fixed prime eventually has a fixed colour. For each colour take the subgroup of $G$ generated by the classes of its limiting primes. Their union is $G$, since every reduced class contains a prime. A group cannot be the union of two proper subgroups: the product of an element exclusive to each lies in neither. Thus at least one subgroup is all of $G$. Call that colour blue.

There is a fixed $K$ such that every class $g\in G$ can be changed into $a_B$ by multiplying by an integer at most $K$ made entirely of limiting blue primes. Indeed, the semigroup generated by any subset of a finite group is a group, allowing the empty product. The finitely many multiplier primes are blue throughout the tail of the subsequence. Classify blue-only integers up to $T=X/K$ by their residue and apply the corresponding multiplication. Within each class it is injective. Our contradiction hypothesis therefore implies
```math
 \Psi(T;B)\ll X/\log X .
```
Here and below $\Psi$ counts products of primes from the specified pool, so all integers counted are coprime to $Q$.

If both limiting subgroups equal $G$, apply the same argument to red, increasing $K$ to work for both colours. This contradicts the [lower bound for unrestricted residue counts](monochromatic-factors.md) at $T$. Removing the fixed primes dividing $Q$ changes that proof only by bounded Euler factors: the reciprocal sums in both high-prime intervals are unchanged, and Mertens' product over primes coprime to $Q$ is comparable to $1/\log T$.

It remains to treat a proper limiting red subgroup $H$. Every fixed prime in any unit class outside $H$ is limiting blue. By Mertens' theorem in a fixed progression and pointwise stabilization,
```math
 W_B(T)=\prod_{p\le T,\;p\in B}(1-1/p)^{-1}\longrightarrow\infty .
```
The analogous $W_R(T)$ must also tend to infinity. Otherwise pass to a subsequence with $W_R(T)\le M$. Take a fixed $v$ so large that $\log v>3+\log M$. Blue then accounts for more than $3/2$ of the reciprocal sum on $(T^{1/v},T]$. [Matomäki–Shao, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.02371), applied with $u=1$, bounds $\Psi(T;B)$ below by a positive constant times $T/W_R(T)$, with a fixed additional factor for the primes dividing $Q$. This contradicts the bound at $T$.

For every fixed $0<d<1/4$ we now have, eventually,
```math
 \sum_{X^d\lt p\le X^{1/4},\;p\in B}1/p < 2 .
```
Otherwise apply the same sieve theorem at $T$ with fixed $v>2/d$. Its lower bound is now $\gg_{d,Q}TW_B(T)/\log T$, again a contradiction.

Choose fixed exponents $0<d_1<d_2<d_3<d_4<1/4$ so that both $\log(d_2/d_1)$ and $\log(d_4/d_3)$ exceed $6|G|$. On each of the prime intervals $I=(X^{d_1},X^{d_2}]$ and $J=(X^{d_3},X^{d_4}]$, each unit residue class supplies more than $3$ in **red** reciprocal prime mass for large $X$. This follows from Mertens' theorem in that progression and the uniform bound $2$ on the blue contribution.

Take red-only integers $m\le X^{1/4}$ supported on primes up to $X^{d_0}$, where $0<d_0<\min(d_1,1/100)$. Their reciprocal sum tends to infinity. The unrestricted Euler product on these small red primes diverges along the subsequence, since the omitted primes between $X^{d_0}$ and $T$ have bounded reciprocal sum. Under its normalized weights proportional to $1/m$, the prime exponents are independent geometric variables, giving
```math
 \mathbb E\log m\le\sum_{p\le X^{d_0}}\frac{\log p}{p-1}
 \le 2d_0\log X .
```
Markov's inequality shows that at least half of the reciprocal weight satisfies the specified size bound.

For each such $m$ take a red prime $q\in I$ of residue $a_Rm^{-1}$. At $t=X/(mq)\ge X^{1/2}$ apply Matomäki–Shao to the pool of red primes in $J$ of residue one. It has reciprocal sum greater than $3$ and lies inside $(t^{1/v},t]$ for any fixed $v>2/d_3$. Consequently there are $\gg_Q t/\log X$ products $l\le t$ of primes from this pool. Every $mql$ is red-only in the target residue $a_R$. Its three factors are recovered using their disjoint prime ranges. Summing over $q$ gives $\gg_Q X/(m\log X)$ products for each $m$, and summing over $m$ contradicts the assumed bound.

## Application to two covers

Suppose the classes of each colour $C$ are the roots of a fixed primitive affine form $h_C n+b_C$ with $h_C>0$. Allow arbitrary classes at finitely many primes, including those dividing either leading coefficient. The forms for the two colours may differ. Even after arbitrary additional changes at primes above $\delta X$ for fixed $\delta>0$, such systems cannot witness [Erdős problem 467](https://www.erdosproblems.com/467) for all sufficiently large $X$.

Indeed, restrict to a progression $n=Fk+r$ avoiding all exceptional classes, where $F$ is the product of the fixed exceptional primes. Colour and class assignments on this list have only finitely many possibilities. Divide each restricted form by the gcd of its two coefficients. This produces a primitive affine form in $k$, and all prime factors removed divide $F$, since the original form was primitive. Use a common modulus $Q$ divisible by both new leading coefficients and $F$. The constant term of each new form is a unit modulo its leading coefficient, so it extends to some unit class modulo $Q$. Positive integers in that class, up to a sufficiently small fixed multiple of $X$, correspond to points of the target progression, apart from boundedly many exceptions at its start.

If all prime factors of such an integer have the opposite colour to the form, the point misses that form's colour. Apply the theorem with the residues exchanged to count the two kinds of missing hits. There are more than any fixed multiple of $X/\log X$ for large $X$, while the additional primes above $\delta X$ can contribute only $O_\delta(X/\log X)$ hits. Arbitrary assignments at intermediate primes are still allowed in the original question.
