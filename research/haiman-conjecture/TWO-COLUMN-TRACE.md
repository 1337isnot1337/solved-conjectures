# Haiman’s conjecture

**Partial research.** The formula below gives the generic-parameter monomial Hecke character for every partition with at most two columns and every permutation. It establishes Haiman’s positivity and unimodality in that entire shape family. Partitions with three or more columns remain outside this argument. [Tracking issue #500](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/500)

## Target and attribution

Haiman’s Conjecture 2.1 asks whether $\phi_q^\lambda(q^{\ell(w)/2}C'_w)$ is a polynomial with nonnegative integer coefficients, symmetric about $\ell(w)/2$ and unimodal, for every $n$, $w\in S_n$ and partition $\lambda\vdash n$. Haiman already proves symmetry for all virtual characters. The November 24, 2026 primary-source screen found McNulty’s general computation through $n=10$ and hook result at $q=1$. The two-column case at $q=1$ is established by Clearman, Shelton and Skandera’s Temperley–Lieb expansions with Rhoades and Skandera’s identification of the corresponding immanants. The generic-parameter formula below applies the established annular trace method to Haiman’s monomial character and keeps track of the sign-induced weight spaces. The bounded search located no earlier statement of this exact formula for all two-column shapes and permutations, and no resolution of Haiman’s unrestricted conjecture. Priority for the formula is provisional. [Haiman, Conjecture 2.1](https://math.berkeley.edu/~mhaiman/ftp/heckeimm/jams1.pdf) · [McNulty, graded check through $n=10$](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41555v2) · [McNulty, hooks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39021v3) · [Clearman, Shelton and Skandera, Theorem 5.6](https://combinatorics.cis.strath.ac.uk/fpsac2011/proceedings/local.pdf) · [Rhoades and Skandera, Proposition 3.2](https://mathweb.ucsd.edu/~bprhoades/klimm.ps.pdf) · [Dubail, Jacobsen and Saleur, annular loop trace](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.2746)

## Formula

Set $v=q^{1/2}$. If $w$ avoids $321$, let $D_w$ be its monomial Temperley–Lieb basis diagram. Close the top of the diagram to its bottom by matching corresponding boundary points on a cylinder. Write $c$ for the number of contractible components and $r$ for the number of components that wind around the cylinder. Then

```math
\phi_q^{(2^a,1^{n-2a})}(q^{\ell(w)/2} C'_w)
 =
 \begin{cases}
 q^{(\ell(w)-c)/2}(1+q)^c & a=(n-r)/2,\\
 0 & \text{otherwise}.
 \end{cases}
```

For a permutation containing $321$, all its two-column values vanish.

## Proof

**The spin representation.** Use the generic Hecke normalization $(T_i-q)(T_i+1)=0$ and $C'_{s_i}=v^{-1}(1+T_i)$. The Temperley–Lieb representation on words in $0,1$ uses the cup vector $v^{1/2}|01\rangle+v^{-1/2}|10\rangle$ and its transpose as the cap. The two elementary bends that cancel each other contribute $v^{1/2}v^{-1/2}=1$ for either spin, and a circle contributes $\delta=v+v^{-1}$. In particular the cup-cap operator $e_i$ satisfies the planar Temperley–Lieb relations with parameter $\delta$. The Hecke quotient sends $T_i$ to $v e_i-1$.

Let $V_j$ be the span of the words with $j$ ones. Its Hecke character is the induced sign character $\epsilon_q^{(n-j,j)}$. In fact, the sorted word $0^{n-j}1^j$ has eigenvalue $-1$ on every simple generator of the two-block parabolic subgroup. Within an adjacent unequal pair the off-diagonal entry of $T_i$ is $v$, so the submodule generated by that word contains all adjacent swaps of opposite spins, hence every basis word of $V_j$. The induced module surjects onto $V_j$, and both dimensions are $\binom{n}{j}$. This also covers $j=0,n$ using the one-block parabolic.

**Closing a diagram.** For an ordinary basis diagram $D$ with $n$ boundary points on each side, trace in the spin basis identifies the two boundaries and requires matching spins at the seam. Each consistent assignment amounts to orienting every closed component independently. Spin $0$ points down and spin $1$ points up along the locally vertical strands. Both a cup and a cap connect opposite spins. Draw the generators with vertical strands and semicircular bends. With the horizontal axis pointing right and the vertical axis pointing up, the orientation with left spin $0$ turns by $+\pi$ at a bend, with weight $v^{1/2}$. The reverse turns by $-\pi$, with weight $v^{-1/2}$. Each orientation therefore contributes $v$ raised to the total turning number of its component in the cylinder’s horizontal and vertical frame.

The turning number is $\pm1$ for a contractible component and $0$ for a component that winds around the cylinder. One way to see this is to embed the cylinder as a planar annulus, taking the horizontal direction to the outward radial direction and the vertical direction to the direction of increasing angle. Positive changes of scale along these two axes preserve the tangent degree. The planar tangent degree equals the angle-frame degree plus the tangent degree in the cylinder frame. For a simple planar curve the planar tangent degree is $+1$ or $-1$ according to its orientation. The angle-frame degree is its winding about the origin. It is zero if the origin is outside the curve, and equals the planar tangent degree if the origin is inside. Thus a contractible component sums to $\delta$ over its two orientations, while each orientation of a winding component has weight $1$.

For a component that intersects the seam in $m$ strands, the two orientations have spin-one counts summing to $m$. Their difference is the signed intersection number with the seam, up to a fixed sign. The counts are therefore equal for a contractible component and differ by exactly one for a winding component. Summing $t^j$ over the trace terms, the factors from contractible components are $\delta t^{m/2}$, and those from winding components are $t^{(m-1)/2}(1+t)$. There are $n$ seam strands in total. Consequently

```math
\sum_{j=0}^n \epsilon_q^{(n-j,j)}(D)t^j
=\delta^c t^{(n-r)/2}(1+t)^r .
```

This evaluation includes components that meet the seam more than twice. A contractible loop formed while multiplying diagrams contributes an extra scalar $\delta$ independently of the seam.

**Extracting the monomial character.** In the symmetric-function characteristic, induced sign corresponds to $e_{n-j}e_j$. Choosing the two squarefree monomials gives

```math
e_{n-j}e_j
=\sum_{a=0}^{\min(j,n-j)}
\binom{n-2a}{j-a} m_{(2^a,1^{n-2a})}.
```

Indeed, the $a$ squared variables occur in both monomials, and $j-a$ of the remaining $n-2a$ variables must occur in the factor of degree $j$. The identity transfers to generic Hecke characters under the deformation correspondence with symmetric-group characters. Summing in $j$ shows that $\phi_q^{(2^a,1^{n-2a})}$ is the coefficient of $t^a(1+t)^{n-2a}$ in the induced-sign trace generating series. These polynomials are linearly independent by their distinct lowest degrees. In particular every two-column character factors through the Temperley–Lieb quotient, and for $D$ only the coefficient at $a=(n-r)/2$ survives, with value $\delta^c$.

**Applying the projection.** Fan and Green’s type-A projection sends $C'_w$ to the monomial diagram $D_w$ if $w$ is $321$-avoiding, and to zero otherwise. For an avoiding permutation the diagram is the product of the elementary diagrams in a reduced word of length $\ell(w)$, with no internal loop scalar. [Fan and Green, Section 3 and Theorem 3.8.2](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid%3A96a622a5-c045-44ab-a035-e723f9502e8d/files/mf793a382afd5653ab0cf5ae1ab7f4df0) · [Green and Losonczy, projection property](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/9908011)

For completeness, on multiplying the $\ell(w)$ spin matrices $v e_i$, every diagonal trace term is a product of powers of $v$ with exponents $0,1,2$. Exponent $1$ corresponds to an adjacent swap of opposite spins. Each such swap reverses the parity of the sum of the positions of the ones, so a term returning to its initial word uses an even number of swaps. Thus each normalized induced-sign trace is in $\mathbb Z[q]$. At $j=(n-r)/2$ it is exactly $v^{\ell(w)}\delta^c$. Its lowest exponent in $v$ is $\ell(w)-c$, which must be nonnegative and even. This proves the displayed formula. Its nonzero value has binomial coefficients on a contiguous range of degrees centered at $\ell(w)/2$, with zeros on either side, so it has all three desired properties.

## Audit and replay

Run `python3 research/haiman-conjecture/verify.py` from the repository root. The checker evaluates transfer matrices directly with integral Laurent coefficients and separately computes the windings from the planar matchings. It checks all 2,055 basis diagrams in ranks $1$ through $8$, an elementary square that adds an internal loop, and $w=3412$, whose coefficient for $(2,2)$ is $q(1+q)^2$ as in McNulty’s Example 2.A. The written argument handles every rank and uses the cited type-A projection theorem. The replay checks the annular trace formula and the normalization example. A second reader and a formal proof have yet to verify the argument. [McNulty, Example 2.A](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41555v2)
