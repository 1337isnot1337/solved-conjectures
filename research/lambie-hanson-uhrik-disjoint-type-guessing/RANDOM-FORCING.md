# Disjoint-type guessing after random forcing

This follows [#1083](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1083), which treats small dense forcings and arbitrary Cohen products. Here the measure replaces the small dense set. Work over a ground model of \(\mathsf{PFA}(\omega_1)\).

**Results.**
1. After arbitrary random-real forcing, the negative direction of the forward-tail width theorem holds for all named families, including prescribed indices \(j_k\ge n_k\). In fact the proof applies to any complete Boolean algebra with a strictly positive countably additive probability measure.
2. After a separable measure algebra (in particular one or countably many random reals), the ordinary depth/reach and mixed-orientation classifications also remain exact for all named families.
3. After adding any number of random reals, full disjoint-type guessing still fails. For the arbitrary-product ordinary result here, the obstruction uses a fixed family repeating each of a sequence of increasingly deep types infinitely often; no all-families claim is made for nonseparable algebras.

Use the finite-reading posets and the strong-properness proof from [#1083](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1083). Work below a nonzero Boolean element forcing the asserted ladder, type and unboundedness hypotheses. Write \(1\) for the top of this cone and normalize its measure to 1. The Boolean value of a finite reading at a vertex is its *event*; no Boolean element has to be stored in the finite record unless explicitly stated below.

## 1. Full-measure dense tests and the forward tail

For each limit \(\gamma<\omega_1\) and integer \(m\ge1\), ask that the union of events of records at \(\gamma\) in the current condition have measure greater than \(1-2^{-m}\). This is upward persistent. To prove density, start with a finite condition and let \(\eta\) be its largest cut below \(\gamma\), or zero if none. Exclude all natural colors already used by the condition. Almost surely some coordinate \(C_\gamma(l)\) is at least \(\eta\), and there is a fresh color of width greater than \(l\). Thus the possible eligible finite readings (color, type, index and full required prefix) have events whose union is 1. There are only countably many readings at one countable vertex. Choose finitely many nonzero events whose union has measure greater than \(1-2^{-m}\).

Several of these readings can use the same fresh color and tag. That is harmless for the tail poset: give **all** new records at \(\gamma\) the same threshold \(h<\gamma\), taken at least \(\eta\) and at least every ordinal in their finitely many prefixes. In particular each of their \(j_k\)-th points is at most \(h\), so no new exact flag can hit any new record. The cut guard holds by eligibility and \(h\ge\eta\). No new flag or record interacts with an old one because its natural color is fresh relative to the starting condition. This proves density with exactly the published tag-interaction and clipping rules.

The auxiliary poset still has size \(\omega_1\) and is strongly proper by the proof in #1083: finite payloads and tags are all that are copied, and event positivity is just the existence of a reading condition. Meet the \(\omega_1\) dense tests by restricted PFA. At each vertex the supremum in the Boolean algebra of the events of its records is 1. Therefore in the measure-forcing extension one can choose a record with correct readings at every vertex. Its exact flag bounds every actual same-color match at the upper vertex, as in #1083. This proves the forward-tail obstruction.

## 2. Arbitrary ordinary families in the separable case

For ordinary depth/reach tests, different readings of the same natural color at one vertex need not be mutually geometry-compatible. If the measure algebra has a countable metric-dense Boolean subalgebra \(B_0\), enlarge the tag to \((k,t,b)\), where \(b\in B_0\). A record is permitted if its finite reading event meets \(b\) in positive measure. Its event for the measure test and actual selection is this intersection. Tags interact exactly if their natural color and type agree and \(b\wedge b'\ne0\).

The strong-properness proof is unchanged, since copies preserve the enlarged tag, all members of the countable \(B_0\) belong to the countable elementary model, and positivity can be tested by the corresponding existential reading condition. All geometric tests with flags and proxies are unaffected by how tag interaction was defined.

To prove density of the same full-measure tests, first choose finitely many eligible readings using fresh natural colors relative to the starting condition, with events \(E_1,\dots,E_s\) whose union has measure strictly greater than the required threshold. Refine these to pairwise disjoint measurable sets \(A_i\subseteq E_i\) with the same union. Use metric density to approximate the \(A_i\) by elements of \(B_0\), and make the approximants \(b_i\) disjoint by subtracting earlier approximants from later ones. The total measure lost can be made arbitrarily small (a union bound on the finitely many symmetric differences suffices). Thus the union of the \(E_i\wedge b_i\) meets the desired strict measure bound. Discard zero intersections. The added records do not interact with one another because the \(b_i\)'s are disjoint, and with old records they have different natural colors. This proves density.

In the actual extension chosen records have both their reading event and their subalgebra element in the generic filter. So chosen records with the same natural color have the same actual type and interacting tags. The ordinary avoiding-color argument from #1083 applies. The mixed result combines this with part 1 and the ZFC reverse-tail positive construction.

## 3. A replicated ordinary obstruction in all random products

For arbitrary product random forcing, fix a deterministic sequence \(w_n\) of types with unbounded depths (for instance \(0^{n+1}1^{n+1}01\) for \(n<\omega\)). Use the single family indexed by \((n,i)\in\omega^2\) that assigns \(w_n\) to every \((n,i)\), with a fixed bijection to \(\omega\).

The auxiliary poset now has natural colors \((n,i)\); equality of those colors is the tag interaction. As in #1083, the names for a ladder system on \(\omega_1\) in any random product depend on at most \(\omega_1\) coordinates: use ccc antichains for each ordinal-valued reading, and the fact that each measure-algebra element is represented modulo null sets using only countably many coordinates. The coordinate subalgebra is a complete subalgebra and the extracted avoiding coloring there remains valid in the full extension. By [Dow–Steprāns](https://doi.org/10.4153/CMB-1994-007-7), the positive elements of that smaller measure algebra are the union of countably many linked sets \(L_i\). This applies because its product index has size at most \(\omega_1\le2^{\aleph_0}\) in the ground model.

Allow a finite prefix reading at \(\gamma\) for color \((n,i)\) if its full event is nonzero and belongs to \(L_i\). The strong-properness proof again copies only the finite data; membership of the corresponding Boolean value in \(L_i\) is part of its fixed reading relation. For density, exclude the finitely many first components \(n\) used in the current condition. Almost every outcome has an eligible reading for some remaining \(n\), by unbounded depth. Take finitely many such events covering the required measure. For each event choose one \(i\) witnessing membership in \(L_i\). Two distinct events for the same \(n\) at \(\gamma\) are incompatible, because they give different prefixes of a single ladder of the same fixed length. They cannot belong to the same linked set. Thus all genuinely distinct newly selected records use distinct colors \((n,i)\), and the earlier density argument applies without any geometry conflict. Recover the avoiding coloring using full-measure coverage. Because a single fixed family fails in every such extension, full disjoint-type guessing does not hold there.

The linked-family trick is specific to the replicated obstruction: enlarging the palette while preserving the assigned type must not be done for a general unrepeated family. This is why part 3 is weaker than the arbitrary-product forward-tail result. None of these conclusions decides the Hajnal–Máté graph question.
