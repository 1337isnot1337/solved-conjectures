# Independent review of the disjoint-type guessing forcing

Reviewed the author-owned draft PR #1062 at 9fede08de1cf374c1b195237eeb8bea52e853932. Its proof blob is e599b4e24fba0fb52986b347c3fb917cb880eeb7. Cinna Davis wrote the proof. This reviewer did not develop the construction. The original problem is ordinary type guessing for every uniformly bounded-depth sequence, with no uniform width bound. [Author's draft](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1062) · [Published primary source, Question 6.1](https://doi.org/10.1112/mtk.12261) · [Author preprint, Definitions 2.5–2.6 and Question 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.01828)

**Verdict.** The new forcing for unbounded second parameter, its density argument and the claimed ZFC-relative consistency pass independent mathematical review. I found no incompatible condition in the proposed amalgamation. Together with the original paper's positive model and existing unbounded-depth negative result, the displayed depth-one family gives the stated independence of Question 6.1. The coordinating reviewer independently checked the positive ZFC argument below.

* A normalized word has the last upper marker before the final nonempty lower run. Its next upper entry, indexed by the depth, gives an exact strict cutoff. Therefore disjointness from the marker tuple, the truncated merge word and that cutoff are precisely the full type test. Either orientation is covered by the exact flag of the corresponding upper ladder; it need not have the larger ordinal index. The fresh color in the density proof can be chosen with reach exceeding the finite number of ladder points below the largest smaller cut, while avoiding all finitely many used colors. Its exact flag overlaps its own ladder and cannot prohibit it. This works when a ladder point equals a cut because the guard is weak and the flag inequalities are strict.
* Take the elementary model cut δ, including all fixed data in the language. Adding δ to a condition in M entails no new requirement on its existing ordinals. A stronger condition coloring δ exists by density. For each bound in M below δ, the finite prefix of the ladder at δ belongs to M and δ itself witnesses a limit ordinal with that prefix above the bound in the universe. Elementarity supplies the desired inner proxy above the finite set of earlier cuts and colored ordinals. In particular the copy does not overwrite any earlier color. Its entire exact flag is copied since the cutoff belongs to the n-prefix. The guard for the copy at any inner cut follows from the original δ guard or an original inner proxy.
* A flag with a marker at or beyond δ cannot prohibit an inner prefix: the last marker must precede the last lower point. All other outer flags have identical effects on such prefixes after replacing any cutoff at or above δ by the sentinel ω₁. This includes a cutoff equal to δ. For an outer colored ordinal, an inner flag cannot hit the guard alternative, as hitting would place the first r points strictly below the last inner marker. In the proxy alternative an inner ordinal of the same color and the identical entire prefix tests every such flag. If the original proxy is δ itself, its strict inner copy supplies the proxy instead. An arbitrary inner extension may add the sentinel flag even though its effect differs from the old one on some outer prefixes. These same two alternatives prove compatibility with those new restrictions as well.
* For a new inner cut η and an outer ordinal, the guard at δ implies the guard at η. If the matching proxy lies at or below η it is already a witness. If it lies above η, the inner condition's guard or proxy for that ordinal at η transfers through the identical prefix. An outer cut has no constraint on an inner colored ordinal. The union is therefore a condition below both. The inner reduction need not itself be below the entire outer condition; what strong genericity requires is compatibility of every inner extension. A reduction found after strengthening an outer extension is also a reduction for the original outer extension by transitivity.
* The poset has size exactly ℵ₁: finite maps, finite ordinal cuts and finite flags give the upper bound; the singleton cut conditions give the lower bound. The family of dense sets indexed by countable limit ordinals consequently has allowable size ℵ₁. The directed filter union avoids each forbidden same-color pair because both finitely supported color decisions occur together in a common stronger condition. Extending its colors arbitrarily to the remaining ordinals supplies the map on all of ω₁ in the original definition. The published proof explicitly extends this coloring to all ordinals in the domain of Definition 2.6.

Section 5 of the original paper explicitly invokes the size-ℵ₁ form of PFA and its consistency over ZFC, referring to Shelah's Chapter VIII, Lemmas 2.4–2.5. Dobrinen, Krueger, Marun, Mota and Zapletal independently recall this consistency proof on page 2, and prove the same consistency strength even for the stronger restricted Martin's Maximum. For the other direction, Theorem 2.8 of the original paper gives strong guessing for every type sequence from club guessing, and diamond holds in the constructible model. Thus no assumption about the large-cardinal strength of full PFA is used. Original Theorem 5.2 concerns unbounded original depth; it does not already handle the draft's constant-depth-one example with unbounded lower reach. Searches of the precise phrase, original authors, title, question number, bounded depth, reach and forcing, as well as the first author's works list, located no prior resolution of that exact case. ## Positive direction and scope of Question 6.1

The second internal reviewer checked the source's definition of depth (Definition 2.1), ordinary guessing (Definition 2.6), and the nested elementary-model argument of Theorem 2.7. After orienting the type to end in 1, its depth counts the 1s preceding the last 0. The nested unbounded-existence assertion needs only one more lower point than the lower reach, regardless of the full width. The next upper marker always exists since the depth is strictly less than the width. Choosing the lower ladder ordinal below that next upper marker traps its entire remaining prefix in the final lower run. For reach zero and positive depth, the initial lower choices are empty and the same final step gives the whole lower run. For depth zero, the separated type follows by choosing the entire lower ladder below the first upper marker. Exchanging the names of the two sides is allowed in the source's ordinary guessing comparison. ## Countable-forcing preservation

Both internal reviewers checked Cinna Davis’s preservation theorem added on the author’s branch at 826f82d4. Countability puts every forcing condition in the elementary submodel. Each of the finitely many decided coordinates of a ladder at the model ordinal is an ordinal of the model. A single existential formula with these parameters therefore copies all the forcing-decision statements, including statements attached to mutually incompatible conditions, to one strictly smaller ordinal. Preserving the actual forcing condition in each tag preserves how it interacts with every other tag. The guard at the model cut handles both possible side assignments in the depth case. In the reach case the last marker of a dropped flag must precede a point of a ladder it forbids, which makes it harmless to an inner ladder.

For each pair of names one works below a condition deciding which parameter is unbounded. The dense sets for pairs of limit ordinals and conditions all belong to the ground model, and there are only ℵ₁ of them. The resulting filter supplies a ground-model dense set of tags for every limit ordinal. A generic filter meets each of these sets, so the two tags used in any same-color pair are compatible. The auxiliary filter contains a common condition deciding both records. Countable forcing preserves the first uncountable ordinal, and the argument covers newly added ladder systems and type sequences. It gives the classification in the extension and excludes the proposed two-step route to Question 6.2. The graph question itself remains open.
