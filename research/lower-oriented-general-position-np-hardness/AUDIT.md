# Scope and audit

- **Exact target:** Conjecture 5.3 in Chandran, Di Stefano, Erskine, Haritha, Thomas and Tuite, [arXiv:2604.15909v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15909v1#S5.Thmtheorem3), is a uniform NP-hardness claim for the minimum over all orientations. The proof shows NP-completeness even on planar bipartite graphs with two leaves and all other vertices cubic. It does not address the spectrum conjecture, Conjecture 4.30, or the nonconstancy conjecture, Conjecture 4.3.
- **Independent mathematical check:** Fix the two extreme endpoints, a shortest path between them, and a vertex off the path. The triple formed by the extreme endpoints and that vertex requires a shortest path of the same length going through it. The first chosen path consequently contains a distinct vertex at exactly that same distance from the source. Those two vertices and the source cannot be on a common geodesic: the source must start it and the middle vertex would have a strictly smaller source distance than the other. This proves the hard implication directly by contradiction. The proof also handles opposite directions, unreachable vertices, same-type extremes, and order two. Finite geodesic equality is tested only when both summands are finite.
- **Source check and attribution:** The conjecture paper proves the Hamilton-path-to-orientation direction (Lemma 4.9), gives nontraceable examples attaining two (Theorem 4.12), and proves hardness of a different optimization problem with the orientation fixed (Theorem 2.18). [Andrea Munaro's Theorem 23](https://pureadmin.qub.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/186487218/paper.pdf#page=12) is the exact NP-complete source problem on the two-leaf cubic planar bipartite class, not a theorem about general position. The new bridge is the converse forced by the two leaves; no graph gadget or stronger unverified version of Munaro is required.
- **Priority check, 2026-11-26:** Checked the original article, [arXiv version history](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15909), [MathDB 374234](https://mathdb.com/p/374234/the-np-hardness-conjecture-for-lower-oriented-general-positi), and subsequent-source searches by paper title, arXiv identifier, author names, statement number, oriented-geodesic definition, and the Hamilton/leaf equivalence, plus formal catalogue keywords and both branches/issue and PR queries. No earlier resolution of this exact target was found. This is a bounded search, not a claim of exhaustive priority or external confirmation.
- **Regression:** [verify.py](verify.py) tests all 8,555 labelled graphs on two through six vertices with at least two leaves, using all 511,102 orientations. For all 2,656 traceable graphs and 6,032 valid orientation certificates combined, it checks exact counts against independently enumerated Hamilton paths and tests the endpoint-geodesic condition. The proof itself is general and does not depend on the finite check. No Lean proof or external referee report is claimed.
