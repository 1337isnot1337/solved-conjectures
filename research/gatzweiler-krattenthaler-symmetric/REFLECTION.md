# Gatzweiler–Krattenthaler Conjecture 3 and singularity weight systems

**Structural partial, not a resolution.** The symmetric exponent [Conjecture 3 of Gatzweiler–Krattenthaler](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12711936/#sec4) is equivalent to a previously studied coefficient-positivity question for relaxed quasihomogeneous singularity weight systems. Their nonsymmetric Conjecture 4 and its known counterexamples are distinct. Below we prove the symmetric conjecture whenever the reduced reflected weight multiset has at most three distinct values, without a bound on multiplicities. We also give an unbounded class in both the number of distinct values and multiplicities.

## Exact equivalence and reduction to shift 1

For positive integers $m,n$, take nonnegative integers $a_i$ supported on $1,\ldots,n$ with $a_i=a_{n+1-i}$. Extend them by zero to all integers. Set $S=n+m+1$ and $b_j=a_{j-m}-a_j$. Then the product from Conjecture 3 is
```math
 \prod_{j=1}^{S-1}(1-q^j)^{b_j},
 \qquad b_{S-j}=-b_j.
```
If it is polynomial and $S/2<j<S$, then $b_j\ge0$: no other integer in the factor range is a positive multiple of $j$, so $b_j$ is the cyclotomic multiplicity of $\Phi_j(q)$. Consequently the product has the form
```math
 Q_{\mathbf v,S}(q)=\prod_{v\in\mathbf v}\frac{1-q^{S-v}}{1-q^v}
 \quad\text{with every }1\le v\lt S/2,
```
where $\mathbf v$ is a multiset and the multiplicity at $v$ is $-b_v$. The midpoint, if integral, has exponent zero.

Conversely, given any multiset $\mathbf v$ in $(0,S/2)$ of positive integer weights, choose $m=1$, $n=S-2$, and let $a_i$ count the weights $v\le\min(i,n+1-i)$. Each weight contributes an interval $[v,n+1-v]$ of unit exponents, so its shifted quotient telescopes to $(1-q^{S-v})/(1-q^v)$. Thus proving Conjecture 3 for shift 1 is equivalent to the full conjecture, and exactly to positivity under polynomiality of all such $Q_{\mathbf v,S}$. The empty multiset is trivial.

The product $q^{\sum v}Q_{\mathbf v,S}(q)$ is the weight-system polynomial $\rho_{(\mathbf v;S)}$ of [Hertling–Mase, equation (5.8)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10801-022-01138-x). Their Lemma 5.4(c) identifies polynomiality with the relaxed condition $\overline{(C2)}$. Remark 6.9 identifies examples for which the stronger condition $(C2)$ fails despite polynomiality and records no coefficient-negativity witness. Compare also [Kreuzer–Skarke, equation (2) and Lemma 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9202039). The equivalence above does not identify polynomiality with existence of an isolated singularity.

## Two sufficient families, with arbitrary multiplicity

Let $A$ be the multiset of reflected values $S-w$, with one entry per copy of each weight $w$ of $\mathbf v$. Polynomiality says, for every positive integer $d$, that the number of $A$-entries divisible by $d$ is at least the number of weight copies divisible by $d$. For $d\ge2$ this is the cyclotomic multiplicity check, and for $d=1$ it is equality.

We apply the standard semigroup condition $(C2)$: for every submultiset of the weights, at least that many entries of $A$ should lie in the additive semigroup generated by its support. This implies $Q_{\mathbf v,S}$ is a Hilbert series and therefore has nonnegative coefficients. See [Hertling–Mase, Theorems 6.1 and 6.4(b)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10801-022-01138-x), or [Gatzweiler–Levicán-Santibáñez–Yoshida, Proposition 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22226). Counts include multiplicities. Given the support of a submultiset, it is enough to check the submultiset containing every copy of each selected weight.

### At most three distinct weight values

A singleton support is immediate by divisibility. The full support always works: for every weight $w$, polynomiality gives some $S-u=Lw$ in $A$, with $L\ge1$. Then $S-w=u+(L-1)w$ belongs to the full semigroup, so every reflected entry is represented.

It remains only to check a two-value support $`\{a,b\}`$, with multiplicities $r,s$, when there is a third weight value. If the sets of $A$-entries divisible by $a$ and by $b$ are disjoint, they supply at least $r+s$ entries in the semigroup. Otherwise let $S-u$ be a common multiple, and put $g=\gcd(a,b)$.

If $g\mid S$, then also $g\mid u$. Divide the identity $`S-u\ge\mathop{\mathrm{lcm}}(a,b)`$ by $g$. In units of $g$ both
```math
 \frac{S-a}{g}\ge\frac{ab}{g^2}+\frac{u-a}{g},
 \qquad
 \frac{S-b}{g}\ge\frac{ab}{g^2}+\frac{u-b}{g}
```
strictly exceed the Frobenius threshold $(a/g)(b/g)-a/g-b/g$ for the coprime pair $(a/g,b/g)$. (If either scaled weight is 1 the semigroup assertion is immediate.) Thus the entries reflected from $a,b$ themselves supply all $r+s$ required copies. If $g\nmid S$, neither $S-a$ nor $S-b$ is divisible by $g$. Only the third weight's reflected value can supply $g$-divisible entries, and polynomiality at $d=g$ requires at least $r+s$ such copies. All are copies of the common multiple $S-u$, hence in the semigroup.

This proves $(C2)$ with at most three distinct weights, extending its standard three-coordinate scope for this particular problem to arbitrary multiplicities.

### Pairwise common divisors divide the total degree

Suppose $\gcd(a,b)\mid S$ for every two distinct weight values $a,b$ in the whole multiset. No comparability or number-of-values bound is needed. In particular, this handles arbitrarily many distinct pairwise coprime weights, and systems that become pairwise coprime after dividing the common divisor of all weights. (That divisor necessarily divides $S$ by polynomiality.)

Take any selected support $T$. Call $w\in T$ represented if its own reflected entry $S-w$ belongs to the semigroup generated by $T$. The entries reflected from represented weights already supply all their copies. If $w\in T$ is not represented, then no reflected entry $S-u$ with $u\in T$ is divisible by $w$: such an equality $S-u=Lw$ would make $S-w=u+(L-1)w$ belong to the semigroup. Thus the entries divisible by a nonrepresented weight are external to $T$ and disjoint from the copies already supplied.

Furthermore, the sets of entries divisible by different nonrepresented weights are pairwise disjoint. If $S-u$ is a common multiple for two of them, $a,b$, let $g=\gcd(a,b)$. The hypothesis $g\mid S$ gives $g\mid u$, and the Frobenius estimate in the preceding section shows both $S-a$ and $S-b$ are represented, a contradiction. Therefore by polynomiality each remaining divisibility set contributes the entire corresponding multiplicity without conflicts. This proves $(C2)$.

The two sufficient conditions leave general systems with colliding pairs whose gcd does not divide $S$ open. In particular, the known degenerate four-value systems are not examples of coefficient-negativity; no such general implication is claimed.
