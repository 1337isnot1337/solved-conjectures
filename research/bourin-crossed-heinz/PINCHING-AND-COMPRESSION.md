# A two-block pinching formulation and an obstruction to separate compression

For positive semidefinite matrices $`X,Y`$ and $`0\lt t<1`$, put $`K_t(X,Y)=X^tY^{1-t}+Y^tX^{1-t}`$. Bourin's Question 4 asks whether the sum of the $`k`$ largest singular values of $`K_t(X,Y)`$ is bounded by the sum of the $`k`$ largest eigenvalues of $`X+Y`$ at every rank. This research note gives an equivalent formulation and disproves a stronger approach to it. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.1954#page=6) · [Tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/214)

## An exact reformulation for all dimensions and ranks

For a Hermitian unitary $`J`$, the pinching $`E(Z)=(Z+JZJ)/2`$ retains the diagonal blocks in the decomposition into its two eigenspaces. The original conjecture is equivalent to the following weak majorization for every positive semidefinite $`Z`$, every such $`J`$ and every $`0\lt t<1`$:
```math
s\bigl(2 E(Z^t)E(Z^{1-t})-E(Z)\bigr)\prec_w\lambda(E(Z)).
```
In fact twice the matrix inside the left side is exactly $`K_t(Z,JZJ)`$ by expansion and spectral calculus. Also $`2E(Z)=Z+JZJ`$, proving necessity. Conversely choose $`Z=X\oplus Y`$ and $`J`$ interchanging the two summands. The pair of inputs $`Z,JZJ`$ gives $`K_t(X,Y)\oplus K_t(X,Y)`$ and $`(X+Y)\oplus(X+Y)`$. Its inequality at rank $`2k`$ is exactly the original comparison at rank $`k`$. No hypothesis that $`X`$ and $`Y`$ commute or share a spectrum is introduced.

## Removing the complementary block changes the problem

Let $`E_i`$ be positive matrices with $`\sum_iE_i=I`$, and let $`v_i\ge0`$ be scalar outcomes. Write $`M=\sum_i v_i^tE_i,\ N=\sum_i v_i^{1-t}E_i,\ H=\sum_i v_i E_i`$. The proposed stronger inequality is $`s(2MN-H)\prec_w\lambda(H)`$ for every such positive operator-valued measure. An arbitrary collection of these moments is realized by a *single* compression: take $`Z=\bigoplus_i v_iI`$ and the isometry $`W`$ whose $`i`$th block is $`E_i^{1/2}`$. Then $`W^*Z^rW=\sum_i v_i^r E_i`$. If the stronger comparison held, it would apply to both complementary blocks of every pinching and would imply the original by taking direct sums. The explicit failure below shows that blockwise domination is unavailable. The pinching conjecture compares the combined singular-value and eigenvalue lists from the two blocks.

At rank $`k`$ the stronger comparison has a finite reduction. In a violating instance let $`U,V`$ be $`k`$-column isometries achieving the largest singular-value sum, so $`\Re\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}(U^*(2MN-H)V)`$ exceeds the top eigenvalue sum of $`H`$. Compress all effects to the span of the columns of $`U,V,MU`$. It has dimension $`h\le3k`$. The linear $`H`$ term is unchanged at $`U,V`$, and so is $`\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}((MU)^*NV)`$ since $`MU`$ belongs to the retained subspace. The largest $`k`$ eigenvalue sum of the compressed $`H`$ cannot increase. This gives another violating instance. Split every effect into rank-one summands. The four moments $`(I,M,N,H)`$ then lie in the cone generated by the associated rank-one quadruples, a real space of dimension $`4h^2`$. Any positive linear combination using more generators has a nontrivial linear dependence; subtract a suitable multiple of it to eliminate one positive coefficient while keeping all nonnegative. Repeating gives at most $`4h^2\le36k^2`$ rank-one terms. This reduction concerns the separate-compression comparison. It supplies no corresponding bound for a genuine counterexample to Bourin.

## Four outcomes at exponent one sixteenth

Let $`P`$ project onto $`e_1`$ in dimension two and let $`Q`$ project onto $`(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2`$. Use the three outer effects $`Q/2,\ P/2,\ I-(P+Q)/2`$; all are positive and their sum is the identity. For each effect distribute its mass according to a scalar two-point law of mean one. Specify each law by the lower and upper endpoints of its support, respectively:
```math
(0,2^{16}),\qquad(2^{-16},2^{256}),\qquad(0,2^{256}).
```
A law on $`a,b`$ with $`a<1\lt b`$ assigns mass $`(1-a)/(b-a)`$ to $`b`$. Combine contributions at common scalar outcomes. This gives precisely four possible outcomes and $`H=I`$. All masses and moments at $`t=1/16`$ and $`1-t=15/16`$ are rational. If a function has conditional expectations $`a,b,c`$ in the three laws, its matrix moment has diagonal entries $`(a+2b+c)/4,(a+3c)/4`$ and both off-diagonal entries equal $`(a-c)/4`$.

Apply the formula to the two powers and form $`w=(2MN-I)e_2`$. Exact substitution gives $`w_1>1/16`$, $`w_2\lt-99999/100000`$, and $`\lVert w\rVert^2>1+1/300`$. Thus the separate-compression comparison fails already in operator norm. Tian and Xu have proved the original operator-norm case in all dimensions. [Exact rational substitution](verify_pinch_obstruction.py) · [Tian and Xu, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22363v2)

A bounded search for the crossed-Heinz sum with pinching, involutions, unitary equivalence, compressions and operator-valued moments found the published original question, two-dimensional and local results, and other types of matrix pinching and covariance inequalities. The elementary pinching identity and finite-dimensional cone reduction are provided as research tools, with no absolute priority claim. This note resolves the separate-compression obstruction alone. The all-rank inequality for the two complementary blocks is open.
