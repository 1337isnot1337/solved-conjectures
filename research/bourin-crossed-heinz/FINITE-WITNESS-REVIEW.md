# Review of the fixed-rank computation theorem

An independent repository reviewer checked both proofs. The common spectral coordinate increases with each input eigenvalue. Rounding downward keeps the positive comparison sum below its original value and controls the two powers even at exponents near zero. For each rounded matrix separately, the retained spectral pieces of the two test frames are invariant under its compression. This verifies the two products against exactly the same frames. Interlacing gives the required inequality for the positive sum. [Dimension and margin bounds](FINITE-WITNESS.md)

The reviewer also checked the expansion around the left contraction, the scalar derivative bounds at singular inputs, the adjoint symmetry and the interior exponent grid. In the finite existential test, the largest singular-value sum is maximized over the two frames and the comparison sum is minimized over the scalar threshold and its positive matrix slack. Both extrema are attained. This gives computability for each prescribed rank and accuracy, across all matrix dimensions and exponents. Exact universal vanishing remains the original open problem. There is no Lean formalization or external referee report. [Computation theorem](EFFECTIVE-GAP.md)

The primary source is Bourin's Question 4. Kittaneh and Ricard proved the all-norm inequality on the central exponent interval. Sababheh and Bourin established the all-exponent two-dimensional case and related higher-dimensional results. Tian and Xu proved particular norms in arbitrary dimensions. The bounded comparison covered those sources and searches for crossed-Heinz finite witnesses, dimension bounds, joint spectral rounding, exponent discretization and computability. It located no prior fixed-rank approximation over all dimensions and exponents. The algorithm uses established real-algebraic decision procedures. [Bourin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0805.1954#page=6) · [Kittaneh and Ricard](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0024379525000540) · [Sababheh and Bourin, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04567v2) · [Tian and Xu, v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22363v2) · [Basu, Pollack and Roy](https://projects.csail.mit.edu/jacm/References/basupr1996%3A1002.html)

The two standard-library scripts check 12 exact compressed-frame tests, 108 noncommuting endpoint identities, 54 eigenvalue-slack comparisons and five rational grid error budgets. These finite checks exercise the algebra and the error allocation. The proof supplies the uniform estimates and the existence of the terminating algorithm.
