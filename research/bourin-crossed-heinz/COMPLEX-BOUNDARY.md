# A sharp obstruction to extending the crossed family by three lines

For positive definite $`A,B`$ put $`C_z=A^z B^{1-z}+B^z A^{1-z}`$. At every nonzero $`y\in\mathbb R`$ the least constant that works uniformly over all positive definite pairs and all unitarily invariant norms in
```math
\lVert C_{iy}\rVert_u\le K\lVert A+B\rVert_u
```
is $`K=\sqrt2`$. The same constant is optimal separately for every Ky Fan rank, allowing dimension to vary. Every fixed Schatten norm of exponent strictly larger than one also fails the boundary estimate with constant one. The trace norm satisfies it. This is an obstruction to imposing a contractive boundary line on this specific holomorphic family in an unweighted three-lines proof of Bourin's real-exponent inequality. The original real problem stays open.

Write $`U=A^{iy}B^{-iy}`$. Then $`C_{iy}=UB+U^*A`$. The row operator formed from $`U,U^*`$ has norm $`\sqrt2`$, and the column formed from $`B,A`$ has singular values equal to the eigenvalues of $`(A^2+B^2)^{1/2}`$. Applying the ideal inequality and the Ando–Zhan square-root inequality, also covered by Bourin–Uchiyama's concave subadditivity, to the square root on $`A^2,B^2`$ proves the bound. For the trace norm the triangle inequality gives $`\lVert A\rVert_1+\lVert B\rVert_1=\lVert A+B\rVert_1`$. [Bourin and Uchiyama, Theorem 1.1 and the square-root case on page 2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0702475)

For sharpness take three unit vectors $`u,v,w`$ with all pairwise real inner products equal to $`c\in[0,1)`$. Their Gram matrix is positive definite. Let $`J_A`$ be the unitary involution fixing $`u`$ and exchanging $`v,w`$. Let $`J_B`$ fix $`v`$ and exchange $`u,w`$. Fix $`y\ne0`$ and take $`R=\exp((2m+1)\pi/\lvert y\rvert)`$ with $`m\to\infty`$. Specify $`A`$ on the orthogonal decomposition into the line of $`u`$, the negative eigenspace of $`J_A`$, and its remaining positive eigenspace. Give these three lines eigenvalues $`1,R^{-1},R^{-2}`$ respectively. Define $`B`$ similarly at $`v,J_B`$. Then $`A^{iy}=J_A`$, $`B^{iy}=J_B`$, while $`A,B`$ tend to $`uu^*,vv^*`$. Consequently
```math
C_{iy}\longrightarrow w(u+v)^*,\qquad A+B\longrightarrow uu^*+vv^*.
```
The first limit has sole nonzero singular value $`\sqrt{2+2c}`$. The positive eigenvalues of the second are $`1+c,1-c`$. Taking $`c=0`$ attains the limiting factor $`\sqrt2`$ for the operator norm. Taking the orthogonal direct sum of $`k`$ identical copies does the same for rank $`k`$. For any fixed $`1\lt p\lt\infty`$, write $`c=1-s`$. The numerator is $`2-s/2+O(s^2)`$, while the Schatten comparison norm is $`((2-s)^p+s^p)^{1/p}=2-s+o(s)`$. Hence a positive definite finite member violates the constant-one bound at that fixed imaginary exponent. The real endpoint $`z=0`$ gives equality under the ordinary zeroth-power convention.

The complex-parameter trace inequality of Bottazzi, Elencwajg, Larotonda and Varela applies to a related four-factor trace in a narrower strip. Kittaneh and Ricard proved the real-exponent central interval using complex analysis. Neither statement asserts a constant-one bound for the full crossed family on the imaginary axis. The upper estimate here uses established matrix concave subadditivity. A bounded search of these primary comparisons and the two recent crossed-Heinz preprints did not locate the fixed-nonzero-height sharpness construction. No claim about optimal real-exponent constants outside the known interval is made.
