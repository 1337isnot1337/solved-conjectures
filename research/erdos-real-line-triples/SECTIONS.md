# Sectionwise smallness without global regularity

This gives a different full-parameter positive class for [Erdős #70](https://www.erdosproblems.com/70), not a general solution. It does not require that the colouring itself have the Baire property. It does not even require a hypothesis excluding blue finite cliques.

**Theorem.** Let P⊆ℝ be nonempty and perfect. Suppose for each x∈P outside a fixed meagre exceptional set, the blue link B_x={ (y,z)∈P² : x,y,z are distinct and {x,y,z} is blue } is meagre in P². Then there is a red-homogeneous set X⊆P meeting every nonempty relatively open subset of P in ℵ₁ points. Thus X contains every countable linear order.

**Proof.** For each good x choose a meagre Borel hull M_x⊇B_x in P². By Kuratowski–Ulam there is a meagre set E_x⊆P such that for y∉E_x the section {z:(y,z)∈M_x} is meagre. Recursively choose distinct x_ξ, ξ<ω₁, arranging that every nonempty basic open subset of P is the designated target at ℵ₁ stages. At stage ξ avoid the fixed exceptional set; the previous points; E_{x_η} for all η<ξ; and the sections (M_{x_η})_{x_ζ} for all η<ζ<ξ. These sections are meagre by the earlier exclusions. A countable union of meagre sets cannot cover the designated nonempty open subset of the perfect Polish space P. All triples in the final set are red because, at their latest stage, the blue section for the first two vertices was excluded. Finally any nonempty perfect subset of the real line has an ordered Cantor subset, which supplies countably many pairwise disjoint relatively open intervals in the order of ℚ. Choose one vertex of X in each interval. To construct these intervals directly in P, enumerate ℚ and ensure an open interval meeting P is still available in every gap between the finitely many intervals already chosen. Each available interval meets P in infinitely many points, so it splits into three ordered subintervals meeting P to provide a selected interval and two reserves. This yields a copy of ℚ inside X and hence the conclusion.

The proof also works on P for measurable null hulls and Fubini instead of category, with a nonatomic probability measure of full support and measurable null hulls; it yields ℵ₁ points of X in each nonempty open subset by the same recursion. These are hypotheses on the sections, not assumptions of category or measure on the whole relation.

**Separation from the global Baire-property criterion.** There is a blue hypergraph on ℝ with pairwise disjoint edges that is nonmeagre as a subset of the space of triples. Enumerate all meagre Fσ subsets of the triple space in length at most c. Recursively choose a blue triple outside the enumerated meagre set with all coordinates unused. At each stage fewer than c vertices have been used, and the complement of the enumerated set contains three-coordinate tuples with unused vertices: in a product of three disjoint open intervals, repeatedly use Kuratowski–Ulam to choose each coordinate from a comeagre set, which has cardinal c. The resulting relation cannot have the Baire property: all its links are finite, so the property of Baire and Kuratowski–Ulam would make it meagre. This example lies in the new sectionwise class, although not in the global regularity class. It is not a counterexample to the partition problem.
