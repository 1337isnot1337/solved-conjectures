# Erdős’s real-line triples problem

[Erdős 1987, Problem 3, pp. 223–224](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-28.pdf) and [Erdős #70](https://www.erdosproblems.com/70): for each countable ordinal β and finite n≥2, must every red/blue colouring of the triples of the **usual real line** give a red set of inherited order type β or a blue n-set? This note establishes restrictions on a counterexample, not a solution.

## Exact reading and literature

[Erdős–Rado 1956, Theorem 31](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9904-1956-10036-5) proves the cited starting result for uncountable orders containing neither ω₁ nor its reverse; the real line satisfies this hypothesis. The [problem wiki](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/problems/set_theory/E0070) discusses the meaning of the original notation. [Jones 2000](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v7i1r24/pdf/) supplies a shorter proof, records his thesis result for all finite n and β<ω+ω, and poses the exact real-line ω+ω versus 4 relation as Question 2. His Theorem 2, which allows **infinite** blue targets, is not a counterexample to #70. In contrast, [Jones 2018](https://doi.org/10.1090/proc/13503) proves a strong triple relation on the **ordinal** ω₁. There is no order embedding of ω₁ in ℝ, so that result does not transfer by restriction to the domain of #70. A report reading the domain as the initial ordinal of the cardinality of ℝ is treating a different statement. Title, exact-formula and bibliography searches did not locate a general solution of the usual-order statement.

## Regularity obstruction: every closed perfect trace must fail Baire measurability

Suppose a colouring with no blue n-set has the Baire property on [P]³ for some nonempty perfect P⊆ℝ. Choose an ordered Cantor subset of P *after* a category thinning: Mycielski's theorem first gives a nonempty perfect subset on which the colouring is relatively open, since it differs from a relatively open colouring on a meagre relation; then a Cantor subset inherits openness. Represent that subset by the lexicographically ordered branches of a binary tree. For an increasing triple there are two shapes, according as its two leftmost elements or its two rightmost elements share the deeper splitting node. The shape map is continuous on distinct triples. Refine the original colour by shape, and apply [Blass's perfect-set partition theorem](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1981-0609665-0) to these four open colour classes. There is a perfect restriction with at most 2!=2 refined colours.

Each shape occurs in every nonempty perfect Cantor subspace. In fact, for the left-pair shape follow an infinite branch taking the left child at each splitting node, extracting a side point in the right child at each of the first n splits. Every triple from these n side points has the left-pair shape: the rightmost side point split off first. For the right-pair shape follow right children and extract left side points instead. Both shapes must therefore survive the Blass reduction, leaving exactly one colour per shape, and neither can be blue. The perfect restriction is red. It contains a copy of every countable ordinal: the countable set of binary sequences with finitely many 1s and at least one 1 is densely ordered without endpoints in the lexicographic order, hence isomorphic to ℚ, into which every countable linear order embeds. Every nonempty perfect subset of the original line has an ordered Cantor subset.

Thus a counterexample has no perfect restriction whose blue set has the Baire property. Blass also discusses thinning Lebesgue-measurable relations, giving a red perfect set for that regularity on the whole real line. These are consequences of classical results, not independent priority claims.

## Finite pair-pattern obstruction

More generally, let a finite vertex label and a finite pair label determine a triple's colour from the labels on its three increasing vertices and three internal pairs, even if the labels are otherwise arbitrary. There is an uncountable vertex-label class X, and every countable partition of X has a class containing an increasing ω: a subset of ℝ without an increasing ω is reverse well-ordered, hence countable. The [Baumgartner–Hajnal theorem](https://doi.org/10.4064/fm-78-3-193-203) then yields a pair-label homogeneous subset of X of any prescribed countable ordinal type, in particular max(β,n). On that set the triple colour is constant. If it is blue, we have a blue n-set; otherwise we have the desired red type.

For instance, any tournament-cycle construction (blue exactly when the three vertices form a directed cycle), or one combining finitely many tournaments by a Boolean rule, belongs to this framework. No finite pair-pattern construction can refute the problem, even when the pair labels are not definable or measurable. The same proof applies if labels take values in a compact metric space, provided that a uniform δ>0 exists such that for each point p all triples of pair-labels in the δ-ball of p give one constant colour. Use a finite cover by such balls to produce the finite pair labelling. Arbitrary countably many pair labels need not meet this hypothesis.
