# Review of the atom-cloud obstruction

**Independently reviewed written partial theorem, November 27, 2026.** [Erdős, 1987, p. 168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2) asks about a positive pinned-distance gap at one third under site-centered circle capacity three. The [new obstruction](ATOMIC-CHILDREN.md) concerns rescalings of entire shrinking atom clouds in a hypothetical near-extremal sequence under an additional fixed global quadratic bound on noncollinear isosceles trapezoids. It does not resolve either the global-bound gap or the full question. [Original tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/969)

The coordinating Codex agent independently audited the manuscript and the specific Appendix C carrier arguments it uses. The review verified that each single base has its independent site marginal after conditioning on a positive-mass pin atom. It checked the use of the limiting parent radius, the exclusion of three bases inside one diffuse child, and the resulting factor of two in the mass of companion atoms. It verified the finite bisector cover, the exhaustion giving the reflected mass on any infinitely occupied line, and the distinction between an individual further subsequence and a simultaneous one. The expanding-disk reduction uses the full finite mass whenever anything escapes at the parent scale. Neither bounded support nor a diameter is required for the conclusion on all but finitely many atoms of an infinite support.

For a diameter attained between positive-mass atoms, the reviewer checked the middle-neighbor argument, positivity of the diameter pairing mass, the finite cutoff in endpoint labels before invoking diffuse projections, and approximation by a symmetric continuous weight with errors relative to that fixed positive mass. Equal radii give exact base-label symmetry of the finite weight. The chosen-carrier and orientation restrictions are the same as in the published one-node proof. A wholly nonatomic root and a continuing hierarchy of atomic limits remain possible. The result has no Lean verification or external refereeing.

**Source and duplicate check.** Both permanent branches and the existing tracker were searched for atom-cloud rescalings, weak limits, heavy reflected partners, bisectors, pinned radius classes and diameter graphs. The previous [atomic-limit theorem](ATOMIC-LIMITS.md) rules out mixed diffuse and atomic mass at a single scale and proves the singular-side criterion. The present proof relates different scales by the mass that must accompany an atom on every parent circle and by the carrier comparison for a common diameter radius. The elementary diameter-neighbor geometry is classical; see Erdős's finite diameter theorem and the middle-neighbor argument in Kynčl's geometric-graph notes. The inspected bisector-energy and continuum projection results concern different hypotheses and conclusions. No original claim is made for the classical diameter graph structure. The [standard-library checker](check_atomic_children.py) exhausts 66,418 diameter graphs from subsets of a 4-by-4 grid, a rational unit-arc fixture and four odd regular polygons. It checks 560 branching neighbor triples, seven minimum-degree-two supports, 3,370 eligible weighted pentagon targets, and 12,245 persistent-mirror tests on a line. These calculations support the local geometric and counting steps in the written proof.
