# Recovering a full-mass scale tree without a local triangle estimate

**Research candidate, recursive argument.** Assume the hypotheses and the [one-node radial profile decomposition](RADIAL-PROFILES.md). The following uses internally near-extremal nodes: the uniform ambient law is restricted to a positive limiting fraction of sites, and all but a vanishing fraction of internal ordered pin–base pairs have all three radius-class members in the node. The root has this property, and a fixed node inherits a finite global quadratic coefficient when counted relative to its own size.

## Children and their total mass

Fix $`1/2<b<1`$, for instance $`b=3/4`$. At every node translate and dilate a smallest disk containing at least $`b`$ times the node order to the closed unit disk; take its center for the radial decomposition. For a node of positive limiting relative mass the order tends to infinity and the required disk radius is positive eventually. No disk of radius strictly smaller than one in these coordinates contains the requisite $`\lceil b n_{\rm node}\rceil`$ sites.

Use all the radial classes of the one-node theorem. Each atom at a **positive finite** chart coordinate is a child, used only for that class. The chart origin represents all smaller profiles, which already have their own charts, and is not an additional child. Infinity is treated likewise. A wholly nonatomic positive finite part, if present, is the terminal part of its class. Class memberships have total conditional probability one, so these children and the optional terminal part account for the whole node. In particular no mass at larger scales is discarded.

Pass to a further subsequence on which each class scale in the normalized parent coordinates has a limit in $`[0,\infty]`$. For a class with bounded scale, a shrinking chart disk at any of its nonzero atoms is eventually smaller than the unit disk in the original node coordinates. The mass of that single child is therefore at most $`b`$ times the node mass. For a class with scale tending to infinity, a compact chart neighborhood of a nonzero atom lies entirely outside the unit disk, so its relative mass is at most $`1-b<b`$. This is uniform over all nodes without a uniform global quadratic coefficient.

In the finite sets choose shrinking chart disks at the positive atoms so that each disk and its triple-radius enlargement capture the same limiting atom mass. To make siblings disjoint, at each finite stage use small disjoint closed neighborhoods of the first finitely many specified atoms within one chart; across different chart scales, their nonzero finite annuli are disjoint for sufficiently large indices. Diagonalize through increasing finite stages, shrinking the radii to zero slowly. This gives membership and chart flags on the finite sets with the prescribed limits. The mass not in the limiting union of children is precisely the nonatomic terminal mass. One need not declare a finite terminal site to be in a separate internally closed node.

A good internal pin–base pair inside a child has its two node mates in the enlarged disk; its annulus has $`o(n_{\rm node})`$ sites, and the asymptotically uniform marginal of each label shows that the probability of a lost mate is $`o(1)`$. Each fixed child has positive conditional mass. It inherits internal near-extremality. Conditioning a uniform ordered parent full class on the pin and first base lying in that child agrees in total variation, up to $`o(1)`$, with its internal law: the good internal pairs are asymptotically all ordered child pairs, and once those two distinct sites are fixed their two other class members are determined up to their order.

## Which sample laws separate

There are at most countably many nodes. Extract data with ancestors before descendants on nested subsequences; an enumeration can dovetail countably many children and depths by increasing sum of positive child indices plus depth. A final diagonal preserves all earlier finite indicator and distribution limits. Record each node membership, all its countably many chart coordinates and bin ranks when visiting, and fixed dummy values elsewhere. Include any desired compact site attributes such as global empirical ranks. The resulting countable product is compact metrizable. Uniform independent sites give the product of the limiting one-site laws. At each fixed node its internal full-quadruple law has the conditional one-site law in every coordinate and the conditional product law for the pin and any individual base. These conclusions follow from the physical subset membership and the total variation comparisons at that node, independent of the size of its global quadratic coefficient.

For two independent sites, the probability that they continue together through $`k`$ atom children is at most $`b^k`$. Indeed at each common node the conditional chance of continuing into a common child is the sum of the squared relative child masses, at most $`b`$ since their sum is at most one and each is at most $`b`$. If both terminate in the diffuse part, their coordinates in that class are distinct almost surely. If they belong to different classes, the chart of the larger makes their coordinates zero and positive finite respectively. If they land at distinct positive atoms of a common class, that chart already separates them. The same chart comparison covers separation involving a terminal site. Consequently almost every independent pair separates at finite depth with two distinct finite coordinates in one chart. All charts employ translations and positive dilations only, so the sign of each directional linear form away from its zero is read from this coordinate difference. For every fixed finite independent sample, all its pairs separate at finite depths almost surely by a finite union bound.

A law of a pair absolutely continuous with respect to the independent product also separates almost surely. In a good quadruple this applies to each pin–base pair; base–base pairs can remain singular and one cannot assert simultaneous separation of all six pairs. The two-null-side criterion controls them separately. For a terminal statement based on a nonatomic site measure, each internal quadruple label shares the corresponding conditional one-site marginal. This permits four-label probability estimates by a union bound; it does not imply independence of all four labels.

## Empirical ranks are measurable functions of the geometric path

Choose a rotation of the eight sectors of aperture $`\pi/4`$ so that no boundary or its opposite is charged by the recovered direction of an independent ordered pair and no finite pair in the sequence lies on a boundary. Only countably many rotations are excluded: the limiting direction law has countably many atoms and the sequence has countably many finite directions. Each sector is an intersection of two oriented half-planes; let $`\ell`$ be either of their defining linear forms. Append the global empirical rank $`U_{\ell,n}`$ of $`\ell(y)`$, divided by the root order, to each site. It has a uniform limiting marginal on $`[0,1]`$. Write $`\pi`$ for its geometric path, $`\rho`$ for the path law, and $`v(\pi,\pi')`$ for the direction from the independent second site's location toward the first as recovered at their first separation. For an independent copy, on the product law,
```math
 \mathbf1_{U'_{\ell}<U_{\ell}}=\mathbf1_{\ell(v(\pi,\pi'))>0}\quad\text{almost surely}.
```
Indeed finite comparisons agree identically, limiting independent ranks do not tie, and the directional projection is nonzero by the rotation choice. Conditioning on the first augmented path and ranks therefore gives
```math
 U_{\ell}=\int\mathbf1_{\ell(v(\pi,\xi))>0}\,d\rho(\xi)\quad\text{almost surely}.
```
This applies to both defining forms of every sector. It determines the global ranks as functions of the path without using any auxiliary ranked-bin information. Their one-dimensional marginals remain atomless upon restriction to any fixed positive-mass node.

## Remaining farthest-hull step

Suppose for some arbitrarily large fixed sample sizes the limiting expected number of extreme sites in a uniform independent sample is a positive fraction of that sample size. The [chain of positive mass](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/ARBITRARY-COLLINEARITIES.md#a-chain-of-positive-mass) argument in the published arbitrary-collinearities proof only needs the independent finite-sample directions and the measurable site path, both recovered above. On a terminal nonatomic chart, separation of distinct coordinates from a positive chain concentrates a positive nonatomic site mass on a Lipschitz graph of slope strictly less than one, violating the published global-bound carrier obstruction. On an infinite sequence of children, martingale conditioning on the node filtration yields an internal node where the chain occupies nearly full conditional site mass. The recorded global ranks remain atomless under conditioning on a positive-mass node, so the terminal rank obstruction from Appendix C also applies there. For this filtration, the terminal class and its coordinate are recorded after termination; on an infinite path each node's class/atomic coordinate is determined by its next child. Every other chart at that node contributes zero or infinity as determined by the ordered class. Raw numerical bin-rank labels at dyadic boundaries are auxiliary to the decomposition and are not needed for the direction or chain filtration.

Thus for any putative sequence under the arbitrary global quadratic bound and any sample sizes $`m_n\to\infty`$, the expected number of convex-hull vertices of $`m_n`$ independent uniform sites divided by $`m_n`$ tends to zero. To handle varying sizes, if a positive fraction were attained along a subsequence then, for every fixed smaller $`m`$, the expected hull fraction of an $`m`$-sample would be at least as large eventually along that subsequence. Indeed, given a larger sample select a representative index for each distinct hull vertex and choose a uniform subsample of indices: each selected representative stays a hull vertex and survives with probability equal to the ratio of sample sizes. These smaller subsamples have the independent uniform site law, even with possible repetitions. The previous chain argument on one common extracted subsequence then gives the contradiction. This supplies a compactness reduction, not a proof of the required positive hull fraction or of the unrestricted pinned-distance gap. The compact disk model in the [atomic-limit theorem](ATOMIC-LIMITS.md) explains why just the limiting pin–base independence and equal radii do not deliver that missing estimate.
