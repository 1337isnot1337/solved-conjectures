# Independent review of the four-grid construction

I checked the construction and its proof separately from their author. The claimed obstruction concerns active pair completions under the local cap of three and the global cap of four. The unrestricted pinned-distance question remains open. [Proof](FOUR-GRIDS.md)

The four grids have sizes $`m^2`$, $`m^2`$, $`m(2m-1)`$ and $`(2m-1)^2`$. Reflection in the vertical axis with real coordinate $`-b/2`$ is independent of the first index of the pin. The rotation identity follows from $`1-\zeta=\zeta/\gamma`$. For each of the $`m`$ axes the first base uniquely labels its reflected pair. The other two base sites are in separate clusters. Every one of the $`m`$ pins counts as an active completion. Removing $`B`$ pairs on that axis and discounting $`K`$ pins from each survivor gives the stated lower bound even if the nominated pairs are chosen optimally.

For the circle caps, the single-grid circle determinant is linear in the Gram coefficients. Rational independence follows from transcendence of the parameter and the degrees zero, one and two. After putting three noncollinear indices in affine coordinate form, the vanishing requirements on the fourth have only the three original sites as solutions. In the separated-cluster argument, three fixed noncollinear sites of one cluster have bounded circumradius. Three different nonzero offsets determine the unit circle and exclude the offset zero. For the mixed triples of offsets, the circumcenter of zero and the two points at directions $`\eta\pm\delta`$ is $`e^{i\eta}/(2\cos\delta)`$. Subtracting either nonzero point gives direction $`\eta\pm2\delta`$ modulo $`\pi`$. Both vary, so finitely many bad phases suffice for all within-cluster difference vectors. Only the cluster at zero can double in this case. The generic phase and large separation can be chosen in this order for each fixed grid size.

The upper bound of four on arbitrary circles is attained. Two pairs reflected across one axis at distinct heights determine a common circumcenter on that axis, since equating the two squared radii gives a linear equation with nonzero slope. The site cap forces the center to be outside the point set. The stated distance lower bound at the second grid gives a limiting mean fraction of at least $`1/3+(5/8-1/3)/8=71/192`$. Thus quadratic excess coexists with a positive gap above one third.

I reran the [exact rational checker](check_four_grids.py) on the three specified instances. It returned sizes 23, 58 and 109, site/global circle caps 3 and 4 throughout, and vertical excesses 4, 42 and 168. These tests reproduce finite examples. The phase and separation arguments establish the arbitrarily large construction. This is an internal written review.
