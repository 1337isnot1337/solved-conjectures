# Atomic and nonatomic limits for site-centered pinned distances

**Independently reviewed written partial theorem for #969.** Let a sequence of planar sets of increasing order $`n`$ have site-centered positive-circle capacity three, global noncollinear isosceles trapezoid count (including rectangles and squares) $`Q(P)\le Cn^2`$, and mean pinned distance count $`(1/3+o(1))n`$. Every conclusion below is necessary for such a sequence. Its existence remains open. The estimates used here do not assume hereditary trapezoid or residual-weight control.

## Two singular sides cannot occur together

Choose a uniform full pin-radius class and permute its three bases uniformly. Each pin–base marginal is within $`o(1)`$ in total variation of the product of two uniform sites. Give sites arbitrary attributes in a compact metric space, take a common weak limit of the four labels, and let $`\mu`$ denote the limiting one-site law. If $`R`$ is any Borel relation with $`(\mu\otimes\mu)(R)=0`$, at most one of the three base pairs can belong to $`R`$ almost surely. One can replace $`R`$ by the union of countably many such relations and their transposes.

Indeed select one most numerous reflected-pair carrier for each bisector of the whole set. Fix any desired error. A base side on an axis with at most $`K`$ pins has its two-attribute marginal dominated by $`O(K)`$ times the product of the independent site law. This passes to weak subsequential limits as a domination of measures, hence applies to every Borel null relation, without an assumption on its boundary. The unchosen pairs on axes richer than $`K`$ have total normalized incidence $`O_C(K^{-1/2})+o(1)`$ by the quadratic transverse energy, the rich-line estimate and maximal collinearity $`o(n)`$. For chosen pairs with richer axes, the normalized cost of carriers below $`c n^{2/3}`$ is bounded by a constant times $`B/K^2+B^{-1/3}+c+o(1)`$. This is the small/intermediate carrier estimate in Appendix C of the arbitrary-collinearities proof, where each axis supplies just its chosen carrier. Choose $`B`$, then $`K`$, then $`c>0`$ to make the sum of errors arbitrarily small. These choices are fixed before sending $`n`$ to infinity. [Transverse estimate](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/SPARSE-TRAPEZOIDS.md) · [Appendix C](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/ARBITRARY-COLLINEARITIES.md)

Thus a side in $`R`$ must, up to the specified probability, lie on a chosen carrier with at least $`c n^{2/3}`$ sites. Mark the corresponding finite sides before taking the subsequence. There are $`O_c(n^{1/3})`$ such lines by point–line incidence. Distinct sides of a full triangle have different carriers since three distinct points on a circle are noncollinear. Two marked sides force their common site into the $`O_c(n^{2/3})`$ pairwise intersections. This has probability $`o(1)`$ under each asymptotically uniform label. Let the error go to zero.

## Atomic and nonatomic planar limits are mutually exclusive

**Theorem.** After any sequence of translations and positive dilations, every vague subsequential limit $`\mu_F`$ of the uniform site laws has either only point atoms or no point atoms. Finite mass may have escaped. A zero vague limit satisfies both descriptions.

For the proof keep the four labels in the one-point compactification throughout a further subsequence; the one-site law restricts to $`\mu_F`$ on the plane. Suppose some finite point $`x`$ has positive mass. Let $`d_x(y)=|y-x|`$ for finite $`y`$ and let $`A_x`$ be the set of atoms of $`(d_x)_*\mu_F`$. The relation on pairs of finite base coordinates
```math
d_x(y)=d_x(z)\notin A_x.
```
has independent-product measure zero. Indeed the diagonal of a one-dimensional measure restricted off its atoms has product measure zero. Condition on the positive-probability event $`X=x`$. Whenever any one base is finite, its common distance from a bounded pin is bounded, so the other two are finite. All three limiting distances from this fixed $`x`$ coincide, by equality of the finite radii. If that radius were outside $`A_x`$, all three sides would be in the displayed null relation, contradicting the two-side assertion. By independence of $`X`$ from each individual base this gives $`\mu_F(\{y:d_x(y)\notin A_x\})=0`$.

It follows that the finite measure lies on countably many circles centered at $`x`$, allowing a radius-zero circle. The first conclusion under “Terminal paths and infinite paths” of the [arbitrary-collinearities proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/ARBITRARY-COLLINEARITIES.md#terminal-paths-and-infinite-paths) says that a vague site limit assigns no positive **nonatomic** mass to any graph of Lipschitz constant strictly smaller than one after an orthonormal coordinate choice. For the present global hypothesis, the needed transfer is exactly the fixed-node marked-carrier argument in [Farthest samples and coincident pairs](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/SPARSE-TRAPEZOIDS.md#farthest-samples-and-coincident-pairs). In Appendix C the pin–base product law, small-carrier incidence bound, marked orientation estimates and reflected-source/target estimate prove the graph conclusion under a bounded spatial localization. Off-chosen incidences on axes with more than $`K`$ pins are $`O_C(n^2/\sqrt K)+o(n^2)`$, and the pair law on axes with at most $`K`$ pins is dominated by $`O(K)`$ times the independent law. These are all the changes needed there under the global quadratic assumption. Neither tightness of the site law nor a positive hull-vertex fraction is a hypothesis of that graph argument. A positive-radius circle is covered by finitely many closed arcs that, after separate rotations, are graphs of Lipschitz constant less than one (take tangent directions within $`\pi/8`$ of the horizontal and extend each function constantly past its endpoints). The radius-zero circle is a singleton. Each circle therefore has zero mass for the nonatomic part, and the countable union has zero nonatomic mass. This proves the theorem.

There is also an extraction consequence. If any normalized vague site limit has positive nonatomic mass then the theorem makes its whole finite part nonatomic. One can extract an internally near-extremal, positive-density subset with a tight nonatomic probability limit. Choose expanding radii $`R_n`$ slowly enough that the site fractions in both $`B(0,R_n)`$ and $`B(0,3R_n)`$ tend to $`m=\mu_F(\mathbb R^2)>0`$; use a diagonal sequence of common continuity radii. Restrict to the inner disk. Whenever a near-extremal good pin–base pair lies there, both its other mates belong to the larger disk by the triangle inequality. The annulus has $`o(n)`$ sites, hence some mate lands in it with probability $`o(1)`$ by the three marginal laws. Thus only $`o(n^2)`$ internal pin–base pairs lose a mate. The restricted set retains the site-centered capacity, has a bounded global quadratic constant (at most $`C/m^2+o(1)`$), and has tight uniform site law tending to $`\mu_F/m`$.

## Escape in a direction atom

After similarities, compactify the plane by recording the direction of any point at infinity, for example by mapping $`x`$ to $`x/(1+|x|)`$ in the closed unit disk. Let $`\mu_F`$ be the finite planar part of the one-site law and $`e=1-\mu_F(\mathbb R^2)`$. Suppose some direction $`u`$ on the boundary is an atom of the escape measure. Write $`\pi_u(y)=u\cdot y`$ and $`D_u`$ for the points whose $`\pi_u`$-coordinate is an atom of $`(\pi_u)_*\mu_F`$.

On the positive-mass pin event of escape in this direction, all finite bases have a common projection. Indeed equal distances give $`2 X\cdot(Y_i-Y_j)=|Y_i|^2-|Y_j|^2`$; divide by $`|X|`$ when both bases stay finite. A pair of finite bases with one outside $`D_u`$ and with equal projection belongs to a fixed product-null relation. Consequently if such a finite base occurs, at most two bases are finite, and all finite bases lie outside $`D_u`$. Pointwise the number of finite bases outside $`D_u`$ is at most twice the number of escaped bases. Pin–base independence gives

```math
\mu_F(\mathbb R^2\setminus D_u)\le 2e.
```

Each fiber of an atomic projection is a line. The global-$`C`$ version of Appendix C excludes positive nonatomic mass on any line in any vague planar limit: its marked-carrier proof of the terminal Lipschitz-graph obstruction uses the global transverse tail and ambient carrier estimates, and no farthest-hull estimate. Thus $`D_u`$ accounts exactly for the total mass of the planar point atoms, since every point atom produces a projection atom as well. In particular the finite nonatomic mass is at most $`2e`$. A finite nonatomic part of mass greater than $`2e`$ forces the whole escaping-bearing measure to have no atoms.

**Two clouds.** Suppose all but $`o(n)`$ sites split into two sets of positive limiting relative sizes such that their diameters are both $`o(d_n)`$, where $`d_n`$ is their separation. In any rescaling at one cloud which keeps its empirical law tight and sends the other cloud to infinity, its limiting law is purely atomic. To prove this, pass to one escaping direction for the opposite cloud. For a pin in the opposite cloud and first base in the target, its full radius is $`(1+o(1))d_n`$ before rescaling. No other base can belong to the pin cloud, whose diameter is $`o(d_n)`$. The omitted $`o(n)`$ sites have negligible marginal probability. All three bases are finite in the limiting conditional law. Thus no base can lie outside $`D_u`$, and the preceding line argument proves the assertion. This applies in particular when the target diameter is normalized to one, with the origin chosen inside it.

## A restriction on projected fibers for diffuse bearings

Normalize the escaping pin-bearing law to a probability $`\nu`$ when $`e>0`$. For each $`u`$, disintegrate $`\mu_F`$ over $`t=\pi_u(y)`$ into probability measures $`\sigma_{u,t}`$. Write $`m_u(t)`$ for the largest atom weight of $`\sigma_{u,t}`$ at a point that is not a global point atom of $`\mu_F`$, or zero if none exists. Then for $`\nu`$-almost every $`u`$,

```math
\int \max(0,3m_u(t)/2-1)\,d((\pi_u)_*\mu_F)(t)\le e.
```

Condition on the escaped pin bearing. Every single finite base still has law $`\mu_F`$, as the pin is independent of each base separately. On a fiber with $`m_u(t)>2/3`$ there is a unique heavy point. These points may be selected measurably: test dyadic half-open squares for conditional mass greater than $`2/3`$, take the limit of the nested squares on such fibers, and retain a non-global-atom limit of conditional mass greater than $`2/3`$. The graph and these tests are measurable for jointly measurable regular conditional distributions. Every tuple with at least one finite base has a common $`t`$ for its finite bases. If that fiber is heavy, let $`N_a`$ count bases at its heavy point and $`N_F`$ all finite bases. The fixed diagonal at nonatomic planar points is product-null, so $`N_a\le2`$ even when the bearing and fiber vary. Hence $`N_a/2-(N_F-N_a)\le N_\infty`$. Enter zero on the left for a tuple without a finite base or with an unselected fiber. Conditional on the bearing, $`\mathbb E N_\infty=3e`$. On the heavy fibers $`\mathbb E[\mathbf1_{\rm heavy}(3N_a/2-N_F)]`$ equals three times the displayed integral by the identical individual base marginals. These two expectations give the bound. It concerns only fiber atoms at locations that have zero global mass under $`\mu_F`$.

For a closed tight target in a conditional pin population, all bases associated with a finite target base remain within that target. The same count then yields the threshold $`m_u(t)\le2/3`$ on its fiber law. In particular, if all projected fibers are singletons at globally nonatomic sites then their total finite mass is at most $`2e`$. The value $`2/3`$ is the exact marginal threshold for three labels avoiding a triple repetition at any single point: the three permutations of $`(a,a,b)`$ attain it.

For finite pins the corresponding radial conclusion has no escape term. In a nonatomic finite part, for $`\mu_F`$-almost every finite pin $`x`$, disintegrate $`\mu_F`$ by $`d_x(y)`$. Almost every resulting probability measure on a radius fiber has no atom greater than $`2/3`$. Indeed if at least one base is finite then all three are finite, with a common $`d_x`$ value. A heavy point on that fiber cannot appear three times because the finite spatial diagonal is product-null. Conditional marginals on finite pin and radius equal the single-base radial conditional measure, by exchangeability, the shared radius, and pin–base independence.

## What the limiting marginal restrictions can and cannot detect

For the directional obstruction take $`U`$ uniform on directions in a plane, independent of a uniform disk point. Let $`t`$ be that point's $`U`$-projection. Given $`U,t`$, choose three independent uniform points on the chord at projection $`t`$. Every individual base is a uniform disk point independent of $`U`$. The three labels are exchangeable and have the same projection.

Their pair law is absolutely continuous relative to two independent uniform disk points, with density in $`L^q`$ for all $`1\le q<2`$. Write $`v`$ for the perpendicular unit vector, $`L(t)=2\sqrt{1-t^2}`$ for the chord length and $`s_1,s_2`$ for chord parameters. The parameter density in $`(\theta,t,s_1,s_2)`$ is proportional to $`1/L(t)`$. The map to $`(tu+s_1v,tu+s_2v)`$ has absolute Jacobian $`|s_1-s_2|`$ and two preimages for a distinct interior pair. Its $`q`$th density moment is bounded by a constant times $`\int_{-1}^{1} L(t)^{3-2q}\,dt`$: integrate $`|s_1-s_2|^{1-q}`$ over the two chord parameters. This is finite for $`q<2`$. Thus the absence of any singular pair and the projection equality permit completely diffuse escaping bearings and diffuse target sites.

A second example concerns the farthest-hull step with the same compact law for pins and sites. Choose a pin $`X`$ uniformly in the disk, draw the distance $`r`$ of an independent uniform disk point from that pin, and conditionally choose three independent points uniformly on the angular arcs at this radius inside the disk. Each pin–base marginal is a product, and the three distinct exchangeable bases are equidistant from the pin. Even their full triple law is absolutely continuous relative to three independent disk points. The parameter law in $`(X,r,\theta_1,\theta_2,\theta_3)`$ has a density; three distinct circle points are noncollinear and locally recover these parameters smoothly by taking their circumcenter, radius and angles. Yet for $`m`$ independent disk sites the expected hull size divided by $`m`$ tends to zero. Around a triangle enclosing a fixed interior disk point, choose three small positive-area neighborhoods inside the disk so that every triangle with one vertex in each still encloses the point. Independent samples hit all three with probability tending to one. By exchangeability and integration over the fixed point, its chance of remaining a hull vertex tends to zero.

These probability examples are models for labeled limits. They do not supply finite Euclidean site configurations attaining the conjectural barrier, or a counterexample to the global quadratic implication.
