# Many triply occupied radii arbitrarily close to the diameter

**Research obstruction for the sampled-hull step.** There are finite planar sets of arbitrarily large size $`n`$ with no three collinear and no noncollinear isosceles trapezoid (rectangles included) having the following properties. Every site has exactly $`k=\Theta(\log n)`$ positive radii containing three sites each; every other positive distance it determines occurs once. Every one of the triply occupied radii lies between $`(1-\varepsilon_n)D_n`$ and $`D_n`$, where $`D_n`$ is the diameter and $`\varepsilon_n\downarrow0`$ can tend to zero arbitrarily quickly along this sequence. For every $`m_n\to\infty`$, the expected number of distinct convex-hull vertices of $`m_n`$ independent uniform sites is $`o(m_n)`$.

The mean pinned count is exactly $`n-1-2k`$. Thus these examples distinguish many near-diameter cubic distance classes at **every** site from the positive mass of farthest-rank triples needed for a hypothetical one-third-barrier sequence. They do not approach that barrier. The [full-mass reduction](RADIAL-SCALE-TREE.md) requires vanishing hull fractions for such a sequence. The [atomic-child theorem](ATOMIC-CHILDREN.md) uses diameter pairs of positive limiting mass with tight diffuse children; it does not assert that every distance metrically close to the diameter has a degree-one endpoint. [Original question, p. 168](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1987-27.pdf#page=2) · [Tracking issue](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/969)

## Two adjacent Boolean levels

Fix $`k\ge1`$. Use $`b=2k-1`$ blocks of five distinct marks. Pick a positive length $`r_i`$ per block, with all $`b`$ lengths distinct and arbitrarily near one, and assign to its marks five planar vectors of length $`r_i`$. Their angles can be chosen independently in any prescribed open interval around zero. A site is the vector sum of a subset of the marks. Keep subsets with two or three marks in each block and with exactly $`k-1`$ or $`k`$ three-mark blocks. There are

```math
 N_k=2\binom{2k-1}{k}10^{2k-1}
```

sites, divided equally between consecutive total subset sizes $`h=2b+k-1`$ and $`h+1`$. If all the vectors approach the positive horizontal unit vector $`e`$, the two classes of sites approach $`he`$ and $`(h+1)e`$. For fixed $`k`$ their radii about those points can be made as small as desired, retaining open choices of the parameters.

For an ordered difference of binary incidence vectors write $`c_a\in\{-1,0,1\}`$ on marks $`a`$, with assigned length $`l_a`$ and angle $`\theta_a`$. Its squared planar length is

```math
 \sum_a c_a^2 l_a^2+2\sum_{a\lt d}c_a c_d l_a l_d\cos(\theta_a-\theta_d).
```

The nonconstant trigonometric characters for different unordered pairs are linearly independent. If two such length expressions agree identically and the first support has at least two marks, the pair coefficients force their supports to coincide and their sign vectors to agree up to an overall sign. This includes support size two, since the cross term determines the relative signs. A one-mark length expression is constant, and can agree only with the one-mark moves in that block. Exclude zeros of all remaining nonzero trigonometric polynomials. One can exclude equalities of distinct formal points or of distinct formal midpoints at the same time, by linear independence of the individual angle characters. Further, a determinant of two planar differences vanishes identically only if every two-coordinate minor of the corresponding formal differences vanishes, by the sine characters. Two distinct nonzero binary displacements from the same source cannot be proportional: equal supports fix all signs at that source. Thus no three sites are collinear for these generic choices. The finite exclusions have empty interior, so the two-cloud geometry is preserved.

From a fixed pin, a nonsingleton formal move therefore has unique length. From a lower-level site, the $`k`$ two-mark blocks can each be enlarged in exactly three ways. From an upper-level site, the $`k`$ three-mark blocks can each be reduced in exactly three ways. There are no other one-mark moves. For any block radius, every nonisolated vertex in its distance graph has degree three. On five free marks with the other blocks fixed, each such component is the incidence graph of two- and three-subsets of a five-set (the Desargues graph).

## Excluding trapezoids

In a convex reflected trapezoid its diagonals have equal lengths and are nonparallel. Hence both must be one-mark moves: any other equal-length pair has the same formal displacement up to sign and the planar segments are parallel. In a nonrectangular isosceles trapezoid the two legs are also equal and nonparallel. The two diagonals and the two legs would form a four-cycle consisting entirely of one-mark moves. Two distinct lower-level subsets have at most one common upper neighbor, namely their union if it has size $`h+1`$. Hence the graph of one-mark moves has no four-cycle. In a rectangle the two distinct diagonals have a common midpoint. A one-mark edge has exactly one odd coordinate in its formal sum of endpoints. This identifies its changed mark, and all other coordinates determine the two endpoints; distinct one-mark edges cannot share a formal midpoint. The generic midpoint exclusions then rule out rectangles as well.

## Many copies and independent sampled hulls

For each $`k`$ use $`k^2`$ copies, so $`n=k^2N_k`$ and $`k=\Theta(\log n)`$. Choose $`\delta_k\downarrow0`$ as small as needed compared with $`\varepsilon_n`$. Translate each two-cloud copy so its nominal lower and upper centers are zero and $`e`$, with within-copy error $`o(\delta_k)`$ uniform in the copies. The parameters above permit this for each finite $`k`$. Give copy $`s`$ one more translation $`\delta_k t_s`$, where the $`k^2`$ bounded points $`t_s`$ have empirical law tending to uniform area on the unit disk. For example, take the images of the centers of a polar grid under the map $`(u,v)\mapsto\sqrt u(\cos(2\pi v),\sin(2\pi v))`$ from the unit square. Allow an additional independent translation and rotation about the lower center of size $`o(\delta_k)`$ for each copy.

Within those open choices exclude every mixed collinear triple, cyclic quadruple and equality of radii from a site involving a different copy. For a centered tie with both targets in the same other copy, that copy's translation makes the target bisector move off the fixed pin. With targets in different copies one free target translation suffices (fix the pin's copy). For a mixed collinear triple one singleton copy moves freely, except that if all three copies are distinct any one suffices. For a mixed cyclic quadruple with a singleton copy, vary its point away from the unique circle through the other three; if they are collinear they cannot lie on a circle. In the remaining two-plus-two split, fix both points of the first copy and the first point of the second copy, generically noncollinear. The second pair can still rotate about its first point using the permitted rotation and translation. Its other point traces a circle centered at a point of the prescribed circumcircle, so these circles are distinct. These finite exclusions preserve density on the two clouds and all designated within-copy distances. The resulting diameter tends to one, and the uniform closeness of all triply occupied radii to one can be made small enough for the prescribed $`\varepsilon_n`$.

In each cloud, translate its nominal center to zero and divide by $`\delta_k`$. The empirical site law tends to uniform area on the disk: each copy supplies the same number of sites within $`o(1)`$ of its assigned $`t_s`$ in these coordinates. For any fixed sample size, independent samples from one cloud therefore converge to independent uniform disk sites. The finite-sample hull count is continuous at configurations of distinct points with no three collinear. For independent disk sites the expected hull fraction tends to zero with sample size. Indeed a fixed interior site is surrounded by a triangle from three positive-area neighborhoods in the disk with probability tending to one; integrate over the fixed site and use exchangeability. For the two-cloud mixture the full hull count is at most the sum of the two within-cloud hull counts, because each independent sample label chooses its cloud by a fair coin. Dividing by the total and taking the fixed site-set limit followed by the sample-size limit gives zero. Lastly the expected hull fraction is nonincreasing in the sample size: designate one drawn index per distinct extreme site and retain a uniform subsample of indices. Each retained representative remains extreme. This proves the assertion for every $`m_n\to\infty`$ without a rate assumption.

Generic subset-sum representations of hypercubes as planar unit-distance sets are classical. The five-mark graph is also classical. The additional features here are the block lengths and adjacent-layer restriction giving logarithmically many saturated near-diameter radii at every site, the simultaneous exclusion of transverse reflected pairs, and the sampled-hull control. [Herman and Pakianathan](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.6029) · [Dumitrescu on forbidden four-point patterns](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2020.111967)

The [finite checker](check_near_diameter_radii.py) tests rational realizations on 20 and 6,000 sites and two mixed copies on 40 sites. It verifies all one-mark edges and the absence of four-cycles, the 99% global diameter cutoff, all pinned comparisons in the 20- and 40-site models, and 323,946 pinned comparisons in the larger model. The general argument uses the generic angle parameters above.
