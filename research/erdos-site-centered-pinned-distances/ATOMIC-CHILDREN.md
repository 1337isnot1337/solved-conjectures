# Diffuse children of atomic limits in the pinned-distance problem

**Independently reviewed written partial theorem for #969.** Assume site-centered circle capacity three, $`Q(P)\le Cn^2`$ for a fixed global constant counting noncollinear isosceles trapezoids including rectangles, and mean pinned count $`(1/3+o(1))n`$. Consider a nonzero purely atomic finite planar part of a rescaled site limit, with distinct atoms $`z_i`$ of masses $`m_i>0`$. At an atom, take the positive-density cloud shrinking to that atom. Call a child limit **tight and nonatomic** if a further planar rescaling of the whole cloud, along the indicated subsequence, converges to a nonatomic probability measure. These statements allow mass to have escaped at the parent scale.

1. If the parent has infinitely many atoms, all but finitely many atoms individually exclude a tight nonatomic child limit, even along separate further subsequences. If any one child admits such a limit, the entire parent atom set lies in a finite union of lines, apart from the specified child atom itself.
2. If the set of atoms is bounded and its positive diameter is attained between two atoms, its diameter endpoints cannot all have tight nonatomic child limits on a single common subsequence. This applies to any finite parent with at least two atoms, and to some infinite parents.
3. Consequently no purely atomic parent with at least two positive-mass atoms, bounded or unbounded, can have every atom admit a tight nonatomic child limit on the same subsequence.

In conclusion 1, individual exclusion is relative to the fixed parent law and concerns rescaling the entire shrinking cloud at that atom, along any further subsequence which preserves that law. A positive-density subset strictly inside the cloud could have a different limiting law.

**Quantitative form.** In any zoom of cloud $`m_j`$, let $`h_j`$ be its finite nonatomic mass in parent units. Equivalently, $`h_j/m_j`$ is the mass of the nonatomic part of its vague probability-subsequence limit. At all but finitely many atoms of an infinite parent, every such zoom has $`h_j\le 2m_j/3`$. In conclusion 2 it is already impossible for charts at every diameter endpoint to satisfy $`h_j\ge 999m_j/1000`$ on a common subsequence. In particular, for a parent with at least two atoms, if every child is normalized by a smallest disk carrying at least $`999/1000`$ of its sites, some nonzero next-scale limit is purely atomic. For an infinite parent, all but finitely many such next-scale limits are purely atomic. The positive retained mass follows from the fixed unit disk after normalization, with no tightness assumption on the remaining mass.

## Closed children and their projections

The expanding-disk extraction in the [atomic-limit proof](ATOMIC-LIMITS.md) retains the entire finite part as an internally near-extremal, positive-density subset with a tight probability limit; its annulus argument applies to atomic finite measures as well. This preserves the relative mass law of every fixed cloud, so we may suppose the parent law tight and normalize its masses to sum to one. Use disjoint disks for growing finite prefixes, shrinking sufficiently slowly so that each fixed selected disk and its triple-radius enlargement carry relative masses tending to $`m_i`$. The union of the clouds of all parent atoms covers all but $`o(n)`$ sites. A good internal pin/base pair has its two remaining mates within the larger disk. By the vanishing annulus and the uniform single-label marginals it therefore has both mates in the cloud except with probability $`o(1)`$ for a fixed $`i`$. In particular the near-extremal and bounded-global-energy hypotheses pass to that positive-density cloud. Changing its representative by $`o(n)`$ sites cannot affect the assertion. Apply the global quadratic version of the strict-Lipschitz graph obstruction to any tight nonatomic child law: every line has zero mass, and hence all its one-dimensional orthogonal projections are atomless. [Fixed-node carrier transfer](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/erdos-general-position-pinned-distances/SPARSE-TRAPEZOIDS.md#farthest-samples-and-coincident-pairs)

Record the cluster memberships, parent coordinate and child-normalized coordinates as site attributes; use a compactification and a separate dummy outside each cluster. Child similarities may be chosen with just translations and positive dilations, taking a convergent subsequence of rotations if necessary. In a diffuse tight child the scale must tend to zero relative to the parent scale, since all its parent sites converge to one point. The center of its rescaling converges in parent coordinates to that point by tightness of a fixed positive portion. For a pin in a different cloud $`i`$ and two bases in the same child $`j`$ of finite child coordinates, equality of the two distances implies equality of their limiting projections in direction $`u_{ij}=(z_i-z_j)/|z_i-z_j|`$. Indeed the pin escapes along $`u_{ij}`$ in the child chart; divide the difference of the squared distances by the norm of the pin. Thus these two bases satisfy a product-null relation in their recorded attributes: condition on both child labels being $`j`$ and use the atomless projected child law. The shared coordinate relation for each such ordered pair is fixed in the extended attribute space. There are at most countably many of these relations, so their union remains product-null. Alternatively one can retain finitely many relevant positive-mass endpoint labels before applying the carrier bounds, as detailed below.

Use the chosen carrier in the *whole* finite set for every bisector. The atomic-limit proof shows that, apart from arbitrarily small total probability, a base side in that union lies on its own carrier of size at least $`cn^{2/3}`$. We may further mark it only when both endpoints are in the unique assigned set on a retained large line. Removing multiple-line sites costs $`o(1)`$. Removing orientations of total assigned mass greater than $`\sqrt{M/n}\,n`$ also costs $`o(1)`$, where $`M=o(n)`$ is the maximum collinearity: at any one pin only its single chosen carrier perpendicular to a given axis can contribute in that orientation, with at most $`M`$ sites. These are the exact marks used in the sparse-trapezoid transfer of Appendix C.

For the quantitative version, any chart with $`h_j>0`$ has wholly nonatomic finite part by the [single-scale dichotomy](ATOMIC-LIMITS.md) applied to the entire internally near-extremal positive-density cloud. Its conditional finite mass is $`h_j/m_j`$. Restriction to slowly expanding chart disks retains a positive-density internally near-extremal subset with a tight site law equal to this finite measure normalized by its mass. The retained law charges no line by the same fixed-node strict-Lipschitz graph result. Thus the projection ties are product-null whenever both bases stay finite in that child chart, even if some of its sites escape. Positive retained nonatomic mass also forces the child scale to vanish relative to the parent: if it did not, the shrinking parent cloud would have only one finite limit point or no finite mass.

## Heavy reflected partners and infinite atomic supports

Suppose child $`j`$ admits a tight nonatomic rescaling. Fix any other parent atom $`i`$. Its circle through $`z_j`$ has radius $`r_{ij}=|z_i-z_j|`$. Condition on the positive-probability limiting event that the pin is $`i`$. If all three bases belonged to $`j`$, their three child-coordinate pairs would share their $`u_{ij}`$-projections. That is impossible by mutual exclusion of two product-null sides. The radius here is the common radius in the limiting parent coordinates: equality follows by continuity when pin and bases are finite, even though the corresponding finite radii can vary with the index. Let $`N_j`$ count bases in this child. On the event that their common parent radius is $`r_{ij}`$ we have $`N_j\le2(3-N_j)`$. The other possible bases are exactly atoms on the same circle with label different from $`j`$. Taking expectations with the radius indicator, and using pin–base independence for all three labels, gives
```math
m_j\le 2\sum_{k\ne j:\, |z_i-z_k|=r_{ij}}m_k.
```

More generally, count only bases whose child-$`j`$ coordinates stay finite in a chart with $`h_j>0`$. All three cannot do so. On the limiting ring their count is at most two thirds of the total number of ring bases. If $`M_{ij}`$ is the mass of the other atoms on that parent circle, pin–base independence of the recorded chart attributes gives
```math
h_j\le \tfrac23(m_j+M_{ij}).
```
For a tight child this is exactly the preceding companion bound.

If $`h_j>2m_j/3`$, the resulting companion lower bound $`B_j=3h_j/2-m_j`$ is strictly positive. Choose a finite set $`F`$ of atoms other than $`j`$ so that the mass outside $`F\cup\{j\}`$ is strictly below $`B_j`$. For every $`i\ne j`$ the ring needs some $`k\in F`$. Thus the perpendicular bisectors of $`(z_j,z_k)`$, $`k\in F`$, cover all parent atoms other than $`j`$.

No atom $`j'`$ on a line $`L`$ containing infinitely many parent atoms can have a chart with $`h_{j'}>2m_{j'}/3`$: its finite bisector cover would need one bisector to coincide with $`L`$, since each other bisector meets $`L`$ at most once. A bisector of a pair with endpoint $`z_{j'}\in L`$ cannot equal $`L`$. If any chart with finite nonatomic fraction greater than two thirds is possible at any one atom, its finite line cover leaves only finitely many atoms outside the infinitely occupied lines. This proves the uniform all-but-finitely-many assertion for a fixed parent law and all further subsequences.

For an eligible $`z_j`$ off an infinitely occupied line $`L`$, its reflected image across $`L`$ must be an atom of weight at least $`B_j`$ (at least $`m_j/2`$ for a tight chart). Indeed, exhaust the possible partners other than this reflection and $`j`$ by finite sets. Their bisectors meet $`L`$ in only finitely many points. Choose another pin atom on $`L`$ and apply the ring inequality; the tail mass tends to zero.

## The diameter graph

If a diameter endpoint $`i`$ has a single neighbor $`j`$, condition on the positive probability event that the pin belongs to $`i`$ and its first base belongs to $`j`$. The equal finite parent radii put all three bases in $`j`$ in the limit. If $`j`$ has a tight nonatomic child law, all three sides satisfy the product-null projection relation above, contradicting mutual exclusion of two singular sides. In the quantitative version the ring through a unique neighbor has $`M_{ij}=0`$, directly giving $`h_j\le2m_j/3`$. Thus if every diameter endpoint retained at least the stated $`999/1000`$ fraction diffusely, the graph on its nonisolated vertices would have minimum degree at least two.

The classical planar diameter argument is local. A diameter vertex with at least three diameter neighbors has a neighbor whose only diameter partner is that vertex. Translate and scale it to the origin and diameter one. All its neighbors are on the unit circle and mutually at distance at most one. Relative to one of them, the others have arguments between $`-\pi/3`$ and $`\pi/3`$. Three distinct neighbors therefore admit a middle one $`y`$ strictly between the other two $`y_1,y_3`$ on an arc of length less than $`\pi`$. Write $`y=\alpha y_1+\beta y_3`$ with $`\alpha,\beta>0`$; strict convexity of the unit disk gives $`\alpha+\beta>1`$. If $`t\ne0`$ were another diameter partner of $`y`$ then $`t\cdot y=|t|^2/2`$ while $`t\cdot y_1,t\cdot y_3\ge |t|^2/2`$, a contradiction. It follows even for infinitely many endpoints that minimum degree two forces every nonisolated endpoint degree exactly two. [Erdős, 1946, Theorem 3](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1946-03.pdf#page=3)

## A symmetric diameter weight

Let $`Z_D=\sum_{ij:\,|z_i-z_j|=D}m_i m_j>0`$, counting both orientations. Given $`\eta>0`$, choose $`\delta>0`$ with the independent pair mass of $`D-\delta\le |z_i-z_j|<D`$ less than $`\eta Z_D/16`$. This is possible by continuity of finite measures from above even if other radii accumulate at $`D`$. Take a continuous $`0\le\psi\le1`$ supported on $`[D-\delta,D+\delta]`$ with $`\psi(D)=1`$. The finite weight $`w(X,Y)=\psi(|X-Y|)`$ is exactly symmetric in pin–base swap and constant for all three bases of every full triangle. Its limiting normalizer is at least $`Z_D`$. The limiting weighted mass of nondiameter pairs is at most $`\eta/16`$.

Choose a finite set $`I`$ of diameter endpoint labels whose omitted total mass among endpoints is below $`\eta Z_D/64`$. On the limiting event of a diameter quadruple, all four labels are endpoints. The unweighted probability of any omitted such label is at most four times that tail by the single-label marginals. Its weighted probability is at most $`\eta/16`$. On the remaining diameter event the pin's two possible target clouds force two bases into the same $`j\in I`$. Tightness of the finitely many selected child charts ensures their limiting coordinates are finite. The product-null projection tie in those charts forces that side onto a retained marked carrier except for arbitrarily small total probability, chosen below $`\eta Z_D/4`$. Thus with weighted probability at least $`1-\eta`$ for sufficiently large indices, at least two base labels are matched.

If only a $`999/1000`$ diffuse finite fraction is assumed, on the exact diameter event the unweighted loss from any of the three bases escaping its own child chart is at most $`3 Z_D/1000`$. For a single base, sum the product masses $`m_i(m_j-h_j)`$ over oriented diameter pairs, using independence of its pin and its recorded chart membership. The equality of the common radius allows the same diameter event to be used for each of the three base labels. Its normalized weighted loss is at most $`3/1000`$, because the finite radius weight is one in the limit on that event and its normalizer is at least $`Z_D`$. The radius, label-cutoff and marked-carrier errors can be taken arbitrarily small compared to $`Z_D`$. Hence the probability of at least two matched labels exceeds $`99/100`$, the threshold used explicitly in Appendix C.

Apply the 'Comparing the sizes of the source and target carriers' argument of Appendix C to this particular weight. It uses positivity of the fixed normalization, exchangeability in the three base labels, the pin–base product comparison under a swap, the bound $`w\le1`$ and matching of at least two labels with probability arbitrarily near one. All hold here, with exact weight symmetry and asymptotic pin–base independence. The argument forces a positive-density set of matched source–target pairs on retained carriers of comparable sizes. The marked one-carrier version of its projection estimate bounds the very same incidences by $`o(n^2)`$. This contradiction rules out the proposed simultaneous rescalings even for the diameter endpoints alone. A wholly nonatomic root and a hierarchy whose successive children remain atomic are still possible under these necessary conditions. The unrestricted global quadratic implication remains open. The assertion about diameter endpoints requires attainment at two positive-mass atoms; the separate finite-bisector argument applies without any diameter assumption for infinite supports.
