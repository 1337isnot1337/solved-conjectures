# Erdős–Hajnal local triangle conjecture

**Exact slope components for concurrent-core diamonds.** For two fixed geometrically incident secants $u,v$, the full graph of possible concurrent and nonconcurrent cores is the disjoint union of $q$ complete bipartite graphs $K_{q-1,q}$ and $q-2$ isolated concurrent cores. No admissibility condition beyond having a geometric diamond is needed. At the published sampling rate, a spindle-free assignment with independence number at most $\sqrt M$ must suppress the great majority of these slope components, not just individual diamonds.

## Geometry

Use the standard affine model of the Hermitian unital, $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(x)=N(y)`$, with the unique unital point $X$ at infinity in the $x$ direction. Send the common point $u\cap v$ to $X$ by unital point transitivity. The two lines then have equations $y=a_0,y=a_1$. One may normalize to $y=0,1$: translating $y$ by $a_0$ introduces a trace-linear term and a constant in its norm, removable by a shear and a translation in $x$ (the trace is onto); then divide $y$ by $a_1-a_0$ and $x$ by $N(a_1-a_0)$. The tangent at $X$ is not a secant. The analysis uses only the usual trace and norm over $\mathbb F_{q^2}/\mathbb F_q$, valid in every characteristic.

A nonconcurrent core meeting both tips in unital points is of the form $x=my+b$ with $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(b)=0`$ and $`\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(m)=1`$. For each slope there are exactly $q$ choices of $b$. A concurrent core is $y=a$ with $a\ne0,1$, and meets this nonconcurrent core in a unital point precisely when
```math
 N(a)=\mathop{\mathrm{Tr}}(ma).
```
The condition is independent of $b$. For $a\in\mathbb F_q$, this asks $a^2=a$; after excluding the tips it never holds. If $a\notin\mathbb F_q$, the trace pairings against $1$ and $a$ are two independent linear functionals, so $a$ belongs to exactly one slope. For a fixed trace-one $m$, the condition is $N(a-\bar m)=N(m)$, with $N(m)\ne0$. The norm fiber has $q+1$ points, of which two are $0,1$. Thus every slope has exactly $q-1$ concurrent cores and $q$ nonconcurrent ones. For each slope all the intersections between its two classes are distinct: $a$ determines the concurrent core and then $x=ma+b$ determines $b$.

## Consequences for fixed and arbitrary cuts

Retain secants independently with $p=(\log q)^2/q$, and write $d=pq$ and $M$ for the retained order. By scalar concentration, simultaneously for all retained tip pairs and all slopes each side of the corresponding component has $\Theta(d)$ retained cores, with probability tending to one. This is a polynomial-size union bound with failure probability $\exp(-\Omega(d))$ per event. The total number of ordered unsplit retained tip pairs is $\Theta(Mq^2d)$, uniformly over all cuts: the lower bound is the pencil pigeonhole count in [the deterministic barrier](DETERMINISTIC-DIAMOND-BARRIER.md), and the upper bound is retained host degree concentration.

For fixed retained unsplit tips, call a slope active if it contains at least one split diamond with those tips. Three active slopes supply three disjoint core pairs. For a fixed apex $u$, tips $v$ with at least three active slopes must be independent in a spindle-free cut graph, by the disjoint-core argument in the deterministic barrier. There are therefore at most $M\alpha$ ordered unsplit tip pairs with three or more active components. At $\alpha\le \sqrt M$ this is an $O(q^{-1/2}d^{-1/2})$ fraction. For every other unsplit pair, all but at most two slopes contain no split diamonds. In particular, if its common pencil has $\Omega(qd)$ retained secants on the opposite side from the tips, then $\Omega(q)$ slopes have an opposite-side concurrent core (there are at most $O(d)$ retained in a slope). An abundance of opposite cores cannot account for the suppression; it must occur at one of the three tests involving the nonconcurrent core.

In contrast, under fair independent incidence bits, conditional on a fixed retained pair being unsplit, each slope has $\Omega(d)$ retained opposite-side concurrent cores and $\Omega(d)$ retained nonconcurrent cores adjacent to both tips, with failure probability $\exp(-\Omega(d))$. For the latter test, the two intersections with the tips are distinct across the nonconcurrent core lines in this slope. The edges between the two resulting core sets are independent fair tests because their intersection points are distinct and not used in the tip tests. The probability that none survive is then at most $\exp(-\Omega(d^2))$. A union bound implies that every slope is active for every retained unsplit pair with high probability in the fair model.

The finite-field calculation classifies the geometry, while the active-slope statement is a necessary condition on arbitrary spindle-free cuts. It does not yet turn the required suppression into a lower bound on independence.
