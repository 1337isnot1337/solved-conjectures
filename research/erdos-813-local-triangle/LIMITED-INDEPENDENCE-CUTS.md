# Erdős–Hajnal local triangle conjecture

**Finite-order independence suffices, but pairwise independence does not.** Consider arbitrary random pencil cuts with a uniform lower bound δ>0 on the probability of separating each pair of lines in its pencil. If every subcollection of at most 22 different pencil cuts is mutually independent, the appearance threshold and positive-fraction edge-cleanup obstruction from the [previous model](INDEPENDENT-PENCIL-BIAS.md) still hold. No ordinary dependency graph is needed. Conversely, there is a pairwise-independent family of exactly fair cuts whose graph is bipartite for every outcome.

## Why 22 pencil variables suffice

Use the chordless spindle family with eleven distinct pencils. An individual pattern uses eleven cut variables, so its split survival probability is at least δ¹¹. A disjoint pair with no pencil in common uses 22 different cut variables, so its survival indicators are independent. The only other pairs were already counted in the [independent-pencil proof](INDEPENDENT-PENCIL-BIAS.md): common vertices use the geometric completion table, and disjoint vertex sets with a shared pencil contribute O(q⁻³) to the relative variance. The upper bounds on joint survival discard all split constraints, so no extra assumption is necessary.

Consequently, if T=q¹⁷p⁷s¹¹ tends to infinity under independent vertex and edge retention, a spindle appears with probability tending to one; if T tends to zero, all feasible copies disappear with probability tending to one by the first moment. If q²ps² tends to infinity, the retention-only rooted-edge argument gives Ω_δ(q⁷p²s) edge-disjoint spindles with high probability. These conclusions apply even when the global cut process is generated by a short seed and its genuine dependency graph is dense.

## The Poisson window under growing finite-order independence

Suppose the independence order tends to infinity with q. If T→λ>0 and all pair separation probabilities approach one half uniformly, the number of copies again tends to Poisson(λ/16384). More generally the corresponding Poisson mean is the actual expected number of favorable copies whenever that mean converges.

Here is a direct factorial-moment argument. Fix j. For all sufficiently large q, the union of the pencil roles of any j favorable copies uses at most 11j variables and has the fully independent law of those pencil variables. We need the contribution of tuples with an overlap to tend to zero. Any tuple with a common vertex or pencil among two members can be ordered with such a pair first, up to a constant depending on j. Distinct chordless copies have different vertex sets. By the earlier pair estimates, the sum of joint **retention-only** probabilities over those first pairs is o(1) in the critical window.

Adding one more distinct copy to the ordered tuple costs only O_j(1) in the sum of retention probabilities relative to the retention probability of the prefix. Indeed, if it shares r vertices with the union of previous copies, choose their identification from a bounded number of labels and use the geometric completion bound
```math
(c_0,c_1,c_2,c_3,c_4,c_5,c_6,c_7)=(17,13,10,8,6,4,2,0)
```
for the exponent of q in the number of possibilities. If it shares k required edges with the union, the extra retention probability is p^(7−r)s^(11−k). For r≤6, the [strict overlap inequalities](SPINDLE-THRESHOLD.md) show this total is O(T), in fact o(T) for 1≤r≤6. They apply because all k shared edges are among the r identified vertices of the new copy, irrespective of whether they come from one or several old copies. At r=7 there are O_j(1) possibilities and retention costs at most one. Thus every extension is bounded, so the total overlapped contribution stays o(1).

The tuples of copies disjoint in vertices and pencils factor fully. Their product sum tends to the j-th power of the mean: removing a pair-overlapping tuple from the unrestricted product sum costs o(1), by the same pair estimates or by domination by its joint retention-only probability for distinct copies. Repeated indices in that product sum separately contribute at most a constant times the largest individual probability times a bounded power of the mean, which is o(1). All factorial moments converge to those of the specified Poisson law. The discarded nongeneric patterns contribute o(1) in probability by their first moment.

## A globally bipartite pairwise-independent example

Assign independent fair bits a(L) to the secants and independent fair bits b(P) to the pencils. The label of L at pencil P is a(L)+b(P) modulo two. Each pencil separately has independent fair labels. Two labels in one pencil differ exactly when the corresponding global vertex bits differ, so **every graph produced is bipartite**, and all its induced subgraphs have an independent set of size at least half their order.

Two distinct pencils share exactly one secant. Their complete label vectors are independent: condition on the global bit of that secant. The vectors then use disjoint remaining random inputs, and each conditionally has the uniform law, because its pencil flip supplies the label of the shared line. Thus this model even gives pairwise independent labeled partitions before forgetting the overall flip.

More generally, any selected family of pencils whose incidence graph with their secants is a forest has independent fair incidence labels. Edge differences of independent vertex bits on a forest are independent, which follows by uncovering one leaf at a time. A cycle, however, forces an even sum of its incidence labels, so higher-order independence can fail. This exhibits a genuine distinction between independence of pairs of cuts and independence sufficient to reproduce the random-spindle argument.

These results isolate restrictions on globally coordinated random splits. They do not rule out a correlated construction whose independence number is small, and do not settle Erdős #813.
