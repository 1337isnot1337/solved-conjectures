# Erdős–Hajnal local triangle conjecture

**The unital spindle count is sharp.** There are Θ(q¹⁷) ordered geometric spindles with all eleven required intersections distinct and unital. Thus every such pattern has split probability exactly 2⁻¹¹ in the independent fair-pencil model. At the published vertex retention rate, the number of actual labeled spindles is Θ(q¹⁰(log q)¹⁴) with probability tending to one. This complements the [positive-fraction deletion barrier](SPINDLE-LINEAR-PACKING.md), which used a smaller collection to pack disjoint copies. It still does not settle Erdős #813.

## A second intersection for almost all projection centers

Use the [two Baer sublines](SPINDLE-COUNTING-SCALE.md) argument for two secants u,v whose intersection X is not unital. Pick unital points P₁ on u and Q₁ on v, defining the first core A=P₁Q₁. Give A coordinates in which P₁=0 and Q₁=∞, and v coordinates in which X=0 and Q₁=∞. Fix the point R of A with coordinate 1. For a center P₂ on u other than X and P₁, projection from P₂ takes A projectively onto v and preserves the coordinates 0 and ∞. It is multiplication by the coordinate of the intersection of RP₂ with v. This multiplier is itself a projective coordinate on u (central projection from R), sending X to 0 and P₁ to ∞.

The unital points on A and v are each a Baer subline containing ∞, hence an affine line over the subfield together with ∞. To verify the affine description, choose a projectivity taking the subfield line to the Baer subline; precompose by a subfield projectivity so that the resulting map fixes ∞. It is then an affine map over the full field. Write the two affine directions as d₁F_q and d₂F_q. Multiplication by k makes them parallel only if k lies in (d₂/d₁)F_q^×. The latter set together with 0 and ∞ is another Baer subline of the field of multipliers. The image of the unital points on u contains ∞ and omits 0. It is therefore a different Baer subline and meets that parallel-direction subline in at most two points, of which one is ∞. Among its finite nonzero multipliers, at most one is exceptional.

Every other center P₂ gives two distinct affine lines with different directions in the two-dimensional space over F_q; they intersect in exactly one finite point Q₂ in addition to their projective common point Q₁=∞. This defines the second core B=P₂Q₂. The intersection of A and B is on the unital, and the five required diamond pencils are distinct. Thus for every choice of P₁ and Q₁, there are at least q−1 choices of P₂, and altogether there are Θ(q³) ordered diamonds for every nonincident pair u,v.

One can forbid a fixed finite list of pencil points and core lines at a cost of only O(q²) diamonds. Fixing any of P₁,P₂,Q₁ costs O(q²) by the unique completion bound; fixing Q₂ does too, by exchanging the core labels. Fixing the meeting point A∩B costs O(q²), since it is off u and each core is determined by its intersection on u and this meeting point. Fixing either core line costs O(q) because that line fixes two pencil points. The bounds here are uniform in the tips and in the forbidden data.

## Count and concentration

Choose nonincident u,v in Θ(q⁸) ways. Choose w geometrically incident to v at a unital point but not to u at a unital point, in Θ(q³) ways. Indeed v has Θ(q³) neighbors, and at most (q+1)² are also adjacent to u, since each must join one unital point on each line. Choose a diamond with tips u,v avoiding the pencil v∩w and the third tip w as a core. Then choose a diamond with tips u,w avoiding the earlier five pencils, the pencil v∩w, and all earlier extra lines as cores. The exclusion estimate leaves Θ(q³) options at each stage. This gives Θ(q¹⁷) ordered geometric spindles with eleven distinct pencil points, and the matching upper bound follows from the [uniform count](SPINDLE-COUNTING-SCALE.md).

Let Z count these ordered spindles surviving random splits and independent vertex retention p. Then the mean is Θ(q¹⁷p⁷). Disjoint vertex sets give independent indicators. Transitivity on secants bounds the number of potential copies containing any one fixed vertex by O(q¹³), by counting incidences, since the family with distinct pencil points is invariant under the projective unitary group. Therefore for a fixed first copy and an r-line identification, there are at most O(q¹³) choices of the second when r≤3. When r≥4 the trivial bound is O(q^(4(7−r))) by choosing all remaining secants. There are only constantly many identifications. As a result, the contribution to the relative variance is at most O(q^(−4)p^(−r)) for 1≤r≤3, or O(q^(11−4r)p^(−r)) for 4≤r≤7. At p=(log q)²/q every term tends to zero. The same bounds work for all p≥q^(−5/4+ε), for fixed ε>0.

This proves a matching lower bound with high probability at the published vertex rate. For a matching high-probability upper bound on all copies, ignore splits and repeat the same variance argument on the invariant family of geometrically feasible copies counted in the [upper bound](SPINDLE-COUNTING-SCALE.md). Here geometrically feasible means all required intersections are unital and none of the four triangles lies in one pencil. This is invariant, every possible surviving copy is included, and the family has Θ(q¹⁷) members by the lower bound above. Thus the observed order is Θ(q¹⁰(log q)¹⁴) with high probability, not merely in expectation.
