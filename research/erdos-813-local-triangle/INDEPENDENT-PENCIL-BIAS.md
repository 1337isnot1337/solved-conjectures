# Erdős–Hajnal local triangle conjecture

**The random-model obstruction is not an artifact of independent fair bits within a pencil.** Allow each unital pencil to be partitioned by an arbitrary random binary coloring with dependencies among its line labels. Different pencils are independent. Suppose that every pair of lines in a common pencil is separated with probability at least an absolute δ>0, uniformly over all pencils and pairs. Independently retain vertices with probability p and eligible edges with probability s. With T=q¹⁷p⁷s¹¹, spindle absence occurs with high probability for T→0, and presence with high probability for T→∞. In the regime q²ps²→∞ a positive fraction of all surviving edges must still be deleted to make the graph spindle-free, with a constant that may depend on δ.

This includes uniform balanced splits, random affine cuts with uniform marginal separation, and nonidentically distributed splits. It does not cover correlations **between** pencils or an arrangement that deterministically prohibits many particular pairs within a pencil.

## Counting correlations between copies

Use the geometrically chordless spindle patterns with eleven distinct pencils from the [exact threshold proof](SPINDLE-THRESHOLD.md). Their number as unordered copies is (1+O(1/q))q¹⁷/8. Because its pencils are distinct, one copy survives all split requirements with probability at least δ¹¹ and at most one, independent of vertex and edge retention. The mean of the favorable count Z is therefore Θ_δ(T).

In this model a pair of copies is independent when it shares neither a vertex nor a pencil. The earlier common-vertex estimates, which discarded split constraints, remain valid. For disjoint vertex sets with at least one common pencil, the number of ordered copy pairs is O(q³¹). To see this, choose the first copy in O(q¹⁷) ways and a common pencil among its eleven. The geometric pattern family is invariant under the action of the projective unitary group, which is transitive on unital points. There are Θ(q³) pencils, and every copy belongs to eleven, so a fixed pencil belongs to O(q¹⁴) copies. The joint retention probability in this new class is p¹⁴s²², because vertices and eligible edges are disjoint. The contribution to the second moment divided by the mean squared is O_δ(q⁻³).

Thus in the variance argument from the [threshold note](SPINDLE-THRESHOLD.md), the only additional term tends to zero. Chebyshev gives presence above threshold. The matching below-threshold statement uses the same upper bound on the number of geometrically feasible copies and needs no lower bound on split probabilities.

In the bounded-mean window, apply the same dependency-graph Poisson estimate but take each copy's neighborhood to include all copies sharing either a vertex or a pencil. Its extra contribution to both pair sums is O(q⁻³T²). The new distribution is therefore Poisson up to an error tending to zero, with parameter equal to the actual expected favorable count (which can vary with q). Omitted geometrically feasible patterns still have expected count O(T/q). In particular, if all pair separation probabilities approach one half uniformly, the Poisson parameter tends to λ/16384 whenever T→λ. This applies to independent uniformly balanced binary cuts on a pencil of q² lines: each pair has separation probability 1/2+O(q⁻²).

## Deleting edges

When q²ps² tends to infinity, Z is at least c_δ T with high probability by the above second moment. The [retention-only mean-square bound](SPINDLE-EDGE-PACKING.md) on potential copies through each retained edge did not use any property of the split distribution. It therefore gives the same upper bound O(q²⁷p¹²s²¹) on overlaps across actual surviving copies, so the elementary independent-set bound for their edge-intersection graph yields Ω_δ(q⁷p²s) pairwise edge-disjoint spindles. The retained graph has O(q⁷p²s) edges with high probability. This proves the claimed obstruction.

The same argument gives a power-scale variant. If δ≥q^(-o(1)) and p=q^(-a), s=q^(-b) with fixed a,b≥0 and a+2b<2, the negative powers in the original variance estimates absorb δ^(-22), so Z≥cδ¹¹T with high probability. The packing size is Ω(δ²²q⁷p²s)=q^(-o(1))·Ω(q⁷p²s). Thus no polynomially small edge cleanup can work in this wider model. These are limitations of random constructions and do not resolve the original Erdős problem.
