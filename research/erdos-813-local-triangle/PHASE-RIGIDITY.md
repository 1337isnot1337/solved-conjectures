# Erdős–Hajnal local triangle conjecture

**Triangle-parity rigidity on the randomly retained Hermitian host.** Let q tend to infinity through prime powers. Retain each secant independently with probability p, where pq is at least C log q for a sufficiently large absolute C. With high probability, the following assertion holds simultaneously for every assignment of binary labels to the retained incidences, even if the labels are chosen after the sample:

If every geometrically nonconcurrent triangle of retained secants has an even number of separated pairs, then there are bits a(L) on the retained secants and b(P) on the unital points such that the incidence label at (P,L) is a(L)+b(P) modulo two. In particular the split graph is bipartite and has independence number at least half its order.

In more intrinsic terms, the cycle space of the sampled incidence graph is generated by its six-cycles. This identifies the globally coordinated triangle-parity model exactly, including at the Mattheus–Verstraëte sampling rate. Spindle-freeness alone does not give the even-triangle hypothesis, so this is not a resolution of Erdős #813.

## The point graph and its two local properties

Let J be the graph on all q³+1 unital points in which two points are adjacent if their unique joining secant was retained. Write d=pq. We prove simultaneously that (i) every triple of distinct points has a common neighbor, and (ii) for every nonadjacent pair P,Q the graph induced by their common neighbors is connected.

For (i), fix three distinct roots. Consider witness points outside the union of the three secants joining the roots. There are Θ(q³) choices. For each witness, success requires three distinct retained lines, one to each root. Two different witnesses share a required line for at most O(q) choices of the second witness per first witness: a shared line necessarily attaches to the same root, as the witnesses avoid all root-pair lines. And two different witnesses cannot share two lines, because two distinct lines have at most one common point. Thus the expected count is Θ(q³p³) and the ordered dependency sum is O(q⁴p⁵). The [Janson lower-tail bound](https://doi.org/10.1002/rsa.3240010209) gives absence probability at most exp(−Ω(min{q³p³,q²p})), uniformly in the roots. This is superpolynomially small at d≥C log q, so a union bound suffices.

For (ii), fix distinct P,Q, and exclude their joining line. The lines through P and through Q give two sets of N=q²−1 secants each. Put an edge between a left and a right secant when they intersect at a unital point. This is a q-regular bipartite graph: every unital point other than P on a left secant specifies exactly one right secant, and vice versa. It has no 4-cycle. Such a cycle would give four lines with six distinct unital intersections, the forbidden O'Nan configuration (see [Mattheus–Verstraëte, Proposition 1](https://doi.org/10.4007/annals.2024.199.2.8)). Cross intersections do not coincide with P or Q because the joining line was excluded, and two cross intersections sharing either line cannot coincide.

Let B be its bipartite 0–1 matrix. Any pair of distinct rows has inner product zero or one. For a fixed row the number of other rows with inner product one is q(q−1), so the number with zero is q−2. Thus
```math
BB^{\mathsf T}=(q-1)I+J_N-F,
```
where J_N is the all-ones matrix and F is symmetric with each row sum q−2. For M=B−(q/N)J_N this yields an operator norm bound at most sqrt(2q−3).

Write R and S for the random sets of retained left and right lines. They are independent because the two full line sets are disjoint. By scalar Chernoff bounds, except with probability exp(−Ω(d)) times a fixed polynomial in q, the selected sizes are at most 1.1pN, every vertex of the host has at least 0.9d neighbors in the selected opposite set, and every full column has at most 2d ones among the selected rows. These assertions include the vertices that were not retained. A centered column of M restricted to R then has squared norm O(d), uniformly: its 0–1 part has squared norm at most 2d and its constant part has squared norm at most N(q/N)²=O(1).

Condition on R with this column bound and sample S independently. The square of the desired restricted norm is the top eigenvalue of the sum of sampled column outer products. Every summand has norm O(d), and the mean sum has norm at most p(2q−3)≤2d, since restricting rows cannot increase the full operator norm. The [matrix Chernoff bound](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.4389) implies that the restricted operator norm is at most 0.1d except with probability q^{−A} for any fixed A, for sufficiently large q. In detail, at threshold 0.01d² its upper-tail exponent is Ω(d log d), overwhelming the dimension prefactor N. The scalar exceptions also have probability q^{−A} if C is chosen sufficiently large in pq≥C log q. Take A large enough for a union bound over all O(q⁶) root pairs.

The selected bipartite graph must be connected. If it had two or more components, take one with total order at most half the selected order, at most 1.1pN. Writing s,t for its side sizes, the centered norm bound gives internal edges at most qst/N + 0.1d sqrt(st). Its average degree is consequently at most q(s+t)/(2N)+0.1d≤0.65d, contrary to the minimum degree 0.9d. The selected sides are nonempty with high probability by scalar concentration.

If P,Q are nonadjacent in J, each common neighbor corresponds bijectively to an edge of this selected bipartite graph, by the two retained lines to P and to Q. Two such edges sharing a left or right endpoint give adjacent common neighbors in J, since they lie on that retained line. The line graph of the selected connected bipartite graph is connected, proving (ii).

## Recover the global phase

For a point-graph edge PQ define f(P,Q) as the sum modulo two of the two incidence labels on the joining line at P and Q. It satisfies the triangle equation on J. For a triangle of collinear points the terms telescope on one retained line. For a noncollinear triangle its three distinct retained joining lines form a nonconcurrent triangle of secants, and the sum of the three f-values is exactly the parity of the separated pairs there.

For any missing point-graph edge PQ, define its extended f-value as f(P,X)+f(X,Q) through any common neighbor X. This is well-defined: for adjacent common neighbors X,Y the two triangle equations with roots P and Q give the same value via X and via Y, and (ii) joins all common neighbors. For an existing edge the same formula via any common neighbor holds by the original triangle equation. Now for an arbitrary triple P,Q,R, property (i) gives one simultaneous common neighbor. Expressing all three extended values through that point makes their sum zero. The completed edge labeling of the complete point graph is therefore the difference of a point potential: fix P₀ and put b(P)=f(P,P₀) (zero at P₀). Then f(P,Q)=b(P)+b(Q).

On each retained line, the original incidence label plus b(P) is constant across its unital points, by the definition of f. Call this constant a(L). On an intersecting pair of retained secants, separation is now exactly a(L)+a(L'), giving the claimed global bipartition.

The cycle-space formulation follows by duality over the two-element field: incidence labelings having zero sum around all six-cycles are exactly the vertex-potential labelings just proved, which annihilate the entire cycle space. Conversely each six-cycle of this bipartite incidence graph is a nonconcurrent geometric triangle of its three distinct secants.
