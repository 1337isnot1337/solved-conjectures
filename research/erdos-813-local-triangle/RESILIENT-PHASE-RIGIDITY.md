# Erdős–Hajnal local triangle conjecture

**The phase-rigidity theorem survives local adversarial deletions.** Let d=pq and retain Hermitian secants independently with pq≥C log q. With high probability, any subsequent deletion that removes at most d/5 retained secants through each unital point preserves the [triangle-parity rigidity](PHASE-RIGIDITY.md) of the remaining host. The deletion and labels can both be chosen after seeing the random host. In particular, if all odd-parity geometric triangles of a cut can be hit by such a deletion set, the cut graph has an independent set of size at least half the number of remaining secants.

The assertion concerns odd triangle parity, not just spindles, and does not solve #813.

## Resilience of the two point-graph properties

The original proof gives, simultaneously for every pair P,Q, a selected bipartite grid with minimum degree at least 0.9d, selected side sizes at most 1.1p(q²−1), and centered operator norm at most 0.1d. These bounds hold whether or not the joining line PQ itself was retained. If at most d/5 secants through each point are deleted, any surviving grid vertex loses at most d/5 opposite neighbors. The side sizes and the centered norm cannot increase under restriction. The same mixing estimate now bounds the average degree in a component occupying at most half the surviving grid by 0.65d, whereas the surviving minimum degree is at least 0.7d. Both sides remain nonempty: before deletion each contained at least 0.9d vertices, and only d/5 can be lost. Thus the grid stays connected.

For any three distinct roots, consider the witnesses off their three joining lines. The [same Janson estimate](PHASE-RIGIDITY.md) in lower-tail form gives at least c d³ fully retained witnesses, for some absolute c>0, with probability tending to one uniformly over all root triples. A single line through one of the roots can destroy only witnesses on that line. On any such fixed line, the O(q) eligible candidate points require two other retained joining lines apiece; these pairs of lines are disjoint across candidates. A repeated line to the same other root would have two intersections with the fixed line, and a line to both other roots was excluded. The candidate-success count on this line (ignoring whether the fixed line itself is retained) is therefore binomial with mean O(qp²). Chernoff and a union bound over the polynomially many triples and possible lines give the uniform upper bound O(qp²+log q).

A deletion set satisfying the local condition can kill witnesses only along the deleted lines through the three roots. It therefore kills at most O(d(qp²+log q)) witnesses. The ratio of this bound to d³ tends to zero, since qp²=d²/q and d≥C log q. Every triple retains a common point-neighbor. The proof recovering global phases from the two local properties now applies without any change.

## A consequence stated only in terms of the cut

A maximal matching of odd-parity geometric triangles is a vertex-disjoint family of such triangles in the graph of secants. Its used secants hit all odd-parity triangles. In particular, a maximal matching of fewer than d/15 members provides a deletion set of fewer than d/5 secants even globally, so it obeys the local condition. If the original cut graph has independence number below (M−d/5)/2, where M is the number of retained secants, such a small maximal matching is impossible. The cut then contains at least d/15 vertex-disjoint odd-parity geometric triangles. More generally a much larger hitting set is allowed if it respects every point's local deletion budget.
