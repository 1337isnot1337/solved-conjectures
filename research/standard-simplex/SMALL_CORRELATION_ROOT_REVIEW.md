## Verdict and scope

No mathematical gap was found in the assembled argument. Subject to the explicitly credited and checked first-moment inputs, it proves the following proposed partial theorem: for every fixed number of cells, regular-simplex partitions uniquely maximize balanced Gaussian noise stability for every sufficiently small positive correlation, with a threshold independent of the ambient dimension.

This is an analytic interval theorem. It is not a finite numerical check. It remains a partial result toward Standard Simplex: larger positive correlations and the negative-correlation clause are not covered. It must not increase the campaign's full-resolution count. The proof is unrefereed, and priority remains a separate source-review question.

## Critical checks

1. **First-moment dependency.** The Gaussian perimeter input is used for positive cell masses with the number of cells at most the dimension plus one, precisely within the published Milman–Neeman theorem. The covariance flux, price derivative, and path deficit identity were read and checked. The quantitative gap is obtained on the first half of the covariance segment, so no singular-endpoint differentiability is assumed.
2. **Higher Hermite degrees.** The normal/tangent Hermite split correctly separates normal degrees zero, one, and at least two. The normalized Mehler formula gives the stated polynomial bound on the unit interval. The geometric-series sum is at most sixteen times the squared correlation. The fiber mass estimate and positive-operator splitting then give the stated constant of seventy-two. It is independent of the tangent dimension and requires neither a support restriction nor a mean-zero hypothesis on each transfer.
3. **Transfer decomposition.** Each cell perturbation is the sum of its antisymmetric pair transfers. Cauchy–Schwarz contributes the factor twice the number of other cells. Each mismatch contributes once to the unordered-pair cost. The first-moment slope is the common edge length of the regular moment vectors, not the edge length of the trace-one score vectors.
4. **Uniform potential.** For a point in a regular cell, that cell's Gaussian score has the largest deterministic mean. The exact integral for the difference of two winning probabilities has a nonnegative integrand by the inverse-Mills-ratio inequality. The restriction to a fixed unit integration interval gives the stated positive constant. Lowering the competing mean proves the bound for larger score gaps. No bound on tangential coordinates is inserted into this tail estimate.
5. **Tail estimates.** The augmented mass/moment estimate has coefficient `r p_1 + m_1` on the large-normal-coordinate part. On the large-tangent-coordinate part, the fiber mass and first-moment bounds have factors four and two. The resulting tail coefficient tends to zero for every fixed simplex dimension. Both the mass vector and the full moment matrix are controlled by the same truncated transfer cost.
6. **Conditioning.** Pair transfers have a fixed sign on each regular cell, so their absolute values commute with conditioning onto the aligned simplex subspace. Their weighted costs and linear potential losses are therefore preserved. The original higher-Hermite quadratic form is still evaluated in the original ambient space. The argument never substitutes the noise value of the conditioned fractional partition for the original noise value.
7. **Core coercivity.** The original core labels are feasible for the unequal-mass score value. Evaluating that value at the balanced prices gives the correct one-sided inequality. Smoothness and the vanishing balanced price at the regular covariance make the mass error quadratic after Young's inequality. The elementary Procrustes argument controls the polar rotation by the skew part of the moment matrix. The core lemma allows a negative moment deficit and therefore does not hide an unrestricted-mass extremum.
8. **Core neighborhood.** A potential noise competitor has first-moment deficit at most the correlation. Global polar alignment bounds its moment distance from the same comparison simplex. The tail radius is chosen so that the discarded moment and mass errors are uniformly small, using that the truncated transfer cost is at most one. These two facts place the core inside the fixed neighborhood required by its lemma. No existence theorem for a noise maximizer is needed.
9. **Order of constants.** Covariance and core constants are fixed first, then the tail radius, then the correlation threshold. All dimension-dependent tail estimates take place in the simplex subspace of dimension one less than the number of cells. The only estimate used on the full ambient space is the dimension-independent Hermite bound. This establishes the claimed ambient-dimension independence.
10. **Absorption and equality.** With the stated Young parameter, the restored moment term has core coefficient at most `2-kappa/2` and tail coefficient at most `b/2`. Substituting the potential and remainder estimates leaves core coefficient at most `-kappa/8` and tail coefficient at most `-b`, each multiplied by the positive correlation. Both costs must vanish at equality. Their weights are positive away from finitely many Gaussian-null tie hyperplanes. The conditioned labels then equal the regular indicators; a conditional expectation of an indicator equal to zero or one forces the original indicator to agree almost everywhere.
