# Erdős–Sauer conjecture

**Limitation of independent local repairs.** The [forced-tree repair](INDISPENSABLE-FACES.md) absorbs any single unhandled simplex without increasing the private-neighbor budget. It cannot simply be applied to every unhandled simplex while holding all nonindispensable packed faces fixed. This obstruction is an actual clique family, not just an abstract subfamily of a Johnson graph.

Fix $k\ge4$, a set $K$ of $k-2$ vertices, and five extra vertices $a,b,c,d,v$. Let $G$ be the $(k-1)$-uniform graph whose edges are all $(k-1)$-faces of every set $K+\{x,y\}$ with distinct $x,y\in\{a,b,c,d,v\}$. Its $k$-cliques are exactly these ten sets. Every included face has at least $k-3$ vertices of $K$. A candidate $k$-clique missing a vertex of $K$ has at most $k-3$ vertices there. If it has any, deleting one of them produces a face with at most $k-4$ vertices of $K$; if it has none then all its faces have none. In either case it is not a clique.

Two such $k$-cliques overlap in a face exactly when their corresponding extra pairs intersect. Hence the maximum packing is the ordinary matching number of $K_5$, namely two. Take the packed cliques for pairs $ab$ and $cd$. Each has exactly two indispensable faces. They are the two core faces $K+x$ for its own two endpoints; the private cliques are those paired to $v$. The candidate repair faces obtained by pairing the two private neighbors of either block coincide, namely $K+v$.

Select all nonindispensable packed faces as in the baseline construction. These faces cover the two packed cliques but no other member of the family, since they contain both endpoints of one packed pair and omit a core vertex. The unhandled cliques consist of the four pairs between $\{a,b\}$ and $\{c,d\}$ and the four pairs from $v$ to $\{a,b,c,d\}$. They form the complete tripartite graph $K_{2,2,1}$ on extra vertices.

The available local private-neighbor budget is two faces. Two faces cannot cover these eight cliques. Every face with the full core is $K+x$ and covers the pairs incident with $x$; every face without the full core is in just one clique and can be replaced, without worsening a cover, by one endpoint face of that clique. Thus the minimum number of additional faces is the vertex cover number of $K_{2,2,1}$, which is three. The four unhandled bridging cliques all trigger the same two-node forced-tree obstruction, so the loss comes from trying to reuse that one repair budget independently.

There is **no counterexample to the conjecture here**. Drop the unnecessarily retained nonindispensable faces and take four of the five core endpoint faces. They cover the entire family at cost four, below $(k-1)\nu=2(k-1)$. The example shows why a global proof must be able to trade selected nonindispensable faces against a group of repairs or otherwise avoid choosing all of them at the outset.
