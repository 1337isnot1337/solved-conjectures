# Coulter–Do–Moskovsky's conjecture: genus-asymptotic roots

Research manuscript, 21 November 2026. Proposed theorem: all fixed profiles have
the conjectured root limits and eventually strict monotone convergence from the
predicted side. Independent mathematical and source-scope review is pending.
Monotonicity from genus zero is not established. No completed-conjecture count
is assigned to this note while that scope distinction remains unresolved.

## 1. Source and precise theorem proved here

Xavier Coulter, Norman Do and Ellena Moskovsky, *Integration on complex
Grassmannians, deformed monotone Hurwitz numbers, and interlacing phenomena*,
Canadian Journal of Mathematics, published online 30 June 2025,
[arXiv:2308.04015](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04015),
[published full text](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-mathematics/article/integration-on-complex-grassmannians-deformed-monotone-hurwitz-numbers-and-interlacing-phenomena/1D1EAFA4F78A147F6B88ECD63EEA9850),
Conjecture 3.15 specifies the genus-asymptotic roots for each positive profile
and says that convergence below −1 is increasing from below, while convergence
between −1 and zero is decreasing from above.

Write μ=(μ₁,…,μₙ), let d be its total size, and let m₁ be the number of parts
equal to one. The nondegenerate domain is d≥2. For d=1 the only nonzero
connected polynomial is the source's initial H₀,₁(1)=1; all positive-genus
polynomials vanish. No assertion about roots of a zero polynomial is used.

Let H_g(t) denote the source's connected deformed monotone Hurwitz polynomial
for this fixed profile. Set

```math
R=d+2g-2+n,
\qquad
L_d(t)=t\prod_{j=1}^{d-2}(d-1-j+jt).
```

For d≥4 define

```math
\rho=\frac{d-2}{d-1},\qquad
\sigma=\frac{d-3}{d-1},\qquad
A_\mu=(d-2)(d-2+m_1),
```

and normalize by a positive factor:

```math
F_g(t)=\frac{d!(d-2)!\,\mu_1\cdots\mu_n}
 {2(d-1)^R}\,H_g(t).
```

**Theorem.** For every fixed profile of total size d≥4, there is an integer K
such that, coefficientwise in the fixed finite-dimensional space of
polynomials in t,

```math
F_g(t)=L_d(t)-A_\mu\rho^R(1+t)L_{d-1}(t)
 +O(R^K\sigma^R).                                      \tag{1}
```

Consequently all d−1 roots are real and simple for sufficiently large genus.
The zero root is exact. The other roots can be indexed increasingly by
j=1,…,d−2, and satisfy

```math
r_j=1-\frac{d-1}{j},
\qquad
\alpha_{g,j}=r_j+C_{\mu,j}\rho^R+o(\rho^R),             \tag{2}
```

where

```math
C_{\mu,j}=A_\mu(1+r_j)
 \frac{L_{d-1}(r_j)}{L_d'(r_j)}.                       \tag{3}
```

The quotient in (3) is strictly positive. Thus, whenever r_j<−1, the root
α_(g,j) is eventually below r_j and strictly increases with g. Whenever
−1<r_j<0, it is eventually above r_j and strictly decreases with g. If
r_j=−1, the root is exactly −1. The cases d=2 and d=3 have only the fixed
roots zero, or −1 and zero, respectively.

This is a theorem for every fixed profile and unbounded genus. It is not a
finite numerical case. Equation (2) by itself does not prove monotonicity
starting at g=0; the final section retains that limitation.

## 2. The connected generating function

Fix a permutation of cycle type μ. Let W_μ(u,t) be the ordinary generating
series in factorization length for all monotone factorizations of that
permutation, weighted by t to the number of distinct larger entries in the
transpositions. Let C_μ(u,t) restrict to transitive factorizations. The source's
Definition 3.1 gives

```math
[u^R]C_\mu(u,t)=\mu_1\cdots\mu_n\,H_g(t).             \tag{4}
```

The character formula in the source's Proposition 3.3 is

```math
W_\mu(u,t)=\frac1{d!}\sum_{\lambda\vdash d}
 f^\lambda\chi^\lambda_\mu
 \prod_{c\in\operatorname{cont}(\lambda)}
 \frac{1-(1-t)cu}{1-cu}.                              \tag{5}
```

Here f^λ is the irreducible dimension, χ^λ is its symmetric-group character,
and the multiset of contents consists of column minus row, both numbered
from zero. Contents equal to zero contribute the factor one.

For a set partition π of the indexed cycles {1,…,n}, let μ_B be the profile
whose parts have indices in the block B. Then

```math
C_\mu(u,t)=\sum_{\pi\in\Pi_n}
 (-1)^{|\pi|-1}(|\pi|-1)!
 \prod_{B\in\pi}W_{\mu_B}(u,t).                     \tag{6}
```

To justify (6), the orbits of the group generated by a factorization are
unions of cycles of its product. Fixing these orbits gives a set partition of
the indexed cycles and one transitive factorization on each block. Conversely
such block factorizations merge in a unique monotone order: the larger
entries of transpositions belonging to different blocks are distinct. There
is no binomial shuffle multiplier. The number of distinct larger entries is
additive over blocks. Standardizing each block preserves the order and the
weight. Thus W is the sum of products of C over set partitions; Möbius
inversion in the set-partition lattice gives (6).

All expressions in (5)–(6) are rational functions of u with coefficients in
Q[t]. Their poles are reciprocal nonzero integer contents. For a partition
of size s, all contents have absolute value at most s−1.

Finally, every factorization length has the parity of its product:

```math
C_\mu(-u,t)=(-1)^{d-n}C_\mu(u,t).                    \tag{7}
```

This also follows by conjugating every partition in (5), which negates its
contents and multiplies its character by the sign of the permutation.
Products in (6) preserve the same sign. Since R≡d−n modulo two, the positive
and negative poles of equal absolute value contribute equally to [u^R]C.

## 3. The largest poles

Only λ=(d) has content d−1; its dimension and character are both one. No
proper cycle-block partition in (6) has a block of size d. Therefore the
coefficient of 1/(1−(d−1)u) in C is

```math
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{u\to1/(d-1)}(1-(d-1)u)C_\mu(u,t)
 &=\frac{t}{d!}\prod_{j=1}^{d-2}
       \frac{d-1-j+jt}{d-1-j}\\
 &=\frac{L_d(t)}{d!(d-2)!}.                          \tag{8}
\end{aligned}
```

The conjugate column partition supplies the negative pole. By (7), their
combined contribution to [u^R]C is

```math
\frac{2(d-1)^R}{d!(d-2)!}\,L_d(t).                  \tag{9}
```

The roots of L_d are simple and are exactly the limits in Conjecture 3.15.
The next section determines the direction of approach, which the leading
term alone would leave undecided.

## 4. The next poles and the singleton cancellation

Put k=d−2 and assume d≥4. A partition of d has content k only if it is
(d) or (d−1,1). Both have a single cell of this content. The only proper
cycle-block partitions in (6) that can have a pole at u=1/k consist of a
block of size d−1 and an isolated fixed point. There are m₁ such choices,
each with Möbius coefficient −1. Every other term has contents of absolute
value at most d−3.

Define

```math
B_k(t)=\frac{t}{(k-1)!}
 \prod_{j=1}^{k-1}(k-j+jt)
 =\frac{L_{d-1}(t)}{(d-3)!}.                         \tag{10}
```

For the row partition (d), removing its denominator 1−ku and evaluating at
u=1/k gives B_k(t)(1−(k+1)t). For the hook partition (d−1,1), the extra
content −1 instead gives B_k(t)(k+1−t)/(k+1). Its dimension is k+1 and its
character is m₁−1: the standard representation is the permutation
representation on d points with its trivial line removed. A row partition
of size d−1 gives B_k(t), with coefficient 1/(d−1)! in its character sum.

Multiplying the positive-pole residue by d! and factoring out B_k(t), the
three contributions are therefore

```math
\begin{aligned}
 &(1-(k+1)t)+(m_1-1)(k+1-t)-m_1(k+2)\\
 &\hspace{35mm}=-(k+m_1)(1+t).                        \tag{11}
\end{aligned}
```

The fixed-point character term and the disconnected singleton term are both
necessary. Omitting the latter would give a wrong, profile-dependent sign.
Thus the coefficient of 1/(1−(d−2)u) in C is exactly

```math
-\frac{d-2+m_1}{d!(d-3)!}(1+t)L_{d-1}(t).            \tag{12}
```

Again the negative pole contributes equally to coefficients of the permitted
parity. The contribution of this pair to [u^R]C is

```math
-\frac{2(d-2)^R(d-2+m_1)}{d!(d-3)!}
 (1+t)L_{d-1}(t).                                   \tag{13}
```

All remaining poles have reciprocal integer contents of absolute value at
most d−3. Pole orders are bounded for fixed μ. A partial-fraction term
(1−cu)^(−a) has coefficient binom(R+a−1,a−1)c^R. The polynomial part in u
has no coefficients for sufficiently large R. Because only finitely many
terms and t coefficients occur, the remainder is O(R^K(d−3)^R) in any norm
on the fixed polynomial space. Equations (4), (9), and (13), followed by the
normalization in Section 1, prove (1).

## 5. Roots and strict monotone tails

Since L_d has d−1 distinct real roots, coefficientwise convergence F_g→L_d
gives one real root of F_g in each of d−1 disjoint small real neighborhoods
for sufficiently large g. This can be proved directly by preserving the
opposite endpoint signs around each simple root and counting the degree.
Each neighborhood then has exactly one root and it is simple. Thus there
are no remaining nonreal roots for large g.

Fix a nonzero limiting root r_j. Expanding (1) there, using L_d'(r_j)≠0,
gives

```math
\alpha_{g,j}-r_j
 =A_\mu\rho^R(1+r_j)\frac{L_{d-1}(r_j)}{L_d'(r_j)}
  +O(\rho^{2R}+R^K\sigma^R),                         \tag{14}
```

which proves (2). The error is o(ρ^R), since 0≤σ<ρ<1.

The nonzero roots of L_(d−1) strictly interlace those of L_d. Indeed, for
j=1,…,d−2, the root r_j=1−(d−1)/j lies strictly between the two adjacent
values 1−(d−2)/(j−1) and 1−(d−2)/j whenever those values exist. Cancelling
the common factor t, both polynomials have positive leading coefficient, so

```math
\frac{L_{d-1}(r_j)}{L_d'(r_j)}>0.                    \tag{15}
```

If r_j≠−1, the constant C_(μ,j) in (3) is nonzero and has the sign of
1+r_j. This proves the side of approach. Since increasing genus adds two
to R, (14) also gives

```math
\alpha_{g+1,j}-\alpha_{g,j}
 =C_{\mu,j}(\rho^2-1)\rho^R+o(\rho^R).               \tag{16}
```

The main term is nonzero and has the opposite sign to C_(μ,j). This proves
eventually strict increasing convergence below −1 and eventually strict
decreasing convergence between −1 and zero.

The zero root is exact because any nonempty factorization contributes a
positive power of t. The source's Proposition 3.9 gives palindromicity of
H_g(t)/t with degree d−2. When d is odd, this degree is odd, so −1 is an
exact root. These facts also settle d=2 and d=3 directly: the nonzero
polynomials are positive multiples of t and t(1+t), respectively.

## 6. Source scope and remaining work

The published Conjecture 3.15 is an assertion as genus tends to infinity, and
the source illustrates its monotonicity claim with genera 10 through 20.
The theorem above proves all the specified limits and the asserted direction
on a sufficiently large tail, uniformly over no finite profile cutoff.

If “the convergence ... is increasing from below” is intended to require
monotonicity at every genus starting with zero, that stronger assertion is
not proved here. A full-resolution label is withheld until independent
source-scope review decides whether the precise asymptotic theorem resolves
the published conjecture or is a substantive partial result. The separate
Conjectures 3.13–3.14, which require all-genus real-rootedness and
profile-increment interlacing, are not proved by this argument.

The source's character formula and its palindromicity theorem are credited
inputs. Initial prior-art searches inspected the original full published
paper, both web engines, the separate MathDB genus-asymptotic entry, global
GitHub issue searches by source identifier and author/root-limit phrases,
and the repo's earlier bt-deformation notes. No earlier full root-asymptotic
proof was located in that bounded scope. Global code search is quota-limited;
it is not claimed complete. Further source review remains necessary.
