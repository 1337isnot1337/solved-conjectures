# A barrier to universal maximum-cut multiflow routing

There are maximal triangle-free unweighted graphs in every open subinterval of the unresolved density band of [Erdős #23](https://www.erdosproblems.com/23) whose maximum cut is unique up to exchange of its two parts, but for which routing its monochromatic edges through its crossing edges needs arbitrarily large congestion. The obstruction does not give a counterexample to the deletion bound. It shows that the [low-congestion certificate](MULTIFLOW.md) cannot hold for all maximum cuts, even if one assumes saturation and removes all ties by allowing arbitrary vertex weights before blowing up.

## 1. Turn a cut-feasible network into an exact maximum cut

Start with a connected undirected network H on n vertices having positive rational supply capacities c_e and arbitrary nonnegative rational demands d_uv between distinct vertices. Suppose every cut's supply is at least its crossing demand.

Give every original vertex weight one. Replace each supply edge e by a two-edge path with a new middle vertex of weight 2c_e, so both edges have product capacity 2c_e. For each positive demand d_uv use two new terminal copies u',v', and attach each to its original vertex by a private two-edge path. Give u' weight one and v' weight d_uv. Choose the private middle weights so both legs of either path have product capacity strictly larger than d_uv. Add one demand edge u'v' of product weight d_uv. No other edges are added.

Put all originals and terminal copies on one side, and all middle vertices on the other. The two-edge paths all cross. Only the terminal demand matching is monochromatic. The graph is triangle-free: the crossing graph is bipartite, and the endpoints of any added matching edge have disjoint neighborhoods and do not touch other matching edges.

This cut is uniquely optimal, up to exchanging sides. In an alternative cut let S record which vertices flip. On the original vertices the restricted S cuts some supplies, and each such subdivided path loses at least twice the original capacity. A demand gained by the alternative cut either crosses the induced original split, or one of its private terminal paths has endpoints with different flip statuses. In the second case that private path alone loses more than the demand weight. Distinct demands have disjoint private edges. The cut condition and the factor of two make the loss strictly greater than the gain if the original split is nontrivial. If all original vertices have the same flip status and some terminal copy differs, there is a strict private loss for each gained demand, and at least one strict positive private loss even if no demand is gained. If all originals and terminal copies agree but a middle vertex differs, the change loses a positive path-edge weight and gains nothing. This exhausts the nontrivial changes.

## 2. The embedded routing obstruction

Use a family of bounded-degree expanders H with edge expansion at least a fixed positive rational h, supply capacities one, and demands h/n on every unordered distinct pair. A split with sides of sizes s and n-s has demand (h/n)s(n-s), at most h min(s,n-s), so it satisfies the cut condition.

Assign length 1/2 to each leg of the subdivided supply edges, and length zero to private legs. The distance between two terminal copies of a demand is the original graph distance of their two originals. The total capacity times length is 2|E(H)|=O(n). For any demand routing with congestion K, the sum of routed flow times its length is at most K times this denominator. It is at least
$
(h/n)\sum_{u<v}\operatorname{dist}_H(u,v)=\Omega(n\log n).
$
Indeed a ball of radius a sufficiently small constant times log n contains fewer than half of the vertices in a bounded-degree graph. Hence the required congestion is unbounded. This is the standard expander flow-cut gap, now realized by product weights in an actual triangle-free maximum cut.

## 3. Density, saturation and an unweighted graph

Fix the desired finite congestion lower bound and an open density window inside (0.2486,0.3197). Choose an expander gadget with congestion lower bound strictly larger than the desired bound. Rescale its vertex weights to a sufficiently small positive total mass. Add a complete bipartite host on two weighted vertices, joined to the gadget by a single bridge. The bipartite host weights can realize any prescribed ordered density strictly between 0 and 1/2 before scaling, so take one in the interior of the desired window. The bridge and the scaled gadget change density by an arbitrarily small amount. Both cuts are uniquely optimal on their two components, and the bridge fixes the relative orientation. Give the bridge and host edge length zero. They do not shorten a gadget path since the bridge is the only connection.

To saturate, for every old nonedge that has no common neighbor, add one new vertex adjacent to its endpoints. The new vertices start independent, so this remains triangle-free. Now add permissible edges until the graph is maximal triangle-free. No added edge can join two old vertices, because every such nonedge already has a common neighbor. Give the new vertices arbitrarily small positive weights. Their incident edge costs have arbitrarily small sum. By the original cut's strict optimality on the finite old support, every optimal cut of the enlarged graph still restricts to the original cut, up to exchange of sides. This holds even though the new vertices may not all have the same placement in those optimum cuts.

Give every new crossing edge length exceeding the diameter of H. No new path can shorten any old demand distance. The old length-times-capacity denominator is positive, and the added contribution is arbitrarily small. Thus the same strict congestion lower bound survives saturation. The density window survives as well. One can perturb all weights rationally and by an arbitrarily small amount to make the new maximum cut unique up to exchange of sides. For a connected graph two inequivalent cuts have distinct crossing edge sets, hence distinct quadratic weight polynomials. There are only finitely many equality hypersurfaces to avoid. The density, original cut restriction and strict congestion inequality hold on an open set of weights, so the rational perturbation can preserve all three.

Finally replace each vertex by an independent class in proportion to its rational weight. Product-weighted cut costs and unweighted blow-up cut costs agree up to the common square scale. More explicitly, sample one representative in every class from any blow-up cut. The expected induced cost is the blow-up cost divided by the square scale. If the blow-up cut is optimal, every sampled cut must be optimal. Uniqueness on the weighted template forces each class unsplit: fixing the others and varying only one vertex cannot produce two complementary cuts. Thus the unique weighted optimum lifts to a unique unweighted optimum, up to exchange. The blow-up is still maximal triangle-free, since distinct nonadjacent template vertices have a common neighbor, and a pair in one class has a neighbor by connectedness. Copy each length to every corresponding crossing edge of the blow-up. Each path projects to a quotient walk, so the flow lower bound scales by the same square factor in its numerator and denominator. Ordered density is preserved exactly.

## What this rules out

Neither maximum-cut optimality, triangle-free saturation, uniqueness of the maximum cut, nor restricting to the middle density band implies any uniform congestion bound at all. The obstruction lives on small attached support, so this does not rule out a routing argument for extremizers of a prescribed support type, or one in which a small share of demands may be discarded. A different certificate must exploit such an additional hypothesis if it relies on improving congestion.

The standard network lower bound is classical; see [Leighton–Rao, JACM 1999](https://snap.stanford.edu/class/cs224w-readings/leighton99mincut.pdf) and [Hoory–Linial–Wigderson](https://webusers.imj-prg.fr/~julien.marche/Ref_GL_GT/LinialHooryWigderson_expander_survey.pdf) for bounded-degree expanders. The construction here concerns its representation by the unique maximum cut of an unweighted maximal triangle-free graph, with prescribed density window.
