# Rooted MaxCut envelopes converge on all triangle-free graphons

This resolves a methodological question left provisional in the [middle-band research notes](https://erdosproblems.ai/wiki/research/triangle_free_bipartization/proof_sketch), **not** the underlying [Erdős bipartization conjecture](https://www.erdosproblems.com/23). It concerns all root counts and all triangle-free graphons, including non-binary ones. A finite-order numerical extension is not involved.

## Statement and definitions

For a symmetric graphon W:[0,1]²→[0,1], let m(W) be the infimum of its ordered monochromatic-edge mass over measurable two-colourings of [0,1]. In the W-sampling model, use k independent uniform root locations, with mutually independent Bernoulli edges conditional on all locations, each with probability W at its two endpoints. Each fresh vertex has a binary adjacency profile to the k roots.

A deterministic **profile rule** chooses, from the labelled sampled root graph alone, a map from binary profiles to two colours. It uses this same map on both endpoints of a fresh test pair, without seeing that pair's edge. Let U_k(W) be the minimum expected ordered monochromatic-edge mass of such rules. This is the labelled formulation of the conditional root-type envelope used in [Ferudun §3](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.28041v1): relabelling a root graph merely permutes profile coordinates, so the conditional optima agree on isomorphic types if profile coordinates are permuted with the roots.

**Theorem.** On the compact space of triangle-free graphons modulo cut-distance zero, U_k decreases uniformly to m. In particular, if the maximum of m on the closed hard density band is strictly less than 2/25, some finite k gives U_k below 2/25 throughout that band.

## Basic inequalities and continuity

For any roots and conditioning data used to pick the rule, the random profiles of two fresh points are conditionally independent given their respective locations. Write q(x) for the resulting conditional probability of colour 1. Its expected monochromatic-edge cost is ∫W(x,y)[q(x)q(y)+(1−q(x))(1−q(y))] dxdy, which is at least m(W). Indeed partition [0,1] into m equal intervals and use an independent uniform threshold per interval to convert q(x) to an indicator. For pairs in different intervals the expected cost is unchanged, and the same-interval pairs contribute at most 1/m to the possible error. Let m→∞. This proves U_k≥m. Ignoring an extra root proves U_{k+1}≤U_k.

For a given labelled root graph and candidate profile map, its unconditioned contribution is a finite sum of labelled induced densities on k+2 vertices. Taking the minimum of these contributions separately for each root graph and then summing is a finite continuous expression in the cut metric by the graphon counting lemma. No division by rare type probabilities is required. The objective m(W) is also cut-continuous: for each cut the two diagonal rectangles differ by at most twice the cut norm, uniformly over cuts.

## Ideal measurable features

Fix a triangle-free W. Let T be its self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt operator on L²[0,1], and let

    K(x,a) = ∫ W(x,z) W(a,z) dz

be the kernel of T². Take infinite independent uniform anchors a_i. Almost surely their K-columns are dense in the essential range of the strongly measurable map a↦K(·,a) into separable L²: every ball of positive probability in a countable base is visited. Let F be the completed sigma algebra generated by the functions K(·,a_i). Its closed subspace L²(F) contains almost all columns by this density, hence contains the range of T². Self-adjointness gives ker T²=ker T and therefore the same closure of the ranges of T and T². In particular T has range in L²(F).

Consequently W is F⊗F-measurable modulo null sets: projection onto L²(F) in the first variable leaves the kernel unchanged since it leaves T unchanged, and symmetry gives the same in the second variable. Equivalently one may check the projection identity first against a countable dense set of L² test functions and then use uniqueness of square-integrable kernels.

Choose a dense list of thresholds in (0,1) avoiding all atoms in the joint law of K(X,A) for independent uniform X,A. Let P_j be the finite measurable partition from the first j anchors, using the first j thresholds in each coordinate. These partitions increase to F. If W_j is the conditional expectation of W on P_j⊗P_j, then ||W−W_j||₁→0 almost surely by martingale convergence.

The within-cell W-mass D_j=∫ W(x,y) 1[P_j(x)=P_j(y)] dxdy also tends to zero almost surely. For a.e. pair x,y whose K-rows differ in L², the iid anchors distinguish them almost surely; Fubini gives this simultaneously up to a null set of pairs. If K-rows agree, T applied to the difference of W-rows is zero. Almost every W-row is orthogonal to ker T (test a countable orthonormal basis of that kernel), so the identical K-rows entail identical W-rows. On the triangle-free graphon, W(x,y)K(x,y)=0 for a.e. pair. For identical W-rows, K(x,y)=||W(x,·)||₂², so W(x,y)=0 except possibly at points of the row that is zero a.e.; these exceptional pairs also have W-mass zero by Fubini. This proves the assertion about D_j.

Given a cut of cost at most m(W)+η, average its indicator on each P_j-cell. The resulting fractional assignment has the same cost as the cut against the block-constant W_j, so its cost against W is at most m(W)+η+2||W−W_j||₁. Round each cell with an independent coin of that cell's fractional probability. Across distinct cells the expected cost is unchanged; within a cell the increase is at most D_j in total. Some deterministic rule on the ideal finite bins therefore costs at most m(W)+η+2||W−W_j||₁+D_j. By bounded convergence its expected best cost tends to m(W) as j→∞ and η→0.

## Simulating and choosing the rule with only a finite graph sample

Fix j. Use q probe roots z_1,…,z_q in addition to the j anchors. For a fresh vertex x, estimate the i-th ideal feature by

    K_hat(x,a_i) = q⁻¹ ∑_l A(x,z_l) A(a_i,z_l).

Here A denotes the sampled adjacency bit; a rule can read the first bit in the fresh profile and the second in the root graph. Conditional on the two locations x,a_i, the summands are iid bounded variables of mean K(x,a_i). Their average converges in probability to K(x,a_i). None of the finitely many thresholds has positive probability of equality, so for each endpoint the estimated bin differs from its ideal bin with probability tending to zero. For any bin-colour map, the expected absolute difference of its true and estimated costs, with all sampled anchors and probes averaged, is bounded by the probability that either fresh endpoint has an incorrect bin. The same bound holds uniformly, in expectation, for the maximum over the finitely many maps: conditional on the base roots, the misbin probability controls their maximum conditional cost discrepancy. Hence the conditional best *estimated* rule approaches the ideal cost in expectation.

The conditionally best estimated rule could still depend on latent root coordinates, so it is not directly admissible. Add l independent validation pairs as additional roots. For each of the finitely many bin-colour maps compute the empirical mean of 'edge and same colour' on these pairs, using only their adjacency to the probes and the known anchor–probe adjacencies. Conditional on the original anchor/probe locations and their induced graph, these validation observations are iid across pairs with means equal to the conditional costs. Their empirical means converge uniformly over the finite rule family, by a union bound and the uniform variance bound, with expected maximum error tending to zero. Choose an empirical minimizer by a fixed tie break. Its expected true cost is at most the expected conditional optimum plus twice that expected maximum error.

The chosen map depends only on the labelled root graph (including all validation vertices). The output colour of a new point depends only on its profile to those roots, via the probes. This is an admissible k=j+q+2l root rule. First take j large, then q large, then l large. Its expected cost comes arbitrarily close to m(W). Together with U_k≥m this proves pointwise convergence.

Finally t(K₃,W)=0 is closed in the compact graphon space by [Lovász–Szegedy](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0408173) and the [counting lemma](https://lovasz.web.elte.hu/bookxx/hombook-almost.final.pdf). Dini's theorem gives uniform convergence. Intersecting with the closed density band gives the stated consequence for any strict gap.

**What remains.** This does not prove that the closed band has a positive gap below 2/25 and gives no effective small root order or zero-error certificate. It closes the approximation step conditional on the structural gap, and shows why a barrier at any one fixed root order need not be a barrier to the full envelope hierarchy.
