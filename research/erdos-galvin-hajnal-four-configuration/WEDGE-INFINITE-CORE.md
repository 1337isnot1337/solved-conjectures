# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s wedge avoidance problem: the infinite link core

This is a reduction for original 9/B (omit the loose triangle T₁ and paired-page configuration T₉), not part of the completed 9/C independence proof. Work on ω₁ and assume Todorčević's finite-to-one pair rainbow partition principle RP when stated.

The [two-heavy-pair reduction](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/d33afee1b4c7b9117cf9421a5da59a0798e7311b) leaves a family W of triples each with a unique centre, exactly two infinite-codegree centre–leaf pairs from the full hypergraph, and a leaf pair private to the triple. The graph of infinite-codegree pairs is triangle-free. Keep these fixed roles throughout further pruning; do not recompute private pairs in the residual family.

**Peeling.** For each centre a, let L_a have its leaves as vertices and its wedges' private leaf pairs as edges. Repeatedly remove all wedges incident to a finite-degree leaf in their current L_a, updating the graphs at each successor and intersecting at limits. Stop at the stable stage. In the removed family each wedge can be assigned to a centre–leaf pair of finite degree at its removal, such that each pair receives only finitely many assigned wedges. Indeed, assign each removed wedge to one eligible leaf. For any fixed ordered centre–leaf pair (a,b), at the first stage at which it receives an assignment, only finitely many remaining L_a-edges are incident to b, and all future assignments lie among those. An unordered heavy pair plays at most its two possible ordered roles, so it too receives only finitely many wedges.

Under RP the whole removed family has a countable proper vertex colouring. For each heavy pair give it the same pair colour as the private leaf pairs of all wedges assigned to it. The fibres are finite and disjoint: private leaf pairs were unique and are not heavy pairs. Give unused pairs freshly distinct colours. Any rainbow set is independent for the removed wedges. Thus an uncountably chromatic 9/B example under RP must still have uncountably chromatic stable core W*, in which every leaf used at any centre has infinite degree in that centre's link.

**Block protection, in ZFC.** Write S_a for the leaves used in a's stable link. Every triple e of W* that meets S_a in two distinct vertices b,c must contain a. Otherwise, choose at centre a a wedge {a,b,d} with d outside e∪{a}; then a wedge {a,c,f} with f outside e∪{a,d}. Infinite degrees guarantee both choices. Along with e these form a loose triangle, with distinct intersections a,b,c. In fact if e contains a and two members of S_a then its centre must be a, because both a–leaf pairs are heavy and the heavy graph is triangle-free. In particular S_a is independent, and a different centre has no leaf edge wholly inside S_a. This is stronger than excluding simultaneous overlap of two local link edges.

**Independent sets, in ZFC.** Every uncountable X⊆ω₁ contains an uncountable W*-independent subset. If some S_a∩X is uncountable, it supplies one. Otherwise all S_a∩X are countable. Recursively choose ω₁ distinct points from X, at each stage avoiding every S_a of a previously chosen point a and the unique centres (where defined) of all pairs of previously chosen points. This excludes only countably many points at each countable stage. Any triple in the selected set would have its centre either earlier than a leaf, contrary to avoidance of S_a, or later than both leaves, contrary to avoidance of the previously determined centre. This does not by itself give a countable partition into independent sets.

**A club localization.** In the stable core, if every local leaf-neighbour set N_{L_a}(b) is nonstationary in ω₁, then the core has a club independent set. Choose a club disjoint from each such neighbourhood and diagonally intersect over all pairs of points below each potential club member. Also intersect with the club of ordinals closed under the partial unique-centre map on pairs. For a triple of the resulting club, its largest member cannot be its centre, by the closure; it also cannot be the later leaf, by the chosen clubs. This is not a countable chromatic result: the complement of one club may still be uncountable and support hard examples.

The unsolved step is a countable colouring of the stable protected-block core in an appropriate model, or a construction showing that RP is insufficient for 9/B. See [#1059](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1059).
