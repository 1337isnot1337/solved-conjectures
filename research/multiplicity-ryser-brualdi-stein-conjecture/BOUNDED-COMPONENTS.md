# Multiplicity Ryser–Brualdi–Stein conjecture

**Partial research.** The conjecture holds for all sufficiently large instances whose positive-quota colours induce connected components of uniformly bounded order. This includes arbitrary quotas, even if some positive quotas stay equal to one. A more general result permits a bounded number of prescribed edges of other colours that can connect the small components into one large graph. The unrestricted conjecture remains open. [Tracking issue #533](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/533)

## Target and sources

Given a decomposition of $K_{n,n}$ into perfect matchings and nonnegative quotas totalling $n-1$, the conjecture asks for a matching with exactly those quotas. Equivalently one can work with any simple bipartite graph that is the union of disjoint perfect matchings, because the regular bipartite complement in $K_{n,n}$ has a perfect matching decomposition. Anastos, Fabian, Müyesser and Szabó formulate the multiplicity conjecture. Boyadzhiyska, Christoph and Szabó prove it for three nonzero quotas and leave the general case open. The November 24, 2026 search located no complete resolution or earlier statement of the bounded-component theorem below. Priority is provisional. [Anastos et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03100v2) · [Boyadzhiyska et al., Theorems 1.2 and 5.1 and Section 5.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15167v2)

## 1. The profile lattice

Work with a palette of $a\ge1$ colours numbered from $1$. Each colour is a perfect matching on the same two sides. Parallel edges with different colours are allowed in the lemmas. A profile lists the number of selected edges of each colour.

Consider a collection of connected components of such a factor, each component containing a perfect matching of each colour. For a component with $s$ vertices per side, let $P$ be the set of its perfect matching profiles. Let $\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb Z^a$ be generated by $p-se_1$ over every component type in the collection and every $p\in P$. The coordinate sum vanishes on $\Lambda$. In $\Gamma=\mathbb Z^a/\Lambda$, let $w_i$ be the class of $e_i$ and let $K$ be the subgroup generated by the differences $w_i-w_j$.

**Potentials.** On each component there are vertex labels $u_x,v_y\in\Gamma$ on its left and right sides, such that every edge $xy$ of colour $i$ satisfies $u_x+v_y=w_i$. To prove this, let $z$ be the signed alternating edge vector of any even cycle. Then $z+\mathbf1$ is nonnegative integral and has constant degree $a$ at every vertex. It decomposes into $a$ perfect matchings by the bipartite matching theorem, applied successively. The same is true for $\mathbf1$. Consequently the signed colour count of $z$ lies in $\Lambda$, so potentials can be propagated from a left root labelled zero without a cycle inconsistency. This also covers parallel edges by a cycle of length two.

The map taking a left vertex along its colour-$j$ edge and back along a colour-$i$ edge is a permutation of the left vertices. It shifts the left potential by $w_i-w_j$. Thus every element of $K$ occurs as a left potential. Connectivity and the edge equations show that all left potentials belong to $K$, and the colour-$1$ bijection then gives exactly the coset $w_1+K$ on the right. The profile class of any matching is the sum of its matched vertex potentials. In particular a perfect matching contributes $sw_1$.

**Exposing a pair.** In any connected regular bipartite graph, deleting any one left vertex and any one right vertex leaves a perfect matching. For a nonempty proper subset of the left side the neighbour set is strictly larger. Equality in the degree count would put all edges incident with those neighbours into that left subset and disconnect the graph. Deleting one right vertex therefore preserves Hall's inequalities for the remaining left side. The case $s=1$ gives an empty matching.

It follows that near-perfect matchings in any one of the components, together with perfect matchings elsewhere, meet every additive profile class of the correct total degree. Fix the omitted vertex on one side and vary the other. Its potential ranges through a full coset of $K$, and profile classes of a fixed coordinate sum form one coset of $K$. Thus a congruence valid for perfect profiles cannot obstruct the near-perfect problem, even when the components are heterogeneous.

## 2. Placing colours with small quotas

Let $A$ be a nonempty subpalette of the colours in a factor on $n+n$ vertices, and let $a=|A|$. Call the other colours rare for the construction, and write $S$ for the sum of their quotas. Put $G_A$ for the union of the colours in $A$ and suppose every connected component of $G_A$ has at most $b$ vertices per side. The components are individually regular, since every colour in $A$ is perfect on each one. Write $m$ for their number.

Put $J=\sum_{j=1}^b (j!)^a$, an upper bound on the number of component types preserving both sides and the names of the colours. Set $q=b^{a-1}$ and $T=S+q+ab^2+2$. A type is frequent for this argument if it has at least $T$ components. The following explicit sufficient conditions will be used.

```math
\begin{aligned}
m &> (S+1)b^S\bigl(J(T-1)+S\bigr),\\
r_i &\ge b\bigl(J(T-1)+S+1+q+Jb\bigr) \qquad(i\in A).
\end{aligned}
```

Here $r_i$ denotes the target quota. We prove that these conditions give a matching of the full prescribed profile, whose coordinate sum is $n-1$.

On the components of $G_A$ form a directed multigraph. An edge of a rare colour contributes an arc from the component of its left endpoint to the component of its right endpoint. Each such colour has at least one outgoing arc and at most $b$ incoming arcs at each component. List the multiset of desired rare colours in any order as a word of length $S$.

We can take a walk with any remaining subword of length at most $S$, while avoiding all infrequent components and all components already used. For a forbidden component and a prescribed prefix of length $j$, at most $b^j$ starting components can reach that component by a walk with that prefix. There are at most $J(T-1)+S$ forbidden components while the procedure is running. The strict bound on $m$ leaves a starting component from which no walk with a prescribed prefix can hit any of them. A whole walk exists from that start because each colour has an outgoing arc everywhere.

If the walk is simple, reserve it as the final path. Otherwise take the simple directed cycle from the first repeated vertex and reserve it. Remove that cycle's contiguous segment of the word, and find a new walk for the remaining word avoiding the reserved components. Continue until there is a simple path, counting a single unused component as the path when the word is empty. The selected cycles and the one path use distinct components, and altogether at most $S+1$ components. The selected original rare edges have no common endpoints, since a component on a path or cycle has at most one selected departure on its left and one selected arrival on its right.

In a component with both an arrival and a departure, fill the remaining vertices with a perfect matching using colours in $A$, after deleting the two occupied vertices. At the start of a nonempty path there is only an occupied left vertex, so choose an arbitrary right vertex to leave unmatched. At the end choose an arbitrary left vertex to leave unmatched. For an empty path expose an arbitrary left and right vertex in its reserved component. Every such choice is possible by the exposure fact. Other components of the selected paths and cycles contribute a matching of their full size after counting the selected rare edges. Globally there is just one unmatched left and right vertex, once the unreserved components are filled perfectly.

## 3. Completing the exact quotas

Use colour $1$ to fill all infrequent components. Let $\Lambda$ now be the profile lattice from Section 1 generated by the frequent types, using colours in $A$. Every reserved component belongs to such a type. Fix the selected rare edges and the unmatched right vertex. As the unmatched left vertex varies in its prescribed component, the profile class of the selected edges of $A$ in the reserved components ranges through every class of the required total degree, by the potential argument. Thus we may choose that vertex so that the residual quota vector $c$ for the unreserved frequent components satisfies

```math
c-Me_1\in\Lambda,
```

where $M$ is the sum of their orders per side. Indeed, the total degree of the prescribed profile minus the fixed infrequent profiles and the reserved profiles is exactly $M$. The rare edges are counted separately and have already met their quotas.

Choose one frequent type as a reservoir and let $d$ be its size per side. Work modulo $d$ in each coordinate. The image of $\Lambda$ is generated by the reductions of the vectors $p-se_1$ from Section 1, and lies in the subgroup with coordinate sum zero. It has size at most $d^{a-1}\le q$. A shortest walk in the finite directed Cayley graph expresses the residue of $c-Me_1$ as a sum of at most $q$ generating vectors with nonnegative multiplicities. In a finite group positive powers also express inverses. Take one unused component of the indicated frequent type for each generator, and fill it with the indicated perfect matching. This changes the residual profile and total to $c',M'$ with $c'\equiv M'e_1\pmod d$.

For each nonreservoir type, divide its remaining components into batches of $d$ and fewer than $d$ leftovers. Fill all leftovers with colour $1$. They have altogether at most $J b^2$ edges, and their total is congruent to $M'$ modulo $d$. The quotas that now remain are multiples of $d$. They are nonnegative because the original lower bound on every abundant quota covers all infrequent components, all reserved components, the at most $q$ correcting components, and all leftovers, even if all their edges were charged to one colour.

Each full batch contains at most $b^2$ edges in a monochromatic perfect matching. Greedily give each such batch one colour with enough remaining quota. The reservoir still contains at least $T-(S+1)-q=ab^2+1$ components, so the sum of the remaining quotas is always greater than $ab^2$ when processing a batch. Some colour therefore has enough quota. Batch sizes are multiples of $d$, so they preserve divisibility by $d$. After the batches, assign each reservoir component one colour, with exactly $c_i/d$ components of colour $i$ for the final residual counts $c_i$. This completes the matching.

## 4. Bounded components for all quota profiles

Suppose the union of all positive-quota colours already has components of size at most $b$ per side. Every subpalette retains that bound. For simple factors, the number $k$ of positive-quota colours is at most $b$. The two sufficient bounds above can be made uniform over $1\le a\le k$. In particular there is a monotone function $B(S)$ depending only on $b,k,S$ such that if every quota in $A$ is at least $B(S)$, the quota inequality holds and $n/b$ exceeds the required bound for $m$. Use $m\ge n/b$.

Initially take every positive-quota colour in $A$ and $S=0$. Whenever the smallest quota still in $A$ is below $B(S)$, move that colour out of $A$ and add its quota to $S$. With $R_0=0$ and $R_{j+1}=R_j+B(R_j)$, after $j$ such moves one has $S\le R_j$. If $n-1>R_k$, some colour remains in $A$, and the procedure stops with the required bounds. This proves the bounded-component theorem, including quota profiles with a mixture of bounded and growing positive entries.

In particular, any sequence of counterexamples of unbounded order must have positive-quota components of unbounded order. The argument also covers some connected factors because the rare edges can join the small components. It does not supply the decomposition into abundant and rare colours in the general case, where the positive-quota part may have large components and all individual quotas may be small.

## Replay

Run `python3 research/multiplicity-ryser-brualdi-stein-conjecture/verify.py` from the repository root. The replay enumerates perfect and specified-hole profiles, generates the corresponding residue subgroup directly, and checks 28 fixed-hole exposures on mixtures of component types. A separate enumeration checks 33 exact profiles with two rare edges, using a loop and a path in the component quotient. The proof gives the stated unbounded scope. A second reader and a formal proof have yet to verify the argument.
