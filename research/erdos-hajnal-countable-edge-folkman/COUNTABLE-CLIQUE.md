# Countable-clique counterpart

The second question in [Erdős #1174](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1174) asks for a K_ω₁-free graph every countable edge colouring of which has a monochromatic K_ω. This note adapts [the compactness descent](COMPACTNESS-DESCENT.md). Unlike the first question, the application below explicitly assumes a measurable above the prepared supercompact. It does not resolve either question in ZFC or reduce their known relative consistency assumptions.

**Theorem.** If κ is indestructibly supercompact and a measurable θ>κ exists, then already in the current universe a graph of size <κ witnesses the second question.

First obtain 2^κ=κ⁺ by collapsing the ground size of 2^κ to κ⁺ with a <κ⁺-directed-closed Lévy collapse. It adds no subsets of κ, preserves κ⁺, and has size <θ (if equality already holds use the trivial forcing). Small forcing preserves the measurability of θ. Apply [Komjáth–Shelah, *A consistent edge partition theorem for infinite graphs*, Theorem 1](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95711/414.pdf#page=2) with their μ=κ, θ measurable, and Y a graph on κ vertices consisting of one countably infinite clique and isolated vertices. The resulting G arrows Y for κ edge colours and, since Y has no K_ω₁, neither does G. In particular G arrows K_ω for countable edge colours.

Here is the directed-closure check needed for indestructibility, strengthening the chain closure stated in the original. In that forcing a condition has support s of size <κ⁺, an induced edge set g, and a function φ assigning cliques of Y to every clique A of size ≥3 in s, with matching cardinality and increasing by proper end-extension. An extension may place a new vertex into an old clique of size ≥3 only above every vertex of the old clique in the printed tuple order.

For a directed family of <κ conditions, take the union of supports and of their edges; previously covered nonedges are not changed. For any clique A of size ≥3 in the union, whenever A∩s_p has size ≥3 it is an initial segment of A. Indeed if x lies outside s_p, for each y∈A∩s_p take a finite old 3-clique containing y in the intersection, and use directedness to get a stronger member covering x and all edges from x to that 3-clique. The extension rule gives y<x. These supported initial segments cover A: any three specified vertices of A occur together in a family member by directedness. The known images φ_p(A∩s_p) form an end-extension chain. To compare any two, choose a common stronger member and compare the two initial segments and their images inside it. Consequently the union of the images is a clique of Y of size |A|: if there is a maximal segment this is immediate; otherwise use a cofinal chain of distinct segments, whose size and union of sizes determine the cardinal of both unions (strict increases of the segments correspond to strict increases of the images). This defines φ(A). It respects proper end-extension, since for any proper A⊂B with A an initial segment, some supported initial segment of B contains A and an additional point. These images extend every original map. The same finite-clique argument verifies the side condition against adding an earlier point to an old clique, so the union is a common extension. Its support still has size <κ⁺.

Thus the two-step forcing is κ-directed closed and introduces no sequences of ground ordinals of length <κ; κ is still supercompact there. Reflect the arrow G→(K_ω)^2_ω to a graph on <κ vertices as in the first note: combine local avoiding edge colourings with a fine κ-complete ultrafilter were there no such subgraph. The only change is intersecting the countably many large sets corresponding to all edges of a candidate monochromatic K_ω, which is still valid. Relabel the resulting graph by a small ordinal. It descends to the ground by closure; its forbidden-clique condition and countable arrow property descend as well. ∎
