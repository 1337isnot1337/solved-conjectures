# Compactness descent for countable edge Folkman graphs

[Erdős #595](https://www.erdosproblems.com/595) and [#1174(i)](https://www.erdosproblems.com/1174) ask the same question: does some K₄-free graph force a monochromatic triangle in every countable edge colouring? [Issue #1090](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1090). This note gives a conditional ground-model construction, not a ZFC solution or an improvement in relative consistency strength.

**Theorem.** If κ is a supercompact cardinal indestructible by κ-directed-closed forcing, then in the current universe, for every μ<κ and finite t≥3 there is a graph H of size <κ with no K_(t+1) such that H→(K_t)^2_μ. One graph of size at most κ for each t works against every μ<κ, by taking disjoint unions. In particular κ suffices for the countable edge Folkman problem in that very ground model.

**Proof.** Fix μ,t. An inaccessible κ satisfies κ^{<κ}=κ. Apply [Shelah, *Consistency of positive partition theorems for graphs and models*, Lemma 5.1, p.188](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95854/289.pdf#page=22) with its support cardinal λ=κ, monochromatic clique size k(*)=t, and a sufficiently large ambient cardinal (the “θ>l(λ)” alternative in the printed statement, not a further measurable cardinal). The forcing consists of K_(t+1)-free graphs on the increasing ℓ-tuples from a support of size <κ, the edges subject to the fixed interleaving condition in the paper; order is by induced extension on supports.

This forcing is κ-directed closed. Given a directed family of <κ many conditions, take the union of the supports. Use the union of the prescribed edges and no edges on pairs not jointly covered by any condition. Prescribed nonedges remain nonedges by directed compatibility. If a forbidden clique appeared, its finitely many edges would have deciding conditions with one common stronger member in the directed family, a contradiction. The union support has size <κ, all allowed edge types are retained, and the resulting condition extends the family. Thus κ remains supercompact in the extension (compare [Laver's indestructibility](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02761175)), and no sequences of ground ordinals of length <κ are added.

In the extension, let G be Shelah's generic K_(t+1)-free arrow graph. Strong compactness of κ reflects its arrow property to a subgraph on <κ vertices. For detail, otherwise choose a μ-colouring c_X avoiding monochromatic K_t on G[X] for each X∈Pκ(V(G)). Take a fine κ-complete ultrafilter U on Pκ(V(G)). For each edge e, fineness makes the supports containing its endpoints U-large. The μ possible values of c_X(e) partition that large set, so κ-completeness provides exactly one large cell; assign its colour to e. If K_t were monochromatic in the resulting colouring, intersect the large cells for its edges to get the same monochromatic clique under one c_X, contradiction.

The small reflected graph, with vertices relabelled by a ground ordinal below κ, has an edge relation coded by a sequence of length <κ. No such sequence was added, so the relabelled graph H belongs to the ground model. The absence of K_(t+1) and the arrow property both descend: a ground forbidden clique or a ground avoiding colouring would still witness failure in the extension. The disjoint-union claim follows by including one component for each cardinal μ<κ. ∎

Exactly what is used in the reflection step is a fine **μ⁺-complete** ultrafilter on the indicated family (for infinite μ), in the extension by Shelah's specific forcing. This identifies a weaker hypothesis than full indestructibility to look for. Closure alone does not suffice: it descends the small graph but does not produce one. The positive answer from a prepared supercompact is a statement about existing witnesses in prepared models; Shelah's old consistency result itself does not need that large cardinal.
