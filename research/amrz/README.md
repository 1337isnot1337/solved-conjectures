# Aboulker–Matamala–Rochet–Zamora conjecture

Status: unresolved research, 20 November 2026. Issue [#114](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/114).

No conjecture is proved or disproved here. This note makes a possible
counterexample route precise and records why the first proposed family does
not supply one. The bridge-elongation idea is already present in the source;
no novelty claim is made for the reduction.

## Exact target

For a connected finite simple graph, let $\ell(G)$ be its number of distinct
metric lines and let $b(G)$ be its number of bridges. A metric line through
distinct vertices $x,y$ contains exactly the vertices $z$ for which one of
the three distances is the sum of the other two.

Conjecture 2.2 of Aboulker, Matamala, Rochet and Zamora asserts that a finite
exceptional family exists such that every connected graph outside it either
has a pendant edge or satisfies

```math
\ell(G)+b(G)\ge |V(G)|.
```

The source is *A new class of graphs that satisfies the Chen–Chvátal
conjecture*, J. Graph Theory 87 (2018), 77–88,
[DOI 10.1002/jgt.22142](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.22142),
[full primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.06011).
This target is distinct from that paper's Conjecture 2.3, which replaces
bridges by pairs generating universal lines.

## Exact bridge-subdivision reduction

**Lemma.** Replace a bridge $uv$ of $G$ by a path of length $L\ge1$, obtaining
$G_L$. Then restriction to the original vertex set is a bijection between
the metric lines of $G_L$ and those of $G$. Consequently

```math
\ell(G_L)=\ell(G),\qquad
b(G_L)=b(G)+L-1,\qquad
|V(G_L)|=|V(G)|+L-1.
```

**Proof.** Let $A,B$ be the components of $G-uv$, with $u\in A$ and $v\in B$.
Distances within either component are unchanged. All distances from $A$ to
$B$ increase by $L-1$.

First consider a line generated by two original vertices. If both generators
are in $A$, a vertex outside $A$ belongs to the line precisely when

```math
d(x,u)=d(x,y)+d(y,u)
\quad\text{or}\quad
d(y,u)=d(x,y)+d(x,u).
```

This condition is independent of the path length and the choice of outside
vertex. Thus either every added path vertex belongs to the line, or none
does. The same argument applies in $B$. If the generators lie on opposite
sides, every path vertex lies between them. For $z\in A$, membership is
equivalent to

```math
d(x,z)=|d(x,u)-d(z,u)|,
```

and the analogous formula holds in $B$. Again this is independent of $L$.
It follows that lines generated by original vertices restrict to their
original lines. Their extensions are determined uniquely: add all new
path vertices exactly when the original line contains both $u$ and $v$.

Every other line is generated by a new path vertex $t$ and an original
vertex $x$, or by two new path vertices. If $x\in A$, its line with $t$
equals its line with $v$; if $x\in B$, it equals its line with $u$.
For two new path vertices, the line is universal and equals the line
generated by $u,v$. Hence all lines have already been accounted for, and
restriction is the asserted bijection. Each new path edge is a bridge,
while all other edges retain their bridge status. The formulas follow.

**Consequence.** If there exists a connected graph $G$ with no pendant edge,
with a bridge, and with $\ell(G)+b(G)<|V(G)|$, then the full finite-exception
conjecture is false. Subdividing that bridge gives graphs of unbounded order,
still without pendant edges, with the same negative gap. The unresolved task
is to find such a seed or a different infinite family. This note supplies
neither.

## Diagnostic computation

Run `python3 verify_amrz.py` in this directory. It uses only integer graph
distances and set comparisons, with no external packages or floating-point
arithmetic.

The bridge restriction bijection was checked for every bridge of every
connected labeled graph with two through six vertices: 28,907 bridge
instances. This checks the implementation and a finite range of the lemma;
the preceding argument, not the sweep, proves the lemma.

A separate structural screen finds the deficient complete multipartite
graphs of order at most nine and cycles of those orders, removes the
duplicate C3/C4 presentations, and joins each pair at every ordered choice
of roots. No such bridge join violates the inequality. The least gap is
four, attained by joining two copies of C4 by one bridge. Labels and
root symmetries are deliberately not quotiented out; this is not an
isomorphism census or an exhaustive graph search.

## Literature screen and limits

Both web search engines were used for the authors' names, the exact
finite-exception statement, the phrases `pendant edge`, `lines and bridges`,
and the primary preprint identifier. The search was also extended to
recent papers and public research repositories. No full resolution of
Conjecture 2.2 was found. This is a bounded search, not proof of priority.

The following primary work must not be confused with a full resolution:

- Matamala and Zamora, *Lines in bipartite graphs and in 2-metric spaces*,
  [DOI 10.1002/jgt.22574](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.22574): the line-plus-bridge
  inequality holds for bipartite graphs except C4 and K2,3; the universal-pair
  version is proved for 2-metric spaces.
- *Locally Connected Graphs: Metric Properties*, published 24 August 2026,
  [DOI 10.1002/jgt.70116](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.70116): at least as many
  lines as vertices for connected locally connected graphs, except K1,2,2,
  K2,2,2, and K2,2,2,2.
- *Lines on digraphs of low diameter*,
  [DOI 10.1016/j.dam.2025.12.015](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2025.12.015),
  published in 2026: directed and low-diameter special classes.
- The original paper proves the property for a substitution-and-gluing
  class containing chordal and distance-hereditary graphs. Its own
  bridge-elongation discussion already explains why the exclusion of
  pendant edges matters.

The complete-multipartite block route is not being promoted to a new
result: these blocks and their natural compositions largely lie inside
classes already covered by the source. Its diagnostic failure is recorded
to prevent repeating this attempt.
