# A target receiving at least three cross colors forces parity

**Theorem.** In the balanced zero-rainbow-six-cycle [three-block setting](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md), an exact parity cut exists whenever some one part $`X`$ receives at least three of the four cross colors from almost every outside vertex. In particular, the target may have arbitrary mixtures of three- and four-color incoming profiles.

This strengthens both [the all-positive target theorem](ONE-POSITIVE-PART.md) and [the exact-three target theorem](EXACT-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md). A nonparity obstruction to the missing-pair reduction must consequently have positive mass of exact-two incoming profiles, and positive mass of profiles of size at least three, into **each** part; the all-exact-two case is [already excluded](SATURATED-TRIPARTITE.md). The unrestricted Erdős problem remains open.

We use the notation and complementary-palette closure from [THREE-COLOR-PROFILES.md](THREE-COLOR-PROFILES.md) and [EXACT-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md](EXACT-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md), with $`t=1/6`$. It suffices to make all incoming color neighborhoods homogeneous in two equal $`A`$-row classes and find a common pairing, by [the paired target theorem](PAIRED-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md). We may assume both profile sizes occur with positive measure, since the pure cases are known. Thus there are at most three heavy missing labels, because the disjoint missing-label classes do not fill the outside of mass $`4t`$.

## At most two heavy missing labels

The two equal row classes and neighborhood homogeneity are precisely the first theorem of [THREE-COLOR-PROFILES.md](THREE-COLOR-PROFILES.md), which allows a mixture. At least two palette matchings are complete at almost every root of $`X`$. The argument in the last section of [PAIRED-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md](PAIRED-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md) uses only comparisons between three-color profiles, so all three-color profiles use the same pairing $`J`$ even when there are also four-color profiles.

If $`J`$ is globally complete, it is same on the double type of a typical three-color vertex. A four-color vertex with a different two-and-two pairing forces $`J`$ opposite on that whole type. A one-and-three vertex forces $`J`$ opposite on that type or on a positive subset of it: if it is the singleton type, all palettes out of it reverse, and if it is the triple type then its color partnered to the singleton has a positive neighborhood and reverses. Thus four-color vertices must use $`J`$ with a two-and-two split.

If $`J`$ is the one possibly incomplete matching, a four-color vertex with a different two-and-two split would make its globally complete pairing same on both types, contradicting the two opposite tests from the singleton of any three-color vertex. Suppose instead a four-color vertex has a one-and-three split. Its singleton type has both complete tests opposite at every root, while its triple type has at least one complete test same at every root. Within that triple type there is a positive set where both complete tests are same, namely the neighborhood of the color partnered to the singleton under $`J`$. A three-color vertex whose pairing is $`J`$ has both complete tests opposite on its singleton type, so its singleton type must be the reference singleton type. On its double type a return to the singleton must use a complete matching (the $`J`$-partner of the singleton is missing), impossible at the positive subset where both complete tests are same. Therefore there is no one-and-three split, and the common pairing is proved.

## Exactly three heavy missing labels: homogeneity

Name the heavy labels $`a,b,c`$ and the light one $`\ell`$. As in the [exact-three proof](EXACT-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md), only heavy pairs can be absent; a root in $`E_{bc}`$ lacks just the matching $`P_a=\{b,c\}\mid\{a,\ell\}`$, and sends $`\ell`$ to every vertex of $`M_a`$, the set missing $`a`$. The same holds with the names permuted. The optional four-color vertices do not change these facts: a color neighborhood of an exceptional root is prescribed only for the two heavy colors of its absent palette. Call an incoming vertex mixed if its $`\ell`$-neighborhood is not in one row class.

Every present nonlight neighborhood is homogeneous using a nonlight companion and a complementary palette containing $`\ell`$. Any mixed vertex of $`M_a`$ must send $`b`$ only to $`E_{ac}`$ and $`c`$ only to $`E_{ab}`$, exactly as before. In particular $`M_a`$ has at least $`t`$ mass of nonmixed vertices, since if mixed vertices occur, the positive set $`E_{ab}`$ sends its $`b`$-degree $`t`$ to nonmixed $`M_a`$ vertices. This argument takes place wholly in $`X`$ and $`M_a`$ and is unaffected by the presence of four-color vertices. A nonmixed three-color vertex gives two equal row classes, as in the previous proof.

The entire “No mixed vertices survive” argument of [the exact-three proof](EXACT-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md) also carries over for three-color vertices: it uses only the exceptional root sets and the profiles of $`M_a,M_b,M_c`$, not any other outside vertex. In particular, if a group $`M_a`$ had mixed vertices then its associated $`E_{bc}`$ would be null, the other two exceptional groups would be in opposite types, and one of the two whole mass-$`t`$ groups $`M_b,M_c`$ would exhaust the $`\ell`$-degree at every root potentially sending $`\ell`$ into the mixed group in one of its types. This is the same contradiction. Thus all three-color vertices have homogeneous color neighborhoods.

A mixed **four-color** vertex would send its three nonlight colors $`a,b,c`$ only to $`E_{bc},E_{ac},E_{ab}`$, respectively, by the same complementary-palette argument. All three exceptional sets would be positive. Each lies in one type, witnessed by the now-homogeneous $`\ell`$-neighborhood of a vertex from its corresponding heavy missing group. These three exceptional types cannot all be the same: the three nonlight neighborhoods then have total at most $`t`$, and the $`\ell`$-degree cap would make $`\ell`$ fill the other type. Thus two of the exceptional types, say those of $`E_{ac}`$ and $`E_{ab}`$, are a bit $`p`$, and the remaining type of $`E_{bc}`$ is $`1-p`$.

At a typical root from the $`a`$-neighborhood of the mixed four-color vertex, the two palettes to its $`b,c`$-neighborhoods are both opposite, and both have complete tests there (the root is in $`E_{bc}`$). Through any vertex of $`M_a`$ this same root sends $`\ell`$; its two complete tests $`\ell b,\ell c`$ then force both colors $`b,c`$ into type $`p`$ for almost all $`M_a`$ vertices. Thus these vertices send $`\ell`$ to the entire other type $`1-p`$, exhausting the $`\ell`$-degree at every root there, since $`M_a`$ is heavy. This contradicts the positive $`\ell`$-neighborhood of the mixed four-color vertex in that type. Hence all four-color profiles are homogeneous too.

## Exactly three heavy missing labels: pairing

For each nonnull $`E_{bc}`$, let $`p_a`$ be its type. The matching $`P_a`$ is complete on the other type. We claim that some three-color vertex uses a pairing $`P`$ complete on its own double type. Such a pairing is same at all roots of that type and precludes any other three-color pairing by the singleton/double comparison. It also precludes any different two-and-two pairing or a one-and-three split at a four-color vertex, by the same comparison used above. Thus the claim will give a common pairing.

Suppose no such three-color vertex exists. Then every three-color pairing is indexed by a nonnull exceptional set and its double type is that exceptional type. Moreover $`M_a`$ cannot use $`P_a`$: if $`E_{bc}`$ is null it is complete everywhere, and otherwise $`\ell`$ is its singleton of type $`p_a`$ and the pairing is complete on its double type $`1-p_a`$. In particular there must be at least two nonnull exceptional sets.

If precisely two sets are nonnull, indexed by $`b,c`$, the group $`M_b`$ must use $`P_c`$ and $`M_c`$ must use $`P_b`$. In these two groups $`\ell`$ is in the double type, so $`p_b=p_c=p`$. Each group therefore sends the same singleton color $`a`$ to the entire type $`1-p`$. Since the groups are disjoint and both heavy, this exceeds the degree $`t`$, impossible.

If all three exceptional sets are nonnull, every $`M_a`$ chooses a pairing indexed by a different heavy label $`i`$ with $`p_i=p_a`$, since $`\ell`$ is in the double type. Thus all three types agree, say they equal $`p`$. At a root of $`E_{bc}`$ the two complete palettes $`\ell b,\ell c`$ pin each of $`b,c`$ to a fixed type across all of $`M_a`$, since the root sends $`\ell`$ to that entire group. Consequently all $`M_a`$ vertices send the same singleton heavy color $`f(a)\ne a`$ to the whole type $`1-p`$. The corresponding choices $`f(a),f(b),f(c)`$ must be distinct by the heavy masses and the degree cap. Hence $`f`$ is a three-cycle. Exactly one of $`f(c)=b`$ or $`f(b)=c`$ holds. But a root of $`E_{bc}`$, itself of type $`p`$, sends $`b`$-degree $`t`$ into $`M_c`$ and $`c`$-degree $`t`$ into $`M_b`$. Whichever of the two colors is the singleton all goes into type $`1-p`$, a contradiction.

The claim follows. Apply [paired propagation](PAIRED-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md) to conclude parity. Coordination: [#870](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/870).
