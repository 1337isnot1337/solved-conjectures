# Three-color incoming profiles and restricted missing labels

Work in the balanced, zero-rainbow-six-cycle three-block model of [PAIR-GRAPHONS.md](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md). Let $`X`$ be one of the three parts, and put $`t=1/6`$. Its measure is $`2t`$. The palette $`K`$ of four cross colors fills all interpart edges. Assume almost every vertex outside $`X`$ sees at least three colors into $`X`$. For each $`a\in K`$, define $`M_a`$ as the set of outside vertices sending zero $`a`$-degree into $`X`$; these sets are disjoint up to null sets. Call a label heavy when $`\mu(M_a)\ge t`$.

**Theorem.** Suppose at most two labels are heavy, and three-color incoming profiles occur on positive measure. Then $`X`$ has exactly two internal $`A`$-row classes of measure $`t`$. Every incoming color neighborhood is contained in one of the two classes. A three-color vertex sends one entire class in its singleton color, and splits the other class between the two remaining present colors. In particular its profile into the other outside part also uses exactly three colors.

If at most one label is heavy and four-color incoming profiles also occur on positive measure, there is a single pairing of the four cross colors compatible with all incoming profiles: the colors in one pair go only into one class, and those in the other pair into the other. For a three-color vertex the absent color is paired with its saturated color. Thus the missing color of its profile into the other outside part is precisely the partner of the missing color into $`X`$.

Neither claim decides the global parity condition. Use [circle refinement](DETERMINISTIC.md) to work with deterministic kernels, with profile supports and their measures unchanged.

## Palette support

For a root $`w\in X`$, an ordered two-cross-color return with colors $`a,b`$ has zero rooted density exactly when almost every $`a`$-neighbor of $`w`$ outside $`X`$ belongs to $`M_b`$. The neighborhood has measure $`t`$, so this requires $`b`$ heavy. A palette $`\{a,b\}`$ is absent in both orders only when both labels are heavy. In fact the graph of absent unordered palettes at a root is a matching: for fixed $`a`$, its neighborhood cannot be contained in two disjoint sets $`M_b`$. In particular, if at most two labels are heavy, all three complementary matchings of palettes are present on both sides at almost every root except possibly the one containing the two heavy labels as a pair. If at most one label is heavy, all three are present on both sides.

For a matching with both palettes present at a root, all their positive-density endpoints lie in one $`A`$-row class. Indeed concatenate any path using one palette with a reversed path using the complementary palette. Two unequal endpoint rows have an $`A,B`$ two-path with positive density, which would close a forbidden cycle. Fubini gives the same class for the entire endpoint supports of both palettes. This argument needs just one order for each palette to have positive density, and covers every order with positive density.

Fix a typical outside vertex $`y`$ and any present color $`c`$. At least two other colors are present from $`y`$ into $`X`$, so one of them, say $`b`$, pairs with $`c`$ in a matching guaranteed present on both sides at almost every root, even if one fixed matching is not guaranteed. Take roots in the $`b`$-neighborhood of $`y`$ in $`X`$. The two-path $`b,c`$ through $`y`$, with Fubini over the roots and intermediate vertices, puts essentially the entire $`c`$-neighborhood of $`y`$ into a single $`A`$-row class. Apply this to each color.

Take a typical $`y`$ with just three present colors. Its neighborhoods partition $`X`$, so there are at most three row classes. Each positive-measure class weighs at most $`t`$ by symmetry and the internal $`A`$-degree $`t`$. Three classes are impossible: the weights sum to $`2t`$ and each row chooses a subset of them of weight $`t`$, so one class would weigh $`t`$ and the two smaller classes would sum to $`t`$. Rows could only choose the large class or both smaller classes. The two smaller classes must choose the same option by symmetry of the edges between them, contradicting their distinctness. Therefore there are two classes, each of weight $`t`$. A three-color profile partitions them as one plus two, so its singleton has degree $`t`$; the two remaining positive degrees are strictly between zero and $`t`$. In the other outside part each degree is the complement in $`t`$, giving again exactly one zero, one $`t`$, and two strict partial degrees.

## One common pairing in the mixed case

Assume now that at most one label is heavy. For almost every root $`w`$, each of the three matchings has a well-defined same- or opposite-row target, independently of the outside intermediate vertex and which palette of the matching is used. Fix a typical outside vertex with four positive colors. If it splits two-and-two, there is exactly one same-row matching at almost every root in $`X`$, and its identity is the pairing of that vertex's two colors on each class. Hence every four-color vertex uses that same pairing. For a three-color vertex the two colors on the double side must be paired because a two-path between them preserves the class. The absent color partners the singleton, proving the second claim in this case.

It remains to exclude a four-color vertex splitting one-and-three. Such a vertex makes the number of same-row matchings zero on the singleton type and two on the other type. Therefore every other four-color vertex must split one-and-three with the same roles. Every three-color vertex must put its singleton on the same singleton type: otherwise its two positive colors there would give a same-row matching at typical roots of that type. Let $`a(y)`$ be the sole color from $`y`$ into the singleton type for any outside vertex. At a typical root $`w`$ of the other type, let $`m_w`$ be its unique opposite-row matching involution on $`K`$. The edge $`wy`$ then has color $`m_w(a(y))`$, since the palette obtained by returning from $`y`$ to the singleton type changes row. The fixed reference four-color vertex has all three colors other than its singleton occurring with positive degree on this second type, so all three distinct involutions $`m_w`$ occur on sets of positive measure there. These three involutions send every given color to all three other colors. Thus every vertex outside $`X`$ sends all four colors into $`X`$, contradicting the positive measure of three-color profiles. This excludes the split and completes the claim.
