# A third-size homogeneous support region forces parity

**Theorem.** Let six balanced graphon colors have zero rainbow six-cycle density. If some measurable $U$ of measure at least $1/3$ has the property that each color is either absent almost everywhere on $U^2$ or positive almost everywhere on $U^2$, then the coloring is an exact parity model. The side of the parity cut containing $U$ may be larger than $U$.

This strengthens the [half-size support result](HALF-HOMOGENEOUS-SUPPORT.md) and does not restrict the rank or impose any condition on the other squares. It is a structured case of [Erdős #811](https://www.erdosproblems.com/811), not the unrestricted conjecture.

Write $t=1/6$ and $s=\mu(U)$. Let $A$ be the missing colors on $U^2$ and $B$ the others. Each hue in $A$ uses all its degree from $U$ on the cut to $U^c$. Each outside vertex has at most $t$ degree of any hue into $U$, so each $A$ hue has a set of outside roots of measure at least $s$ from which it is positively available into $U$. Outside roots have total degree $s$ into $U$. If $s>1/2$ the balance-capacity argument in the half-size note shows $A$ is empty, immediately giving a rainbow. Assume $1/3\le s\le1/2$. Since $|A|t\le1-s$, there are at most three missing hues when $s>1/3$, and at most four when $s=1/3$.

We will use the following two cycle completions. If two distinct crossing hues are available from one outside root and the four remaining hues are in $B$, the two inside endpoints can be joined by a path of four distinct inside hues. Alternatively, if two outside roots each have two distinct crossing hues available and the four hues are all different with two remaining hues in $B$, join the two resulting two-edge paths by two inside edges in those remaining hues. Almost-everywhere positivity on $U^2$ makes these choices have positive density, by Fubini. Distinct actual vertices can be chosen almost everywhere.

## Zero, one, two or four missing colors

With no missing hues there is a rainbow inside $U$. With one, an outside root seeing that hue into $U$ also sees another hue, since $s>t$. Use the first completion.

With two missing hues $a,b$, use the first completion if their outside availability sets overlap positively. They necessarily overlap if $s>1/3$, since their measures are at least $s$ and $\mu(U^c)=1-s$. Otherwise $s=1/3$, their availability sets have measure exactly $s$ and partition $U^c$, and each supplies the corresponding hue with degree exactly $t$ into $U$. On each such outside part there is another available hue from $B$, because $s=2t$. If the two parts cannot select distinct extra hues, they both use the same single hue $d$ throughout the remaining crossing degree. Then the crossing mass of $d$ from $U^c$ is $t(1-s)>ts$, contrary to degree capacity on $U$. Hence the second completion works.

Four missing colors require $s=1/3$. They account for all crossing edges, since their degrees from $U$ sum to $1-s$. At each outside root at least two hues of $A$ are available. Let the edges in a graph on the four hues be pairs that are simultaneously available from a positive-measure set of outside roots. Its edges cover all four hues. If two disjoint edges occur, use the second completion. Otherwise its edges are pairwise intersecting, so they are a star or a triangle: two intersecting edges and an edge avoiding their common endpoint must form a triangle, after which no outside edge meets all three. The triangle cannot cover four hues. In a star each outside root has exactly two hues available, with the center hue always present. Both must have crossing degree $t$ since their sum is $s=2t$. The center hue then violates the same crossing capacity $t(1-s)>ts$.

## Three missing colors: the availability sets overlap

Suppose $A$ has three hues. If $s>1/3$, every pair of these hues overlaps on positive outside measure. If $s=1/3$ and two, say $b,c$, have disjoint availability sets, those sets partition $U^c$ into equal parts of measure $s$, with $b$ and $c$ respectively supplying crossing degree $t$ there. If the third hue $a$ overlaps positively with both parts, any outside root with any $B$ hue available can use that hue together with its $b$ or $c$, and the other two $A$ hues are jointly available on the opposite part. Such a $B$ root exists because the total $B$ degree from $U$ across is $1/2-s>0$. This gives the second completion. If $a$ overlaps only one part, say the $b$ part, its support has measure $s$ and its crossing degree there is identically $t$. That part uses exactly $a,b$, while the other part has $c$ together with some $B$ hue. Again the second completion works.

We may thus assume every pair of $A$ hues overlaps positively on outside roots. Any outside root with both an $A$ hue and a $B$ hue available would give a four-crossing completion, since the other two $A$ hues overlap somewhere. Thus almost every outside root sees either only $A$ or only $B$ into $U$. Let these parts be $Y,Z$ respectively. The total $A$ crossing mass from $U$ is $s/2$ and each $y\in Y$ supplies $s$ such mass per row, so $\mu(Y)=1/2$ and $\mu(Z)=1/2-s$. The three $A$ colors each have degree exactly $t$ from $U$ into $Y$. If $Z$ is null we already have the parity cut between $Y$ and $U$.

## Three missing colors: prohibit a wrong edge from $Y$ to $Z$

We first show that almost every $y\in Y$ starts a rainbow three-edge path of the form $Y,U,Y,U$ using all three $A$ hues.

If $s>1/3=2t$, every $Y$ row has all three $A$ hues into $U$, since its crossing mass is $s$ and at most $t$ in any hue. Start in any hue, extend from $U$ in the next one using degree $t$ into $Y$, and finish in the third from $Y$.

If $s=2t$, each $Y$ row has at least two $A$ hues. Suppose one typical $y$ has zero density of such three-edge paths. For a hue $c$ positively available at $y$, for almost every intermediate $u$ reached through $c$, all neighbors of $u$ in hue $a\ne c$ must have no neighbor of the third hue $b$ in $U$. Otherwise Fubini gives the required path. Each of these neighbors therefore sees exactly $a,c$ into $U$, with degrees $t,t$ since the total is $2t$. Write $Y_{ac}$ for this type. The degree $t$ from $u$ in hue $a$ shows that $\mu(Y_{ac})\ge t$, and the other remaining hue similarly gives $\mu(Y_{bc})\ge t$. The root $y$ has a second hue into $U$, so applying the argument to it makes all three two-hue types have measure at least $t$. As $\mu(Y)=3t$, they have measure exactly $t$ and exhaust $Y$. Now take a typical $u$ reached through $c$ again. Its $a$ and $b$ edges into $Y$ saturate $Y_{ac}$ and $Y_{bc}$, respectively. Its degree $t$ in $c$ must go to $Y_{ab}$, whose vertices by definition have no $c$ edges into $U$. This is a contradiction.

Suppose the crossing rectangle $Y\times Z$ has positive weight of some $B$ hue $d$. At almost every such positive edge $yz$, use the rainbow three-path from $y$, terminating at $u_2\in U$. The vertex $z$ has total degree $s\ge2t$ into $U$ using only $B$, so some hue $e\ne d$ is positively available from $z$ into $U$. Take such an edge to $u_1$ and join $u_1,u_2$ in the remaining $B$ hue on $U^2$. This closes a rainbow six-cycle with $yz$. Consequently $B$ is absent on $Y\times Z$. It is also absent on $Y\times U$ by the definition of $Y$. The union of $B$ has row degree $1/2$, so with two halves $Y$ and $U\cup Z$ it saturates both within-part squares. The colors of $A$ are exactly the crossing palette, proving the theorem.

The proof supplies a new sufficient hypothesis for the [parity-cut criterion](GRAPHON-CRITERION.md), without assuming disconnected triangle links or an operator rank bound. It does not show that an arbitrary zero-cycle graphon possesses a region of the stated kind. Coordination: [#870](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/870).
