# One positive target part forces parity

**Theorem.** In the [three-block setting](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md), with two colors $`A,B`$ filling each of three equal parts, suppose there is one fixed part $`X_1`$ such that almost every vertex outside it sends positive degree of each cross color into $`X_1`$. No lower bound is required, and degrees out of $`X_1`$ into either other part can be zero. If the rainbow six-cycle density is zero, the graphon has an exact parity cut.

This strengthens [the all-interface positivity theorem](POSITIVE-TRIPARTITE.md). The opposite extreme, in which every outside vertex sends just two colors into one fixed part, is [already excluded](SATURATED-TRIPARTITE.md). The unrestricted missing-pair case remains open. Write $`t=1/6`$ and pass to deterministic colors by circle refinement, which preserves all interpart color degrees.

## Two local facts

**Difference test.** Suppose an interface has the cross table $`u(x)+v(y)`$ in $`\mathbb F_2^2`$. Assume all four labels are present in the first part and at least two in the second. For every difference $`d`$ between labels present in the second part, the internal $`A,B`$ rule at difference $`d`$ in the first part is monochromatic and agrees with the second part wherever that difference occurs.

Indeed take internal edges of opposite colors in the two parts with the same label difference $`d`$, were they to exist. Use one cross step, of color $`c_1`$, between an endpoint of the first edge and one of the second. Let $`b`$ be the label of the endpoint of the second edge on which the remaining three cross steps begin. Choose another available label $`b'\ne b`$ on that part. The four colors can be paired in two pairs of the same nonzero sum $`b+b'`$; choose the pair not containing $`c_1`$ and use those colors for the next two cross steps. They go via an available label in the first part and end at $`b'`$. The remaining cross color closes to the other endpoint of the first edge, since all four colors sum to zero and the two internal differences cancel. All chosen label cells have positive measure. This contradiction rules out a difference used in both internal colors of the first part whenever it is available in the second, and forces agreement. In particular, if both sides have all four labels, the common rule uses two differences for $`A`$, by averaging its degrees over the four *labels*, and the rule is the fiber of a nonzero linear form.

**Complete two-path palettes.** Whenever every ordered two-cross-color path from a root in a part back to that part has positive rooted density, the endpoint-class argument in the [positive-interface proof](POSITIVE-TRIPARTITE.md) applies. All endpoints of any palette and its complement lie in one $`A`$-row class. Then every outside color neighborhood into that part lies in one row class, provided the outside vertex has another color also present there. Two colors are always present by the cap $`t`$ and total interpart degree $`2t`$. A family of outside vertices using all four colors covers the part with at most four such classes. The classes each weigh at most $`t`$; three classes collapse to two as in the positive-interface proof. With two classes both weigh $`t`$, and an internal $`A,B`$ two-path joins any pair of opposite class bits. If an entire mutual interface has positive degrees of every color, all ordered palettes are present into either side via that interface alone. The four-type centralizer argument and the one-and-three argument from the positive-interface proof then apply locally to that pair of parts and use only that interface. Thus both such parts have two types, and the two-and-two pairing of cross colors into each part is the same, with an interface sign shift: $`\pi(c)=p_i(x)+p_j(y)+g_{ij}`$ for the pairing bit $`\pi`$. The proofs here use only the two-part difference test and two-path constraints; no assumption on the third part is needed.

## The first part cannot have four types

Into $`X_1`$ all ordered two-path palettes are present: every first color has degree $`t`$ from a root, and the outside vertices have every second color available back into $`X_1`$. Thus $`X_1`$ has two or four row classes. If four, the Klein-four argument gives labels on $`X_1`$ and on $`X_2\cup X_3`$ with the cross rule $`u(x)+v(y)`$ on $`\mathbb F_2^2`$. Every outside label occurs with mass $`t`$ across the whole outside, by the color degrees from a fixed root in $`X_1`$. Consequently each individual outside part uses at least two labels. If their difference sets together include all four differences, the first difference test makes the $`A`$-rule on $`X_1`$ translation-invariant and dependent on a bit only, a contradiction to four types.

Otherwise each outside part has exactly two labels and both label pairs have the same nonzero difference $`d`$; they are disjoint complementary cosets, with every outside label of mass $`t`$ in its own part. The difference test for $`0,d`$ forces the two internal rules on each outside part to be the preserving and reversing rules on its two labels. Also every type in $`X_1`$ has mass strictly below $`t`$: a single type of mass $`t`$ would force every row to choose that type alone or its complement, and symmetry would merge all the remaining types. So every outside vertex sends strictly positive degree less than $`t`$ of every color into $`X_1`$, and hence also positive degree of every color into the other outside part. The interface $`X_2,X_3`$ is therefore positive in both directions. By the local two-part conclusion, its pairing bit $`\pi`$ gives a cross relation
$`\pi(c)=p_2(y)+p_3(z)+g`$, where $`p_j`$ distinguishes the two labels, with a suitable shift $`g`$.

The pairing defining $`\pi`$ differs from the one whose pairs have difference $`d`$: a two-path from $`X_2`$ via a vertex of $`X_1`$ using its two colors into $`X_2`$ necessarily reverses the $`X_2`$ label bit, and these two colors have difference $`d`$. The unique pairing preserving the bit in the positive interface is therefore not that pairing. Hence $`\pi(d)=1`$, and we may take $`p_j(y)=\pi(v(y))`$ on $`X_j`$ with a shift included in $`g`$. Let $`\lambda\ne0`$ have kernel $`\{0,d\}`$; it is constant on each outside part, with opposite constants.

Take $`X_1`$ endpoints with the same $`\pi`$-bit and opposite $`\lambda`$-bits. Use a four-step return via $`X_2,X_3,X_2`$, choosing both $`X_2`$ intermediate vertices in the same label. The first and last colors have opposite $`\lambda`$-bits. Choose the $`X_3`$ label with $`\pi`$-bit $`1+\pi(u(x))+g`$, where $`x`$ is the starting $`X_1`$ label. Then each of the middle edges has $`\pi`$-bit opposite the first and last colors. Choose the middle colors to have the two respective $`\lambda`$-bits of the first and last colors; both are available into the chosen $`X_2`$ label from each typical $`X_3`$ root by positivity on that interface. All four colors are distinct. This gives positive-density returns between those two $`X_1`$ label classes, so they must be $`A`$-twins. This contradicts four types.

## Align the two-type rules

Now $`X_1`$ has two types. Each outside vertex sends positive neighborhoods of all four colors into it, each wholly within one type. The argument from [the all-interface positivity theorem](POSITIVE-TRIPARTITE.md) gives the alternatives two-and-two or a common one-and-three split. In the latter case the cross rule into $`X_1`$ is a Klein-four table, with $`X_1`$ labels $`0`$ for the singleton type and the three nonzero labels for the triple type. All four labels have positive measure on $`X_1`$, and every outside label has mass $`t`$ across $`X_2\cup X_3`$ by color degree at a singleton root. Thus each outside part uses at least two labels. Each nonzero difference occurs in both internal colors in $`X_1`$ because it meets the zero label once and also occurs between two nonzero labels. This contradicts the difference test for any outside two distinct labels.

Hence there is a common two-and-two pairing into $`X_1`$, with bit $`\pi_1`$. Both colors sent into an $`X_1`$ row class by an outside vertex have positive degrees summing to $`t`$; each such degree is strictly less than $`t`$. Thus every vertex on the $`X_2,X_3`$ interface has all four colors positive into the other part. The local two-part conclusion gives two classes there and a common pairing bit $`\pi`$ for that interface. The same pairing describes any vertex's color neighborhoods into $`X_j`$, $`j=2,3`$, even if it lies in $`X_1`$ and has absent colors: any two colors actually present from that vertex into $`X_j`$ land in the same type precisely when they are paired in $`\pi`$, by the complete two-path palette constraints already determined from the positive interface. Therefore the present colors' target bits can be labeled by $`\pi(c)+s_j(x)`$ for some bit $`s_j(x)`$.

If the pairings $`\pi_1,\pi`$ differ, identify colors with their two bits. For the $`X_1,X_2`$ interface the rule is a Klein-four table with labels $`(p_1(x),s_2(x))`$ in $`X_1`$ and $`(s_1(y),p_2(y))`$ in $`X_2`$, where $`s_1`$ similarly assigns the target bit in $`X_1`$. All four labels occur in $`X_1`$ by positivity into it; at least two occur in $`X_2`$ since $`p_2`$ takes both bits. The difference test at zero forces the internal $`A`$-rules on $`X_1,X_2`$ to have the same bit effect. At any available difference in $`X_2`$, the two coordinate bits must therefore agree, or the internal colors would disagree. Thus the label support in $`X_2`$ is an affine diagonal pair, giving $`s_1(y)=p_2(y)+h`$ for some constant $`h`$.

Choose $`X_1`$ endpoints with the same $`s_2`$-bit and opposite $`p_1`$-bits. In a return path $`X_1,X_2,X_3,X_2,X_1`$, take both $`X_2`$ intermediate vertices in the same $`p_2`$-class. The first and last colors then have the same second bit $`\pi`$ and opposite first bits $`\pi_1`$, by the displayed two-coordinate table. Choose the $`X_3`$ class so the two middle colors have the opposite second bit, and use the two first-bit choices. This is possible by positivity of each color into its compatible class on the $`X_2,X_3`$ interface. The four-color return joins opposite $`X_1`$ types, contradicting the $`A,B`$ two-path closure. Thus the pairings agree everywhere.

As in the all-positive proof, equality of the pairing bits at two ends of every interface now makes its sign shift constant. The internal $`A`$-rules agree on $`X_2,X_3`$ by their positive interface. They also agree between $`X_1,X_2`$: for the one-plus-three alternating cross path with an internal $`B`$-edge in $`X_2`$, take the first two cross colors to be partners in the common pairing. The second is available from the $`X_2`$ endpoint into $`X_1`$ by hypothesis; the third can use one of the opposite pair, since every $`X_1`$ vertex is adjacent to the compatible $`X_2`$ half; the fourth is again available from $`X_2`$ into $`X_1`$. If the $`A`$- and $`B`$-bit effects match in different parts, this path closes into a rainbow six-cycle with an $`A`$-edge. Therefore the $`A`$-rules agree. Absorb the three interface shifts as in the all-positive proof to obtain the half-cut, with exactly three crossing colors.
