# Two-color triangle omissions in the graphon limit

**Theorem.** Suppose a balanced six-color graphon has zero rainbow six-cycle density and zero density of rainbow triangles using some fixed pair of colors \(A,B\). Then the union \(A+B\) is exactly the union of three complete blocks, each of measure \(1/3\). Furthermore, both \(A\) and \(B\) are indicator-valued countable-step kernels, even if the original graphon was fractional. Each step comes from a positive-measure class of identical rows. This is a reduction for graphons with a missing pair, not a parity classification or a proof of the unrestricted conjecture.

Write \(t=1/6\), and call the remaining four colors \(1,2,3,4\). First assume all colors are indicators.

## Three equal blocks

Merge the four remaining colors into a single color \(K\), so the three-color graphon has no rainbow triangle. Sample \(N\) independent vertices. Almost surely the sample is Gallai; for arbitrarily large \(N\) it also has every \(A,B\)-degree between \((t-\epsilon)N\) and \((t+\epsilon)N\) and every \(K\)-degree at least \((4t-\epsilon)N\), for \(\epsilon=o(1)\). This follows from conditional binomial concentration at each root and a union bound, for example with \(\epsilon=N^{-1/4}\).

The [finite Gallai merger](PAIR-TRIANGLE-TRANSVERSALS.md) works with this slack. At the top, \(K\) must occur between parts because two parts of size at least \((4t-\epsilon)N\) cannot fit. If \(A\) is the other missing interpart color, all parts are at least \((t-\epsilon)N\), so the interpart \(B\) pairs form a matching: two neighboring parts would force more than \((t+\epsilon)N\) edges. Merging those pairs gives at least two blocks, all \(A,B\) edges internal. Each block has size at least \((2t-2\epsilon)N\), so at most three blocks fit. If there are only two, look inside the larger block of size \(s\ge N/2\). If \(K\) does not occur between its new Gallai parts, each part has size at least \(s-2(t+\epsilon)N\) by the internal \(K\)-degree, and at most \((t+\epsilon)N\) by any interpart edge. For small \(\epsilon\), there must be exactly three parts. Some part sees both others in the same color, forcing more than \((t+\epsilon)N\) edges of that color. Thus \(K\) occurs between the new parts, and the same merger splits the larger original block further. There are now exactly three blocks. Their sizes are \(N/3+O(\epsilon N)\), and since all residual \(A,B\) edges are internal and have combined degree at least \((2t-2\epsilon)N\), the union \(A+B\) misses only \(O(\epsilon N^2)\) pairs inside the blocks.

In particular the induced \(P_3\) density of the union in the sample tends to zero; one edge edit changes at most \(O(N)\) induced triples. This implies the graphon induced \(P_3\) density is zero, either by expectation and concentration or the variance bound \(O(1/N)\) for a fixed sampled triple pattern (disjoint sampled triples are independent). Write \(H=A+B\). For almost every \(H\)-edge \(xy\), the \(H\)-rows of \(x,y\) agree almost everywhere, by the zero induced \(P_3\) density in both orientations. Thus almost every row has a positive-measure twin class containing its full neighborhood. Symmetry makes \(H\) constant on each pair of positive-measure row classes; no edges between different classes are possible, and degree \(2t\) makes every class a complete block of measure \(2t\). Hence there are exactly three.

## Returning four-color walks and row types

Fix one block \(X\), with complement \(Y\) of measure \(4t\). Within \(X\), \(A,B\) partition all pairs and each has degree \(t\). Therefore the integral giving an \(A,B\) two-edge path between \(x,z\in X\) vanishes exactly when their \(A\)-neighbor sets agree: it is the measure of \(N_A(x)\setminus N_A(z)\), and both neighborhoods have measure \(t\). Since a four-edge rainbow walk in colors \(1,2,3,4\) closing along such a two-edge path would give a rainbow six-cycle density, almost every pair of endpoints of a four-color walk from \(X\) back to \(X\) are \(A\)-twins. Formally, the product of the nonnegative two-path and four-path kernels integrates to zero in each order. All graphs in these integrations are on independent coordinates, so coincidence of vertices is a null event on the atomless underlying space.

Call a point of \(X\) silent if for almost every other endpoint in \(X\) all four-color four-path kernels from it vanish. Almost every point in a zero-measure \(A\)-row class would be silent by the preceding observation. We prove the silent set is null.

For \(w\in X\), let \(\mathcal F_w\) be the two-element color palettes of positive-density two-paths rooted at \(w\) with their other endpoint in \(X\) and middle point in \(Y\). For almost every \(w\), this palette graph on four colors contains two disjoint edges. First, no color is isolated: for each \(a\), \(w\) has \(t\) many \(a\)-neighbors \(u\in Y\), and \(u\) sends a total of \(2t\) edges into \(X\), with at most \(t\) in color \(a\). If the palette graph has no matching of size two, it must be a star (an intersecting set of pairs lies in a star or a triangle, and a triangle would leave the fourth color isolated). Let \(c\) be its center. For each of the three leaf colors \(a\), almost every \(u\) among the \(t\) many \(a\)-neighbors of \(w\) can send edges into \(X\) only in \(a,c\); since the total is \(2t\) and each color degree at most \(t\), its \(c\)-degree into \(X\) is exactly \(t\). The three neighbor sets of \(w\) are disjoint, giving at least \(3t^2\) total \(c\)-degree into \(X\) from \(Y\), whereas the actual total is \(|X|t=2t^2\) by symmetry and balance. This proves the matching claim.

Fix a typical silent \(x\). For almost every \(w\in X\), put
\[
M_{ab}(x,w)=\mu\{u\in Y:\ \operatorname{color}(xu)=a,\ \operatorname{color}(wu)=b\}.
\]
Every palette with \(M_{ab}(x,w)>0\), \(a\ne b\), belongs to \(\mathcal F_w\) by Fubini, but its complementary palette cannot belong to \(\mathcal F_w\): their product would make a positive-density four-path from \(x\) via \(w\) to \(X\). The six pairs are divided into three complementary pairs of palettes. Since at least one of these pairs is wholly contained in \(\mathcal F_w\), at most two palettes have no complement there, and if there are two, they intersect. Thus the off-diagonal entries of \(M(x,w)\) are supported on changes involving one common color. The total mass of these changes is at most \(2t\), since the two rows each use that common color on a set of measure \(t\). In other words the measure of the disagreement set between the cross rows of \(x,w\) is at most \(2t\).

For the reverse average inequality, for almost every \(u\in Y\), the color at \(xu\) can agree with the row at \(wu\) for a set of \(w\in X\) of measure at most \(t\), by the full degree bound at \(u\). Integrating over \(Y\), the average over \(w\in X\) of the disagreement measure is at least \(|Y|/2=2t\). It must therefore equal \(2t\) for almost every \(w\). A positive-measure family of such silent \(x\) would have cross rows with mutual \(L^1\) distance bounded away from zero for almost all pairs. This is impossible in the separable space of four-tuples of \(L^1(Y)\) functions: cover it by countably many balls of radius less than \(t/2\) in the disagreement metric, one of which would contain a positive-measure set. Hence almost every \(A\)-row type in \(X\) is an atom of positive measure. Symmetry makes \(A\) constant on pairs of its countably many row classes, and \(B\) has the same conclusion because it is the complement of \(A\) inside each block.

## Return from purification to the original graphon

For a fractional coloring use the [circle refinement](DETERMINISTIC.md); it preserves both forbidden-pattern density zero statements. The above theorem applies to its refined indicators. Simultaneous circle translations preserve the refined color, and send positive-measure row classes of the refined \(A\) kernel to row classes of equal measure (global \(A\)-rows cannot agree between different blocks because of their disjoint supports and positive degree). There are only finitely many classes of any given positive mass. Translations act continuously on a class indicator in \(L^1\); its orbit in this finite set is constant by connectedness of the circle. Each class is therefore independent of the circle coordinate modulo null sets, as follows by averaging its translates. The same reasoning applies to \(B\) and to the finite block classes of \(A+B\). The refined kernels are constant indicators on pairs of classes, so integrating the extra coordinates recovers indicator-valued countable-step kernels in the original graphon, with the same three-block union.

The remaining obstruction is to show that the countable-step kernel and the other four colors force a parity cut (or to produce a counterexample). The [finite pair-transversal theorem](PAIR-TRIANGLE-TRANSVERSALS.md), [Gallai partition theorem](https://users.renyi.hu/~pach/publications/gallaicoloring031113.pdf), and [original Erdős–Tuza source](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-5060(08)70377-7) give context; no prior result with this row-type conclusion was found in the focused searches.
