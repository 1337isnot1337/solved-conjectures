# A globally missing rainbow-triangle color

Two results relevant to the [rooted triangle obstruction](PARITY-COVER.md):

1. In every completely balanced coloring of \(K_{mt+1}\) with \(m\ge4\) colors and \(t\ge1\), each prescribed color lies in some rainbow triangle.
2. In a balanced six-color graphon, suppose a color is absent from all positive-density rainbow triangles. If that color is an indicator-valued graphon, or if its support graphon is disconnected, then the coloring has positive rainbow six-cycle density.

The first statement is finite and does not say every color appears at every root. The second excludes a graphon model motivated by the earlier feasible local tensor, but does not handle a varying exceptional color or all fractional graphons.

## Finite argument

Suppose the color \(z\) is in no rainbow triangle and let \(H\) be its \(t\)-regular spanning graph. If \(x,y\) are \(H\)-neighbors, and \(v\) is adjacent in \(H\) to neither, then the two colors of \(vx,vy\) agree: otherwise \(vxy\) is a rainbow triangle with color \(z\). If \(H\) had several components, a vertex outside one component would consequently see that whole component in a single non-\(z\) color. This gives at most \(t\) vertices in the component, whereas \(t\)-regularity gives at least \(t+1\). Thus \(H\) is connected.

For any \(v\), put \(S=N_H(v)\) and \(U=V\setminus S\). The vertex \(v\) is isolated in \(H[U]\). Every other component of \(H[U]\) is monochromatic from \(v\) in some non-\(z\) color, so all components have order at most \(t\). In particular \(H\) has diameter at most two: if \(u\) had distance at least three from \(v\), its closed neighborhood of size \(t+1\) would lie connected inside \(H[U]\).

Fix \(v\). At least two components \(C,D\) of \(H[U]\) have order more than \(t/2\). Otherwise at least \((m-2)t+1\) vertices belong to components of order at most \(t/2\); every such vertex sends more than \(t/2\) edges to \(S\), contrary to the total boundary bound \(t|S|=t^2\). By diameter two a vertex \(s\) has a neighbor in each of \(C,D\). Necessarily \(s\in S\). Every \(u\in U\setminus(C\cup D)\) must be adjacent to \(s\): otherwise \(H-N_H(u)\) still contains both components and \(s\), which joins them into a component of order greater than \(t\). There are at least \((m-3)t+1>t\) such \(u\), contradicting the degree of \(s\).

## Disconnected color in the graphon

Write \(Z\) for the exceptional color kernel, \(t=1/6\). A connected component of \(Z\) means a positive-measure indecomposable part in [Janson's graphon component decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/0802.3795). If \(Z\) has several components, take one component \(C\) and a typical outside root \(v\). For almost all \(x,y\in C\) with \(Z(x,y)>0\), the label supports of \(vx,vy\) must be the same singleton non-\(z\) label. In fact they are nonempty and exclude \(z\), and any two different choices would form the forbidden rainbow triangle. Positive \(Z\)-degree and Fubini now show that almost every \(x\in C\) has just one label from \(v\); two positive-measure label groups would separate \(C\), so the label is constant. Balance at \(v\) gives \(\mu(C)\le t\), whereas the \(Z\)-degree of vertices of \(C\) gives \(\mu(C)\ge t\). Hence all components have size \(t\), with \(Z=1\) on each internal block. There is no null-degree dust, since the degree is positive everywhere up to a null set.

By symmetry the non-\(z\) color between two components is a single constant label on the full cross rectangle: the same argument shows it is constant along either coordinate separately. The five cross colors form a proper one-factorization of the complete graph on the six components, by degree balance.

Every one-factorization of \(K_6\) has a rainbow five-cycle on any fixed set of five vertices. There are twelve five-cycles on this set. Each color has two disjoint edges there, and exactly four five-cycles contain both, for a total of twenty duplicate-color occurrences over the twelve cycles. Any two distinct matchings on \(K_6\) form an alternating six-cycle (their union is simple and has no alternating two-cycle). Delete the sixth vertex to obtain a four-edge path, and close that path within the five-set. This yields a five-cycle duplicating both chosen colors. The ten choices of color pair give ten distinct such cycles because a five-cycle cannot duplicate three colors. Those ten already account for all twenty duplicate-color occurrences. Hence the other two cycles are rainbow. In the graphon, insert a \(z\)-edge inside one component of such a five-cycle. The six-cycle type walk has positive weight on a product of six positive-measure parts.

## Indicator color cannot be connected

Assume now \(Z\) is an indicator. For a typical root \(v\) write \(U_v=\{x:Z(v,x)=0\}\). In the restricted graphon \(Z|_{U_v}\), every connected component has measure at most \(t\): along each positive \(Z\)-edge the two label supports from \(v\) have the same singleton non-\(z\) label, as above, and its degree at \(v\) is \(t\). The restriction may have zero-degree dust, which is harmless.

Suppose \(Z\) is connected, and choose a typical root \(w\); set \(S=\{s:Z(w,s)=1\}\) and \(U=U_w\). Then \(\mu S=t,\mu U=5t\). At least two components \(C,D\) of \(Z|_U\) have measure more than \(t/2\). Otherwise a subset of \(U\) of measure at least \(4t\) belongs to smaller components or dust and sends degree at least \(t/2\) into \(S\) in every row, contradicting the total boundary bound \(t^2\).

Let \(S_C\subseteq S\) be the set of rows with positive \(Z\)-weight to \(C\), and let \(S_D\) be the analogous set. Both have positive measure because \(Z\) is connected and \(C,D\) have no \(Z\)-edges to the other parts of \(U\). For almost every \(v\in U\setminus(C\cup D)\), the sets \(C,D\) are fully inside \(U_v\) up to null sets. If both \(S_C\cap U_v\) and \(S_D\cap U_v\) had positive measure, \(C,D\) would be connected to each other in \(Z|_{U_v}\). Here is a justification that does not use a single root vertex as a positive-measure connector. The map \(x\mapsto Z(x,\cdot)\) into \(L^1\) is measurable and has separable range. For each fixed \(v\), almost every \(w\in U_v\) has at every positive \(L^1\) radius a positive measure of other rows in \(U_v\) within that radius: use a countable basis to remove all balls of zero pushforward measure. By Fubini the originally chosen typical \(w\) has this property for almost every \(v\) for which \(w\in U_v\), which is almost every \(v\in U\) by symmetry. For a radius smaller than the two positive set measures, all these nearby rows see a positive part of each of \(S_C\cap U_v,S_D\cap U_v\). As every row in these two sets has positive weight to its respective component, a positive-measure set of connectors in \(U_v\) has positive two-walk weight to both \(C,D\). Thus both connected sets lie in one component of \(Z|_{U_v}\). Its measure exceeds \(t\), a contradiction.

Consequently almost every \(v\in U\setminus(C\cup D)\) is adjacent in \(Z\) to almost all of \(S_C\), or to almost all of \(S_D\). Fubini then gives two rows \(s\in S_C,s'\in S_D\) whose neighborhoods together cover that set up to null sets. They have total measure at most \(2t\), but \(\mu(U\setminus(C\cup D))\ge3t\). This contradiction proves disconnectedness, and the preceding classification gives the rainbow six-cycle.

In the general fractional case, missing all rainbow triangles containing \(z\) still forces the \(Z\)-support degree to be at most \(1/3\) almost everywhere. If a row has an overlapping \(z,i\) edge to \(x\), then the entire \(Z\)-support of that row must be colored only \(z,i\) from \(x\), or there would be a rainbow triangle with three different labels including \(z\). That support has measure at most \(2t\); if the row has no such overlap its support measure is \(t\). The connected fractional case is not settled by the argument above.

The [completely balanced rainbow-coloring literature](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13867) and [public #811 report](https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/811) were checked for the prescribed-color statement.
