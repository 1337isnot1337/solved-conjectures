# Uniform tripartite interfaces force parity

**Theorem.** Partition a balanced six-color graphon into three parts \(X_1,X_2,X_3\) of measure \(1/3\), with two colors \(A,B\) filling precisely the within-part pairs. Assume that, for every \(i\ne j\), each of the other four colors has degree \(1/12\) from almost every vertex of \(X_i\) into \(X_j\). If the graphon has zero rainbow six-cycle density, it has an exact parity cut. There is no group-table, commutativity, or finite-type assumption. The unrestricted [Erdős #811 criterion](GRAPHON-CRITERION.md) remains open; in the [missing-pair reduction](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md) the across-part degrees need not be uniform at every vertex.

Write \(t=1/6\). By the [circle refinement](DETERMINISTIC.md) we may assume all six colors are indicators: the parts lift, the row-degree hypotheses survive for each part, forbidden density zero is preserved, and parity descends. Denote the four other colors by \(1,2,3,4\).

## 1. At most four row types per part

Fix a part \(X\). As in the [missing-pair analysis](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md), an \(A,B\) two-path between \(x,z\in X\) exists with positive measure exactly when their \(A\)-rows differ: the path kernel is the measure of \(N_A(x)\setminus N_A(z)\), and both neighborhoods have measure \(t\).

At an intermediate root \(w\in X\) consider two-paths returning to \(X\) via an outside vertex, in two distinct cross colors \(a,b\). Every ordered pair has positive rooted density, since each first-color neighbor outside \(X\) has \(b\)-degree \(t/2\) into \(X\). For every palette \(\{a,b\}\), the endpoints of its two-paths in either order and of its complementary palette must lie in a common \(A\)-row class. Otherwise composing the paths and an \(A,B\) two-path would give a rainbow six-cycle of positive density. Formally, the nonnegative product of the two rooted path kernels and the \(A,B\) kernel integrates to zero; Fubini gives this assertion for almost every root and in every order. Denote the common class by \(C_{ab}(w)=C_{cd}(w)\), where \(\{c,d\}\) is complementary.

For almost every outside \(y\) and for every cross color \(b\), its entire \(b\)-neighborhood into \(X\) is contained, up to a null set, in one \(A\)-row class: choose \(w\in X\) with color \(a\ne b\) on \(wy\), which is possible with positive measure by the uniform degrees. Then every \(b\)-neighbor of \(y\) in \(X\) lies in \(C_{ab}(w)\) by the two-path support statement. Thus these classes have measure at least \(t/2\). Almost every vertex of \(X\) belongs to a class of this size: every vertex is reached by a positive mass of two-paths from \(X\), using any given ordered distinct colors, by symmetry and the same uniform degrees.

Each row class has measure at most \(t\): its common \(A\)-row has integral \(t>0\), so by symmetry some \(A\)-neighbors see the whole class in color \(A\), and their degree is \(t\). The positive-measure classes therefore partition \(X\) into between two and four parts. The \(A\)-kernel is constant on every pair of classes by symmetry. Three classes are impossible: some subset of their weights must sum to \(t\), so one class has weight \(t\) and the other two sum to \(t\). Each \(A\)-row chooses either that singleton class or the other two together. The latter two classes cannot have different choices, by symmetry of the edge between them, so they would in fact be one row class. Thus there are either two classes of weight \(t\) or four of weight \(t/2\).

## 2. Four types would force a group table, then contradict four types

Suppose \(X_i\) has four row classes. For almost every \(y\) outside it, each of the four colors fills precisely one class in its neighborhood into \(X_i\), since all these degrees are \(t/2\). Let \(\sigma_y\) be the resulting bijection from colors to classes. Let \(m\) be one of the three perfect-matching involutions on the four colors. For a typical \(w\) in type \(p\), color \(a=\sigma_y^{-1}(p)\) reaches \(y\), followed by color \(m(a)\) to type \(\sigma_y m\sigma_y^{-1}(p)\). This is the common endpoint class of the two complementary palettes of \(m\), so it does not depend on \(y\).

All four types have positive measure; Fubini shows that the three conjugate involutions \(\sigma_y m\sigma_y^{-1}\) are independent of \(y\) for almost every \(y\). Relative to a fixed reference bijection, all the \(\sigma_y\) therefore belong to the centralizer of the regular Klein-four group generated by the matching involutions. This centralizer is itself the Klein-four group, because a commuting permutation is determined by the image of any one point. After identifying the four colors and types with \(\mathbb F_2^2\), we have a cross-color rule \(c=u(x)+h(y)\) between \(X_i\) and any other \(X_j\), where \(u\) labels the four types and \(h\) is uniformly distributed on \(X_j\) by the four color degrees there.

We use the following two-part consequence of the [tripartite group-table proof](TRIPARTITE-GROUP-TABLES.md), which uses only cycles in the two chosen parts: for a cross table with colors \(u(x)+h(y)\) in \(\mathbb F_2^2\), zero rainbow six-cycle density forces the internal \(A\)-rules on the two parts to be one common half-set of quotient differences. Indeed four cross colors sum to zero. If a difference is realized by an \(A\)-edge on one part and a \(B\)-edge on the other, join the edges by one cross step and three cross steps using the four colors once; the quotient equations close. At every difference each part uses at least one of \(A,B\), so all edges of each difference use the same color in both parts. By degree balance \(A\) uses two differences, which form a coset of a one-dimensional subgroup. This makes \(A\) depend on a single nontrivial character of the label, so it has only two distinct rows in \(X_i\), contradicting four types.

Consequently every part has two equally sized \(A\)-row classes. Label them by \(p_i(x)\in\mathbb F_2\). In each part \(A\) either preserves or reverses this bit, and \(B\) does the opposite, by balance and symmetry.

## 3. The cross-color pairings agree across the three parts

Fix \(i\). For almost every outside vertex \(y\), its four cross colors split into two pairs according to the \(A\)-row class in \(X_i\) they meet: each has degree \(t/2\) and its neighborhood lies in one class, so exactly two go to each class. At a root \(w\in X_i\) in its own class \(p\), and across any outside neighbor \(y\) of color \(a\), the three two-path palettes \(\{a,b\}\) reach \(p\) for exactly one choice of \(b\): the partner of \(a\) in \(y\)'s pairing. The other two reach the other class. Since the endpoint class agrees under palette complementation, \(w\) determines one of the three perfect matchings for which the two paths stay in its own class. For every outside \(y\) it is precisely \(y\)'s pairing into \(X_i\). Because every pair \(wy\) is present and between the parts, Fubini implies this matching is constant in \(w,y\). Denote it by \(P_i\), and choose a bit label \(\pi_i(c)\) on its two color-pairs.

For outside \(y\), there is then a bit \(s_i(y)\) such that for the edge \(xy\) with \(x\in X_i\) of color \(c\) we have \(\pi_i(c)=p_i(x)+s_i(y)\). If \(P_i\ne P_j\), the map \(c\mapsto(\pi_i(c),\pi_j(c))\) is bijective onto \(\mathbb F_2^2\). Between \(X_i,X_j\) the colors would then be the group table with labels
\[
x\mapsto(p_i(x),s_j(x)),\qquad y\mapsto(s_i(y),p_j(y)).
\]
Both label distributions are uniform by the equal four color degrees into each part. The two-part rule in Section 2 says that the internal \(A\)-rules must be one common half-set of differences in \(\mathbb F_2^2\). But they are the fibers of the first and the second coordinate, respectively, which cannot agree. Thus \(P_i=P_j\).

Choose the same labeling \(\pi\) of the two color-pairs everywhere. For a fixed interface \(i,j\),
\[
p_i(x)+s_i(y)=\pi(c)=p_j(y)+s_j(x)
\]
on almost every pair. This implies \(p_i(x)+s_j(x)\) and \(p_j(y)+s_i(y)\) are a common constant \(g_{ij}\). Thus the edge color always satisfies \(\pi(c)=p_i(x)+p_j(y)+g_{ij}\).

The internal \(A\)-rule must also be the same on every part. Otherwise \(A\) on some \(X_i\) and \(B\) on some different \(X_j\) have the same parity effect. Starting in \(X_i\), take one cross-colored edge to \(X_j\), a \(B\)-edge there, and three more cross steps back to \(X_i\) using the other three cross colors. The four cross steps have total parity effect zero (two colors of each bit and the same interpart shift four times), so the endpoint differs in its \(p_i\)-bit from the start by exactly the effect of the \(B\)-edge. It is therefore connected to the start in color \(A\). All choices have positive path measure by the row-degree assumptions. This gives a rainbow six-cycle, a contradiction.

Finally choose constants \(e_i,\gamma\in\mathbb F_2\) such that \(e_i+e_j=g_{ij}+\gamma\), by taking \(\gamma=\sum_{i<j}g_{ij}\). The sides labeled \(p_i(x)+e_i\) give an exact half-cut, crossed by exactly one of \(A,B\) and the two cross colors with \(\pi(c)+\gamma=1\). This is the claimed parity cut.
