# Tripartite group tables force parity

**Theorem.** Suppose a balanced six-color graphon is partitioned into three blocks \(X_1,X_2,X_3\) of measure \(1/3\), with colors \(A,B\) filling every within-block pair. Let the other four colors be given across the blocks by the following common finite abelian group table. Each \(X_i\) has uniformly distributed labels in a finite abelian group \(D\), and \(D\) is partitioned into four equal nonempty sets \(D_1,\ldots,D_4\). For \(i<j\), a pair of labels \(x\in X_i,y\in X_j\) has color \(c\) precisely when \(y-x+\eta_{ij}\in D_c\), for arbitrary fixed offsets \(\eta_{ij}\in D\). The kernels of \(A,B\) within the blocks may initially be fractional and otherwise arbitrary. Then either there is positive rainbow six-cycle density, or there is an exact parity cut.

This is a special case of the [missing-pair graphon analysis](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md), with a shared algebraic description of the remaining four colors. In the absence of cycles it reduces to a quotient of order two or four.

## Consecutive internal colors force periods

Suppose the rainbow six-cycle density is zero. Consider a four-edge path alternating between any fixed pair of blocks, starting and ending in one of them and using each of the four cross colors once. The possible label displacements are
\[
S=\bigcup_{\{a,b,c,d\}=[4]}(D_a+D_b-D_c-D_d).
\]
Indeed the two forward and two backward offsets cancel. Every displacement in \(S\) is possible starting at every label, with positive measure for the intermediate labels.

Within a block \(X_i\), the two-color path between \(x,z\) in order \(A,B\) has kernel
\[
\int_{X_i} W_A(x,u)(1-W_A(z,u))\,d\mu(u).
\]
It vanishes only if the two \(A\)-rows are the same indicator almost everywhere. To see this, the support of the first row must lie where the second has value one. Both rows have integral \(1/6\), so the support, the value-one set, and both rows coincide up to null sets. Zero rainbow six-cycle density forces this equality of rows across every pair of label classes differing by an element of \(S\). Slicing over a common other endpoint also makes \(A\) constant in the root within each label class, even if \(0\notin S\). By symmetry and the equality for all \(S\)-shifts, within each block the \(A,B\) kernels are deterministic and depend only on labels in \(Q=D/H\), where \(H\) is the subgroup generated by \(S\).

The quotient \(Q\) is nontrivial: if \(H=D\), the indicator \(A\)-kernel in a block is constant, incompatible with its degree being exactly half the block. For each color \(c\), differences of two elements of \(D_c\) belong to \(H\), by subtracting two sums in \(S\) with the other three representatives fixed. Thus every \(D_c\) lies in a single coset modulo \(H\), denoted \(q_c\). The four equal color sets cover \(D\), so \(|Q|\) divides four. If \(|Q|=4\), every element is used by exactly one color, and the defining equations for \(S\) require
\[
2(q_a+q_b)=\sum_{c=1}^4 q_c
\]
for every pair of distinct \(q_a,q_b\). A cyclic group of order four cannot satisfy this: the values on \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{0,2\}\) differ. Therefore \(Q\) is either \(\mathbb F_2\) or \(\mathbb F_2^2\). In both cases \(\sum_c q_c=0\), and each \(q\in Q\) is used by \(4/|Q|\) colors.

## Separating the internal colors forces one common rule

For each internal color in each block, consider the set of quotient differences realized by its edges. No difference can occur in color \(A\) in one block and \(B\) in a different block. Otherwise orient the two corresponding quotient edges as \(x\to y\) in \(X_i\) and \(u\to v\) in \(X_j\), with the same difference. Choose one cross color that connects the quotient labels \(y,u\), and follow it by the \(B\)-edge and a three-edge path alternating from \(X_j\) back to \(X_i\) using the other three cross colors. At the quotient level this path must end at \(x\): the four cross colors sum to zero, the four interblock shifts cancel, and the two internal differences sum to zero in characteristic two. This is realized with positive measure at the original group level. Start with any actual label in class \(y\), choose the next label at each cross edge using any representative from its nonempty \(D_c\), and freely choose any actual label in quotient class \(v\) for the end of the \(B\)-edge. The last actual endpoint belongs to class \(x\), so it closes by the \(A\)-edge to the starting point in class \(y\). Every specified label cell has positive measure. This contradicts zero rainbow cycle density.

Every difference occurs in at least one of the two internal colors in each block, because together they fill the block. The cross-block exclusion therefore assigns each difference to precisely one color, the same in all three blocks. The differences assigned to \(A\) have cardinality \(|Q|/2\), by degree balance. In either group \(Q\), every such subset is a fiber of a nontrivial character \(\chi:Q\to\mathbb F_2\). Thus exactly one of \(A,B\) crosses a \(\chi\)-cut inside each block.

## Align the cut across the three blocks

Choose constants \(e_i,\gamma\in\mathbb F_2\) with
\[
e_i+e_j=\chi(\bar\eta_{ij})+\gamma\quad(i<j).
\]
They exist by taking \(\gamma=\sum_{i<j}\chi(\bar\eta_{ij})\), which makes the three right sides sum to zero. Put a label \(x\) of block \(X_i\) on side \(\chi(\bar x)+e_i\). Its two sides have equal measure. Across any two blocks, the colors crossing the cut are exactly the two \(c\)'s with \(\chi(q_c)+\gamma=1\). Together with the one internal crossing color, this gives exactly three crossing and three within-side colors, hence an exact parity cut.

The [finite regular-action theorem](ALL-CAYLEY.md) concerns a globally transitive odd-order coloring. Here the two internal colors may have no translation symmetry at all at the start, and the three block shifts may differ. The two placements of \(A,B\) in the cycle give separate constraints: consecutive placement forces periods, whereas placement in separate blocks forces the common internal rule.
