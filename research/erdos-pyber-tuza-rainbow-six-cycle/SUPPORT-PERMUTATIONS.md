# The cross support is controlled by one pairing per source part

Assume the balanced zero-rainbow-six-cycle [three-block model](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md). By [TWO-TYPES-ALL-PARTS.md](TWO-TYPES-ALL-PARTS.md), every part has two row types of equal measure \(t=1/6\). This note adds a finite structural description of the possible cross degrees, with no restrictions on the incoming palette sizes.

**Theorem.** All three internal \(A,B\)-rules use the same bit effect. For any one source part \(Y\), there is a pairing of the four cross colors and a pairing of the four target half-blocks of the other parts, the latter matching each half-block of one part to a half-block of the other. Every degree-support permutation at a typical vertex of \(Y\) takes the target pairing to the color pairing. In particular, for either cross-color pair the degrees into paired target half-blocks agree, and the degrees into the two different target pairs sum to \(t\). This does not yet force these pair degrees to be only \(0\) or \(t\), or make the color pairing agree between source parts.

## Support permutations and internal effects

Work with indicator kernels. For a vertex \(y\) in a source part \(Y\), form the four-by-four matrix of its degrees into the two half-blocks each of \(X\) and \(Z\), by cross color, divided by \(t\). Its rows and columns sum to one. A *support permutation* assigns one color to each target half-block, all distinct and each of positive degree. Each such matrix is a convex combination of support permutations, by the doubly stochastic matrix decomposition. A permutation is called available in a part if it is supported at a positive mass of its vertices. Finitely many are possible, so almost every local support permutation is available.

Any one available permutation in \(Y\) proves equality of the internal effects in \(X,Z\). Choose two independent vertices supporting it. Choose \(X\)-endpoints of opposite bits, each using its assigned color from one of these source vertices, and do the same in \(Z\). All four cross colors are distinct. If the opposite-bit internal edges in \(X,Z\) had different colors, they would close a rainbow cycle. All four endpoint choices can be made independently on positive neighborhoods; the internal colors depend only on the endpoint bits and not their identity. Thus the effects in \(X,Z\) agree. As this works for any source part \(Y\), all three effects agree.

## Compatibility of permutations

Index the target positions \(0,1,2,3\) with \(0,1\) in \(X\) and \(2,3\) in \(Z\), and write \(R_0=\{01,23\}\) for this pairing. Let \(R_1=\{02,13\}\) and \(R_2=\{03,12\}\) be the two cross-part pairings of positions.

For any two available permutations \(\sigma,\tau\) in \(Y\), the following cannot happen: choose one position in each target part from \(\sigma\) and one in each from \(\tau\), producing four different colors, while the two internal endpoint bit differences (one in \(X\), one in \(Z\)) disagree. That would be the cycle from the preceding argument with the two different permutations and opposite internal colors.

Normalize \(\sigma\) to the identity by relabeling colors and treat \(\rho=\sigma^{-1}\tau\) as a permutation of the four positions. The compatibility rule holds exactly when \(\rho\) fixes \(R_1\) or \(R_2\) as an unordered pairing.

- If it fixes a cross-part pairing, read that pairing as a binary label on positions, equal to the position bit up to an independent fixed shift in each part. Under \(\rho\) the pairing-bit either stays or flips globally, contributing the flip twice. The sum of the four color pairing-bits therefore equals the sum of the two internal endpoint bit differences. Four distinct colors have pairing-bit sum zero, so unequal internal differences do not occur in a rainbow choice.
- Conversely, the action of the 24 permutations on the three pairings has kernel the Klein four of identity and the three double transpositions. Right composition with a kernel element does not affect the compatibility test: on the two positions chosen from \(\rho\) it still chooses one in each part and preserves their total position-bit sum. The actions fixing neither \(R_1\) nor \(R_2\) are the transposition of these two and the two three-cycles on pairings. The first is represented by \((0\,1)\); one three-cycle is represented by \((0\,1\,2)\), and its inverse is equivalent by interchange of the two permutations and relabeling. For either displayed representative the identity chooses positions \(0,2\), and \(\rho\) chooses positions \(0,3\), giving colors \(0,2,1,3\) with unequal internal differences. Thus neither action is compatible.

Fix one reference \(\sigma\) in \(Y\). Every relative permutation fixes \(R_1\) or \(R_2\). They all fix **one common** cross-part pairing: otherwise two relative permutations are, on the three pairings, transpositions with different fixed points \(R_1,R_2\), respectively. Their relative product acts as a three-cycle and is incompatible. Therefore all local degree-support permutations send one common position pairing \(R\) to the common color pairing \(\sigma(R)\). In each permutation the aggregate paired degree is identical across positions in the same pair; across distinct pairs the two values sum to one. The convex combination for an arbitrary local degree matrix proves the claimed equalities.

## A saturated profile imposes additional same-half constraints

Here is one subsequent constraint that applies when an actual saturated profile is present. Suppose a positive set \(S\) of vertices in the **same** \(Y\)-half sends constant colors \(a_0,a_1,c_0,c_1\), respectively, to the four blocks \(X_0,X_1,Z_0,Z_1\). Then no vertex of that \(Y\)-half sends positive degree of \(c_{1-j}\) into \(Z_j\), or of \(a_{1-i}\) into \(X_i\). For the first statement, use two independent vertices of \(S\) and that putative edge in a cycle with one internal edge in \(Y\) from a vertex of \(S\), and one internal edge between opposite bits in \(X\). The internal colors differ because the effects agree, and the cross path uses \(a_0,a_1,c_j,c_{1-j}\). Precisely, choose the cyclic order \(X_0,S,Y,Z_j,S,X_1\). The other statement interchanges \(X,Z\).

In terms of the compatibility theorem, after normalizing the saturated profile to the identity, there is only one support permutation of the opposite color-pair sign allowed for vertices of its own half: exchange the two target parts so that the original target position pairing is preserved but its pairs are swapped. This follows directly because a color of opposite pairing-bit has two choices at each position; the within-target swap is forbidden by the preceding argument. This is a restriction on support, not a proof that opposite signs cannot occur.
