# An isolated hue in a half-palette leaves only a six-block prism model

Consider a balanced six-color graphon and a half-palette $`Q=\{a,b,c\}`$. Suppose no rainbow triangle of positive density uses $`a`$, one of $`b,c`$, and one color outside $`Q`$. Then either there is an exact parity cut with $`Q`$ inside, or there is a partition into six blocks $`X_0,\ldots,X_5`$ of measure $`1/6`$, with this coarse description:

- All edges inside individual blocks have color $`a`$.
- Edges between cyclically consecutive blocks have colors $`b,c`$.
- Edges between all other distinct blocks use the three colors outside $`Q`$.

The second interblock graph is $`C_6`$; its complement on the six blocks is the triangular prism. There is no assertion here that every refinement of the six-block model has, or lacks, a rainbow six-cycle. This is a reduction of an additional case left by [HALF-PALETTE-TRIANGLES.md](HALF-PALETTE-TRIANGLES.md).

Sample a large finite colored graph, so each color degree is $`(1/6+o(1))N`$ uniformly over vertices and there is no forbidden rainbow triangle. Form $`H`$ from colors $`Q`$, and distinguish the two coarse hues $`a`$ and $`B=\{b,c\}`$ in $`H`$. Every induced $`P_3`$ of $`H`$ has both edges in the same coarse hue.

If $`H`$ is disconnected, its large minimum degree forces two almost equal clique blocks as in the preceding note. If its complement is disconnected, its components are two almost equal blocks, and all edges from a fixed vertex across the cut have one coarse hue by the forcing along a path in the other complement component. Both coarse hues have degree at most $`(1/3+o(1))N`$, giving a contradiction to the cross degree $`(1/2+o(1))N`$.

Otherwise the prime modular quotient gives at least four modules and all intermodule $`H`$-edges have a single coarse hue, by precisely the representative and $`P_3`$-forcing argument of [HALF-PALETTE-TRIANGLES.md](HALF-PALETTE-TRIANGLES.md). If that hue is $`a`$, each module must contain the full $`B`$-degree and has size at least $`(1/3-o(1))N`$, impossible. Thus it is $`B`$. Consequently each module contains the full $`a`$-degree, so every module has size at least $`(1/6-o(1))N`$ and there are at most six.

An intermodule neighbor contributes the entire size of that module to the $`B`$-degree, which is at most $`(1/3+o(1))N`$. The quotient has maximum degree two. Being connected on at least four vertices, it is a path or a cycle. Every module in such a quotient is adjacent to a degree-two module. For any degree-two module, both its neighbors must each have size $`(1/6+o(1))N`$, since they already each have at least that size and their total cannot exceed the $`B`$-degree. All modules therefore have this size, forcing exactly six. A path endpoint would send only $`(1/6+o(1))N`$ intermodule $`B`$-edges, while almost all its internal pairs are already in color $`a`$ by the $`a`$-degree. It could not have $`B`$-degree $`(1/3+o(1))N`$. Hence the quotient is $`C_6`$.

Passing to the sampled colored cut limit gives either the parity model or the six equal blocks with the indicated coarse palette, after a subsequence. In the latter case, before passing to the limit the coarse kernels already differ from the fixed six-block template by $`o(N^2)`$ pairs (within blocks color $`a`$ fills their nearly full capacity, and all intermodule palettes are prescribed). The template is indicator-valued and its six classes are distinct $`a`$-row types, so its weak-isomorphism class gives the same measurable partition on the original graphon space.

**A solved refinement.** If every interblock rectangle is monochromatic in this prism model, then it contains a rainbow six-cycle. The two colors $`b,c`$ alternate along the quotient $`C_6`$ by balance. The other three colors properly edge-color the prism. Take two distinct even positions, joined by a two-edge $`b,c`$ path through their common odd neighbor in $`C_6`$. In the prism, go from the second even position along its matching edge to its odd partner, take the triangle edge joining the two odd partners, then use the matching edge back to the first even position. In a properly three-edge-colored prism the triangle uses its three colors, and the matching edge at a triangle vertex uses its missing triangle color. Thus the three-edge prism path uses all three distinct outside colors. This makes a closed five-edge quotient walk with five distinct hues. Split one occurrence of its initial block into two distinct vertices joined by color $`a`$, giving the rainbow six-cycle on the original graphon. The same deterministic conclusion follows if almost every vertex uses a whole color on each individual other block, since symmetry then makes each rectangle monochromatic.
