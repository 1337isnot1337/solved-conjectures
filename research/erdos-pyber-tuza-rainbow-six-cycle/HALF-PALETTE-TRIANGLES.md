# A half-palette with no mixed rainbow triangle forces a parity cut

**Theorem.** Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_k\) be a group of colors in an edge-colored complete graphon. Assume their union \(V\) has degree \(1/2\) everywhere, each color in it has positive constant degree less than \(1/4\), and every edge not in \(V\) lies in other colors. Suppose there is zero density of rainbow triangles with exactly two edges in \(V\) (in different hues of \(V\)) and the third outside \(V\). Then \(V\) is exactly the union of the two half-cliques of a parity cut.

In particular, for a balanced six-color graphon, any choice of three colors with this restricted triangle exclusion forces parity. This does not assume zero rainbow six-cycles, nor forbid all-outside rainbow triangles as a premise. It gives a new structural subcase of [Erdős #811](https://www.erdosproblems.com/811) that is compatible with every pair of colors occurring somewhere in rainbow triangles.

## Finite forcing lemma

Consider a finite complete colored graph on \(N\) vertices and let \(H\) be the graph of the edges in the specified half-palette. Assume each induced \(P_3\) in \(H\) has its two edges the same color. Assume also that both \(H\) and its complement have minimum degree \((1/2-o(1))N\), and that for every hue \(a\) in \(H\) the number of \(H\)-edges of other hues incident with every vertex is greater than \(N/4\).

If \(H\) and its complement are connected, the [Gallai modular decomposition](https://repository.up.ac.za/bitstreams/d84852c0-04f0-4819-b209-53a1c57c0033/download) gives at least four modules with a prime quotient \(J\) (the parts are complete or anticomplete to one another). Choose one representative in every module. Because edges of a prime graph form one [induced-\(P_3\) forcing class](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.08057v1#Thmtheorem1), all intermodule \(H\)-edges seen in the chosen transversal have the same color: every induced \(P_3\) of the quotient lifts to the same induced pattern on representatives. Changing one representative leaves the color unchanged because \(J\) has an edge avoiding that module. Indeed a connected graph in which all edges touch one vertex is a star, whose complement is disconnected. Thus there is one hue on **all** intermodule \(H\)-edges. Each module must contain all the other hues incident to its vertices and so has size greater than \(N/4\). Four such modules are impossible.

If the complement of \(H\) is disconnected, its large minimum degree implies exactly two components, each with \((1/2+o(1))N\) vertices. Take a vertex in one component. It has \(H\)-edges to all vertices of the other. Two consecutive vertices of a complement-path in the other component give two incident \(H\)-edges at the chosen vertex with nonadjacent ends in \(H\), so the two edges have the same hue. By connectivity all edges from that vertex across the cut have one hue. That leaves at most \(o(N)\) edges of other \(H\)-hues at that vertex, contrary to the assumption greater than \(N/4\). Thus this case is also impossible.

It follows that \(H\) must be disconnected, necessarily into two components of sizes \((1/2+o(1))N\) by its own degree lower bound. It has no cross edges and misses only \(o(N^2)\) inside its two parts.

## Passage to the graphon

Sample increasingly large finite edge-colored graphs from the graphon. The forbidden edge-color pattern has probability zero for each triple, so every induced \(P_3\) in the sampled half-palette graph has both edges in the same hue almost surely. Degree concentration around the constant graphon degrees, conditional at each sampled vertex, gives the two \(N/2-o(N)\) minimum degrees simultaneously with high probability. The positive fixed gap between \(1/4\) and each half-palette color's degree gives more than \(N/4\) edges of the other half-palette hues at every vertex with high probability. This permits the finite forcing lemma.

The sampled complement thus differs from a balanced complete bipartite graph by \(o(N^2)\) pairs. Its triangle density tends to zero, so \(U=1-V\) has zero triangle density by convergence of the sampled densities. Write \(\mu\) for vertex measure. Since \(U\) is \(1/2\)-regular, zero triangle density forces it to be exactly a complete bipartite graphon: on almost every positive \(U\)-edge \(xy\), the positive neighborhoods of \(x,y\) are disjoint up to null sets. Both have measure at least \(1/2\) by their degrees, hence exactly \(1/2\), and each row is one on its positive neighborhood. Fix a typical \(x\) and let \(B\) be its \(U\)-neighborhood. For almost every \(y\in B\), the \(U\)-neighborhood of \(y\) is the complement of \(B\), giving all cross edges. The degrees leave no mass for edges on either side. This proves the assertion for \(U\), and \(V=1-U\) gives the desired half-cliques.

The implication-class and modular-decomposition facts are due to Gallai. The color-degree obstruction at a prime quotient and its use to infer an exact graphon parity cut are the additions here. Coordination: [#870](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/870).
