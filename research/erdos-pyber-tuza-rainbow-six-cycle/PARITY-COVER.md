# The color-parity cover

This is a structural reformulation of the remaining rainbow six-cycle obstacle in [Erdős #811](https://www.erdosproblems.com/811). It does not equate a closed walk of arbitrary length with a simple rainbow cycle.

For a finite balanced six-coloring of $K_{6t+1}$, lift each vertex to $(v,g)$ for $g\in\mathbb F_2^6$. If $uv$ is color $i$, join $(u,g)$ to $(v,g+e_i)$, with the six standard basis vectors $e_i$. A walk tracks the parity of the number of occurrences of each color. For a fixed root $v$, its closed-walk parity vectors form a subspace $H$ of $\mathbb F_2^6$, by concatenation. It is independent of the root: attach a path to the other root and its reversal, whose vector cancels. Each component of the lift projects onto the whole complete graph, and its fiber over $v$ is a coset of $H$.

**Rigidity of the cover.** A linear functional annihilates $H$ precisely when its corresponding palette $S$ is a cut palette: all and only the $S$-colored edges cross a fixed bipartition. In fact, define the bit of a vertex by the functional evaluated on any path from a root. Its bit is well-defined by vanishing on closed walks, and the bit difference along an edge is exactly whether its color is in $S$. The converse follows because any closed walk crosses a cut an even number of times.

In the finite exactly balanced case no nonempty $S$ can be a cut palette. The cut sides would be nonempty since each chosen color occurs, and for a vertex on either side the opposite side would have size $|S|t$, forcing even order. Thus the lift is always connected.

## At most four rooted triangles

In fact, at **every** vertex, there is an all-odd closed walk of length at most 12, obtained by concatenating at most four triangles through that vertex. To see this, fix a root $v$. The parity vectors of its rooted triangles span every closed-walk parity vector, by triangulating the closed walk from $v$ and cancelling the doubled chords. (The rooted triangles arise by doing this first for simple cycles; length-two backtracks have zero parity.) Therefore the span is all of $\mathbb F_2^6$.

Each triangle vector has Hamming weight one or three. There are at least seven distinct such vectors at the root. Here is a proof from the exact degree constraints. Choose an ordered pair $x,y$ of distinct nonroot vertices, and set $(i,j,k)=(c(vx),c(vy),c(xy))$. Normalize the count tensor by $t^2$ and symmetrize its three coordinates. Each pair of coordinates then has the same marginal, equal to $1$ on different colors and $r=1-1/t$ on the diagonal. Indeed the ordered choices into two root-neighborhood classes are $t^2$ or $t(t-1)$, and given a vertex in root color $i$, its $k$-neighbors among nonroot vertices number $t$ or $t-1$ according as $i\ne k$ or $i=k$. Symmetrization does not introduce any new parity vector.

Suppose there were exactly six vectors, as fewer cannot span. Let $a$ be the number of singleton vectors present and let $b=6-a$ be the number of triple vectors. If $a=0$, every diagonal marginal is zero, impossible. If $b=0$, the sum of all 15 off-diagonal marginals is at most the sum of the six diagonal marginals: the off-diagonal counts then come entirely from entries with a repeated index, each of which also contributes to a diagonal. This contradicts $15>6r$.

For an absent singleton $i$, all tensor entries with two equal indices and odd color $i$ vanish. The diagonal marginal on $(i,i)$ is $r$; all of that weight occurs at entries $(i,i,j)$ with $j\ne i$. Summing the five off-diagonal marginals in row $i$, the repeated entries contribute exactly $r$ (only $(i,j,i)$ can remain), so each absent $i$ lies in distinct triple palettes of total weight $(5-r)/2>2$. Since each triple entry has weight at most one, each of the $b$ absent colors must belong to at least three of the $b$ present triple palettes. This exhausts their capacities, so all present triple palettes use only absent colors. But then for any absent $i$ and present singleton $j$, the marginal at $(i,j)$ can only come from $(i,j,i)$. Its value is at most $r<1$, contrary to the off-diagonal marginal one. This proves there are at least seven vectors.

Choose six rooted triangle vectors that form a basis. In that basis the all-ones vector is the sum of an even number of basis vectors, because all six basis vectors have odd weight but all-ones has even weight. If it is not the sum of all six, at most four suffice. Otherwise take any additional rooted triangle vector. Its basis expression uses an odd number of vectors, at least three because it is not a basis vector. Substitute it for those basis terms in the all-ones expression, leaving at most four triangle vectors. This gives the asserted walk.

The desired shortest length is six. At length six, the only non-simple projected walk consists of two rainbow triangles with complementary palettes sharing one vertex: loops and two-edge closed walks are impossible without a repeated color. The length-12 bound does not yet remove that degeneracy or the possible longer parity walks.

For a balanced graphon a cut palette must have exactly three colors and have two sides of measure $1/2$, by the analogous degree calculation. Moreover there is at most one nonempty cut palette: the symmetric difference of two cut palettes would be another such palette via addition of their bit functions; two distinct sets of size three have nonzero even-sized symmetric difference. This restates the [graphon criterion](GRAPHON-CRITERION.md) in terms of uniqueness of a possible parity obstruction. The unproved step is to force the cut from exclusion at length six alone, rather than from exclusion at every possible walk length in the finite cover.

## Positive-density rooted triangles in the limit

For a typical root $x$ of a balanced graphon, let $H_x$ be the span of the parity vectors of the ordered rooted triangle patterns of positive density at $x$. If a nonzero palette functional annihilates $H_x$, almost every rooted triangle has even palette count. By Fubini, for almost every $y$, all colors positively supported on $(x,y)$ must have the same palette bit: otherwise one could fix the other two colors using any typical third vertex and obtain both parities. Write $f(y)$ for this common bit. The rooted triangle condition then says every color positively supported on $(y,z)$ has bit $f(y)+f(z)$ modulo two, for almost every $(y,z)$. This is a parity cut. Consequently in every nonparity balanced graphon $H_x$ is the full space at almost every root. In the parity case exactly one nonzero functional is possible, so the rooted span has codimension one almost everywhere.

If the rainbow six-cycle density is zero, at almost every root the rainbow triangle palettes have no complementary pair. In fact, if specific ordered complementary triples have both rooted triangle densities bounded below by $\alpha$ on a positive-measure root set, write $F(x,y)$ and $G(x,y)$ for their ordered three-walk kernels. The lower bounds are $F(x,x),G(x,x)\ge\alpha$. Changing the final root of $F$, or the first root of $G$, changes the kernel by at most the $L^2$ distance of the corresponding final or first color row: this follows immediately by integrating out the first two bounded factors and applying Cauchy–Schwarz to the remaining row. In a positive-measure subset the two relevant rows have pairwise $L^2$ distance less than $\alpha/2$ by separability. Thus for each root pair there $F(x,y)G(y,x)>0$, giving positive rainbow six-cycle density, a contradiction.

A hypothetical nonparity zero-density graphon therefore has full rooted triangle parity span and no complementary rooted rainbow palettes almost everywhere. The separate pair marginals of the local triangle tensor do not suffice to rule that combination out. Any proof along this route needs a cross-root compatibility argument.

### An explicit marginal-only barrier

For every real $5/6<r\le1$, including $r=1-1/t$ for even $t\ge8$, the exact local marginal conditions alone allow full parity span and forbid all complementary rainbow triangle pairs. Use five ordinary colors and one exceptional color $z$. On sorted triples of ordinary colors use weight $a$ for three distinct colors and $b$ for either a repeated color or all three equal. For ordinary $i$, put weight $c$ on $(i,i,z)$ and $d$ on $(i,z,z)$; set every other value involving $z$ to zero. Extend symmetrically, and take
```math
d=r/5,\qquad c=1-d,\qquad b=(r-c)/5,\qquad a=(1-2b)/3.
```
For an ordinary off-diagonal pair the marginal is $2b+3a=1$; for an exceptional–ordinary pair it is $c+d=1$; the diagonal marginals are $5b+c=r$ for an ordinary color and $5d=r$ for $z$. All six singletons have positive weights, so their parity span is full. The only rainbow palettes use three ordinary colors, and none of their complements can occur. This is a tensor obstruction to a proof based only on one-root pair marginals, not a construction of a balanced graph or graphon.
