# The color-parity cover

This is a structural reformulation of the remaining rainbow six-cycle obstacle in [Erdős #811](https://www.erdosproblems.com/811). It does not equate a closed walk of arbitrary length with a simple rainbow cycle.

For a finite balanced six-coloring of $K_{6t+1}$, lift each vertex to $(v,g)$ for $g\in\mathbb F_2^6$. If $uv$ is color $i$, join $(u,g)$ to $(v,g+e_i)$, with the six standard basis vectors $e_i$. A walk tracks the parity of the number of occurrences of each color. For a fixed root $v$, its closed-walk parity vectors form a subspace $H$ of $\mathbb F_2^6$, by concatenation. It is independent of the root: attach a path to the other root and its reversal, whose vector cancels. Each component of the lift projects onto the whole complete graph, and its fiber over $v$ is a coset of $H$.

**Rigidity of the cover.** A linear functional annihilates $H$ precisely when its corresponding palette $S$ is a cut palette: all and only the $S$-colored edges cross a fixed bipartition. In fact, define the bit of a vertex by the functional evaluated on any path from a root. Its bit is well-defined by vanishing on closed walks, and the bit difference along an edge is exactly whether its color is in $S$. The converse follows because any closed walk crosses a cut an even number of times.

In the finite exactly balanced case no nonempty $S$ can be a cut palette. The cut sides would be nonempty since each chosen color occurs, and for a vertex on either side the opposite side would have size $|S|t$, forcing even order. Thus the lift is always connected. In particular, every vertex lies on a closed walk on which every color occurs an odd number of times. A shortest such walk has length at most 190: along a shortest path between $(v,0)$ and $(v,(1,\dots,1))$, balls of radius one about every third vertex are disjoint. Each has size $6t+1$, the lift has $64(6t+1)$ vertices, so the path has length at most 191; even parity of its length improves this to 190. The desired shortest length is six, which is the smallest possible. At length six, the only non-simple projected walk consists of two rainbow triangles with disjoint color palettes sharing one vertex: loops and two-edge closed walks are impossible without a repeated color.

For a balanced graphon a cut palette must have exactly three colors and have two sides of measure $1/2$, by the analogous degree calculation. Moreover there is at most one nonempty cut palette: the symmetric difference of two cut palettes would be another such palette via addition of their bit functions; two distinct sets of size three have nonzero even-sized symmetric difference. This restates the [graphon criterion](GRAPHON-CRITERION.md) in terms of uniqueness of a possible parity obstruction. The unproved step is to force the cut from exclusion at length six alone, rather than from exclusion at every possible walk length in the finite cover.
