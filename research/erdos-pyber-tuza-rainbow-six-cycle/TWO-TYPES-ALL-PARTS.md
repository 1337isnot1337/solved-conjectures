# Every part has exactly two internal row types

**Theorem.** In the balanced [three-block setting](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md) with zero rainbow-six-cycle density, each part has exactly two $`A`$-row classes, each of measure $`t=1/6`$. Consequently the within-part $`A,B`$-rule in each part is precisely a preserving/reversing bit rule (one color for pairs with equal bit, and the other for differing bits). This needs no restriction on the mixture of incoming profile sizes. Moreover every two-color outside vertex fills one whole type in each of its two colors.

This sharpens the countably-many-row-types conclusion in the missing-pair reduction. It does not identify a common cut for the cross colors, and the two internal rules across parts are not assumed to agree.

Fix a part $`X`$, work with indicators via [circle refinement](DETERMINISTIC.md), and note that every outside vertex sends total degree $`2t`$ into $`X`$, with cap $`t`$ in each color. If there are no two-color incoming vertices on positive measure, the [at-least-three target theorem](AT-LEAST-THREE-COLOR-TARGET.md) already gives parity and the two types.

Otherwise choose a realized two-color palette $`\{a,b\}`$ of positive-measure occurrence, with complement $`\{c,d\}`$. The total degree of $`c,d`$ into $`X`$ from the outside is $`4t^2`$ by balance at the roots of $`X`$. Since the outside has measure $`4t`$, the average of the sum of these degrees is $`t`$. It is zero on the positive-measure set of vertices with two-color palette $`\{a,b\}`$. Thus there is a positive-measure set of outside vertices sending more than $`t`$ total degree of $`c,d`$ into $`X`$. Such a vertex sends both colors with positive degree, so the rooted $`c,d`$ return palette has positive density at roots in a positive-measure subset of $`X`$.

For a typical two-color vertex $`y`$ of palette $`\{a,b\}`$, the two color neighborhoods in $`X`$ partition $`X`$ and have measure $`t`$ each. At every typical root $`w`$ where the $`c,d`$ palette is positive, the complementary-palette argument says that all endpoints of the $`a,b`$ palette lie in a single $`A`$-row class. In particular, for almost every pair $`w,y`$ where $`y`$ has this two-color profile, the full neighborhood in the opposite color from $`wy`$, of measure $`t`$, is in one class. (This follows by factoring the rooted return through $`y`$ and applying Fubini.) This proves the existence of an $`A`$-row class of measure $`t`$, using also the universal upper bound $`t`$ on its mass.

A class $`S`$ of mass $`t`$ forces exactly two classes. By symmetry, for almost every vertex its $`A`$-neighborhood either contains essentially all of $`S`$ or none of it. As every $`A`$-degree is $`t`$, its neighborhood is then exactly $`S`$ or exactly $`X\setminus S`$. Both options give uniform rows across their types; symmetry forces the rule to be preserving or reversing the partition bit. The other type also weighs $`t`$.

The same degree argument applies to **every** palette $`\{a',b'\}`$ realized as a two-color profile on positive measure: its complementary palette is positive at a positive set of roots. Thus for almost every vertex of that profile one of its two $`t`$-neighborhoods is an entire row class, and the other is the complementary class. All classes descend from circle refinement by the earlier [row-type descent](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md), or directly because the original internal kernel is already an indicator kernel.

In particular the missing-pair graphon now has six fixed internal half-blocks. What remains is to constrain the distribution of the other four colors between them, including incoming vertices using two colors to one part and three or four to another.
