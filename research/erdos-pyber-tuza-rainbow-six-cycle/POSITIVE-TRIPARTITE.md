# Positive tripartite interfaces force parity

**Theorem.** In a balanced six-color graphon let \(A,B\) fill precisely the within-part pairs of three parts of measure \(1/3\). Suppose each of the other four colors has positive degree from almost every vertex into each of the other two parts. The degrees need not have any common positive lower bound or be equal. Zero rainbow six-cycle density then forces an exact parity cut.

This improves [the uniform interface theorem](UNIFORM-TRIPARTITE.md). The positivity hypothesis is not automatic in the [missing-pair reduction](PAIR-GRAPHONS.md). Any remaining nonparity obstruction in that setting must have a positive-measure set of vertices some cross color of which is entirely directed to just one of the other two parts.

Use [circle refinement](DETERMINISTIC.md) to make the kernels deterministic; it preserves the exact block pattern and every per-part degree, and parity descends. Write \(t=1/6\).

## Row classes and a two-part table principle

The endpoint argument of [Section 1 of the uniform-interface proof](UNIFORM-TRIPARTITE.md) only needs positivity: each ordered two-cross-color path from a typical root in \(X_i\) back to \(X_i\) has positive rooted density. For any complementary pair of two-color palettes, all their endpoints lie in one common \(A\)-row class. For a fixed typical outside vertex \(y\), each of its four color neighborhoods into \(X_i\) is wholly in one \(A\)-row class, by factoring through a \(w\in X_i\) of another color. Those four neighborhoods partition \(X_i\), so the row classes are now finite, with at most four. Each has measure at most \(t\) by symmetry and the degree bound. The argument excluding three classes still holds without a lower size bound: one class would weigh \(t\) and the other two would be complementary to it. The two smaller classes then have equal rows, since each can choose only the singleton class or both smaller classes and symmetry forbids different choices. Thus there are two or four classes; if two, both have measure \(t\).

We will use the following two-part fact, with no uniformity of label masses. Between two parts, suppose the cross colors form the table \(f(x)+g(y)\) on \(\mathbb F_2^2\), with every label on both sides of positive measure. Then zero six-cycle density forces both parts to use the same translation-invariant \(A\)-rule: \(A\) is exactly the differences in a common two-element subset of \(\mathbb F_2^2\). For clarity, the four distinct cross colors sum to zero. The \(A,B\) adjacency functions must be constant on label cells: a four-color return path through the other part has endpoint label difference zero and positive support across the whole same-label cell, and an \(A,B\) two-path can fail only when the two \(A\)-rows agree. If some difference is used by \(A\) on one side and \(B\) on the other, take the two internal edges in those respective parts and connect them by one and three alternating cross steps so as to use every cross color once. The quotient equations close, and every intermediate label is available. Thus each difference is assigned exclusively to one internal color, the same in both parts. For a fixed part, averaging the \(A\)-degrees *over the four labels with equal test weights* gives \(|S_A|\,|X_i|/4=t\), so \(|S_A|=2\) even when the four actual label masses differ. This is a fiber of a nontrivial character.

If \(X_i\) has four row classes, every outside \(y\) permutes the four cross colors across those classes, since all four color neighborhoods are positive and each lies in one class. The Klein-four centralizer argument in [Section 2 of the uniform-interface proof](UNIFORM-TRIPARTITE.md) uses only positivity of the classes and gives a group table between \(X_i\) and any \(X_j\). Every label on the other side has positive measure by positivity of all four cross degrees from a fixed root. The two-part table principle would give only two \(A\)-row types on \(X_i\), a contradiction.

## The split of cross colors must be two-and-two

We now have two types of equal measure in every part. Fix one part \(X_i\), and write \(p_i(w)\) for its type bit. For a root \(w\in X_i\) each of the three complementary palette matchings specifies one target type \(C_m(w)\), independently of which of its two palettes is used. A typical outside vertex \(y\) assigns each of its four positive color neighborhoods into \(X_i\) to a type. If \(wy\) has color \(a\), the number of other colors \(b\) that \(y\) assigns to \(p_i(w)\) is exactly the number of the three matching targets \(C_m(w)\) that equal \(p_i(w)\). This is because the three pairs \(\{a,b\}\) lie in the three different matchings. In particular the number of colors it assigns to that type does not depend on \(y\), for almost every \(w\) of the type. The possible splits are two-and-two or one-and-three, with the singleton type fixed on the latter case.

Suppose the split is one-and-three. Write \(a(y)\) for the singleton color of \(y\). All vertices in the singleton type therefore see \(y\) in the same color \(a(y)\). At a root \(w\) of the triple type, exactly one matching target lies in the opposite type. Let \(m_w\) be its fixed-point-free matching involution on the four colors. The color on \(wy\) must be \(m_w(a(y))\), since the pair with \(a(y)\) is the unique two-path palette through \(y\) reaching the other type. Identifying the four colors with \(\mathbb F_2^2\), the matching involutions are the three nonzero translations; give roots in the singleton type label zero, and other roots the nonzero translation labeling \(m_w\). This makes the cross rule \(m(w)+a(y)\). Every root label has positive measure: the singleton has measure \(t\), and at any typical \(y\) each of the three other colors has positive neighborhood in \(X_i\). Every \(a(y)\) label has positive measure inside any other part, by using a singleton root and the positive degree hypothesis into that part. Hence the two-part table principle applies. It is incompatible with an internal \(A\)-rule based on the split \(\{0\}\) versus the three nonzero labels. In fact every nonzero difference has both a pair between zero and a nonzero label, and a pair between two nonzero labels; one is \(A\) and the other \(B\). This rules out one-and-three.

Thus every outside vertex assigns two colors to each type. The rest is the proof of [Section 3 of the uniform-interface theorem](UNIFORM-TRIPARTITE.md), with positivity in place of prescribed degrees: there is a single pairing \(P_i\) of the four colors for each part. Distinct pairings \(P_i,P_j\) would give a cross group table with all four labels present on both sides, by the two independent pair bits. The two-part principle would force the two different coordinate characters to agree, a contradiction. The interpart sign shift is then constant on each interface by Fubini. The internal \(A\)-bit effect is the same in every part, since equal \(A\)- and \(B\)-effects in different parts would give a cycle by a path of all four cross colors; the needed sequence of paths has positive measure by the hypotheses. Absorb the three interface shifts by the same sign alignment as in the uniform theorem. This gives exactly three crossing colors, and therefore the parity cut.

Scope: this handles arbitrary positive interpart degree profiles but does not handle a color with some degree zero into an entire other part. Coordination: [#870](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/870).
