# Preserving overlapping cycles in high-girth covers

A generalization of [the three-edge switch](COVERS-PRESERVING-CYCLES.md) for the cover approach to [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585). This is not a resolution of the extremal question.

**Theorem.** Let $`B`$ be a finite connected simple graph with at least one cycle, and let $`\mathcal C`$ be any nonempty finite set of its simple cycles. The cycles may share vertices and edges. For every $`g`$, and every sufficiently large prime $`p`$ (depending on $`B,\mathcal C,g`$), there is a connected $`p`$-sheet cover of $`B`$ of girth at least $`g`$ in which the full preimage of every cycle in $`\mathcal C`$ is one cycle. In particular, take $`\mathcal C`$ to be all simple cycles of $`B`$ to simultaneously preserve all base witness pairs. An upstairs cycle not projecting to a repeated traversal of a base simple cycle is not characterized by the theorem.

**Initialization.** Orient all base edges arbitrarily, and assign each an independent uniform increment in $`\mathbb F_p`$, negated when traversed backwards. Form the cyclic cover as in the three-edge note. Each base simple cycle has some edge occurring exactly once, so its signed sum is uniform on $`\mathbb F_p`$ and is zero with probability $`1/p`$. A union bound shows that all sums for $`\mathcal C`$ can be nonzero when $`p>|\mathcal C|`$. Each nonzero translation on $`\mathbb F_p`$ is a single $`p`$-cycle, so all designated preimages are connected. This cover and every subsequent one are connected because at least one designated preimage spans the whole fibre at a vertex and every component of a cover maps onto a connected base.

**Choosing five aligned edges.** Suppose the current shortest cycle has length $`\ell<g`$. Pick an edge $`e`$ on one such cycle, lying over $`ab`$. All edges over $`ab`$ form a matching. Greedily choose a constant-size set of additional edges in that matching so that the distance between distinct selected endpoint sets is greater than $`g`$. This is possible for all sufficiently large $`p`$: a chosen edge prohibits at most $`2\sum_{j=0}^g D^j`$ further edges, where $`D`$ is the maximum degree of $`B`$.

For every designated cycle that contains $`ab`$, orient its connected preimage so its lifts of $`ab`$ go from the $`a`$-fibre to the $`b`$-fibre. Cut its cyclic order of the selected edges at the distinguished edge $`e`$, and view the remaining positions as a linear order. If there are designated cycles containing $`ab`$, use the order of one of them as reference and apply Erdős–Szekeres repeatedly to the sequence of ranks in each of the other orders, restricting to the last surviving subsequence at each step. The recurrence $`M_0=4`$, $`M_{i+1}=(M_i-1)^2+1`$ gives enough edges besides $`e`$ when there are at most $`i`$ other orders. Thus there are four additional edges whose relative order is increasing or decreasing in every designated cyclic order when cut at $`e`$. With $`e`$ included, the five edges are in the same cyclic order or its reversal along every relevant preimage. If no designated cycle contains $`ab`$, choose any five well-separated edges including $`e`$.

**Five-edge switch.** Label these edges $`x_i y_i`$ for $`i\in\mathbb Z/5`$, with $`x_i`$ above $`a`$ and $`y_i`$ above $`b`$, in their reference order. Replace them by $`x_i y_{i+2}`$. This still gives a matching over $`ab`$. Removing the five edges from any affected designated preimage left five directed paths from $`y_i`$ to $`x_{i+\epsilon}`$ with $`\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}`$ fixed for that preimage. After switching, the successor on the outgoing endpoints is $`i\mapsto i+2+\epsilon`$, a single 5-cycle for either sign. All five paths therefore remain on one cycle. Designated preimages not containing these edges are unchanged.

As before, a new cycle of length at most $`\ell`$ cannot use an old-edge segment between distinct endpoint groups. The five new edges connect the groups in a 5-cycle, with degree two at every group. If any new edge occurs in such a short cycle then all five must occur, and the old segment in each group must join the endpoints of its deleted edge. Each segment then has length at least $`\ell-1`$. So the supposed new cycle has length at least $`5\ell`$, a contradiction. At least the selected old $`\ell`$-cycle disappears. The switch process terminates by lexicographic decrease of the short-cycle counts. This proves the theorem.

**Related work and scope.** [Perarnau–Santos](https://dmd2024.web.uah.es/files/ProceedingsDMD2024.pdf), pp. 77–79, study high-girth graph lifts and use a two-edge switch in a fibre; here the additional invariant is connectedness of all designated preimages. The two signs above are why shifting by two on five ports works even though a three-port switch cannot accommodate both orders. The final cover need not be a cyclic or regular covering map (that is a different use of “regular” from degree-regularity). Choosing a cover preserves witnesses in a graph we construct, not in a given arbitrary graph of large degree.
