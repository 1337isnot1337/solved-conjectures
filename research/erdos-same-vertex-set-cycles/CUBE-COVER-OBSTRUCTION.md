# Cube covers obstruct expander extraction

For [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585), [the spectral criterion](BIPARTITE-SPECTRAL-CERTIFICATE.md) does give a cycle pair once the right subgraph is present. But density and girth do not force that certificate, even well above the conjectured edge threshold.

**Theorem.** There are connected simple bipartite $`D`$-regular graphs $`G_D`$ of order $`n_D`$, with girth tending to infinity and $`D/\log\log n_D\to\infty`$, such that every nonempty-edge subgraph has a nontrivial vertex cut in the sense of a bipartition of its vertices whose **crossing edges form a matching**. In particular:

1. Every regular subgraph of positive degree $`d`$ has adjacency spectral gap $`d-\lambda_2\le2`$, where $`\lambda_2`$ is the second largest eigenvalue, with multiplicities.
2. For each fixed integer $`C\ge4`$, none of the $`G_D`$ has a balanced bipartite $`C`$-expander subgraph with at least $`4C`$ vertices per side, in the sense of [Definition 3.1 in the bipartite Hamilton-path paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.22807v3). This rules out extracting such a subgraph even without requiring regularity.

**Construction.** Let $`g=\max(4,\lfloor\sqrt D\rfloor)`$. Take the finite set $`W`$ of all reduced words of length $`\le g`$ on $`D`$ involutive letters. A reduced word has no identical consecutive letters, and two adjacent identical letters cancel in multiplication. For each letter $`i`$, form a permutation $`s_i`$ of $`W`$ by swapping every pair of words interchanged by right multiplication by $`i`$ for which both words belong to $`W`$, and fixing the unmatched boundary words. This is an involution. Any reduced sequence of at most $`g`$ letters acts on the empty word by sending it to the corresponding nonempty word, so it is not the identity permutation.

Let $`\Gamma`$ be the subgroup of $`(\mathbb Z/2)^D\times\mathrm{Sym}(W)`$ generated by $`t_i=(e_i,s_i)`$, with $`e_i`$ the $`i`$-th unit vector. The Cayley graph using these generators is connected, simple and $`D`$-regular. The parity of the first coordinate gives a bipartition, since each generator flips one bit. A simple cycle gives a nonempty reduced word in the generators, with no consecutive backtracking. No such word of length at most $`g`$ can be the identity because of its permutation component. Thus the girth exceeds $`g`$.

Write $`M=|W|\le(g+1)D^g`$. The construction gives $`2^D\le n_D\le2^D M!`$; the lower bound is surjectivity of first-coordinate projection. For natural logarithms and large $`D`$, $`\log n_D\le D\log 2+M\log M`$, hence $`\log\log n_D=O(g\log D)=o(D)`$. This finite permutation construction and its product form are standard; see [Otto, Highly Acyclic Groups, Hypergraph Covers and the Guarded Fragment](https://www2.mathematik.tu-darmstadt.de/~otto/papers/acycinhypJ.pdf). The consequence here uses the additional prescribed cube projection.

**Hereditary matching cut.** The $`i`$-th bit gives a cut whose crossing edges are exactly the label-$`i`$ edges. Each vertex is incident to just one such edge. In any subgraph with an edge, take the label of that edge; both sides of the restricted cut are nonempty and the crossing edges still form a matching.

For a regular subgraph $`H`$ of degree $`d>0`$, let the matching cut have sides $`S,T`$ and $`N=|V(H)|`$. The test function $`1_S-(|S|/N)1`$ has squared norm $`|S||T|/N`$ and Laplacian energy $`e_H(S,T)\le\min(|S|,|T|)`$. The variational formula gives $`d-\lambda_2\le N/\max(|S|,|T|)\le2`$, including the disconnected case. In the bipartite spectral certificate, the nontrivial singular bound $`\lambda`$ is at least the second largest adjacency eigenvalue for these positive-degree regular subgraphs of sufficiently large order.

**Why a matching cut also rules out the joinedness certificate.** Suppose $`H`$ were a balanced bipartite $`C`$-expander, with left and right sides of size $`m\ge4C`$. Put $`p=\lfloor m/(2C)\rfloor\ge2`$ and $`q=\lceil m/(2C)\rceil`$. Let $`S,T`$ be any nontrivial cut with crossing edges a matching. In each of $`S,T`$ some bipartition class has more than $`p`$ vertices. Otherwise its nonempty larger class $`A`$ satisfies $`1\le|A|\le p`$ and $` |N(A)|\le 2|A|`$, since inside that shore it meets at most the smaller class, and across the cut each vertex contributes at most one neighbour. This contradicts $`C`$-expansion. For each shore, a $`p`$-subset of the class with more than $`p`$ vertices has at most $`p`$ neighbours across the cut, so the other class in that shore has at least $`(C-1)p`$ vertices. Apply this again in the opposite direction to obtain that bound for both classes of both shores. Since $`C\ge4`$ and $`p\ge2`$, $`(C-1)p\ge 2q`$. Choose $`q`$ left vertices in $`S`$. They have at most $`q`$ neighbours in $`T`$, so there are $`q`$ right vertices in $`T`$ with no edge to them. This contradicts bipartite joinedness.

**Scope.** The family does not disprove the conjectured extremal order: none of the arguments excludes a pair of edge-disjoint cycles on the same vertex set. A matching cut can have many crossing edges, and both cycles can cross it. What fails is a proof route that first extracts a strongly mixing subgraph, or a globally joined bipartite $`C`$-expander, using only degree, order and girth. Those conditions hold here at densities arbitrarily greater than $`n\log\log n`$; the failure is hereditary and does not disappear by discarding a poor connected component. Methods for this case must use a weaker expansion condition, exploit colour structure, or avoid global joinedness altogether.
