# Girth-raising covers preserving selected cycles

This gives positive examples for [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585) beyond the reach of the extraction route ruled out in [CUBE-COVER-OBSTRUCTION.md](CUBE-COVER-OBSTRUCTION.md). It does not settle the general extremal question.

**Lemma.** Let \(B\) be a finite connected simple graph of maximum degree \(D\), and let \(\mathcal C\) be a nonempty collection of edge-disjoint simple cycles in \(B\), not necessarily vertex-disjoint. Fix \(g\ge4\). For every integer \(s>4\sum_{j=0}^{g}D^j\), there is a connected \(s\)-sheet cover of \(B\) of girth at least \(g\) in which the full preimage of each cycle in \(\mathcal C\) is a single cycle.

By an \(s\)-sheet cover we mean that each base vertex has \(s\) lifts, and the edges over each base edge form a perfect matching of the two corresponding fibres. Start with a cyclic cover: label the fibres by \(\mathbb Z/s\), orient base edges arbitrarily, and match equal indices except on one chosen edge per cycle in \(\mathcal C\), where the matching is a shift by \(1\). These chosen edges are distinct by hypothesis. Traversing a selected base cycle changes the index by \(1\) or \(-1\). Therefore its preimage is one cycle. Any cover with a single full preimage for at least one selected cycle is connected: every component maps onto the connected base by the matching property, but only one component can contain vertices over any point of that selected cycle.

Let the current girth be \(\ell<g\). Pick an edge on a shortest cycle and call it \(x_1y_1\), with \(x_1\) over a base vertex \(a\) and \(y_1\) over its neighbour \(b\). Choose two more edges \(x_2y_2,x_3y_3\) in the same base-edge matching with their endpoint groups pairwise at distance greater than \(g\) in the current cover. Greedily exclude the radius-\(g\) balls around the endpoints already chosen. There are at most \(4\sum_{j=0}^{g}D^j\) vertices to exclude, and each excluded vertex rules out at most one edge in this matching. Thus both choices exist.

If \(ab\) belongs to a selected cycle, orient its single lifted cycle in the direction that uses \(x_1\to y_1\); every chosen edge over \(ab\) is then traversed from \(x_i\) to \(y_i\). Relabel 2 and 3 if necessary so that their cyclic order along this lift is 1, 2, 3. Delete the three chosen edges and insert \(x_i y_{i+1}\) for \(i\) modulo 3. This preserves the perfect matching over \(ab\). If the lifted cycle was prescribed, the remaining oriented paths go from \(y_i\) to \(x_{i+1}\). The new edge out of \(x_i\) and the path that follows take it to \(x_{i+2}\), so all three paths are again on one cycle. The other prescribed preimages are unchanged by edge-disjointness of the base cycles. If \(ab\) is not in any selected cycle, use either cyclic ordering of the three edges.

No new cycle of length at most \(\ell\) is created. If a new cycle that short uses any of the inserted edges, break it into inserted edges and paths of old edges. None of the old paths can join two different endpoint groups, by their distance. As each group has exactly two endpoints of inserted edges, the cycle must follow all three inserted edges round their triangle of groups. In each group it must also join \(x_i\) to \(y_i\) by an old path of length at least \(\ell-1\), since the deleted edge would have closed it to an old cycle. This gives length at least \(3\ell\). At least one old shortest cycle was destroyed. Therefore the vector of cycle counts at lengths \(3,\dots,g-1\) decreases lexicographically, and the switch terminates, since the fibre sets and the possible matchings over each base edge are finite. This proves the lemma.

## An application to cube covers

Use the \(D\)-cube as base, \(D\ge4\), and choose a four-dimensional face. Its 16 vertices can be numbered by the integers 0 through 15, with adjacent integers differing in one binary bit. The two cyclic lists, each closed back to 0, give edge-disjoint spanning cycles in that face:

- `0, 1, 3, 2, 6, 7, 5, 4, 12, 13, 9, 11, 15, 14, 10, 8`.
- `0, 2, 10, 11, 3, 7, 15, 13, 5, 1, 9, 8, 12, 14, 6, 4`.

In the cover from the lemma, the two preimages are disjoint as edge sets and use identical vertex sets: the full fibres over the face vertices. The coordinate matching cuts hold hereditarily, exactly as in [CUBE-COVER-OBSTRUCTION.md](CUBE-COVER-OBSTRUCTION.md). In particular, their witness cannot lie in a large balanced joined bipartite expander of the type ruled out there. With \(g=D\) and \(s=1+4\sum_{j=0}^{D}D^j\), the total order \(2^Ds\) is \(\exp(O(D\log D))\), and hence \(D/\log\log(2^Ds)\to\infty\).

The 3-edge switch differs from the 2-edge switch in [HAMILTON-MODULES.md](HAMILTON-MODULES.md): a 2-edge switch restricted to one base matching and made in the common orientation splits a prescribed lifted cycle. The 3-edge switch preserves both the matching over the base edge and the prescribed connected preimage. No claim is made that an arbitrary lift preserves these preimages or has some other cycle pair.
