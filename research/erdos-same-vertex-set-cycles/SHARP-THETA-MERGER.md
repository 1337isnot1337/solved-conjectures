# A minimum-size parity trade that actually merges a cycle cover

Partial progress on [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585). This proves existence of a shortest-possible parity-changing move that reaches two common-support cycles **in a constructed graph**. It does not force such a move in a general high-density host.

**Theorem.** For every even $`g\ge4`$, there is a connected simple bipartite quartic graph of girth $`g`$, with a proper four-edge-colouring and a three-colour trade on exactly $`2\lceil3g/4\rceil`$ edges, such that one initial factor has two cycles and the other is Hamiltonian, while after the trade both factors are Hamiltonian. Here the two factors are the unions of two fixed pairs of colours. This number of changed edges is optimal for this transition by [the parity-trade girth theorem](PARITY-TRADE-GIRTH.md), since the sum of the two component counts changes from three to two.

## Plant the trade

Use the minimum even theta of that theorem: if $`4`$ divides $`g`$, its three lengths are $`g/2`$; otherwise they are $`g/2-1,g/2+1,g/2+1`$. Let its branch vertices $`x,y`$ be on the left. Give the three legs alternating colour pairs 12, 23, 31 respectively, starting at $`x`$ with 1, 2, 3 respectively. Designate the two equal, longer legs as 12 and 31; if all lengths are equal choose arbitrarily. Swap the two colours along each of the three legs to specify the new colouring. The incident colour sets are unchanged at every vertex, so the incomplete colour assignments can have the same completion outside the theta.

Use factor groups red $`=\{1,4\}`$, blue $`=\{2,3\}`$. Let $`C`$ be the simple circuit of the two mixed legs 12 and 31. Its half-length $`m`$ is even, since the two legs have equal even lengths. Along $`C`$, in each state every vertex has exactly one red edge (of colour 1) and one blue edge, and the state change interchanges their memberships. The third leg stays blue, although its two labels interchange.

## Prescribe the exterior red order

At every vertex of $`C`$ there is one missing red stub, of colour 4. The induced red pairings there in the old and new states consist of the two alternate matchings of $`C`$. Index the equal left and right port sets so the old pairing has permutation identity and the new pairing is the one-step cyclic shift. Use the **two-step** cyclic shift as the exterior red pairing. Its union with the old pairing has exactly two cycles, because $`m`$ is even; its union with the new pairing has exactly one. Coincident pairs in this port calculation are counted as parallel edges, not as coincident realized paths.

The internal vertices of the third leg have no red theta edge. Insert all of them, in any order, along one of the exterior red routes, treating each as two consecutive stubs and breaking the route into segments between distinct old vertices. The route goes from its left to its right end, with red colour 4 prescribed on every step from left to right, and colour 1 on every step from right to left. Equivalently at every inserted old vertex the incoming and outgoing stubs are distinct and have these prescribed alternating labels. Each segment can therefore be realized by a bipartite path alternating its red labels, regardless of the sides of its two endpoints. It still counts as the same single connector on the original red port pairing.

## Prescribe a blue order that works in both states

There is a single blue stub at every vertex of $`C`$ other than $`x,y`$; no blue stub is needed on the remainder of the theta. This terminal set is identical in the two states. The blue part in each state consists of paths. In its port pairing, the extra blue leg $`xy`$ joins the two blue neighbours of $`x,y`$ in that state's matching on $`C`$. Thus the old and new blue pairings have connected union. Indeed, removing $`x,y`$ from $`C`$ leaves the interiors of its two mixed legs. Since the two legs have even length, the old blue neighbours of $`x,y`$ lie in different interiors, as do the new blue neighbours. Each interior is a path in the union of the shortened pairings. The two shortcut pairing edges join its ends to the other interior's ends, forming one cycle; when the interiors are singletons this is a doubled edge.

Partition the blue ports into two **type classes** given by the product of their left/right sign and the sign of the missing matching label 2 or 3. Every old or new blue pairing goes between the two types: for any alternating blue path, its two missing endpoint labels are equal if the vertices lie on opposite host sides and differ if the vertices lie on the same side. The two type classes have equal size $`m-1`$ because one of these pairings already matches them. The relative permutation of the old and new pairings is an $`(m-1)`$-cycle by the connectedness just proved, so their signs agree, since $`m-1`$ is odd. The [common port lemma](COMMON-PORT-CLOSURE.md) now gives an exterior pairing between the two types that closes both states into one; for $`m-1=1`$ just take the unique pair. Each requested blue exterior pair has compatible endpoint labels for a bipartite alternating path, by the definition of types.

## Complete both colours on shared new vertices

The total left-minus-right stub count in each colour is twice the left-minus-right vertex imbalance of the theta, because every planted factor edge contributes once on each bipartite side. This counts both stubs of each red isolated vertex and shows the two stub imbalances agree. Apply the [paired connector construction](PAIRED-CONNECTOR-CONSTRUCTION.md) to all the red segments and blue pairs. This provides one common set of new vertices with the required edge-disjoint red and blue paths. Each path alternates the matching labels of its own factor as prescribed. Both initial and final edge-colourings are proper and every vertex sees all four colours: on the theta we filled exactly the missing stubs, and every new vertex has degree two in each factor. The same exterior paths and labels apply before and after the theta trade. The graph is simple and has girth at least $`g`$ by the connector lemma, and girth exactly $`g`$ because its shortest theta cycle remains. It is connected because the final factors span as single cycles. Only the theta edges change colours, as claimed.
