# Erdős's same-vertex-set cycles problem

## Large girth does not repair a bare four-regular core

A structural obstruction to one proof route for [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585); it is not a counterexample to its proposed asymptotic upper bound.

**Replacement lemma.** Let d=2k, and let H be a simple, bipartite, d-regular, d-edge-connected graph with no partition of its edges into k Hamilton cycles. For every integer g there is a simple, bipartite, d-regular, d-edge-connected graph of girth at least g containing no k edge-disjoint cycles on the same vertex set.

In particular there are such graphs for k=2. Use as H the published bipartite, 4-regular, 4-connected non-Hamiltonian graph of [McCuaig–Rosenfeld](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0004972700021213). This shows why extracting a 4-regular subgraph at the sharp n log log n scale does not suffice, even with large girth and maximal possible edge connectivity.

### Replacement

Choose a finite connected d-regular bipartite d-edge-connected graph J of girth at least max(g,4); existence is proved below. Delete one vertex v of J and call its d neighbors the ports. At each vertex x of H put a separate copy of J−v. Assign the d incident base edges bijectively to the d ports. Replace each base edge by an edge between its two assigned ports. This restores degree d everywhere. It is simple by the construction. All ports in a module lie in the same bipartition side, so reverse each module bipartition on one base colour to obtain global bipartiteness.

Any cycle staying in a module has length at least g. A cycle using external edges includes some path inside a module joining two distinct ports, because each port is on only one external edge. That path plus the two edges to the deleted vertex would form a cycle of J, so the path has length at least g−2. The original cycle uses at least two external edges, and therefore has length at least g as well.

### Preserve edge connectivity

For any nontrivial cut of the new graph, call a module split if it has vertices on both sides. If a split module has r ports on the first side and d−r on the second, it contributes at least max(r,d−r) internal cut edges. Indeed, restoring v on either side makes a nontrivial cut of J, giving the two inequalities.

Two split modules give at least d cut edges since each contributes at least d/2. With no split module the cut equals a base cut, which has at least d edges. With one split module, assign its base vertex entirely to one colour so that the resulting base cut is nontrivial; this is always possible because H has other vertices. Relative to the actual external cut edges, this change adds at most the number of ports changed to that colour. The split module contributes at least that many internal cut edges by the preceding bound. The new cut therefore has at least the size of the chosen base cut, namely d.

### Project a hypothetical pair or packing back to the base

In a connected 2k-regular graph, k edge-disjoint cycles on the same vertex set must use every vertex and every edge. Indeed at each used vertex all 2k incident edges are used, so no edge leaves their common support. Connectivity makes the support full. In the replaced graph, each of the k Hamilton cycles must cross each nontrivial module cut at least twice. There are exactly 2k external edges at each module, so each cycle crosses exactly twice. Contracting modules now projects each of the k connected cycles to a connected spanning 2-regular subgraph of H: it uses exactly two distinct base edges at each vertex. The projections are edge-disjoint because external edges correspond to base edges one to one. They would partition H into k Hamilton cycles, contrary to hypothesis.

The projection uses the whole packing, not a claim that every individual Hamilton cycle projects to a Hamilton cycle. A single cycle could cross a module cut four or more times.

### High-girth modules by finite permutations

Use the free group on k generators. Take its finite ball of reduced words of length at most L, where L>=max(g,4). Right multiplication by each generator induces a partially defined injective map on the ball, defined when the image stays in the ball. Extend each to a permutation, arbitrarily matching unused domain elements to unused range elements. A nontrivial reduced word of length at most L sends the empty word to that word: the prefixes remain in the ball, including when a generator inverse is used. Hence there is no relation of length <=L among these permutation generators.

To force bipartiteness, use the subgroup generated by the images of the generators paired with 1 in the product with Z/2. The Cayley graph using these generators and their inverses is connected, vertex-transitive, simple, bipartite, and d-regular; any simple cycle of length <=L would give a nontrivial reduced word relation. Its edge connectivity is d. One proof of this last standard fact takes a minimum-cardinality nonempty side A of a minimum edge cut, with |A|<=|V|/2. Distinct automorphic images of A cannot intersect: their union is proper when they intersect, so submodularity of cut sizes would make the intersection a smaller minimum-cut side. The images partition the vertices and the stabilizer is transitive on A. Every vertex in A therefore has the same number b>=1 of external neighbors and at most |A|−1 internal ones. If |A|>=d the cut has at least d edges. Otherwise |A|b>=|A|(d+1−|A|)>=d. This establishes the desired connectivity.

The construction is a vertex-splice argument of Meredith type. The degree-saturating projection and high-girth modules are what is used here. No priority claim is made. In particular the construction only establishes an obstruction at degree d where a seed lacking a full k-cycle Hamilton decomposition is available; it does not give high-average-degree graphs violating the proposed bound for a pair of cycles.
