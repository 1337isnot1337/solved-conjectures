# Path seeds do not automatically close

For [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585), the [paired-path lower bound](PAIRED-PATH-LOWER-BOUND.md) shows that even finding the first two paths costs an order-$`N\log\log N`$ density threshold in general. Their existence, however, does not imply the desired cycles.

**A positive criterion for seeds.** If a connected 4-regular simple graph has a Hamilton cycle $`R`$ whose complementary 2-factor has exactly two components, it has two edge-disjoint Hamilton paths. An edge $`uv`$ of $`R`$ joins the two complementary cycles. Delete it from $`R`$ to make the red path. Delete one incident edge at $`u`$ from its complementary cycle and one incident edge at $`v`$ from the other. Join the two resulting paths by $`uv`$. The blue path spans, uses no edge of the first path, and has the same vertex set.

**An unbounded obstruction to closure.** For any desired girth choose a [simple bipartite 4-regular graph with a Hamilton decomposition](HAMILTON-MODULES.md). Take the two-sheet cover obtained from two copies by crossing the matching over exactly one red edge. The red lift is a single Hamilton cycle: traversing the red base cycle interchanges sheets once. The blue lift is two cycles, one per sheet. Thus the criterion above applies to the lift. But the lift has only two edges between the sheets, since only one base-edge matching was crossed. In a connected 4-regular graph a common-support pair of cycles would exhaust the degree at every vertex in its support, so it would have to span. Each spanning cycle crosses the two-edge cut at least twice; two edge-disjoint ones are impossible. Connectivity also follows directly from the red Hamilton cycle. Bipartiteness and at least the base girth are retained by the covering map. Hence this gives simple bipartite examples in every girth range that admit spanning path seeds but no common-support cycle pair at all.

This does not preclude a seed-to-cycle argument with substantial spare degree or robust hypotheses. It shows why a transference argument would need to use those extra hypotheses, rather than only the existence of two paths.
