# The smallest parity-breaking three-colour trade at prescribed girth

Partial work on [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585). In the [Kempe obstruction](KEMPE-REACHABILITY.md), two-colour switches could not change the factorization sign. The following determines the exact number of changed edges a parity-breaking three-colour move can require as girth grows. This is a theorem about the allowed moves, not about existence of a Hamilton decomposition.

**Theorem.** For every even $`g\ge4`$, the smallest number of changed edges in a sign-reversing proper recolouring confined to three colours of a regular simple bipartite graph of girth at least $`g`$ is $`2\lceil 3g/4\rceil`$. The upper bound can be achieved in a connected quartic graph of girth exactly $`g`$, leaving its fourth colour fixed.

## Local sign and the lower bound

Use the change subgraph $`T`$ consisting of every edge whose colour differs between the two proper factorizations. Each affected vertex changes either two colours by a transposition or three colours by a 3-cycle. Thus $`T`$ has degrees two and three, and the ratio of the factorization signs is $`(-1)^{n_2(T)}`$ by the [local sign formula](KEMPE-REACHABILITY.md), where $`n_2(T)`$ counts its degree-two vertices. Select a connected component $`C`$ of $`T`$ with $`n_2(C)`$ odd. It cannot be a cycle: the host is bipartite. Consequently $`C`$ has a degree-three vertex, hence cycle-space dimension at least two (the degree sum also gives an even number of degree-three vertices).

Pick two distinct simple cycles in $`C`$. If edge-disjoint they use at least $`2g`$ edges. Otherwise their symmetric difference contains a cycle, and summing the two lengths and the size of the symmetric difference gives twice the size of their union. It follows that $`|E(C)|\ge3g/2`$.

If equality holds at $`3g/2`$, every two distinct simple cycles in $`C`$ must cover its entire edge set, by that same estimate. If the cycle-space dimension were at least three, delete any one edge. Taking the kernel of its edge coordinate leaves a cycle space of dimension at least two. This gives two distinct simple cycles avoiding that edge, a contradiction. Thus the dimension is two, so $`C`$ has exactly two degree-three vertices by $`2|E(C)|=2|V(C)|+n_3(C)`$. Then $`|E(C)|=n_2(C)+3`$ is even. This rules out equality when $`g\equiv2\pmod4`$, giving a lower bound of the least even integer at least $`3g/2`$.

## Construction and high-girth completion

Take a theta graph with its two branch vertices on the same bipartition side: three internally vertex-disjoint paths of even lengths. If $`4`$ divides $`g`$, use three lengths $`g/2`$. Otherwise use lengths $`g/2-1,g/2+1,g/2+1`$. The two shortest paths have total length $`g`$, and the theta has precisely $`2\lceil3g/4\rceil`$ edges. Properly colour the three paths by alternating the pairs 12, 23, 31, starting with colours 1, 2, 3 respectively at one branch vertex. Because all path lengths are even, at the other branch vertex the incident colours are 2, 3, 1. This is a partial proper colouring with four colours available. On the theta, interchange the two colours along each path, leaving all other edges alone. This is a legal recolouring whenever each colour outside the theta is unchanged: the colours incident to every vertex are the same before and after. Its two branch vertices undergo 3-cycles, and its $`|E(T)|-3`$ internal vertices undergo transpositions. The latter number is odd, so the trade reverses the sign.

Here is an elementary high-girth completion of the partially coloured theta, using the same idea as [the girth-raising switch](HAMILTON-MODULES.md). Enlarge the two sides to the same sufficiently large size. Extend each colour independently from its existing partial matching to a perfect matching, initially permitting parallel edges of different colours. Keep all theta edges protected. If a shortest cycle has length $`\ell<g`$, including length two for a parallel pair, it contains an unprotected edge since the theta itself has girth $`g`$. Within the same matching choose a second unprotected edge farther than $`g`$ from both endpoints of the first. There is such an edge if the side size exceeds the number of protected edges plus the size bound for two radius-$`g`$ balls of degree at most four. Switch the right endpoints of those two matching edges. A new cycle using only one inserted edge requires a long old connector between the two distant endpoint groups. A new cycle using both either requires such a connector or uses two old paths within the two groups, each of length at least $`\ell-1`$ by the old girth. Hence no new cycle of length at most $`\ell`$ appears, and the old shortest-cycle count strictly decreases lexicographically. This terminates on the fixed vertex set. The resulting graph is simple and quartic, still respecting the protected theta and its colouring. Keep the component containing the theta; it stays regular and has girth exactly $`g`$. This proves attainability.

## Structure of a general three-colour trade

For insight beyond the minimum, ignore components that are cycles. Suppress degree-two vertices in the other components of the change subgraph. Along each suppressed path the moved unordered colour pair is constant, since both changed edges at an internal vertex are precisely the two it transposes. The cubic multigraph of branch vertices has three distinct pair types at each vertex, and no loop (a loop would require the same pair twice at one vertex). Let $`\sigma(v)\in\{+1,-1\}`$ distinguish the two cyclic orders of its local 3-cycle (both are even permutations), and let $`b(v)\in\{+1,-1\}`$ denote its original bipartition side. For a suppressed path of length $`\ell`$ from $`u`$ to $`v`$,

```math
\sigma(u)\sigma(v)=(-1)^{\ell-1},
\qquad b(u)b(v)=(-1)^\ell.
```

The first identity holds because each internal transposition reverses the direction of the colour change on its unordered pair; the second holds by bipartiteness. Thus $`\sigma b`$ is a bipartition of the cubic skeleton. If its sides have size $`h`$ and $`r`$ of its branch vertices were on the original negative side, its sign change is $`(-1)^{h+r}`$. Indeed the product of $`\sigma(u)\sigma(v)`$ over skeleton edges equals both $`(-1)^{n_2}`$ and the product of $`\sigma`$ over branch vertices, since their degrees are odd. This gives a test for when a permitted three-colour move actually escapes the two-colour sign classes. It does not force such a move in an arbitrary factorization, or force it to reduce cycle counts.
