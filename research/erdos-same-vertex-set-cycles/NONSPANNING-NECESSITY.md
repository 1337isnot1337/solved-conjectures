# Nonspanning witnesses are necessary in every degree

A further limitation on spanning-cycle approaches to [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585). For every integer $`d\ge3`$ and girth threshold $`g`$, there is a simple bipartite $`d`$-regular, $`d`$-edge-connected graph of girth at least $`g`$ with **no Hamilton cycle**. The construction does not rule out a pair of cycles on a proper support.

## A weighted fixed non-Hamiltonian core

Fix a non-Hamiltonian cyclically 5-edge-connected simple cubic bipartite graph $`K`$ from [Brinkmann–Goedgebeur–McKay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00943). Its two colour classes have the same size because it is cubic. Factor its edges into three perfect matchings by Hall's theorem. Write $`d=3m+r`$ with $`m\ge1`$, $`0\le r\le2`$. Replace each edge by $`m`$ parallel copies and add another copy on the first $`r`$ matchings. The resulting bipartite multigraph $`K_d`$ is $`d`$-regular and its underlying simple graph is still $`K`$.

It is $`d`$-edge-connected. If a cut has a singleton shore, its size is $`d`$. If a shore has size two, its size is at least $`2d-2(m+1)`$ when $`r>0`$, which is at least $`d`$; for $`r=0`$ replace $`m+1`$ by $`m`$, with the same conclusion. If both shores have at least three vertices, any acyclic shore on $`u`$ vertices in the cubic graph has boundary at least $`3u-2(u-1)=u+2\ge5`$. If both shores contain cycles, cyclic 5-edge-connectivity gives the same bound of five on the base cut. Its multiplicity in $`K_d`$ is therefore at least $`5m\ge d`$.

## Partial vertex substitution

Take a connected bipartite $`d`$-regular $`d`$-edge-connected graph $`J`$ of girth at least $`g`$. One can use the vertex-transitive Cayley construction of [the cube-cover note](CUBE-COVER-OBSTRUCTION.md), setting its ball radius to the desired girth; the standard edge-connectivity argument is given in [the high-girth replacement note](HIGH-GIRTH-REPLACEMENT.md). This use of involutions works in odd as well as even degree.

Inflate each vertex in **one** colour class of $`K_d`$ to a copy of $`J-v`$, for a fixed vertex $`v`$ of $`J`$. Retain the other colour class as singletons. The $`d`$ neighbours of $`v`$ are distinct ports all in one bipartition class. Attach every incident edge of the original vertex at a distinct port; parallel base edges now go from the same singleton to distinct ports. The result is simple, bipartite and $`d`$-regular, with no edges between singletons or directly between modules. A cycle through a singleton contains a path in a module joining distinct ports, which has length at least $`g-2`$ because going through $`v`$ would close it in $`J`$; it also uses at least two external edges. Cycles internal to a module already have girth at least $`g`$.

The connectivity argument is the usual split-module calculation, and it does not require a Hamilton decomposition of $`J`$. If a module is split by a cut, with $`s`$ of its ports on one side, placing the removed vertex $`v`$ on either side gives two cuts of $`J`$. The retained internal crossing count is at least $`\max(s,d-s)`$. Two split modules already give at least $`d`$; if only one is split, move it wholly to a side keeping the quotient cut nontrivial and charge newly crossing ports to that bound. When no modules split the cut already is a cut of $`K_d`$. Singletons are not split. Thus the inflated graph is $`d`$-edge-connected.

Finally suppose a cycle visits all inflated modules. It must cross each module boundary at least twice. Every crossing edge meets a singleton, and each singleton on the cycle accounts for exactly two crossing edges. There are as many singletons in the entire graph as there are modules, so the cycle visits all singletons and crosses every module exactly twice. Contracting its one internal path per module gives a simple spanning cycle of the underlying $`K`$: the alternation lists each module and singleton exactly once, and the base has more than two vertices. This contradicts the choice of $`K`$. In particular not even a single spanning Hamilton factor can be forced by taking high regular degree, arbitrary girth and maximal edge connectivity. By using equally large modules, one can additionally bound circumference by $`N-|V(J-v)|`$, where $`N`$ is the order of the inflated graph, since every cycle misses at least one entire module.

The obstruction differs from [the balanced quotient packing note](BALANCED-QUOTIENT-PACKING.md): there all modules are balanced and one can realize an exact positive packing; here one class is retained as singletons, which enforces tight visit counts that block every spanning cycle. No priority claim is made for vertex substitution or the seed graph.
