# Balanced quotient obstructions to spanning packings

This is a structural result for the generalized common-support cycle question studied by [Chakraborti–Janzer–Methuku–Montgomery](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.aim.2025.110228). It does not settle [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585).

**Theorem.** For every integer $`k\ge2`$ and girth threshold $`g`$, there is a simple bipartite $`2k`$-regular graph of edge connectivity $`2k`$ and girth at least $`g`$ in which the maximum number of edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles is exactly

```math
b+\lfloor 2a/3\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{2k+\lceil k/2\rceil}{3}\right\rfloor,
\qquad a=\lfloor k/2\rfloor,\quad b=\lceil k/2\rceil.
```

In particular, $`k`$ edge-disjoint cycles on a common support are impossible: they would saturate degree $`2k`$ on their support and therefore span. For $`k>2`$ this does not exclude a smaller pair on a proper support. Indeed, in the constructed examples the same-support packings are limited here only at the degree-saturating scale.

## General transfer and realization

Let $`Q`$ be a finite loopless directed multigraph with indegree and outdegree $`k`$ at every vertex. Assume its underlying undirected multigraph (retaining arc multiplicities) is $`2k`$-edge-connected. At each vertex of $`Q`$, start with a large simple balanced bipartite $`2k`$-regular graph $`J`$ of girth at least $`g`$ and with a $`k`$-Hamilton decomposition. Such graphs exist at all sufficiently large even orders by [the girth-raising module lemma](HAMILTON-MODULES.md).

Delete $`k`$ edges whose endpoint pairs are all at mutual distance at least $`g`$ in $`J`$; call the resulting graph a module. Each deleted edge makes one left and one right port. For each quotient arc, connect a left port of its tail module to a right port of its head, using each port once. This gives a simple balanced bipartite $`2k`$-regular graph $`G`$. A cycle not confined to a module must have an internal path between distinct ports. If its ports were ends of one deleted edge, their path has length at least $`g-1`$ by the original girth. If the ports came from different removed edges, their original separation gives at least $`g`$. Adding an external edge gives the girth bound.

For any cut, a split module with $`r`$ ports on one side has at least $`2k-\min(r,2k-r)`$ internal crossing edges. Restoring its deleted edges recovers $`J`$, whose corresponding cut crosses each Hamilton cycle at least twice, and at most $`\min(r,2k-r)`$ restored edges could cross. Two split modules already contribute $`2k`$. With only one split module, round it wholly to a side that keeps the quotient cut nontrivial. The $`\max(r,2k-r)`$ internal-edge lower bound pays for every new external crossing caused by moving ports. With no split module, directly use the quotient connectivity. This proves edge connectivity at least $`2k`$, and the degree gives equality.

Each Hamilton cycle in $`G`$ projects, by recording its external edges, to a connected spanning subset of quotient arcs with balanced indegree and outdegree. Balance follows by subtracting its degree sums on the equal left and right colour classes of a module; internal edges cancel. Edge-disjoint Hamilton cycles use disjoint quotient arcs, counted with multiplicity.

**Conversely, the best quotient packing can be realized by an appropriate choice of the ports.** Take any packing of $`h`$ pairwise arc-disjoint connected spanning balanced subgraphs of $`Q`$, and choose a directed Euler tour of each. At module $`v`$, tour $`j`$ visits $`v`$ a positive number $`m_{j,v}`$ of times. Assign its inside edges to one distinct Hamilton factor of $`J`$. Orient that factor cyclically and delete $`m_{j,v}`$ edges oriented left-to-right in it. The remaining paths go from right ports to left ports. Pair these paths bijectively with the incoming-to-outgoing transitions of that Euler tour at $`v`$. Since $`\sum_j m_{j,v}\le k`$, fill any unused ports by removing the remaining number of edges from a spare factor. Such a factor exists if any unmatched ports remain, because $`h=k`$ would force every $`m_{j,v}=1`$. Every required deletion can be chosen far from every earlier one: the left-to-right edges of each oriented Hamilton cycle form a spanning perfect matching, and each earlier deletion excludes a bounded number of edges in the current matching at a fixed distance threshold. Now glue the ports according to the quotient arc labels. For each tour, its associated paths span every module without repetition and concatenate into one Hamilton cycle. They are edge-disjoint for distinct tours. Thus the maximum quotient packing equals the maximum spanning Hamilton packing attainable by this module design. The converse is not asserted for a prescribed port assignment.

## A four-vertex quotient with an exact deficit

Use vertices $`0,1,2,3`$. Put $`a`$ arcs in both directions on $`0\!-\!1`$ and $`2\!-\!3`$, and $`b`$ copies of the four forward arcs of the directed cycle $`0\to2\to1\to3\to0`$. Its singleton cuts have size $`2k`$; two-vertex cuts have size $`4b`$ or $`4a+2b`$, both at least $`2k`$. Write $`A,B,C,D`$ for the four forward arcs in their cyclic order. They cross $`01|23`$.

In any connected spanning balanced arc set, the crossing count is even and at least two. If it is exactly two, the outward and inward arcs share one endpoint in one of the two groups. The other vertex in the shared-endpoint group needs both directions of the internal link to meet connectedness and balance. The other group has distinct external endpoints and needs at least one internal arc for balance. Therefore such a set consumes at least three internal arcs. In a packing of $`h`$ sets with $`j`$ two-crossing sets, capacities give $`4h-2j\le4b`$ and $`3j\le4a`$. This yields $`h\le b+\lfloor2a/3\rfloor`$.

For attainability, the four two-crossing patterns can use $`AB,BC,CD,DA`$, respectively. In each pattern use three of the four directed internal arc types, with the omitted arc respectively $`0\to1,\ 2\to3,\ 1\to0,\ 3\to2`$. Each resulting pattern is connected, spans, and has equal indegree and outdegree at all four vertices, as is checked from these lists. Put $`t=\lfloor2a/3\rfloor`$, use $`\lfloor t/2\rfloor`$ copies of each of the two opposite patterns $`AB,CD`$, and $`\lceil t/2\rceil`$ copies of each of $`BC,DA`$. This uses each cross-arc type exactly $`t`$ times and each internal arc type at most $`2t-\lfloor t/2\rfloor=\lceil3t/2\rceil\le a`$ times. Add $`b-t`$ sets consisting solely of the cross cycle. The quotient then packs $`2t+(b-t)=b+t`$ sets, as desired. The realization step turns this into equality for a suitable $`G`$.

The saturated-degree issue is related to [the four-edge interface example](FOUR-EDGE-CUTS.md). This construction also limits the number of Hamilton cycles to at most about five sixths of a decomposition, in every even degree and at arbitrary girth. The argument does not limit two cycles that avoid some modules or use a proper support inside them, so it does not contradict the candidate extremal bound for #585.
