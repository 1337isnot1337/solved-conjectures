# Four-edge cuts and compatible closures

Partial work on [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585), not a resolution.

## Exact criterion

Let a four-edge cut of a bipartite graph have two labels \(a,b\) whose endpoints on shore \(X\) are left vertices and two labels \(c,d\) whose endpoints on \(X\) are right vertices. On the other shore \(Y\) the left/right roles of these labels are reversed. A signature is either \(ac|bd\) or \(ad|bc\). Say a shore admits a signature if there are two edge-disjoint simple paths on exactly the same vertex set within that shore whose endpoints are the corresponding two pairs of ports, one pair per path. A port means the shore endpoint of a cut edge with the specified label. Even coincident ports are allowed in the definition; the two endpoints of each required path are always in opposite sides of the bipartition.

There is an edge-disjoint common-support pair of cycles meeting both shores **if and only if** their admitted signature sets intersect. In particular, any cut with at most three edges already confines such a pair to one shore.

To prove the criterion, each cycle in a pair meeting both shores must use a positive even number of cut edges. Since the cycles are edge-disjoint they use exactly two cut edges each, partitioning the four labels. On a shore each cycle gives a path between its two ports (possibly a single vertex before the bipartition argument). The difference between the numbers of left and right vertices of a bipartite path is \(1,0,-1\) as its endpoint colours are both left, opposite, or both right. The two paths have equal vertex sets, so these differences are the same. The partition \(ab|cd\) gives differences \(1\) and \(-1\) on \(X\), and is impossible. The two remaining partitions give exactly the allowed signatures. For the converse, choose the paths on each shore for a shared signature. Join matched paths with their two cut edges; this gives two simple cycles with the same vertex set and disjoint edges. There is no ambiguity about repeated vertices because each cycle uses just one simple path on each of the disjoint shores.

For a \(D\)-regular bipartite graph with \(D>4\), every four-edge cut has the prescribed port colours: subtract the sum of degrees of the right vertices of a shore from the sum for the left vertices. This is \(D(|L\cap X|-|R\cap X|)\), and it also equals the difference of the two counts of cut edges by shore endpoint colour, of absolute value at most four. Hence both differences are zero.

A signature can be checked by closing the two specified port pairs with artificial edges on a shore. Each path receives its own artificial edge. This is a labelled multigraph closure: a real edge between the same ports as an artificial edge must not be identified with it, and a two-edge cycle is allowed only in this auxiliary closure. Finding a pair in a closure that does not use the two artificial edges separately does **not** certify the signature.

## Unbounded family showing compatibility is necessary

For every integer \(g\ge4\), there exist two simple 4-regular bipartite 4-edge-connected graphs of girth at least \(g\) made from the same two labelled shores. Both shores admit exactly one signature; one gluing yields a pair of cycles and the other yields none.

Take a sufficiently large bipartite \(J\) of girth at least \(g\) decomposed into a red and a blue Hamilton cycle, using [HAMILTON-MODULES.md](HAMILTON-MODULES.md). Remove one red edge and one blue edge whose endpoint sets are at distance at least \(g\) from one another. Such a blue edge exists once \(J\) is large enough since it has bounded degree and its blue cycle spans. In this module the four ports are distinct, one of each bipartition colour for each Hamilton path. Any path inside the module between distinct ports has length at least \(g-1\): for ports from the same edge use girth and for ports from different edges use the chosen separation. Removing the edges leaves a spanning red path and a spanning blue path.

Make a shore from two copies \(U,V\) of the module. The two external ports of \(U\) are its red left port \(a\) and its blue right port \(c\); those of \(V\) are its blue left port \(b\) and its red right port \(d\). Use the four remaining ports for the two internal links: connect the red right port of \(U\) to the red left port of \(V\) and the blue left port of \(U\) to the blue right port of \(V\). This gives paths realizing \(ad|bc\). Any other admissible pair would have to use the same common support in both modules, since all four external ports occur as endpoints among the paths. Both paths must therefore cross the two-edge separator between \(U,V\). If either had both endpoints in one module it would require at least two crossings by itself, leaving none for the other. Hence each path crosses once. Of the two allowed signatures only \(ad|bc\) has both paths crossing.

Take a second copy of the shore with ports \(a',b',c',d'\) in the same bipartition colours. The compatible gluing uses \(ac',bd',ca',db'\), mapping \(ad|bc\) to \(b'c'|a'd'\). The incompatible gluing uses \(ac',bd',cb',da'\), mapping \(ad|bc\) to \(a'c'|b'd'\). It has no crossing pair by the criterion. It has no internal pair either: in a connected 4-regular graph the union of the two cycles saturates degree four on their support. That support is then a nonempty union of connected components, so has to be the whole graph.

Both gluings are simple, bipartite and 4-regular. Girth is at least \(g\): a cycle staying inside a module has this property; any other cycle contains at least one intra-module path between distinct ports and at least one external edge. Here distinctness follows because each port has only one edge out of the module. For edge connectivity, the four-module multigraph has degree four with at most two edges joining any pair of vertices; every cut of its four vertices has at least four edges. A split module in a cut of the full graph contributes at least \(4-\min(r,4-r)=\max(r,4-r)\) internal edges when \(r\) of its four ports lie on the first shore of that cut: restoring its two deleted edges gives \(J\), in which each nontrivial cut crosses both Hamilton cycles at least twice. At most \(\min(r,4-r)\) restored edges could cross. With two or more split modules the internal edges alone give four. With exactly one split module, assign it wholly to a side that makes a nontrivial module cut. At most \(\max(r,4-r)\) changed port positions can turn new crossing inter-module edges into noncrossing edges of the original cut, and the internal edges compensate. With no split module the cut already is a module cut. This proves the claimed connectivity.

## Consequence for the attempted regular reduction

Finding one two-path certificate on each side of a balanced four-edge cut is not enough to merge them, even at arbitrarily large girth and maximum edge connectivity in degree four. The signatures must match. The example is an exact-degree-four obstruction; for higher degree an internal pair need not use all vertices and the saturation argument does not apply. A way forward would be to track which boundary signatures survive after setting aside reserve edges of an extracted higher-degree factor, or bypass four-edge cuts entirely by selecting a strongly expanding subgraph. This note proves neither step.
