# High-girth Hamilton-decomposable modules

Partial tool for [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585), not a resolution. This supplies positive modules to go with the negative replacement in [HIGH-GIRTH-REPLACEMENT.md](HIGH-GIRTH-REPLACEMENT.md).

**Lemma.** For every fixed $`k\ge2`$ and integer $`g\ge4`$, every sufficiently large even order admits a simple bipartite graph of girth at least $`g`$ whose edges partition into $`k`$ Hamilton cycles.

**Proof.** Let the two sides have size $`m>2\sum_{j=0}^{g}(2k)^j`$. Start with $`k`$ labelled copies of any spanning bipartite cycle on these vertices. Treat parallel copies as distinct edges, so a pair of parallel edges is a cycle of length two. The degree is $`2k`$, counted with multiplicity. Maintain the decomposition into labelled Hamilton cycles.

Suppose the current shortest cycle has length $`\ell<g`$, and choose an edge $`xy`$ on one such cycle. Orient the Hamilton cycle of its label to traverse $`x\to y`$. In this Hamilton cycle the edges traversed in the opposite bipartition direction to $`xy`$ form a perfect matching. The union of the radius-$`g`$ balls about $`x,y`$ contains at most $`2\sum_{j=0}^{g}(2k)^j`$ vertices. Some edge $`u\to v`$ in that matching has both endpoints outside the union. Delete $`xy,uv`$ and insert $`xu,yv`$ with their old label. The insertion is bipartite. In the affected Hamilton cycle, deleting the two edges leaves paths from $`y`$ to $`u`$ and $`v`$ to $`x`$; the two new edges join these paths into one spanning cycle, reversing one path. Neither new edge had been present before.

No cycle of length at most $`\ell`$ is created. With just one new edge, its complementary path in the old graph joins the distant groups $`\{x,y\},\{u,v\}`$. With both new edges, the other two paths either also join the distant groups, or join $`x`$ to $`y`$ and $`u`$ to $`v`$. In the latter case each path has length at least $`\ell-1`$, since it could be closed by the corresponding deleted edge to make an old cycle. The new cycle then has length at least $`2\ell>\ell`$. These arguments also handle the initial multigraph, where an alternate path can have length one. At least one $`\ell`$-cycle has disappeared. Thus the vector of counts of cycles of lengths $`2,\ldots,g-1`$ decreases lexicographically. There are finitely many labelled multigraphs on the fixed vertex set with maximum degree $`2k`$. Termination yields the claim, and the elimination of 2-cycles guarantees simplicity. $`\square`$

**Two-way substitution.** In the vertex-replacement construction of [HIGH-GIRTH-REPLACEMENT.md](HIGH-GIRTH-REPLACEMENT.md), use the graph just constructed as $`J`$. It is $`2k`$-edge-connected because any nontrivial cut crosses each of its $`k`$ Hamilton cycles at least twice. If the base has a Hamilton decomposition, assign its incident edges at each base vertex to the ports of $`J-v`$ pairwise by colour; the paths in $`J-v`$ then join the base cycles into a decomposition upstairs. Conversely, a decomposition upstairs projects to one downstairs for every assignment, by saturation and the $`2k`$-edge module cuts. Accordingly a decomposition of the base is equivalent to the existence of an assignment yielding a decomposition upstairs, not to decomposability for every assignment.

**Prior work.** Existence in the bipartite case follows also by combining [Greenhill–Kim–Wormald's random bipartite decomposition theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jctb.2003.07.001) with [McKay–Wormald–Wysocka's short-cycle distribution](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v11i1r66): for fixed girth, the latter event has asymptotically positive probability. This note uses the elementary deterministic switch to make the needed module property explicit; no priority claim for the switching argument.
