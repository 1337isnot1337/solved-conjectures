# A common bipartite closure for two port pairings

Partial work on [Erdős #585](https://www.erdosproblems.com/585). This separates the abstract problem of pairing path endpoints from the problem of finding disjoint connectors with exactly the same internal vertex set in an arbitrary graph. The latter is not solved here.

**Port lemma.** Let $`L,R`$ be disjoint sets of $`m\ge2`$ ports each, and let $`P,Q`$ be any perfect matchings on $`L\cup R`$, possibly with same-side pairs. There is a perfect matching $`K`$ between $`L`$ and $`R`$ such that each of $`P\cup K`$ and $`Q\cup K`$ is one alternating cycle, counting edges with multiplicity, unless both $`P,Q`$ are cross matchings of opposite signs. In that case no such $`K`$ exists. In every feasible case there are at least $`(m-2)!`$ choices of $`K`$.

The case when both $`P,Q`$ are cross matchings is the full-cycle case of [Bertram's even-permutation factorization theorem](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165%2872%2990102-1). The proof below covers also the same-side case, where that permutation formulation does not apply.

## Proof by contraction

Given a proposed cross edge $`uv`$, with $`u\in L,v\in R`$, assume it is neither a pair of $`P`$ nor a pair of $`Q`$. Delete $`u,v`$ and join their former partners in each of $`P,Q`$ separately. Denote the resulting pairings by $`P',Q'`$. A successful connector matching on the remaining ports extends by $`uv`$: in each alternating cycle, expand the edge between the former partners to a three-edge path through $`u,v`$. This preserves connectedness and is reversible.

If $`P,Q`$ are both cross matchings and have equal sign, the shortened pairings are cross matchings of equal sign. To see the sign change for either, first swap its two right images to set the image of $`u`$ equal to $`v`$, then delete row $`u`$ and column $`v`$ of its permutation matrix. The sign multiplier depends only on the positions of $`u,v`$ in the fixed orders, not on the pairing. If the original signs differ, a common connector would make the two relative permutations $`m`$-cycles, which forces the signs equal, a contradiction.

Otherwise assume $`P`$ is not a cross matching. Its numbers of left-left and right-right pairs coincide and are positive. If there are at least two of each, contraction at any cross edge removes at most one old pair of either kind and $`P'`$ is still not cross. If there is exactly one pair of each, choose $`u`$ outside the left-left pair, possible when $`m\ge3`$. This leaves the old left-left pair untouched. In all cases for $`m\ge3`$ there are at least $`m-2`$ choices of $`v`$ that avoid the two forbidden partners of $`u`$, and every such choice preserves feasibility for induction. For $`m=2`$ there are exactly two cross matchings. A noncross pairing is compatible with both; a cross pairing is compatible with the other one. This proves existence in the base case. The induction also multiplies the lower bound on the number of choices by $`m-2`$, since the distinct edges out of $`u`$ give distinct $`K`$. This proves the lemma.

## Application to alternating switches without fixing a perfect matching

For a red and blue pair of 2-factors, cut the red and blue edges on any simple alternating circuit of length $`2m`$. In each colour its deleted edges form a matching, so its affected cycles split into paths with the same $`2m`$ ports. The endpoints of those paths define $`P,Q`$ in the lemma; their pairs may be on the same side depending on path parity. Reconnecting either colour using a proposed perfect matching at the ports gives exactly the connected components of the union of that pairing and the proposed matching, plus the old untouched cycles. This is an exact count, with no requirement that one half of either 2-factor stay fixed. On a theta trade using colours 1,2,3 with factor groups 12 and 34, only the legs of types 23 and 31 change factor membership; they form this kind of alternating circuit when their endpoint changes constitute a proper theta trade. The third leg changes the matching labels but not whether its edges belong to the red or blue factor.

There is a necessary limitation in applying the port lemma. The *same abstract $`K`$* cannot be supplied to both colours as the same set of actual edges. It would need to be realized by two edge-disjoint path systems with matching internal vertex sets. Such routers can be constructed when the ambient graph is ours to build: for an opposite-side terminal pair, take a bipartite quartic graph with two Hamilton factors, remove one edge of each factor with the two edges far apart, and attach each new terminal to the two exposed ports of the opposite side. This produces two edge-disjoint paths between the designated terminals, both spanning the router. Because its four old ports are either far apart or formerly joined by a removed edge, using the [high-girth Hamilton modules](HAMILTON-MODULES.md) makes the router arbitrarily high-girth. Disjoint routers, one per pair of $`K`$, then give both closures on exactly the same enlarged vertex set when all original components are touched. This does not provide the routers inside a given extremal host. It identifies a smaller task than finding two independently compatible closing orders: realize one order twice with disjoint edges and common support.
