# Multiple quotient switches

This extends the [quotient transfer note](QUOTIENT-CHORD-TRANSFER.md) for [Erdős #642](https://www.erdosproblems.com/642). Several supplied alternating cycles can sometimes be joined into one cycle retaining every vertex of all visited seed copies. Connectivity of their union alone does not suffice.

## Permutation test

Use a simple bipartite quotient $`K`$ with perfect matching colors assigned to seed vertices, and a seed $`H`$ with a spanning rim of length $`h`$ and $`k_H=e(H)-h`$ chords. For one **switch**, take a two-color alternating quotient cycle whose colors are assigned to two ends of a rim edge. Remove that rim edge in every copy visited by the switch, and add the corresponding inter-copy edges. In a family of switches require different color pairs to use vertex-disjoint seed rim edges, and permit switches on a common color pair only on disjoint quotient components. Thus all cuts at a copy have distinct endpoints and all modifications commute.

The result is a spanning 2-factor on all visited copies. Orient seed rims forward on one quotient shore and backward on the other. Each cut has a tail and head, and the inter-copy edge leaving a tail goes to a head of the same switch. On incidences $`(v,W)`$ of a quotient copy and a switch, let $`\pi`$ follow this directed order around each switch, and let $`\sigma`$ take the next cut along the oriented rim of a copy. Starting at a tail, the external edge moves to the head of incidence $`\pi(v,W)`$ and the intact rim segment to the tail at $`\sigma\pi(v,W)`$. Hence the number of circuits is exactly the number of cycles of $`\sigma\pi`$.

If this number is one, with $`S`$ the support of all visited copies and $`m=|S|`$, the resulting cycle has length $`hm`$ and $`mk_H+e(K[S])`$ chords. This follows from subtracting its length from the exact edge count on its full support. It meets the conjectured target precisely if $`e(K[S])\ge(h-k_H)m`$.

## Tree, order and parity criteria

For a connected incidence graph on copies and switches, write $`\beta=|I|-|S|-w+1`$ for its cycle rank, where $`I`$ is its incidence set and $`w`$ the number of switches. The two rotations give an orientable bipartite map in the standard [Walsh correspondence](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0095895675900428). Thickening the incidence vertices to disks and the edges to bands gives boundary walks counted by $`\sigma\pi`$; cap off each boundary circle. Euler's formula shows that the circuit count is $`1+\beta-2g_R`$, with $`g_R`$ the genus of this rotation system. In particular a tree always yields one circuit. Odd cycle rank forbids one, regardless of any port orientation choices. This is a rotation-system statement, not a claim that the maximum genus of the abstract incidence graph is achieved by allowable port choices.

Two switches meeting in $`r\ge1`$ copies have circuit count equal to the cycle count of $`qp`$, where $`p,q`$ are their two cyclic orders on the shared copies. Indeed, each private copy only lengthens the walk, and a shared copy exchanges the switches. The first return map to one of them follows $`p`$ and then $`q`$. For $`r=1`$ this gives a single circuit, for $`r=2`$ it gives two, and for $`r=3`$ it gives one if the orders agree and three if they are opposed. If assignment is free, exchanging the assignments of the two matching colors of one pair reverses its directed cycle and preserves all port multiplicities. Thus at three shared copies one can align the orders.

Even odd overlap does not always allow this correction. For $`r=5`$, take $`p=(0\,1\,2\,3\,4)`$ and $`q=(0\,1\,2\,4\,3)`$. Then $`qp=(0\,2)(1\,4)(3)`$ and $`q^{-1}p=(0)(1)(2\,4\,3)`$, so both choices give three circuits. Reversing $`p`$ cannot help either: the two other products are inverses up to conjugacy of the products already tested.

## One-or-three incremental merging

Suppose there is an ordered family of switches on distinct, freely swappable color pairs. Assume the rim edges of these pairs are vertex-disjoint. Suppose that each switch after the first shares exactly one or three copies with the **union** of the earlier switches. Then orientations can be chosen sequentially to give one full-support cycle at every step. There is no restriction on how many earlier switches are visited by a new one.

Inductively, the earlier full cycle still contains the unused rim edge assigned to the next switch in every shared copy. Cut it at those edges, and cut the rim in every new copy. When there is one shared copy the operation splices in the new switch in one circuit. When there are three, let $`p`$ be the current full cycle's cyclic order on these three cuts and $`q`$ their cyclic order along the new switch. The same two-circuit first return test applies: the earlier full cycle, after cutting, supplies its three directed segments between the cuts, and the new switch plus the rims of new copies supplies the other three. Thus the outcome has the cycle count of $`qp`$. Choose the new orientation so $`q=p`$, giving one cycle. Swapping the new matching colors does not modify any of the earlier routes, since this color pair is new. Continuing proves the claim. The density criterion on the final union is the same as in the permutation test.

## High-girth realizability of the pair examples

Place the shared quotient copies on one shore. Replace each cyclic interval between successive shared copies in either order by an even path of length at least the desired girth, with all interiors private. Use a separate pair of alternating colors for each of the two cycles. For the pair examples with at least two shared copies every cycle in this planted graph uses at least one entire private path. Complete the four matchings by the distant-edge augmentation in the [quotient transfer note](QUOTIENT-CHORD-TRANSFER.md), protecting all planted edges. In the no-distant-pair case the matching has more than twice the ball bound plus the number of protected edges if the shores were chosen sufficiently large. So there is an unprotected matching edge outside both balls, and the same switch grows the matching without deleting a planted edge. The planted cycles remain entire components of their two-color factors. This realizes both successful and unsuccessful orders in arbitrarily high-girth quotients, including the five-copy obstruction.

The permutation and pair formulas originated in a background research pass and were checked before inclusion. The incremental criterion and the five-copy orientation obstruction follow from the same return-map proof. None guarantees a switch family with the required edge density in an arbitrary quotient, and none resolves Erdős #642.
