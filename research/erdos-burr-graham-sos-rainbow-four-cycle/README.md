# The Burr–Erdős–Graham–Sós rainbow four-cycle conjecture

**Partial.** [The arbitrary-ambient result](ARBITRARY-GROUPS.md) rules out fixed-density edge sets with linearly many realized products, even in infinite noncommutative groups and with bounded splitting of the product colors. [Ordered-corner supersaturation](ORDERED-CORNERS.md) separately applies to arbitrary proper colorings. The unrestricted coloring problem remains open.

The exact target is [Erdős #810](https://www.erdosproblems.com/810), from [Burr–Erdős–Graham–Sós, pp. 273 and 277](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1989-10.pdf). In their notation they expect that for no fixed $\epsilon>0$ can $\chi_S(n,\epsilon n^2,C_4)\le n$ hold for all sufficiently large $n$. The following results are separate from both the odd-cycle question and the question about six balanced colors in complete graphs.

## Prior finite group-table theorem

[József Solymosi, Theorem 5 (2013 preprint, 2015 journal)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1309.0133#page=4) already proves that every fixed-density subset of a sufficiently large finite group table contains a rectangle with two equal opposite products. This is precisely the finite group-table case described in earlier checkpoints here, and it is **prior work**, not a repository discovery. The source is titled for the Brown–Erdős–Sós problem, but the theorem proves the actual repeated-product rectangle, not merely an unspecified four-edge configuration. Bounded splitting follows immediately by retaining one common split index.

The stronger setting below and in [the arbitrary-group note](ARBITRARY-GROUPS.md) does not require the ambient group to be finite or of size comparable to the vertex parts. Sparsity of the realized palette replaces ambient table density.

## Arbitrary ambient abelian groups with linearly many colors

Let $A,B$ be subsets of **any** abelian group, with sizes at most $n$. Regard the two sets as separate vertex parts. Let $E\subseteq A\times B$ have at least $\epsilon n^2$ edges and at most $Kn$ different edge sums $x+y$, for fixed $\epsilon,K>0$. Then for all sufficiently large $n$, $E$ contains a four-cycle whose opposite edges have equal sums.

**Retain a dense rectangle with small full sumset.** Apply the bipartite Szemerédi regularity lemma with a sufficiently small fixed parameter $\eta$ depending on $\epsilon$. There is a regular pair of parts of sizes $\Omega_\epsilon(n)$ and density $\Omega(\epsilon)$: otherwise the irregular pairs, exceptional vertices, and low-density regular pairs cannot account for the original edge density. In that pair retain the vertices on each side with neighbor sets of at least $(d-\eta)$ times the opposite part, where $d$ is the pair density; at most an $\eta$ fraction on each side is discarded. Call the resulting parts $A',B'$; they still induce $\Omega_\epsilon(n^2)$ original edges.

Each $x\in A',y\in B'$ is joined by $\Omega_\epsilon(n^2)$ three-edge walks through the original regular pair. Indeed, the two neighborhoods of $x$ and $y$ within the corresponding original clusters are large, and regularity supplies a positive density of edges between them. For a walk $x,b,a,y$ with edge sums $s_1,s_2,s_3$, its endpoint sum is $s_1-s_2+s_3$. Fix one endpoint pair for each realized value $x+y$ in **the full sumset**. For a fixed pair, its walks give distinct ordered triples of edge sums (recover $b$ from $x,s_1$ and $a$ from $b,s_2$). Distinct full-sumset values use disjoint triples. There are at most $(Kn)^3$ triples in total. Hence $|A'+B'|=O_{\epsilon,K}(n)$.

**Model all the retained vertices.** The [Plünnecke–Ruzsa inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3507), applied once with base $A'$ and once with base $B'$, gives $|2A'|,|2B'|=O_{\epsilon,K}(n)$. Thus $A'\cup B'$ has bounded doubling (all its two-fold sums lie in $2A'$, $A'+B'$, or $2B'$). The [Green–Ruzsa good-model theorem, Proposition 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0505198) gives a Freiman two-isomorphism of this entire set into a finite abelian group of size $O_{\epsilon,K}(n)$, preserving equality and nonequality of pairs' sums. The $\Omega_\epsilon(n^2)$ original edges remain dense in that model's square, and its group order grows without bound. Apply Solymosi’s finite group-table theorem and pull back the four cells and the opposite sum equality.

This also works if each sum is split into a bounded number of subcolors: retain one split index globally before starting, and use the matching index and equal base sums on the two opposite edges. In the original group-table result, merging product colors is also allowed since merging does not destroy equalities; in the arbitrary ambient case the stated linear bound is on the *base sums*.

## What remains

The original article gives the $(7,4)$ hypergraph construction as a lower bound in a different direction; it does not assert the existence of a group description for arbitrary putative dense rainbow-four-cycle colorings. There is no such assumption in the main question. The new scope under investigation is arbitrary ambient groups with a small restricted palette, not the finite-table theorem. In the priority check, Solymosi’s finite-table result was found by tracing the related $(7,4)$ problem; searches for the stronger arbitrary-ambient small-restricted-product statement and later citations did not find an exact earlier source. This is a bounded priority check, not external confirmation. Coordination: [issue #879](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/879).

## Further group classes and the exact matching reduction

The arbitrary-ambient conclusion applies to multiplicative labels in any group with an abelian subgroup of bounded index: restrict rows to one left coset and columns to one right coset preserving a constant-order fraction of the original edges, then cancel the two outer coset representatives. The index bound and not the subgroup's size is the fixed parameter here.

It also applies if the ambient group maps to an abelian group with bounded kernel, even without a bounded-index abelian subgroup. On each side independently retain one uniformly chosen representative of every occupied quotient fiber. Each edge survives with probability at least the reciprocal of the square of the kernel bound, so a dense bipartite restriction exists with injective quotient labels on both sides. Project product colors to sums in the quotient. Each projected color has at most the kernel bound many actual product colors, and so the bounded-split argument applies. The number of projected sums is at most the original palette bound. In particular this includes groups with bounded commutator subgroup.

For the unrestricted target, it is enough to consider proper bipartite edge colorings, while keeping any fixed positive density and the same linear palette bound. First cut the graph bipartitely retaining at least half the edges. If two same-colored edges at a vertex have other endpoints $u,v$, then $u,v$ have no other common neighbor, since that would close a nonrainbow four-cycle. Hence there are at most $\binom n2$ unordered clashing pairs of incident edges in total. In the auxiliary graph whose vertices are edges and whose adjacencies are these clashes, the number of vertices is $\Omega(n^2)$ and the average degree is bounded. A greedy independent set by the random-ordering argument (retain each vertex if it precedes all its neighbors) has size at least $\sum 1/(1+d)\gg n^2$. Its edges are properly colored and all four-cycles remain rainbow.

In a proper bipartite coloring each class is a matching; rainbow-four-cycle avoidance is now exactly the requirement that for any two edges of one matching, at least one of their two cross edges is missing. These are cross-free matchings, also called fooling sets. Requiring both cross edges to be absent would turn them into induced matchings and make the Ruzsa–Szemerédi theorem applicable, but it is a genuine strengthening and cannot simply be substituted. The linear tripartite hypergraph of vertex–vertex–color triples likewise need not be $(7,4)$-free: the four edges of an alternating two-colored path on five vertices have seven labels but need not close to a four-cycle. This isolates why the original one-way hypergraph construction is not an automatic equivalence.
