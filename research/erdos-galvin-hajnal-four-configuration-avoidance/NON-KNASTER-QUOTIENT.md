# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s four-configuration avoidance problem

## A non-Knaster specialization quotient between Cohen algebras

There is a complete embedding of the Cohen algebra for \(\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_1)\) into another copy of the same algebra whose generic quotient is ccc but **not Knaster**. The quotient specializes a designated witness to the [four-pattern problem](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1082). This strengthens the non-\(\sigma\)-linked conclusion for the [previous variant](COHEN-SPECIALIZATION.md) by making an extra finite change to the graph forcing. We do not claim the unmodified quotient is non-Knaster or that the forcing-algebra phenomenon is itself new.

### Cohort conditions

For every \(\xi<\omega_1\), fix a disjoint cohort \((a_\xi,b_\xi,z_\xi)=(\omega\cdot\xi,\omega\cdot\xi+1,\omega\cdot\xi+2)\). Use the same sealed graph conditions as in [the Cohen refinement](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/commit/39676805360f6a8f01ba4c4715aef6bfcb5e788f), with two extra rules:

1. The support contains either all or none of a cohort.
2. If \(r<a_\xi\), then \(r\) is not a graph neighbour of both \(z_\xi\) and one of \(a_\xi,b_\xi\).

An unmarked ordinal needs no additional rule. Free amalgamation of underlying graph conditions with agreement on their common support preserves the cohort restrictions, provided the supports each contain complete cohorts. Each cohort therefore lies in only one support or wholly in the intersection. In the former case any newly considered external vertex has no edges to it, and in the latter case any violation was already entirely in one condition. This combines with the old sealed-page checks. Dense extension by an isolated whole cohort is always available.

For ccc and for the chromaticity witness, use the positive proof from the Cohen refinement. In its isolated-mark step take \(x=\omega\cdot\xi\) from the club of nonzero limit multiples of \(\omega\), and add its entire cohort, with \(x\) the mark and \(b(x)=h\) the prescribed threshold; the other two vertices can be isolated as well. The two press-down steps are unchanged, and the root and petals again satisfy agreement and separation. The added cross triangle involves just one mark from each of three different petal cohorts. It cannot create a common lower neighbour of \(z_\xi\) and either of its two cohort mates: each new lower-neighbour incidence affects at most one vertex of a given cohort, and the other cross incidences to that cohort were absent. Thus the generic triple system still has the four exclusions and chromatic number \(\aleph_1\).

Cuts at \(\delta=\omega\cdot\eta\) never split a cohort. Initial restrictions are complete projections, by the sealed-page argument and the cohort amalgamation check. The countable successive quotients remain atomless: choose two unused points of the new interval with finite offsets at least three, set their thresholds to zero, and toggle their mutual edge. This shows that the modified \(P\) is again equivalent to \(\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_1)\).

### Quotient and failure of Knaster

As before, \(Q\) in \(V^P\) consists of finite proper partial colourings of the generic triple system. Dense conditions for \(P*Q\) colour the whole finite support and forbid monochromatic triangles in the condition. All ccc and projection checks from the previous variant go through with the additional cohort amalgamation check. The toggled edge has no triangle, so the same countable successor quotients are atomless with the extra colour data as well. Consequently \(P*Q\) is also equivalent to \(\operatorname{Add}(\omega,\omega_1)\), and \(P\) forces that \(Q\) is ccc.

Call a cohort eligible if its three thresholds are zero, its only internal graph edge is \(a_\xi b_\xi\), and this pair's immutable low-page flag is unmarked. Every condition can add such a cohort beyond any prescribed bound, so there are \(\aleph_1\) eligible cohorts in the generic extension. For each eligible \(\xi\), let \(f_\xi\) be the constant-zero condition on all three vertices of its cohort. It is proper because those vertices are not a triangle.

Suppose some \(p\) forces that an uncountable subfamily of these \(f_\xi\)'s is linked. Choose uncountably many distinct \(\xi\) and stronger \(p_\xi\) including the corresponding cohort and forcing that its \(f_\xi\) belongs to that subfamily. Thin to a delta system whose petals are disjoint, above the common root and increasing, whose root data agree, and whose three marked positions have the same root-neighbourhood profile across the conditions. Because the cohorts are pairwise disjoint, discard the finitely many touching the root. The cohort rule makes the root neighbourhood of the \(z\)-position disjoint from the union of those of the \(a,b\)-positions.

Choose two petals with \(\xi<\zeta\); freely amalgamate their conditions. Add just the cross edges \(a_\xi z_\zeta\) and \(b_\xi z_\zeta\). The only new triangle is \(a_\xi b_\xi z_\zeta\). Any other triangle involving a cross edge would need either another cross edge or a common root neighbour of the two endpoints; the only two cross edges give the intended triangle and the common root case is excluded by the uniform profiles. The old marked pair is unflagged and its new page is higher than both endpoints, while the two newly introduced pairs can record their low-page flags immediately. Both added edges respect thresholds. Finally, the lower endpoints are earlier than the entire \(\zeta\)-cohort and are not adjacent to its \(a,b\)-positions; so the new incidences at \(z_\zeta\) do not violate the cohort rule. At the earlier cohort the additions go upwards, which cannot trigger the lower-neighbour prohibition.

The resulting condition forces that \(f_\xi,f_\zeta\) lie in the proposed linked subfamily but their union colours a triple zero. They are incompatible, a contradiction. Thus \(Q\) specifically fails the Knaster property, despite both \(P\) and \(P*Q\) being Cohen. The final model still has another witness to the existence assertion; only the first-stage designated hypergraph is specialized.
