# Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal’s four-configuration avoidance problem

## Compatibility with a restricted forcing axiom

Relative to ZFC, the positive answer to Problem 9/C is consistent with \(\mathsf{MA}_{\omega_1}(\sigma\text{-}3\text{-linked})\), and with the continuum arbitrarily large. Here \(3\)-linked means that every subfamily of at most three conditions has a common lower bound. This does not assert consistency with the axiom for all ccc posets or even all \(\sigma\)-linked posets.

### Preservation and iteration

If \(H\) is a fixed 3-uniform hypergraph of uncountable chromatic number and \(P\) is \(\sigma\)-3-linked, \(P\) preserves uncountable chromaticity of \(H\). If \(p\) forced a countable proper colouring, choose for every vertex \(v\) a condition \(q_v\le p\) deciding a colour \(i_v\). Colour \(v\) in the ground model by the pair of \(i_v\) and the linked-class index of \(q_v\). A monochromatic triple would have three deciding conditions with a common strengthening, giving a forced monochromatic triple. The same argument works with a dense \(\sigma\)-3-linked presentation.

In a model with continuum at least \(|\delta|\), a finite-support iteration of length \(\delta\) of names for \(\sigma\)-3-linked posets has a dense \(\sigma\)-3-linked presentation. To see this, strengthen each finite-support condition until it has a decided linked-class index at every coordinate of its support, using its subcondition on earlier coordinates to force that decision. This process terminates: handle the largest undecided coordinate, and any newly introduced undecided coordinates are strictly below it. At each coordinate, compatibility follows by amalgamating up to three conditions whose recorded indices agree wherever supports meet. Induct along the finite union of supports, choosing a name for a lower bound from the linked class below the prefix already built. Thus the problem reduces to finding countably many total functions \(\delta\to\omega\) extending every finite index signature. Assign distinct binary reals to the coordinates of \(\delta\). For each finite length, use every finite table from the possible binary prefixes into a finite subset of \(\omega\). A sufficiently long prefix distinguishes any given finite support and one of the tables realizes its signature. The conditions with each fixed table are 3-linked.

### Build the model

Start in a GCH model and form a witness \(H\) on \(\omega_1\) by the [direct CH construction](CH-WITNESS.md). Choose any regular \(\mu>\omega_1\) with \(\mu^{\omega_1}=\mu\) in the ground model; under GCH this includes arbitrarily large successor cardinals. Add \(\mu\) Cohen reals first. This preserves the uncountable chromatic number of \(H\): a name for a countable colouring on \(\omega_1\) uses at most \(\omega_1\) Cohen coordinates by the ccc and nice names, and the corresponding subproduct is \(\sigma\)-centred in the ground model by the same finite-table argument. In the resulting model \(2^\omega=\mu\) and \(\mu^{\omega_1}=\mu\), by counting nice names (the Cohen poset has size \(\mu\) and each antichain is countable).

Now use the usual bookkeeping for \(\mathsf{MA}_{\omega_1}\), iterating only \(\sigma\)-3-linked posets of size at most \(\omega_1\) with finite support for \(\mu\) stages. This size restriction is sufficient: given any \(\omega_1\) dense sets in a \(\sigma\)-3-linked poset, close a subset of size at most \(\omega_1\) under chosen witnesses for each of those dense sets below each current point, and chosen common lower bounds for compatible triples and smaller subfamilies. After \(\omega\) steps the inherited order is still \(\sigma\)-3-linked, and its generic filter generates a filter in the original poset meeting the prescribed dense sets. For the iteration, names for the small posets with their linked decompositions and dense families use at most \(\omega_1\) coordinates; their supports are bounded in \(\mu\). At most \(\mu^{\omega_1}=\mu\) such names need bookkeeping, and the iteration has a dense subset of size at most \(\mu\).

By the iteration argument, the whole final iteration is \(\sigma\)-3-linked over the model just before it (which has continuum \(\mu\)), not merely at each successor stage. Hence it preserves \(\chi(H)=\aleph_1\). The finite exclusions are absolute for this fixed hypergraph. The final ccc forcing keeps the continuum \(\mu\) by the same name count, and forces the stated restricted axiom. No stronger linkedness preservation is used. In particular, the [ccc specialization quotients](COHEN-SPECIALIZATION.md) show why replacing the iteration hypothesis by bare ccc is not justified.
