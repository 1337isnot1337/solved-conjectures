# A monotone-path construction for two-configuration avoidance

[Problem 9/B of Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal (1975), p. 482](https://old.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=58) asks for an \(\aleph_1\)-chromatic triple system on \(\omega_1\) avoiding the loose triangle \(\mathcal T_1\) and \(\mathcal T_9=\{abx,aby,xyz\}\). The construction below proves that **CH implies a positive answer**. In ZFC it gives an exact-\(\aleph_1\) witness on continuum many vertices, not necessarily on \(\omega_1\). It is separate from the forcing proof of simultaneous four-pattern avoidance in [#1082](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1082).

## Path transfer

Let \(D\) be an acyclic orientation of a graph \(G\), with at most one directed path from any vertex to any other. Equivalently, \(G\) has no simple cycle formed by two internally disjoint directed paths with the same start and end. A simple undirected cycle made from at most three directed segments, each traversed forward or backward, is also impossible. The cyclic sequence of signs on the segments is not constant (acyclicity) and has an even number of changes. Thus there are exactly two changes, giving two directed paths between the same endpoints.

Declare the vertices of each directed path of length two to be a triple of \(P(D)\). Any proper vertex coloring of \(G\) is proper for \(P(D)\). Conversely, in a color class containing no directed two-edge path, there is no vertex with both an incoming and an outgoing edge within that color. The incoming-side versus outgoing-side split properly 2-colors that class. In particular the graph and the triple system have the same chromatic number whenever it is infinite.

- **No loose triangle.** For each of the three proposed path triples, connect its two distinct intersection vertices by the subpath between them. The three subpaths have disjoint interiors and give a simple cycle with three directed segments. This is impossible.
- **No \(\mathcal T_9\).** The two paths for \(abx\) and \(aby\) must induce the same directed subpath between \(a\) and \(b\), by uniqueness. Since \(x\ne y\), that common subpath is the single edge \(ab\), with the same orientation in both paths. The remaining vertices \(x,y\) connect by a simple path through the union of \(abx\) and \(aby\). This path has at most two directed segments: they are attached either to the same endpoint of \(ab\) (a fork) or to opposite endpoints (a directed path). The path for \(xyz\) contains a directed subpath connecting \(x,y\), whose possible interior point \(z\) is outside the first two triples. These subpaths form a simple cycle of at most three directed segments. Again impossible.

For completeness the same method works in every finite uniformity \(r\ge3\), taking directed paths of length \(r-1\). A color class with no such path has a proper coloring by the maximum length of a directed path ending at each vertex, in \(\{0,\ldots,r-2\}\). It still forbids a loose triangle. It also forbids two paths sharing at least two vertices and a third path meeting their union in exactly one exclusive vertex from each: the first two paths share a common subpath, and their exclusive points are joined in their union by at most two monotone segments. This gives the same forbidden cycle of at most three segments.

## The \(\omega_1\) application under CH

Take the special-cycle-free \(T\)-Hajnal–Máté graph of [Lambie-Hanson–Uhrik, Theorem 3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.01828#S3.Thmtheorem6) on \(T={}^{<\omega_1}\omega\). Orient each edge from the shorter sequence to its strict extension; the graph only joins comparable nodes. Directed paths have strictly increasing lengths. Two distinct directed paths with common endpoints would have a first divergence and next reconvergence, forming a special cycle, which the theorem excludes. The graph is uncountably chromatic, and its height map colors it properly with \(\aleph_1\) colors. Hence the transfer gives a triple system of chromatic number exactly \(\aleph_1\).

The vertex set has cardinality \(2^{\aleph_0}\): level \(\omega\) already has that size, and each of the \(\aleph_1\) levels has size at most \(2^{\aleph_0}\). Under CH this is \(\aleph_1\), so identify the vertex set with \(\omega_1\). In addition, their Theorem 3.5 directly gives the required special-cycle-free graph on \(\omega_1\) after adding one Cohen real, so the lift works in every such extension.

## Scope and literature

The original paper's Theorems 11.6 and 11.11 gave separate omissions and left the simultaneous question. Lambie-Hanson–Uhrik credit related special-cycle-free graphs to Komjáth, Hajnal–Komjáth and Soukup. The new inference here is the common directed-segment transfer and its consequence for simultaneous omission on \(\omega_1\) under CH. It does not prove an \(\omega_1\)-vertex witness in all models of ZFC, and cannot settle 9/C as written: four-edge directed paths lead to triples of the type \(\mathcal T_4\) forbidden there. The separate [infinite-pair core reduction](TWO-HEAVY-PAIR-CORE.md) concerns a possible negative model for 9/B, not a contradiction to CH.
