# Preserving the selected-path witness in forcing extensions

The following proof applies to any ground \(r\)-uniform hypergraph \(H\) with \(\chi(H)>\aleph_0\), fixed finite \(r\ge2\), and \(|V(H)|\le\mathfrak c\). In particular it applies to all the selected finite path systems on \(\omega_1\) used for [EGH 9/C](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1082).

## Arbitrary length finite-support iterations

Let \(P\) be a finite-support iteration of arbitrary ordinal length, whose iterands are forced to be \(\sigma\)-\(r\)-linked. Then \(P\) cannot add a countable proper colouring of \(H\). There is no restriction that the length of the iteration be at most the ground continuum.

Choose in advance names for countably indexed \(r\)-linked covers of the iterands. There is a dense set of conditions \(p\) for which \(p\upharpoonright\alpha\) decides a covering-class index of \(p(\alpha)\) at every \(\alpha\) of its finite support. To see this, make the decision at the greatest coordinate still undecided by strengthening below it. This keeps earlier decisions at higher coordinates valid, and any new support coordinates are strictly below the one just treated. The greatest unfinished coordinate strictly decreases at each step, so the process terminates.

Now suppose \(p\) forces that \(\dot f\) is a proper colouring by \(\omega\). Choose, for each vertex \(v\), a refined condition \(q_v\le p\) in that dense set deciding \(\dot f(v)\). The union \(S\) of these finite supports has cardinality at most \(\mathfrak c\), even if the entire iteration is much longer. Give the coordinates in \(S\) distinct binary reals. To each \(q_v\) assign the following countable data:
1. Its decided vertex colour.
2. A prefix length distinguishing all coordinates in its support.
3. The finite table from the chosen coordinate prefixes to the decided covering-class indices.

Any common actual coordinate has the same class index among conditions of the same data. For \(r\) such conditions, build a common extension on their finite union of supports in increasing order. At a coordinate \(\alpha\), the common restriction constructed so far is below all their restrictions, and forces all nontrivial entries there into the same \(r\)-linked class. Extend by a name for their common lower bound. This proves \(r\)-wise compatibility inside each data class. Since \(H\) is uncountably chromatic in the ground, some hyperedge lies in one class, giving a common condition forcing it monochromatic, a contradiction.

This is a local version of the standard finite-support preservation of \(\sigma\)-\(r\)-linkage at length at most the continuum: the only coordinates needing countable coding are the supports used to read one hypothetical colouring. See [*Distinguishing Martin's axiom from its restrictions*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001870824005486) for the global chain conditions.

## Arbitrary probability algebras

Forcing with **any** complete Boolean algebra carrying a strictly positive countably additive probability measure also preserves this chromaticity, regardless of its ambient Maharam type. For each \(v\) choose one positive Boolean condition \(q_v\le p\) deciding its colour. Form the complete subalgebra generated by all \(q_v\) and \(p\). It has Maharam type at most \(\mathfrak c\): these generators order-generate it by definition. The restricted measure remains strictly positive. By [Fremlin, *Measure Theory*, 524M(f)](https://www1.essex.ac.uk/maths/people/fremlin/chap52.ro.pdf#page=11), its positive part is \(\sigma\)-\(r\)-linked (the purely atomic case has at most countably many atoms). Colour the vertices by their decided colour and their covering class in that subalgebra. A monochromatic hyperedge has a nonzero common meet of its conditions, still nonzero in the full algebra, giving the same contradiction. In particular, any number of random reals preserve the one-Cohen four-pattern witness.

The host bound cannot simply be dropped. For any infinite \(\kappa>\mathfrak c\), consider the incompatibility graph of finite partial maps \(\kappa\to2\), a graph on \(\kappa\) vertices. If it had a countable proper colouring, the members of each class would have coherent union. Extend those countably many unions to total assignments. The cylinders assigning 0 at \(\alpha\) and 1 at \(\beta\) show that these assignments distinguish every two coordinates, embedding \(\kappa\) into \(2^\omega\), a contradiction. Conversely an injection into \(2^\omega\) gives a countable proper colouring by a prefix length and finite table of assigned values. Thus forcing with the product probability algebra adding \(\kappa\) random reals makes this graph countably colourable, since the generic random reals give such an injection. For fixed \(r>2\), take hyperedges to be \(r\)-sets containing an incompatible pair. Ground colour classes of size at least \(r\) would have to be linked, and the smaller classes can be refined into singletons. Hence these finite-uniform hypergraphs also witness failure beyond the ground continuum.

This note establishes preservation of existing witnesses. It does not claim that random forcing over an arbitrary ground creates a 9/C witness when none is known in that ground.

## Any infinite palette size

With \(\aleph_0\) replaced by an infinite cardinal \(\lambda\), the same statements apply to \(\lambda\)-colourings on a host of size at most \(2^\lambda\), using iterands covered by \(\lambda\) many \(r\)-linked sets. To code a finite support, use distinct binary sequences of length \(\lambda\) for its coordinates, and select a **finite set of bit positions** distinguishing its members. There are only \(\lambda\) possible choices of that finite set and of the finite class-index table. No assumption on \(2^{<\lambda}\) is needed. In the probability-algebra argument Fremlin's formula gives at most \(\lambda\) linked classes for the subalgebra of Maharam type at most \(2^\lambda\). The finite-map incompatibility example likewise gives sharpness: a covering family of \(\lambda\) coherent classes yields an injection into \(2^\lambda\), and such an injection yields the covering family.
