# The one-Cohen witness survives all subsequent sigma-linked forcing

**Theorem.** Let \(V\) be a ZFC ground model, let \(C\) add one Cohen real, and let \(\dot Q\) be any \(C\)-name for a \(\sigma\)-linked forcing. Then \(C*\dot Q\) forces that the **same** one-Cohen triple system on \(\omega_1\) from [PR #1082](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/1082) is uncountably chromatic. More generally, in the labelled construction on \(\omega_1\), the same conclusion holds at every finite uniformity simultaneously. No special interpretation of \(\dot Q\) as a ground-model poset is needed.

The earlier preservation by \(\sigma\)-3-linked forcing could put three preassigned vertex-colour decisions together at once. Here linkedness only combines two conditions. The proof instead grows a monochromatic prefix with a single joint colour decision, records the new class of that decision, and repeats stationary selection.

## Stationary reservoirs

Use the same regressive \(h:L\to\omega_1\) with stationary fibres, on the nonzero countable limit ordinals \(L\), as in the [one-Cohen construction](ONE-COHEN.md). Call \(S\subseteq L\) rich if for unboundedly many \(\xi<\omega_1\) the set \(S\cap h^{-1}(\xi)\) is stationary. A countable partition of a rich set has a rich cell: for every relevant \(\xi\), choose a cell stationary in its fibre, and one cell is chosen for unboundedly many fibres. Also for each rich \(S\) there is a club \(E\) such that every \(\beta\in S\cap E\) has \(h\)-cofinally many predecessors from \(S\). Take the club of limit points of the unbounded set of relevant \(\xi\)'s and diagonally intersect the clubs of limit points of their stationary fibre intersections. In particular the set of such good \(\beta\)'s is rich.

## Prepared monochromatic prefixes

Work in \(V\). Fix names for countably many linked classes covering \(\dot Q\). Suppose that a \(C*\dot Q\)-condition \((p,\dot s)\) forces \(\dot f:\omega_1\to\omega\) to be proper. For a vertex \(\beta\), a package of size \(k\) consists of:

- A ground tuple of \(k\) increasing vertices ending at \(\beta\).
- A Cohen condition \(p_\beta\le p\) forcing that tuple to be a selected labelled prefix (labels \(0,\ldots,k-2\), with correct backwards selectors), specifying the stage of its last edge when \(k\ge2\).
- A \(Q\)-condition name \(\dot s_\beta\) which \(p_\beta\) forces to extend \(\dot s\), to lie in a particular linked class, and to force all vertices of the tuple to have one particular colour.

A package's **code** consists only of \(p_\beta\), its last edge stage (or a dummy symbol), its linked class index, and its colour. The tuple and the \(Q\)-name themselves are not part of the code. There are countably many codes, since the Cohen poset is countable.

For \(k=1\), make a package at every \(\beta\in L\): strengthen in the two-step forcing to decide its colour and strengthen the Cohen coordinate to decide the chosen \(Q\)-name's class. Thus the packaged vertices form a rich set.

Given a rich set of size-\(k\) packages, thin to a rich cell of one code \((q,j,\ell,i)\), then intersect with the club of good vertices for this cell. For each surviving \(\beta\), take a fresh stage \(m\) beyond all coordinates of \(q\) and beyond \(j\) if relevant. Complete unspecified Cohen reads at \(\beta\) below \(m\) with neutral records. There is a bound below \(\beta\) on all earlier accepted predecessors, on \(h(\beta)\), and on the ladder value at \(m\). Choose \(\alpha<\beta\) of the same code with \(h(\alpha)\) above that bound. At stage \(m\) make \(\beta\) select \(\alpha\) with label \(k-1\), and selector \(j\) when \(k\ge2\). All former records forced by \(q\) are retained. This extends the selected prefix at \(\alpha\) to a selected prefix of \(k+1\) vertices ending at \(\beta\).

The new Cohen condition forces that \(\dot s_\alpha,\dot s_\beta\) lie in the same linked class \(\ell\). Choose a name for one common extension in \(\dot Q\); it forces colour \(i\) on the whole prefix at \(\alpha\) and on \(\beta\). Further strengthen the Cohen condition to decide the class of this common extension. That yields a size-\((k+1)\) package at every surviving \(\beta\), so the packages remain rich. At \(k=r\) a package forces a monochromatic \(r\)-edge, a contradiction. For the original triple construction the first recursion step selects a base and the second selects a top pointing to that base. Both forcings are ccc and the four exclusions are absolute for the fixed hypergraph.

## Local linkage is enough

The theorem still holds if \(C\) forces only that every family of at most \(\aleph_1\) conditions in \(Q\) can be partitioned into countably many sets linked **in \(Q\)**. Assume \(Q\) has a largest condition, adding one if necessary. At a given prefix length, choose all packages first without recording their class indices. Make their names total by taking the largest condition outside the corresponding Cohen condition. In the Cohen extension these names form a family of at most \(\aleph_1\) elements of \(Q\), so there is a Cohen name for a countable linked cover of that family. Strengthen each package's Cohen condition to decide its index in the cover. The rest of the rich-cell argument uses only this one cover. At the next prefix length the cover can be chosen afresh for the new family.

In particular this applies when \(Q\) is a finite-support iteration of \(\sigma\)-linked iterands of **any** ordinal length, computed after adding the Cohen real. Given a family of size \(\aleph_1\), strengthen its members to decide all the covering indices on their finite supports. Code just the union of those supports, of size at most \(\aleph_1\), by binary reals as in [the local finite-support lemma](LOCAL-LINKING-PRESERVATION.md). The linked classes of the strengthened conditions give linked classes of their originals. The length bound used below is thus unnecessary for witness preservation, though it makes the entire bookkeeping iteration globally \(\sigma\)-linked.

## Martin's axiom for sigma-linked posets

Consequently it is consistent with ZFC that 9/C has a positive answer and the full restricted axiom \(\mathrm{MA}(\sigma\text{-linked})\) holds, with arbitrarily large regular continuum. This strengthens the earlier \(\sigma\)-3-linked version.

For the usual construction, start from a GCH model and take a regular \(\mu>\omega_1\) with \(\mu^{<\mu}=\mu\). Add \(\mu\) Cohen reals, viewing one fixed coordinate as a single Cohen real over the model with the other coordinates. Then take a finite-support bookkeeping iteration of \(\sigma\)-linked posets of length \(\mu\) giving \(\mathrm{MA}(\sigma\text{-linked})\) and preserving continuum \(\mu\). One may keep the iterands of size below \(\mu\): for fewer than \(\mu\) dense sets first take a suborder of size below \(\mu\) meeting these sets densely and closed under a choice of joint extensions for same-class pairs, by countably many rounds of closure. The bookkeeping works by the regularity of \(\mu\), the support bounds for nice names and \(\mu^{<\mu}=\mu\). The iteration as a single forcing is \(\sigma\)-linked because its length is at most the continuum of that ground: use the finite-support decided-class and finite-prefix-table argument of [LOCAL-LINKING-PRESERVATION.md](LOCAL-LINKING-PRESERVATION.md) with \(r=2\) and all iteration coordinates. Combine this with the first theorem.

This is not a claim about full MA. In fact the finite proper-colouring poset of the one-Cohen witness has precaliber \(\aleph_1\), so the axiom restricted to that class would countably colour this particular witness. For the standard preservation of \(\sigma\)-linkedness at iteration length at most the continuum, see [*Distinguishing Martin's axiom from its restrictions*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0001870824005486).
