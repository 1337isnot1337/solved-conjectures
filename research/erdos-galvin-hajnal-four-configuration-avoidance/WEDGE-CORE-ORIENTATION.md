# Structure of the two-heavy-pair core

This continues the [reduction for EGH 9/B](TWO-HEAVY-PAIR-CORE.md). Let $`G`$ be the graph of infinite-codegree pairs of a loose-triangle-free triple system. It is triangle-free. Each triple in the remaining two-heavy-pair core is a wedge $`a-x-b`$ whose leaves $`a,b`$ have $`x`$ as their unique common $`G`$-neighbour. The next statements isolate parts of such a core that can be removed by a countable vertex colouring on $`\omega_1`$.

**Closure orientation.** Take a continuous chain of countable elementary submodels containing $`G`$ and the core. Give vertices distinct colour tags within each successive countable block. For any surviving constant-tag wedge there is one vertex in each of three distinct blocks. Its last vertex cannot be the centre: the two leaves would lie in an earlier model, which already contains their definable unique common neighbour. Thus on each tag class the last vertex of every wedge is a leaf. The model order need not coincide with the position of the centre among the first two vertices.

**One clique per centre.** For each centre $`x`$, let $`A_x\subseteq N_G(x)`$ be a set on which every two distinct vertices have $`x`$ as their only common $`G`$-neighbour. In particular $`A_x`$ can be any clique in the graph of selected wedges at $`x`$. The subhypergraph of wedges $`a-x-b`$ with $`a,b\in A_x`$ is countably chromatic. More generally the graph $`F`$ consisting of all pairs $`xy`$ with $`y\in A_x`$ is countably chromatic.

For $`x\ne y`$, the uniqueness property gives $`|A_x\cap N_G(y)|\le1`$. View each generating pair as pointing from its centre into its chosen set (both orientations may occur). For a common neighbour $`z`$ of $`x,y`$ in $`F`$, if $`x\to z`$ or $`y\to z`$, the preceding bound allows at most one such $`z`$ in each case. Otherwise $`z\to x`$ and $`z\to y`$; then $`x,y\in A_z`$, so their unique common $`G`$-neighbour is $`z`$, giving at most one more. Hence every pair has finitely many common neighbours in $`F`$. Close a chain of countable initial segments under finite common-neighbour sets. Outside such a closed segment, each vertex has at most one neighbour inside it. Colour the countable blocks injectively and at each vertex avoid the colour of that one earlier neighbour. This gives a proper countable $`F`$-colouring and hence the claimed wedge colouring. A fixed finite number of such clique layers can be removed by a finite product of palettes.

**Countably many clique layers at once.** More generally choose local sets $`A_{x,n}\subseteq N_G(x)`$, $`n<\omega`$, with $`|A_{x,n}\cap N_G(y)|\le\aleph_0`$ for all $`y\ne x`$. This holds in particular when $`A_{x,n}`$ is one of the cliques above. All wedges whose two leaves occur in one $`A_{x,n}`$ can be coloured **with one countable palette**, not a countable product of palettes.

Take the countable model chain containing the full assignment. If $`v\notin M`$ belongs to $`A_{x,n}`$ for some $`x\in M`$, then $`v`$ has no other $`G`$-neighbour $`y\in M`$. Otherwise $`v\in A_{x,n}\cap N_G(y)`$, a countable set belonging to $`M`$ and hence contained in $`M`$. Now colour successive countable blocks injectively. At $`v`$ additionally avoid the colour of the unique earlier-model centre $`x`$, if any, for which $`v`$ belongs to some $`A_{x,n}`$. A monochromatic wedge must have its last vertex as a leaf by the closure orientation. This leaf would have been assigned a colour different from its centre.

The same proof allows an arbitrary number of local sets when the one covering a wedge is recovered from its centre and its other leaf. For instance, take $`A_x(a)`$ to be the connected component of $`a`$ in the selected leaf-pair graph at $`x`$. If every such component meets $`N_G(y)`$ in at most countably many vertices for every $`y\ne x`$, the entire core is countably chromatic: when the last vertex is a leaf the other two vertices are in the earlier model, which defines the relevant component. A star shows that the component hypothesis is not automatic; the finite-transversal case below can cover such a star instead.

**Finite local transversal.** If at each centre $`x`$ the selected leaf-pair graph has a finite vertex cover $`A_x`$ (for example if it has no infinite matching), then the whole core is countably chromatic. Again consider the directed pairs $`x\to y`$ for $`y\in A_x`$. Partition vertices by the size $`n`$ of $`A_x`$. Within each class every finite induced subgraph of the undirected support has at most $`n`$ times as many edges as vertices, so it is $`(2n+1)`$-colourable by greedy degeneracy. By graph compactness the whole class has that colouring, and a countable sum of those palettes colours the support. Every wedge contains one of the support pairs.

**Centred forbidden copies have small inverse fibres.** In a loose-triangle-free system, any fixed leaf path $`a-b-c-d`$ has at most one centre $`v`$ whose link contains that path. If $`v,w`$ were two, the triples $`vab,vcd,wbc`$ would be a loose triangle. Any fixed three leaf vertices have at most two centres whose links contain all three pairs of leaves: three distinct centres let one use a different centre on each pair, again making a loose triangle. Consequently the same countable-model colouring by block tags removes copies of $`T_3`$ or $`T_4`$ whose centre is in a strictly later block than all leaves. It does not remove copies with a late leaf.

These arguments still leave the case of large, incompatible local leaf-pair graphs at uncountably many centres. They do not give a negative consistency proof or a ZFC answer for 9/B.
