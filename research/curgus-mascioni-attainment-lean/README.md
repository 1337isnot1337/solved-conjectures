# Ćurgus–Mascioni’s Fréchet root-matching extremizer question

**Lean progress on general attainment, not a complete verification.** This checkpoint works on the cluster-limit section of the [existing written proof](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/blob/main/results/curgus-mascioni-frechet/PROOF.md) for the exact question in [Ćurgus–Mascioni, Example 12.3](https://faculty.curgus.wwu.edu/Papers/RootsOperFinal.pdf#page=21). The separate nonuniqueness disproof is already [verified on main in #608](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/pull/608). No change to the catalogue status or mathematical priority.

- `CurgusMascioniCluster`: every sequence with finitely many labelled roots admits one subsequence along which every pairwise extended distance converges. The finite limiting distance relation is an equivalence relation. Choose a representative for each class; all recentered positions in all classes converge on a common subsequence in any proper normed group.
- `CurgusMascioniMatchingCut`: a permutation with no cross-class pairs is equivalent to a family of within-class permutations. When every cross-class pair has cost greater than a specified budget, feasibility for that budget is exactly feasibility in each class.
- `CurgusMascioniLocalPolynomial`: proves the exact translation/normalization factorization; the finite product converges coefficientwise once near roots converge and inverse distant differences vanish. Finite constant-coefficient differential operators preserve that convergence.

The remaining bridge is recovery of the translated output root multisets with multiplicity, followed by the optimization and lower-degree limit claims. One possible route is a fixed-degree reverse polynomial after translating away from a root of the limit. Its nonzero leading coefficient then has a nonzero limit, while disappearing degree corresponds to roots accumulating at zero in the reversed coordinates. This route is a proposal, not checked here; the written proof uses small disjoint discs and Rouché's theorem.
