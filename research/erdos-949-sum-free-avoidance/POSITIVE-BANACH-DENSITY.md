# Erdős’s sum-free avoidance conjecture

**Partial result.** [Erdős Problem 949](https://www.erdosproblems.com/949) asks whether every sum-free subset of the real line admits a continuum-sized set $A$ with $A\cup(A+A)$ disjoint from it. This note proves the conclusion for sets with positive discrete upper Banach density, including sets with positive density in a coset of a subgroup of continuum cardinality. Neither Lebesgue measurability nor any Baire-property assumption is needed. The unrestricted problem is not resolved.

## Difference-set theorem

Let $H$ be an infinite torsion-free abelian group, let $\mu$ be a translation-invariant finitely additive probability on all subsets of $H$, and suppose $\mu(T)=\delta>0$. For each positive integer $r$ there are $A\subset H$ of cardinality $|H|$ and $\epsilon>0$ such that
\[
\mu\big(T\cap(T+(j a+k b))\big)\ge \epsilon
\qquad(a,b\in A,\ -r\le j,k\le r).
\]
In particular, all listed combinations lie in $T-T$, even when $a=b$.

To apply this, if $S\subset\mathbb R$ is sum-free and $T=(S-c)\cap H$ has positive discrete upper Banach density in a subgroup $H$ of cardinality continuum, take an invariant mean witnessing positive measure of $T$. Then $T-T\subset S-S$, and $(S-S)\cap S=\varnothing$: if $s-t\in S$ for $s,t\in S$, then $(s-t)+t=s$ violates sum-freeness. Taking $r=1$ proves the desired assertion and also $A-A\subset\mathbb R\setminus S$.

### A finite intersection estimate

If $E_1,\dots,E_n$ all have measure at least $\eta>0$, finite additivity gives
\[
\sum_{i,j}\mu(E_i\cap E_j)
=\int \Big(\sum_i 1_{E_i}\Big)^2\,d\mu
\ge n^2\eta^2.
\]
The inequality follows by grouping into the finitely many atoms generated by the $E_i$ and applying Cauchy–Schwarz. If every distinct pair has intersection below $\eta^2/2$, the sum is at most $n+n(n-1)\eta^2/2$, impossible for sufficiently large $n$. Thus whenever positive-measure sets index an infinite vertex set $U$, the graph of pairs with intersection below $\eta^2/2$ has no countably infinite clique. The [Dushnik–Miller partition theorem](https://fa.ewi.tudelft.nl/~hart/set_theory/material/dushnik_miller-partially_ordered_sets.pdf), $\kappa\to(\kappa,\omega)^2$, gives an independent set of size $|U|$. Repetitions of the indexed sets do not affect the argument.

### Proof for all bounded coefficients

Choose $N$ so that every subset of $\{1,\dots,N\}$ of density at least $\delta/2$ contains a $(2r+1)$-term arithmetic progression, by [Szemerédi’s theorem](https://doi.org/10.4064/aa-27-1-199-245). Fix $v\in H$. The average of $\#\{i\in[1,N]:x+i v\in T\}$ against $\mu$ is $\delta N$. The set on which this count is at least $\delta N/2$ therefore has measure at least $\delta/2$.

That set is covered by the at most $N^2$ intersection sets associated with possible progressions of indices. One such set has measure at least $\eta=\delta/(2N^2)$. If the progression has step $t\in[1,N]$, translation to put its midpoint at index zero shows that
\[
F_{t v}:=\bigcap_{\ell=-r}^{r}(T+\ell\,t v)
\]
has measure at least $\eta$. Choose one such $t$ for each $v$. The map $v\mapsto t v$ is at most $N$-to-one because $H$ is torsion-free, so the resulting set $U$ of steps has size $|H|$.

Use the finite intersection estimate and Dushnik–Miller on $(F_u)_{u\in U}$ to get $A$ of size $|H|$ with $\mu(F_a\cap F_b)\ge\eta^2/2$ for distinct $a,b$. The intersection lies in both $T+j a$ and $T-k b$. Translating by $k b$ therefore witnesses the claimed overlap for $j a+k b$. If $a=b$, already $F_a$ witnesses the overlap, by containment in $T+j a$ and $T-k a$. We may take $\epsilon=\eta^2/2$.

### An elementary shortcut for the original coefficient range

The case $r=1$ needs no progression theorem. Write $K(u)=\mu(T\cap(T+u))$, an even function. Color a distinct pair $x,y$ bad if $K(x-y)$ or $K(2(x-y))$ is below $\delta^2/2$. A countably infinite bad clique would have, by the ordinary countable Ramsey theorem, an infinite clique whose pairs are bad at one fixed multiplier $d\in\{1,2\}$. The translates $T+d x$ indexed by that clique contradict the finite intersection estimate. Dushnik–Miller gives a full-size good set $B$. Translate $B$ so that it contains zero. Then every resulting $a$ satisfies $K(2a)\ge\delta^2/2$, including $a=0$, so $F_a=(T-a)\cap T\cap(T+a)$ need not be used: instead use $E_a=(T-a)\cap(T+a)$, of measure at least $\delta^2/2$. A second application of the intersection estimate and Dushnik–Miller gives a full-size $A$ whose distinct sums lie in $T-T$ by comparing $T+a$ with $T-b$. The first round handles $A$, $A-A$, and the diagonal sums. This shortcut establishes the original assertion, but the bounded-coefficient proof supplies the uniform overlap for every signed combination.

## Scope and priority

Discrete upper Banach density is the supremum of $\mu(T)$ over invariant means on the discrete group. The relative-density assumption allows non-Lebesgue-measurable sets. A concrete example is $H=\ker f$ for a surjective, discontinuous $\mathbb Q$-linear map $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb Q$ and $c$ with $f(c)=1$. The coset is sum-free, has positive relative density (one) and is not Lebesgue measurable: the countably many cosets cannot all be null, while a measurable positive-measure subgroup has an interval in its difference set and must be all of $\mathbb R$.

The [problem discussion](https://www.erdosproblems.com/forum/thread/949) and [problem report](https://www.erdosproblemaday.com/report/949) discuss partial cases, including the Sidon and Lebesgue-measurable cases. The assertion here is an explicit difference-set statement with uniform overlap, not a claim that those cases or the classical tools are new. The case of arbitrary sum-free sets of zero upper Banach density in every eligible coset is not addressed.
