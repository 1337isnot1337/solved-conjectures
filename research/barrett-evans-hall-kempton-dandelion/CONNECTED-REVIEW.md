# Independent mathematical and arithmetic review of the connected-complement extension

27 November 2026 UTC. The WadeDavis research session used separate proof-development and review agents. The review covers the exact minimum and equality family for all connected complementary pairs, strengthening the authors' diameter-three conjecture. The [theorem](CONNECTED-EXTENSION.md) and [diameter-two lemma](DIAMETER-TWO-GAP.md) state their respective hypotheses. This is a computer-assisted internal review. Lean formalization and external peer review are outstanding.

## Structural coverage and spectral bounds

The signed extremal row sum charges two full ranges for a direct edge and one for each common neighbor. For a competing pair the smaller connectivity is below one. Its extrema therefore have neither an edge nor a common neighbor. In the other color these vertices form a dominating edge. If this graph has diameter three, the two distance-three witnesses give the earlier source theorem. Otherwise its diameter is two and its extrema below eigenvalue two have exactly one common neighbor. The overlapping case forces the common neighbor to equal the opposite first-color extremum. The disjoint cases divide according to whether this common neighbor is one of the first-color extrema. Reversing either signed pair gives exactly the five templates. An exhaustive independent enumeration through order six checked all 172,212 eligible labeled choices against those templates.

The gap above one for the diameter-two graph passes two independent derivations. When the exclusive neighborhoods of the signed extrema have a crossing edge, its two endpoint row sums give the bound directly. If there is no such edge, all exclusive neighbors see the common neighbor. Its nonneighbor must have a two-step route to each extremum, providing two exclusive vertices with two common neighbors. Their row sums give the same lower bound on the surplus. Connectedness of the complement bounds both degrees by the order minus two. The result gives exactly the conservative lower eigenvalue used in [the finite checker](check_connected_dandelion.cpp).

Deleting impossible completions, using the two-leaf theorem, forcing a unique common neighbor, and aggregating exterior types under actual transposition symmetries retain all competitors. The forced bits of a sorted suffix belong to every type still admissible. The five full enumerations at each order also check their binomial counts independently. The explicit superlevel argument for the doubly adjacent form passes the independent algebraic review, including the strict cubic comparison at all ten orders. All five forms also run through the general engine. [Rational check](check_form_two_cut.py)

## Real coordinate enclosures

The final interval engine rounds lower divided bounds downward and upper divided bounds upward. The scaled Fiedler coordinates remain real. An inward-rounded exploratory implementation was withdrawn before the final run. Each contribution of an undecided edge is either zero or its signed row difference. The extremal budget subtracts mandatory spending only. Its correlated subset-sum bounds reserve the budget left after those forced contributions. The known-neighbor row update adds its eigenvalue residual and subtracts a conservative interval for all undecided incident contributions. Solving back for one neighbor uses independent interval sums for the others. The joint degree test uses the complement-degree identity and the Cauchy–Schwarz norm bound, and the energy test pays at least the smaller color gap squared on every undecided pair. A separate harness with undefined-behavior sanitization checked that the intervals enclosed all 69,750 actual Fiedler coordinates and 18,000 subset sums across 2,250 partial-board pairs, and retained their joint degrees. ## Complete replay

An independent reviewer compiled the checker with undefined-behavior sanitization and ran every one of the 50 order/template pairs. The per-pair outputs were byte-identical to the proof author's run. The 50 cases cover 6,692,599 attachment multiplicity vectors and 415,482 visited partial boards. Every run reported completion.
