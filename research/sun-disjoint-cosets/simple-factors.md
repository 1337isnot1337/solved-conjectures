# Lifting through nonabelian simple factors

The prime-power-gcd conclusion of the [composition-series proof](README.md) also holds for **every finite group whose nonabelian composition factors are isomorphic to $A_5$**. This includes arbitrarily many such factors and extensions with solvable groups. Menon proved that a tuple of disjoint cosets in $A_5$ can fail to have an integer residue-class realization, so reducing all such tuples to the cyclic case would lose examples. [Menon, Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.15873)

In a putative counterexample the incidence argument forces a prime $p$ common to all indices. Remove the $p$-parts from the indices and the remaining factors are pairwise coprime. At a composition-series step with factor $Q$, the indices $c_i$ of the live cosets in $Q$ likewise have pairwise coprime parts outside $p$. If there is a probability measure on $Q$ giving each live coset mass at least $1/(c_i)_p$, multiplying conditional lower bounds down the series gives $\sum_i 1/(n_i)_p\le1$. The proof of the prime-power-gcd case then finishes as before. The measure is allowed to vary with the node and the list of live cosets.

**One exceptional coset in any finite group.** Suppose all relative indices but one are powers of $p$ (including 1). Choose a Sylow $p$-subgroup $P$ of $Q$ containing a Sylow $p$-subgroup of the exceptional subgroup $H$. Choose a left coset $U$ of $P$ meeting the prescribed left coset of $H$. Uniform measure on $U$ gives the latter mass $|P\cap H|/|P|=1/([Q:H])_p$. For any subgroup $J$ of $p$-power index, its index and $[Q:P]$ are coprime. Thus every left coset of $J$ meets $U$, and its fraction there is $1/[Q:J]$ by the coprime index product formula. Without an exceptional coset, uniform measure on $Q$ suffices.

**No proper subgroup of $p$-power index.** Suppose that $Q$ has at most $p$ distinct prime divisors other than $p$, and has no proper subgroup of $p$-power index. At most $p$ live cosets can be proper, since each needs its own prime divisor outside $p$. All cosets of index coprime to $p$ have a simultaneous intersection. For each remaining proper coset choose one point lying both in it and in this intersection, which is possible by pairwise coprimality for this particular subfamily. Give the point mass $1/(c_i)_p\le1/p$ for that coset, adding masses if points coincide. The total is at most one. Distribute any leftover mass on the intersection of the cosets of index coprime to $p$ (or anywhere if there are none). This is the required measure.

**The factor $A_5$.** Its possible indices are $1,5,6,10,12,15,20,30,60$. For $p$ outside $\{2,3,5\}$ all local indices are pairwise coprime. For $p=2,3$ the preceding no-proper-subgroup criterion applies. For $p=5$ there are at most two cosets whose indices have a factor outside 5. If there are two, one of their indices must be 10 or 20 and the other must be 15. Every such pair meets a common left coset $U$ of a subgroup of order 5. Taking the uniform distribution on $U$ gives each of them mass at least $1/5$, and gives every prescribed index-5 coset exactly $1/5$. The one-exception criterion covers the other possibilities.

The [finite checker](alternating_five.py) establishes both assertions about $A_5$ used here. It generates the even permutations of five letters and enumerates all subgroups by adjoining one generator in every possible way, starting at the identity. Closure is exact in a finite group, and induction on a list of generators proves exhaustiveness. The checker forms each subgroup's left cosets; it checks all 7,500 pairs of indices 10 and 15 and all 15,000 pairs of indices 20 and 15 against the 72 cosets of subgroups of order 5. No disjointness assumption on the local pair is needed. The full conjecture still permits general gcd patterns even in a cyclic group.
