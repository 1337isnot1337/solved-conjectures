# Prior-art and proof audit

The target is exact Problem 8/D of [Erdős–Galvin–Hajnal (1975)](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=49), a positive arrow on $`\aleph_1`$ with a finite forbidden 3-graph rather than a complete hypergraph or an ordinal order type. Checked Diagram 5 against the scan. The neighboring Problem 8/C uses the Pasch configuration.

- **Original comparisons.** Their Corollary 4.2 forces the loose triangle and a four-triple configuration obtained by taking three pairs joined to the same apex and adding a transversal triple. Problem 8/D's configuration is a quotient of the latter, with one repeated pair; a subhypergraph embedding into the original configuration does not follow from being a quotient. Theorem 10.5 and Corollary 10.6 construct canonical triple systems under CH, with no free set and avoiding $`\mathcal T_3,\mathcal T_4`$; the printed statements do not assert avoidance of either Diagram 5 configuration. Theorem 10.13(A) discusses avoiding Steiner subsystems in a model with a Suslin tree. The Pasch system is a partial Steiner triple system, not a Steiner system on all pairs of its vertex set. These distinctions matter for statements of priority.
- **Rainbow model and related methods.** [Abraham–Cummings–Smyth, Theorem 2 and Remark 4](https://www.math.cmu.edu/users/jcumming/papers/polyx_revised_dec2006.pdf) states the countable rainbow partition and attributes relative consistency over ZFC to [Todorčević (1983)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0). The reduction from forbidden $`\mathcal T_6`$ to normal two-bounded pair colors uses a standard countable closure chain after establishing the countability of two-codegree intersections. The chain method itself is not new (see EGH Lemma 2.3). [Soukup](https://www.renyi.hu/~soukup/setop/otka61600/rainbow.pdf) treats related positive and negative pair-rainbow relations; failure of a general rainbow relation does not by itself supply a $`\mathcal T_6`$-free triple system.
- **Nearby uncountable hypergraph results.** Checked [Komjáth (2001)](https://zanotowane.pl/190/3900/), the abstract of [Hajnal–Komjáth (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-007-6231-2), the statement of [Komjáth’s uncountably chromatic triple system](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10474-008-7179-6), [Reiher (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.11223), and the abstracts of modern papers associated with Erdős Problems 593 and 1177. Most concern uncountably chromatic hosts, possibly larger than $`\aleph_1`$, or linear hosts. In the exact Problem 8/D every host has cardinality $`\aleph_1`$ and no equally large independent set; $`\mathcal T_6`$ itself is non-linear. Those results did not yield the positive consistency statement for this problem.
- **Other searches.** Searched by exact 8/C and 8/D problem numbers, the original paper's title, both author's names and hypergraph terminology, and Pasch/quadrilateral together with uncountability, forcing, partition relations, and triple systems. No previous resolution of exact 8/D or earlier statement of this simultaneous construction was located. This is not an exhaustive priority certification. The subsequent independent audit checked the complete 1983 AMS paper. Theorem 1(iii), p. 706, supplies an uncountable rainbow set; §1 Remark (4), p. 715, explicitly states the countable rainbow decomposition. [Archived original](https://web.archive.org/web/20250306142100/https://www.ams.org/journals/tran/1983-280-02/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0.pdf#page=13)

The positive direction needs the no-uncountable-independent-set condition twice: first to get countability of the two-codegree intersections, then to contradict the rainbow set. It does not assert that every $`\mathcal T_6`$-free triple system on $`\omega_1`$ is countably colorable. The negative construction uses ordinary ccc forcing: induced extension preserves earlier finite decisions, free amalgamation respects the common root, and three new marks share no pre-existing pair.

There is also a direct distinction for the linear-host literature. Every linear triple system on the first uncountable ordinal has an uncountable independent subset: at each countable stage of the recursion, the pairs among the previously chosen points have only countably many possible completions in total. Choose a fresh point outside those completions. More generally, the same argument works if every pair has countably many completions. Komjáth’s linear construction therefore does not furnish the host required by the two Diagram 5 questions. The original authors explicitly distinguish Pasch avoidance from omission of full Steiner triple systems in Definition 10.12 and Theorem 10.13(A).
