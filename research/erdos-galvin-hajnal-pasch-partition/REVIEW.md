## Mathematical review

I compared both edge lists to the scan of Definition 10.12, Diagram 5 and Problems 8/C–D. The Pasch system has four pairwise-intersecting triples with six distinct intersection points. The other configuration has one repeated pair. The questions use non-induced copies and require an independent set of cardinality aleph-one in a host of cardinality aleph-one. [Original diagram and questions, p. 473](https://users.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1975-24.pdf#page=49)

Each member of a thinned delta-system is an isomorphic finite ordered condition with its petal above the common root. Every pair of triples sharing vertices from distinct petals shares two old root points whenever it shares a pair. Root-fixing projection of a putative Pasch with two participating petals either produces an injective copy in one condition or two incompatible matchings in one lower link. A petal with only one participating Pasch triple is impossible since its three intersections would all belong to the root. In the repeated-pair pattern, both common-pair points are below both apices. This places the three specified triples in one condition. Transferring the last vertex from another petal cannot identify it with an old vertex, since such an identification would create a repeated pair with a vertex above the root as one of its endpoints in a triple with two root vertices. The resulting copy in one condition is forbidden. The same argument allows every finite subfamily of the thinned conditions to have a common extension.

For a name forced uncountable, there are uncountably many distinct ordinals that some stronger condition puts in the set. Add each ordinal to its support and thin again so the marks occupy one role in distinct petals. Adding the triple of three marks affects none of the old induced conditions and repeats no pair. A crossing Pasch would project three edges of a triangle to one lower matching. If the new triple occupies one of the repeated-pair positions it would repeat an old pair, which is impossible. In its two other possible positions the order of the common pair or a repeated non-bottom pair rules it out. Thus every name for an uncountable independent set has an extension giving a triple in that set. The generic union avoids both obstructions because a finite witness would already appear in one condition. The size and chain-condition proof gives preservation of CH over a CH ground model. For the labelled extension, labels on root triples stay in the root by their order bound. Placing a specified label in the root before thinning permits every prescribed label in the new triple. This proves the separate layer claim.

For the positive model, an uncountable common completion set of the two incident pairs would contain a triple, since the host has no uncountable independent set. Its three distinct elements give the required six distinct vertices of the repeated-pair pattern. Closure under the resulting countable sets on triples is preserved along the continuous chain of countable ordinals. On an uncountable transversal with at most one point per interval, any repeated lower-link vertex would put its apex strictly below its own interval. Coloring the two incident upper pairs of each triple alike is consequently well defined and two-bounded. Every rainbow subset is independent. Todorčević’s Theorem 1(iii), p. 706, provides the needed positive model. His §1 Remark (4), p. 715, gives the stronger countable rainbow covering principle in that model. [1983 primary source](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0) · [Archived original, p. 715](https://web.archive.org/web/20250306142100/https://www.ams.org/journals/tran/1983-280-02/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0/S0002-9947-1983-0716846-0.pdf#page=13)

This proves independence of the positive arrow in 8/D relative to the consistency of ZFC. For 8/C it proves the simultaneous consistency of the negative arrow and thereby refutes the positive arrow’s ZFC provability under the same consistency assumption. Whether the positive Pasch arrow can also hold remains open in [the separate follow-up](https://github.com/1337isnot1337/solved-conjectures/issues/1052).

## Independent finite check

The [projection checker](verify_amalgamation.py) assigns each of the six obstruction vertices to the root or to a petal and enumerates all consistent identifications of positions under projection, up to renaming petals and positions. It treats a free union and a union with the added triple of three equal-role marks. It discards an arrangement only if a triple would already cross petals, the projected template already contains a forbidden pattern, or the required precedence of a repeated bottom pair and the root creates a directed cycle. These alternatives account for all 38 surviving projection cases, including eight cases with the new marked triple. Seven produce a forbidden template and the other 31 violate the order. Run `python3 verify_amalgamation.py` from this directory. The finite enumeration checks the combinatorial cases; the chain condition, forcing density and relative consistency use the written argument.
