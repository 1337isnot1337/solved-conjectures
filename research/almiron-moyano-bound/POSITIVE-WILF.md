# Strictly positive Wilf-number counterexamples and a negative gap-threshold result

Let $S$ be a numerical semigroup, meaning an additive submonoid of the nonnegative integers with finite complement. Write $c(S)$ for its conductor, $\delta(S)=|S\cap[0,c(S))|$ for its number of elements below the conductor, and $e(S)$ for its minimal number of positive generators. Its Wilf function is $W_S(k)=k\delta(S)-c(S)$.

For a gap $g$ of $S$ write $\Delta_g=S\cup(g+S)$, the $S$-semimodule generated by $\{0,g\}$, and write $W(g)=2\delta(\Delta_g)-c(\Delta_g)$. The [Almirón–Moyano-Fernández preprint, latest v3, Conjecture 4.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.01358v3#S4.SS1) asks for $\min_g W(g)\ge-W_S(e(S))$. Their [Conjecture 1.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.01358v3#S1), with the same name, instead asks that at least one gap satisfy that bound. These are different formulations; the constructions below refute both. They do not refute Wilf's original conjecture. The unrestricted conjecture already fails by the source's own theorems (§1); the family in §2 excludes that zero-Wilf explanation.

## 1. A general upper bound

For every gap $g$ we have
$$
W(g)\le4\delta(S)-c(S).
\tag{1}
$$
This is the authors' Theorem 4.2; here is the short direct argument. Write $c=c(S)$ and $c'=c(\Delta_g)\le c$. Each copy $S$ and $g+S$ supplies at most $\delta(S)$ elements below $c$, because $g>0$ in the second case. Their union below $c$ contains all $c-c'$ integers in $[c',c)$ in addition to its $\delta(\Delta_g)$ elements below $c'$. Therefore $\delta(\Delta_g)\le2\delta(S)-(c-c')$, which gives (1), in fact with the extra subtraction of $c-c'$ on the right.

The source's own Theorem 3.8 and Theorem 4.2 already entail a direct obstruction on its equality family:
$$
S=\{0,m,2m,\ldots,(q-1)m\}\cup[qm,\infty),\qquad m\ge5,\quad q\ge1.
$$
This semigroup has $c=qm$, $\delta=q$, $e=m$, and ordinary Wilf number zero, whereas (1) is at most $(4-m)q<0$. This logical consequence of previously stated ingredients is not presented as a new theorem by itself. The next section gives a more robust family with strictly positive ordinary Wilf number.

## 2. Counterexamples with strictly positive ordinary Wilf number

For any integers
$$
m\ge5,\qquad q\ge3,\qquad q(m-4)>m+4,
$$
put $c=qm$ and define
$$
S_{m,q}=\{0,m,2m,\ldots,(q-1)m,c-2\}\cup[c,\infty).
\tag{2}
$$
It is closed under addition: multiples of $m$ add to multiples or into the full tail, and $c-2$ plus any positive element is at least $c$. Because $c-1$ is absent, $c$ is exactly its conductor. It has $\delta(S_{m,q})=q+1$.

Its complete list of positive minimal generators is
$$
m,\quad c-2,\quad c+j\quad(1\le j\le m-1,\ j\ne m-2).
\tag{3}
$$
The elements $m$ and $c-2$ are indecomposable; any smaller positive elements than $c-2$ are multiples of $m$. In a decomposition of an integer $c+j$ with $1\le j<m$, a summand at least $c$ is impossible because the other summand is at least $m$. Below $c$ two multiples of $m$ cannot suffice, one copy of $c-2$ plus a positive multiple of $m$ works exactly when $j=m-2$, and two copies of $c-2$ sum to $2c-4\ge c+m$. The number $c$ is itself a multiple of $m$, and every integer at least $c+m$ splits as $m$ plus a tail element. This proves (3) and $e(S_{m,q})=m$.

Consequently
$$
W_{S_{m,q}}(e(S_{m,q}))=m(q+1)-qm=m>0.
$$
Yet every gap obeys, by (1),
$$
W(g)\le4(q+1)-qm=4-q(m-4)<-m=-W_{S_{m,q}}(e(S_{m,q})).
$$
Thus even the existential formulation fails in every member of an infinite two-parameter family with positive ordinary Wilf number. For instance, $m=5,q=10$ gives $S=\langle5,48,51,52,54\rangle$: its ordinary Wilf number is $5$, while every gap has Wilf number at most $-6$. $\square$

## 3. Negative answer even at the minimum threshold in Question 4.8

The adjacent [Question 4.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.01358v3#S4.SS1) asks whether there is always a gap with $W(g)\ge0$ if $\mu_S\le4$, where $\mu_S=\min\{k\in\mathbb N:W_S(k)\ge0\}$. There need not be one even when $\mu_S=2$.

For any $m\ge5$ choose the symmetric numerical semigroup
$$
T_m=\{0\}\cup[m,2m-2]\cup[2m,\infty).
$$
It is additively closed since any two positive elements sum to at least $2m$. Here $c=2m$ and $\delta=m$, so $\mu_{T_m}=2$. Its gaps are $1,2,\ldots,m-1,2m-1$. For a gap $1\le g\le m-1$, the interval $[m,2m-2]$ was already present and $2m-1$ lies in $g+[m,2m-2]$. Thus $\Delta_g$ contains all integers from $m$ on and only $0,g$ below $m$. Regardless of the precise conductor when $g=m-1$, it follows that
$$
(c(\Delta_g),\delta(\Delta_g))=
\begin{cases}
(m,2),&1\le g\le m-2,\\
(m-1,1),&g=m-1.
\end{cases}
$$
For $g=2m-1$, the pair is $(m,1)$ because no new element lies below $m$, and the last hole is filled. Therefore the three Wilf values are respectively $4-m,3-m,2-m$, all strictly negative. In fact the largest gap Wilf number is $4-m$, which tends to minus infinity at the fixed threshold $\mu_S=2$. This is a separate question, not an additional named conjecture count.

## Scope and attribution

Both conjecture formulations above are in [*An extension of the Wilf conjecture to semimodules over a numerical semigroup*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.01358), with v3 dated June 2021. The general gap bound (1) is theirs; the family with strictly positive ordinary Wilf number (2) and the threshold-two counterexample strengthen the source-level obstruction or answer the adjacent question without depending on their inconsistent minimum/existence wording. A targeted search through the source's versions, related work by the authors, conjecture-specific queries, MathDB entry 348895, and the TruReTuring index did not locate an earlier explicit counterexample. This does not establish exclusive priority.
