# Exact Frobenius selection and finite Newton precision

Status: research. This note identifies two distinct missing operations in
the adaptive-prime attack: exact rational selection of a noncentral
Frobenius action, and conversion of a finite p-adic approximation into an
exact root map. The counterexamples are explicit. No root-count bound or
BSS separation is proved.

## 1. Exact Frobenius maps and their coefficient fields

### Proposition 1.1

Let f in Q[x] be squarefree of degree D, with root set Omega,
splitting field K, and Galois group G. For each g in G, there is
a unique interpolation polynomial Q_g in K[x] of degree <D
such that


```math
Q_g(\alpha)=g(\alpha)\quad(\alpha\in\Omega).
\tag{1.1}
```


For h in G, acting on coefficients,


```math
h(Q_g)=Q_{hgh^{-1}}.
\tag{1.2}
```


The field generated over Q by all coefficients of Q_g is
K^(C_G(g)), and therefore has degree


```math
[G:C_G(g)].
\tag{1.3}
```


In particular, Q_g has rational coefficients if and only if g
belongs to the center of G.

Proof. Lagrange interpolation gives existence and uniqueness in
(1.1). For alpha in Omega,


```math
h(Q_g)(\alpha)
   =h\bigl(Q_g(h^{-1}\alpha)\bigr)
   =hgh^{-1}(\alpha),
```


which proves (1.2). The action on Omega is faithful because the
roots generate K. Hence Q_g=Q_(g') if and only if g=g'. The
stabilizer of the complete coefficient vector is exactly C_G(g).
The Galois correspondence now gives (1.3). QED.

The elementary obstruction can also be seen without interpolation.
Any q in Q[x] satisfies q(h alpha)=h q(alpha), so its evaluation
map on the roots is G-equivariant. The permutation alpha->g(alpha)
has that property exactly when g is central.

For example, take f=x^3-2 and let g be complex conjugation. Its
Galois group is S_3; g fixes the real cube root alpha and exchanges
the other two roots. If a rational polynomial q represented this
permutation, q(alpha)=alpha would force the irreducible x^3-2 to
divide q-x. That would make q fix all three roots, a contradiction.
The selected fixed-point factor x-alpha is not rational either.

At an unramified good prime, choose a prime of K and let g be its
Frobenius. The decomposition group is cyclic, generated by g.
Equation (1.2) shows that the coefficients of Q_g are fixed by
this group, so their images under the chosen p-adic embedding
lie in Q_p. They need not lie in Q. For an S_D splitting group
and a D-cycle g, their global coefficient field has degree
(D-1)!, by (1.3).

The common-fixed-point lemma in [CIRCUIT_HISTORY.md](CIRCUIT_HISTORY.md), Section 11, supplies O(log D) elements
whose common fixed points are exactly the rational roots. It
does not produce rational polynomial maps for these elements.
One may make an intermediate fixed-point set G-invariant by
intersecting with all conjugate fixed-point sets, but no
polynomial-cost construction of those intersections, norms, or
quotient operations has been proved. The final rational-root
factor does lie in Q[x]; this does not make every intermediate
selection a rational operation of small size.

## 2. Newton lifting: exact cost, precision, and failure of finite equality

Assume in this section that f is monic and squarefree in Z[x],
of degree D>=2, and that p does not divide its discriminant.
These are actual hypotheses. An arbitrary repeated-root circuit
output has no prime where its reduction is squarefree, and a
short circuit for its radical has not been established.

Let A=Z_p[x]/(f). Since f modulo p is squarefree, A is a finite
etale Z_p-algebra. Start from q_0=x^p in A and define


```math
q_{t+1}=q_t-\frac{f(q_t)}{f'(q_t)}.
\tag{2.1}
```


The denominator is a unit: modulo p, raising roots to p-th
powers permutes the distinct roots, and f' does not vanish
there. The congruence f(x^p)=f(x)^p modulo p gives f(q_0)=0
modulo p. Taylor expansion in the algebra then proves


```math
f(q_t)\in p^{2^t}A,\qquad
 q_t-q_\infty\in p^{2^t}A.
\tag{2.2}
```


The unique limit q_infty is the lift of the Frobenius permutation
on the roots. In the notation of Section 1, its degree-<D
representative in Q_p[x] is Q_g for the selected local Frobenius.

### 2.1 The unreduced rational circuit is short

Suppose f has a length-s constant-free division-free program.
Simultaneous evaluation of f and f' costs at most 4s+O(1)
ordinary operations: differentiate each gate, using the product
rule at multiplication gates. Binary exponentiation computes
x^p with O(log p) multiplications. Each Newton step then adds
one division and a subtraction. Thus the unreduced rational
function q_t has a division-allowing program of length


```math
O(\log p+t(s+1)).
\tag{2.3}
```


This does not assert that reduction modulo f, inversion in the
quotient by a division-free circuit, or extraction of the
coefficient vector is comparably cheap.

### 2.2 Exact numerator and denominator growth

Write f(x)=sum_(i=0)^D a_i x^i, let H=||f||_1, and represent
q_t=A_t/B_t without cancellations, starting from A_0=x^p and
B_0=1. Homogeneous clearing of (2.1) gives


```math
A_{t+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{D}(i-1)a_i A_t^iB_t^{D-i},
\qquad
 B_{t+1}=\sum_{i=1}^{D}i a_i A_t^{i-1}B_t^{D-i+1}.
\tag{2.4}
```


The B_t are units in A. Moreover,


```math
\max(\deg A_t,\deg B_t)\le pD^t.
\tag{2.5}
```


If T_t=max(||A_t||_1,||B_t||_1), then T_0=1 and
T_(t+1)<=DH*T_t^D. Hence


```math
\log T_t\le\frac{D^t-1}{D-1}\log(DH).
\tag{2.6}
```


For an integer root r of f, the Frobenius limit fixes r. Since
B_t(r) is a p-adic unit, (2.2) implies


```math
p^{2^t}\mid A_t(r)-rB_t(r).
\tag{2.7}
```


But the elementary archimedean estimate only gives


```math
\log|A_t(r)-rB_t(r)|
 \le\log 2+\frac{D^t-1}{D-1}\log(DH)
       +(pD^t+1)\log\max(1,|r|)
\tag{2.8}
```


when that integer is nonzero. The precision in (2.7) grows as
2^t, while the supplied height estimate grows as D^t. For D>2
this comparison deteriorates with t. It cannot force the
integer in (2.7) to be zero. The next example shows that this
is a real obstruction, already for a quadratic, rather than
just slack in (2.8).

### 2.3 A constant-size example never becomes exact

Take


```math
f(x)=(x-1)(x-2),\qquad p=3.
```


The discriminant is 1, so this is squarefree with good reduction
at every prime. At the integer root r=2, the Newton sequence
starts at u_0=2^3=8. For a quadratic with roots 1 and 2,
Newton iteration squares the ratio (u-2)/(u-1). Thus for
K=2^t,


```math
u_t=\frac{2\cdot7^K-6^K}{7^K-6^K},\qquad
 u_t-2=\frac{6^K}{7^K-6^K}.
\tag{2.9}
```


The latter is nonzero for every finite t and has 3-adic
valuation exactly K. The numerator and denominator of u_t
are coprime; its denominator has logarithmic size asymptotic
to K log 7, larger than K log 3, the logarithm of the stated
precision. In particular, finite precision does not certify
the desired equality.

Both roots are rational, so the limiting Frobenius in A is
simply x. The class of q_t nevertheless differs from x for
every finite t. Since q_t(1)=1, its degree-1 representative is


```math
1+(u_t-1)(x-1),
```


and its difference from x is (u_t-2)(x-1), which is nonzero.
Even an explicitly available quotient remainder does not
repair the finite-stage equality in this example.

Multiplicity adds a separate obstruction. For
f=(x-2)^2 at p=3, Newton iteration at u_0=8 is
u_(t+1)=(u_t+2)/2. The error is 6/2^t, whose 3-adic
valuation stays equal to 1. It does not converge to the root.
Removing multiplicities is therefore a substantive extra
operation, not a harmless omitted convention.

### 2.4 What remains unresolved

For squarefree outputs, finite local computation can identify
root classes modulo each prime and Newton iteration can lift
them. The common-fixed-point lemma concerns consistent
algebraic root labels across primes. No operation with cost
polynomial in s has been supplied for making those labels
consistent, extracting the common fixed-root factor, or
turning the p-adic limits into exact rational circuit data.
The noncentral coefficient-field obstruction in Section 1
and the explicit nonzero finite-stage error in (2.9) explain
two distinct gaps. General outputs also require control of
multiplicity without assuming a cheap radical circuit.
