# Scope checks after the collision-energy test

Status: research. The recent resultant, prime-selection, and collision-
energy attacks have produced no new unrestricted upper bound on the
number of integer roots in terms of program length alone. The full tau
conjecture remains unresolved.

## 1. What unrestricted estimates actually remain established

The elementary coefficient argument already recorded in `ARITHMETIC.md`
gives N_Z(f)=O(2^s/s). The stronger previously recorded combination of
degree and real additive complexity in `GATE_COUNTS.md` gives


```math
N_{\mathbb Z}(f)\le N_{\mathbb R}(f)
 \le 2^{s-h_K(s)},\qquad
 h_K(s)=\frac{\sqrt{1+4Ks}-1}{2K},
```


for an absolute K. Its imported input is Risler, *Complexite et
geometrie reelle*, Bourbaki expose 637, Asterisque 133--134 (1986),
Proposition 3.1, with the preceding additive-complexity model:
<https://www.numdam.org/article/SB_1984-1985__27__89_0.pdf>.
The primary statement and model were inspected in the source audit.
If a gates are additions/subtractions and s-a are multiplications,
the two bounds are 2^(K a^2) and 2^(s-a); balancing them gives the
displayed formula. This is a routine consequence of an imported real
root theorem, not a new integer-specific asymptotic theorem.

The recent prime arguments have not improved that unrestricted
estimate. They establish exact identities, counterexamples to proposed
gate rules, and restricted theorems. In particular, the desired upper
bound for truncated collision energy is proved equivalent to tau in
[COLLISION_ENERGY.md](COLLISION_ENERGY.md), Section 4; it is not a proved intermediate
upper bound. The stronger attempted Rojas-based arithmetic estimates
remain explicitly conditional because of the audited proof gap.

## 2. A small missing prime would require a stronger factorial bound

For a nonzero integer C, let ell(C) be the least prime not dividing C.
Let tau'(n!) be the minimum constant-free complexity of a nonzero
integer multiple of n!. Suppose one proposed the uniform selection rule


```math
\ell(C)\le K(1+\tau(C))^A
 \quad\hbox{for every nonzero integer C},
\tag{2.1}
```


with absolute constants K,A>0. Every nonzero multiple C of n! has
ell(C)>n. Therefore (2.1) would imply


```math
\tau'(n!)\ge(n/K)^{1/A}-1.
\tag{2.2}
```


This is a fixed positive power lower bound in n, stronger than the
usual hypothesis excluding every polylogarithmic upper bound.

Conversely, such a positive power lower bound for tau'(n!) would give
a rule of the form (2.1). If ell(C)>n, every prime up to n divides C.
Let e be the least power of two with e>=n. For every p<=n,
v_p(n!)<n<=e, so n! divides C^e. Repeated squaring gives


```math
\tau'(n!)\le\tau(C)+\lceil\log_2 n\rceil.
\tag{2.3}
```


If tau'(n!)>=c n^epsilon for all sufficiently large n, apply (2.3)
at n=ell(C)-1. The logarithm is smaller than (c/2)n^epsilon for
large n, giving tau(C)>=(c/2)n^epsilon. This rearranges to (2.1),
after increasing its constants to cover the finite small cases.

Thus a polynomial bound for the value of a missing prime is not an
established replacement for the failed prime-support bounds. It is
equivalent, up to absolute constants and exponents, to this stronger
ultimate-factorial lower bound.

The primorial-to-factorial powering step is standard. Primary context:
K. Markstrom, *The straight line complexity of small factorials and
primorials*, Integers 15 (2015), A6, pp. 1--2,
<https://emis.de/ft/19793>. It is also Lemma 1 of the author's version
of Q. Cheng, *On the Ultimate Complexity of Factorials*,
<https://qcheng2023.github.io/paper/factorial.pdf>, dated 25 May 2004;
the journal version
is Theoretical Computer Science 326 (2004), 419--429,
DOI 10.1016/j.tcs.2004.06.020. The argument (2.3) is proved here and
does not import Cheng's conditional smooth-number result.

## 3. Difference-group rank does not presently give a polynomial bound

For a finite integer set R with N>=2, let Gamma be the subgroup of
Q* generated by -1 and all nonzero pairwise differences, and let r
be its multiplicative rank. Fix distinct a,b in R. For every
c in R\{a,b}, the pair


```math
\left(\frac{c-a}{b-a},\frac{b-c}{b-a}\right)
```


belongs to Gamma x Gamma, has nonzero coordinates, and sums to 1.
The pairs are distinct, and Gamma x Gamma has rank 2r. Thus
Beukers--Schlickewei, *The equation x+y=1 in finitely generated
groups*, Acta Arithmetica 78 (1996), Theorem 1.1, yields


```math
N\le 2+2^{16r+8}.
\tag{3.1}
```


The primary author PDF is
<https://webspace.science.uu.nl/~beuke106/s-units.pdf>.
A polynomial circuit bound on r, even if supplied, would therefore
give only an exponential root bound through this theorem.

The matrix route has the same quantitative limitation. Alon--Solymosi,
*Rank of matrices with entries from a multiplicative group*, Theorem 1,
its quantitative proof in Section 4.2, and Theorem 11 in Section 5.2,
apply the Subspace Theorem to the matrix ((a-b)^2)_(a,b in R), whose
rank is at most 3. Their resulting bound has log N=O(r+1) at this fixed
matrix rank, not N=poly(r). The inspected version is the 12-page
author PDF retrieved on 24 November 2026 at
<https://www.cs.tau.ac.il/~nogaa/PDFS/subspacem8.pdf>.
The preprint metadata are arXiv:2109.00618v1, 1 September 2021;
the journal metadata are
International Mathematics Research Notices 2023(14), 12383--12399,
DOI 10.1093/imrn/rnac183.

Requiring r=O(log s) is not a valid universal repair. For
R={1,...,N}, the difference group has rank exactly pi(N-1), since
its generators include every prime at most N-1 and every difference
factors over those primes. The polynomial product_(j=1)^N(x-j) has
a program with at most 3N-2 gates. The elementary fact that
pi(N)/log N is unbounded already rules out r=O(log s) for this
family. To recall why that fact needs no prime number theorem: if
pi(x)<=C log x for every sufficiently large x, the sum of 1/p over
primes would converge by dyadic grouping. Its divergence follows
from the finite Euler products dominating sum_(m<=x)1/m. Hence no
such C exists.

No circuit upper bound for a stronger rank-based quantity has been
proved here. Introducing another quantity with an unproved
tau-equivalent bound would not resolve the missing addition step.
