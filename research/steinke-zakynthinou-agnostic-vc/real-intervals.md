# Proper agnostic learning of real intervals with constant original CMI

Let $`\mathcal I`$ consist of the intervals of the real line, allowing open or closed endpoints, rays, the whole line, singletons and the empty set. There exists one proper learner for every $`n`$ whose original conditional mutual information is bounded by an absolute constant for every iid labeled distribution. Its expected empirical excess on every fixed sample is at most $`C/\sqrt n`$. The two endpoints are unknown and may interact. This proves a case of the [general VC conjecture](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/steinke20b/steinke20b.pdf). The interval result has no separate prize in that offer.

The gain of including an observation with label $`y`$, relative to always predicting zero, is $`2y-1`$. For $`w\in\mathbb R`$, write $`\mathcal F_w`$ for the empty set and all nonempty intervals whose closure contains $`w`$. We first learn an anchor $`w`$ for which the best gain in $`\mathcal F_w`$ is close to the best unrestricted gain. A fresh part of the sample then fits the two endpoints.

## Choosing the anchor on distinct ordered observations

Write the $`m\ge1`$ sorted observations in the cyclic list of length $`M=2m`$ that walks forward through them and then backward. The two occurrences of each observation are given distinct orientation labels. Rotate by a uniform number of positions. Start at the whole list. For each nonempty consecutive node, mark its middle position as pivot; its left and right children are the positions strictly before and after the pivot within the node. Only nonempty children are offered. The projection of a node is its set of distinct physical observations. It consists of consecutive original ranks, since the folded walk takes unit steps or repeats a rank at a turn.

Let $`G(N)`$ be the best subinterval gain in a node projection, including the empty interval. Its three possible action scores are $`G`$ of each nonempty child, and the best gain in the node projection for an interval containing the pivot observation, with zero always also allowed for this stop action. The maximum action score equals $`G(N)`$. Indeed a nonempty optimum avoiding the pivot lies strictly on one side of its physical rank. Each child projection on that side, if nonempty there, reaches to or immediately next to the pivot, by the unit-step walk. The parts on that side are nested, and one child covers the whole optimum. This argument applies at a turning point and the cyclic seam as well.

At depth $`j`$ sample an action with probability proportional to $`\exp(g/\sigma_j)`$ for its score $`g`$, where $`\sigma_j=256\sqrt m\,2^{-j/4}`$. Continue into the sampled child or return the pivot on a stop. The expected loss of gain from one choice is at most $`\sigma_j\log3`$, by the entropy formula for a finite exponential distribution. These losses telescope through the node optima to the final stop score. The expected best gain of an interval containing the returned observation (or empty) is therefore within $`O(\sqrt m)`$ of the full optimum on every distinct ordered sample.

### The information calculation

We give the changes needed for the [shifted-tree likelihood comparison, Part I §§3–6](../../results/steinke-zakynthinou-thresholds/PROOF.md). Augment the returned oriented observation $`a`$ by the shift minus its unrotated position modulo $`M`$. This augmented output identifies one abstract terminal pivot position, so both replacement samples follow the same sequence of abstract actions. Their probabilities for a common output are $`1/M`$ times the products of their own action probabilities on that path.

Form two samples of size $`m`$ from $`m-1`$ common observations and two different fresh observations, all keys distinct. In the sorted folded union, each sample list deletes two oriented entries. Starting at a common oriented anchor, two entries at the same forward offset differ by at most two positions of the union walk: the numbers of skipped entries are between zero and two. Thus corresponding physical union ranks differ by at most two. This controls in particular the minimum rank, maximum rank, and pivot rank of any aligned node or child being compared.

Every corresponding action score differs by at most six. To see this on the union order, take an optimal rank interval in one projection. Clamp its two endpoints to the other projection's minimum and maximum. If the action requires covering the pivot, extend the clamped interval just enough to include the other pivot. Each endpoint has moved at most two ranks: for an anchored interval its old left endpoint was at most the old pivot, and its right endpoint at least that pivot. At most four unit-gain terms change from moving the endpoints. Passing from one sample to the other changes at most two more unit-gain terms. The produced interval is feasible in the second projection (its endpoints can be interpreted on the union order), and zero gain is feasible on both sides. Reverse the comparison for the opposite inequality.

Call a node active if the distinguished observation occurs in its own node on either side of the comparison. If neither occurs, the ordered node lists agree: they are contiguous in the common circular order and have the same oriented anchor at the same relative position. Their projections, gains, pivots, and action scores are identical. The active depths form an initial segment since children are nested. It follows from the softmax formula that the log ratio $`\Lambda`$ of a common augmented output obeys
```math
 |\Lambda|\le12\sum_j I_j/\sigma_j,\qquad
 \Lambda^2\le\frac{C}{m}\sum_j2^{j/2}I_j,
```
where $`I_j`$ is the active-node indicator; the squared bound follows by summing a geometric series through the deepest active node. A one-action singleton contributes zero. Internal depths satisfy $`2^j\le2m`$, so the same geometric series gives $`|\Lambda|\le12(2m)^{1/4}/(256\sqrt m(1-2^{-1/4}))<1`$.

Under its own sample law, the expected mass of paths whose depth-$`j`$ node contains that sample's distinguished observation is at most $`\lceil2m/2^j\rceil/m=O(2^{-j})`$ for existing internal depths. Conditional on the unordered iid sample the distinguished observation is uniform, and the algorithm is permutation invariant. To charge activity from the other sample on common outputs, transport its mass at cost at most $`e`$ by the likelihood bound. The expected noncommon output mass in either law is at most $`1/m`$ because the selected pivot is one observation. For two common masses $`p,q`$, their JS contribution is at most $`(p+q)(\log(p/q))^2/8`$; unmatched masses cost at most a constant times their mass. Summing over outputs and then over depths gives expected neighboring JS at most
```math
 \frac{C'}m\left(1+\sum_{j\ge0}2^{-j/2}\right).
```
Tensorizing the independent selector bits, as in the linked proof, yields $`O(1)`$ original CMI. The calculation averages over the iid paired pool as required by the sponsor.

## Repeated real features

On any fixed input, independently tag each observation by a uniform real and sort lexicographically by (feature, tag). Return only the ordinary feature $`w`$ of the chosen anchor. In the information proof give both entries of every pool row independent tags before selecting: they are independent of the selector, the tagged pool is iid with almost surely distinct keys, and projecting the chosen key can only reduce the resulting information bound.

For the accuracy proof, within each group $`g`$ of equal features, write $`s=|g|`$ and subtract its average signed gain from each observation's signed gain. Let $`D_g`$ be the maximum absolute centered prefix sum in the random tag order. Then $`\mathbb E D_g^2\le32s`$. Indeed the centered bridge $`S_k`$ satisfies that $`S_k/(s-k)`$ is a martingale for $`k\lt s`$, by the mean of the remaining population. At $`h=\lfloor s/2\rfloor`$ the usual without-replacement variance formula gives $`\mathbb E S_h^2\le s`$, since the original population variance of the signs is at most one. Doob's squared maximal inequality up to $`h`$, and then the same argument in reverse order, give the stated bound (the singleton bridge vanishes). Hence $`\mathbb E\max_gD_g\le\sqrt{32m}`$.

A nonempty tagged interval through the returned observation may include a partial suffix of its first feature group and a partial prefix of its last one. Each partial gain differs from its fractional share of the full group gain by at most $`D_g`$. Keep the whole group or discard it according to which gain is greater. If both ends are in one group, its contiguous partial gain differs by at most $`2D_g`$ from its fractional share. The rounded trace has gain at least the original tagged gain minus $`2\max_gD_g`$. It belongs to $`\mathcal F_w`$ on this sample: an excluded extreme group at $`w`$ can use the open endpoint $`w`$, a retained group can include $`w`$, and the empty trace is allowed. With an interior anchor its whole group was retained. Every original interval trace is available in the tagged order. Combining the rounding estimate and the telescope proves the required $`O(\sqrt m)`$ expected anchor deficit on each fixed real sample.

## Fitting the endpoints and combining the samples

For fixed $`w`$, $`\mathcal F_w`$ is a union of three classes. Intervals containing $`w`$ make independent upper-ray and lower-ray choices, respectively, on the open left and right half-lines; the prediction at $`w`$ is one. The other classes extend only to the left or only to the right of $`w`$, with the endpoint $`w`$ open, and admit the empty set. On any finite sample, optimal traces on each half-line are attained by ordinary finite closed thresholds in the appropriate orientation (any needed empty or full trace is attainable). Their joined outputs are proper members of the indicated interval class, allowing the endpoint conventions stated above. The [disjoint-product theorem](disjoint-products.md) and the proper threshold result thus give uniform constant CMI and $`O(1/\sqrt m)`$ samplewise excess for each of these three classes. Points with fixed predictions contribute a common additive term to the empirical losses inside their class.

A finite union of these three learners incurs only constant additional information. Randomly split the sample into two parts of equal size, discarding one observation if its size is odd. On the first part choose the class with smallest possible empirical error. Run only that class's learner on the second part. For the CMI proof reveal the independent permutation used for splitting: the first part's index costs at most $`\log3`$, and, conditional on it, the second iid pool and its selectors retain their original law. For every fixed whole sample, the expected supremum of the absolute difference between either part's mean loss and the whole mean, over all of $`\mathcal I`$, is $`O(1/\sqrt m)`$. This is the [finite-population VC estimate](disjoint-products.md) with dimension two. Using it twice transfers both model selection and training error to the whole sample. Bounded sample sizes can use the empty interval and enlarge the absolute constant.

Finally apply an independent random split in the same way to the original input: learn $`w`$ on the first part and fit $`\mathcal F_w`$ on the second. The first part reveals only its constant-CMI anchor. The second-part bound is uniform in $`w`$ and its iid pool is independent of the first part. More explicitly, once the permutation is revealed the learner kernels factor into the anchor kernel on the first selected sample and the fixed-anchor kernel on the second. The information chain rule therefore bounds the total by the sum of their CMI bounds. For error, the same fixed-sample VC discrepancy holds simultaneously for every interval and hence for every adaptive $`\mathcal F_w`$. Transfer the first part's anchor deficit and the second part's fitting deficit to the whole input. This proves the two claimed inequalities for arbitrary noisy labels and arbitrary atoms.
