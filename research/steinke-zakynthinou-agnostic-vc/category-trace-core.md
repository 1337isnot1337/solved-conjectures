# Countable representatives for nonmeager trace cells

Let $`X`$ be standard Borel and let $`H`$ have a Polish topology generating its standard Borel structure. Assume evaluation $`H\times X\to\{0,1\}`$ is Borel and the class has finite VC dimension $`d`$. A trace cell is the set of all proper hypotheses with prescribed predictions on a finite feature tuple. There is a single countable set of proper hypotheses that meets every nonmeager trace cell. The list can also be chosen Borel in a parameter when evaluation is Borel in that parameter and the VC dimensions are uniformly bounded. We derive a one-feature reconstruction theorem by identifying the exceptions where this countable list may fail.

## Meager sections of a VC family

Let $`Y`$ be a nonempty Polish space and let $`B\subseteq Z\times Y`$ be Borel with $`Z`$ standard Borel. Suppose every section $`B_z`$ is meager and the family of sections has VC dimension less than $`m`$. Define the analytic relation

```math
T=\{(y_1,\ldots,y_m):\exists z\ \forall i\le m\ (z,y_i)\in B\}.
```

Then $`T`$ is meager in $`Y^m`$. Otherwise its Baire property gives a nonempty product of open sets $`V_1\times\cdots\times V_m`$ on which it is comeager. For each subset $`J\subseteq\{1,\ldots,m\}`$, the $`J`$-coordinate projection of this restriction of $`T`$ contains a comeager set, by Kuratowski–Ulam. Consequently, for comeager many values of $`(y_j)_{j\in J}`$ in the corresponding product, there is a section $`B_z`$ containing those values. Use Jankov–von Neumann to choose such a $`z`$ as a function of those coordinates, defined on the analytic domain where such a section exists. The selector is Baire measurable. For the empty $`J`$, choose one section, which exists since $`T`$ is nonempty.

With the $`J`$-coordinates fixed in that domain, the remaining coordinates avoid the chosen section on a comeager set, since every $`B_z\cap V_i`$ is meager in $`V_i`$. This avoidance event has the Baire property in the full product. Its definition uses the Baire measurable selector lifted along the open coordinate projection, and then the Borel membership test in $`B`$. Kuratowski–Ulam gives a comeager set of full tuples on which there is a section with precisely the pattern $`J`$. Intersect these finitely many comeager sets. Any tuple in their intersection is shattered, which contradicts the dimension bound. This proves the assertion. [Kuratowski–Ulam and analytic selection, Kechris, Theorems 8.41 and 18.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

If $`Y`$ has an isolated point, it avoids every meager section. Otherwise there is a countable list of distinct points such that each $`B_z`$ contains at most $`m-1`$ of them. Indeed, the lists in $`Y^{\mathbb N}`$ with all entries distinct and with every $`m`$-tuple at distinct indices outside $`T`$ form a comeager set. The projections to distinct indices are continuous and open, so the inverse images of the meager exceptional sets are meager. Thus the list meets the complement of any finite union of the sections.

For the parameterized form, let $`P`$ be standard Borel and let the starting relation be Borel on $`P\times Z\times Y`$, with the same hypotheses separately for each $`p\in P`$. The relation $`T\subseteq P\times Y^m`$ is analytic with meager fibers. It has a Borel superset with meager fibers. Apply the first reflection theorem to the property that every $`P`$-section is meager. This property is coanalytic on analytic families by closure of analytic sets under the nonmeager-section quantifier. Requiring each $`m`$-tuple at distinct indices to avoid the Borel superset now gives a Borel relation of acceptable lists with comeager sections in $`Y^{\mathbb N}`$, also requiring distinct entries. The category uniformization theorem gives a Borel list map. With an isolated point, use the same constant for all parameters. [Reflection and category uniformization, Kechris, Theorems 29.22, 35.10 and 18.6](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

## Application to hypothesis classes

The family on $`H`$ consisting of all failure sets $`\{h:h(x)\ne b\}`$, for $`(x,b)\in X\times\{0,1\}`$, has finite VC dimension bounded in terms of $`d`$. The unsigned family $`\{\{h:h(x)=1\}:x\in X\}`$ has VC dimension less than $`2^{d+1}`$. If it shattered that many hypotheses, index them by the binary vectors of length $`d+1`$, then choose one feature for each coordinate to recover its bit on the indexed hypotheses. The original class would shatter those $`d+1`$ features. Adding the complement of every set at most doubles the growth function, which still has a polynomial bound by Sauer's lemma. The signed family therefore has a finite VC bound depending only on $`d`$. The same bound holds on any restricted set of hypotheses.

For each nonempty basic open set $`U\subseteq H`$, retain the failure sections that are meager in $`U`$. This is a Borel restriction of the parameter $`(x,b)`$ by the category quantifier for Borel relations. Apply the meager-section result on $`Y=U`$. It supplies a countable list in $`U`$ that meets the complement of every finite union of the retained failures. If a finite trace cell is nonmeager, it is comeager in some nonempty basic open set $`U`$ by the Baire property. Each of its individual requirements then holds on a comeager subset of $`U`$. Thus the list for $`U`$ meets that trace cell. The union over the countable basis is the desired global set. The argument applies Borel uniformly in any parameter indexing the evaluation because the basis and the bound $`m`$ can be fixed. [Borel category quantifiers, Kechris, Theorem 16.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

## The exceptional relation

For each feature $`x`$ and bit $`b`$, let $`U_x^b`$ be the union of all basic open sets on which $`h(x)=b`$ holds comeagerly, and put

```math
M_x=\{h:h\notin U_x^{h(x)}\}.
```

The relation $`(x,h)\mapsto h\in M_x`$ is Borel by the same category tests on the countable basis. Each $`M_x`$ is meager. A Borel set differs from the union of the basic open sets where it is comeager by a meager set, and we apply this to each bit. If a trace cell contains a hypothesis outside the finitely many $`M_x`$ for its features, then the hypothesis belongs to the intersection of the open sets $`U_x^b`$ for its required bits. This common neighborhood is nonempty, and the finite requirements all hold comeagerly there. Hence the trace cell is met by the global countable list. Every trace missed by that list has its entire realizing set contained in the union of these exceptional sections.

In particular, suppose every $`M_x`$ is countable. Then Lusin–Novikov enumerates $`M_x`$ by countably many partial Borel maps of $`x`$. Extend outside each domain by a fixed proper name. For a feasible trace missed by the global list, choose any realizing hypothesis. It occurs in the exceptional list of at least one observed feature and so is recovered by one of the one-feature maps. This gives exact reconstruction with $`k=1`$ and a Borel learner. The [local reconstruction theorem](measurable-local-reconstruction.md) yields actual iid CMI at most $`197{,}120d+32`$ nats and expected empirical excess at most $`583\sqrt{d/n}`$ on every sample. A sufficient condition is that, for this topology on $`H`$, every set $`\{h:h(x)=1\}`$ have a countable topological boundary. Off that boundary the prediction is locally constant. The general proof still needs to deal with trace cells supported on the potentially uncountable sets $`M_x`$. [Lusin–Novikov, Kechris, Theorem 18.10](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

## Reconstruction with bounded intersections modulo countable differences

Fix a proper reference $`h_0`$ and work in disagreement coordinates $`R_x=\{h:h(x)\ne h_0(x)\}`$. Call two sections equivalent if their symmetric difference is countable. Suppose that, for some integer $`s\ge 1`$, the intersection of any $`s+1`$ pairwise inequivalent disagreement sections is countable. There is a countable universally measurable reconstruction using at most $`s+1`$ observed features. The information bound is $`197{,}120d+32(s+1)`$ nats with the same empirical rate.

For any condition specified by finite lists of section inclusions and exclusions, all elements of its realizing set can be listed measurably whenever the set is countable. To see this, code the spaces in Baire space and fix a Borel code for evaluation. Every point in a countable analytic section is hyperarithmetic in the constraint parameters and the code by the effective perfect set theorem. The measurable digit lists in the [countable-disagreement construction](countable-disagreement-reconstruction.md#constructing-the-lists) enumerate them. Their outputs outside $`H`$ may be replaced by $`h_0`$. This use of the lists requires no test deciding whether the section is countable. In particular, a trace with $`s+1`$ pairwise inequivalent positive sections is covered by the list for their intersection. The all-zero trace uses $`h_0`$.

For an uncountable realizing set specified by a finite constraint parameter $`p`$, we can instead choose a continuous injection $`e_p:2^{\mathbb N}\to H`$ into that set, universally measurably in $`p`$. Give the space of finite constraint parameters a compatible Polish topology and represent the joint realizing relation as the projection of a closed $`F\subseteq P\times H\times\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}`$. Any uncountable section contains a Cantor set. On that set take a Baire measurable witness for membership in $`F_p`$ by analytic selection. Its restriction to some dense $`G_\delta`$ set is continuous, so it is continuous on a smaller Cantor set. This yields a continuous $`\varphi:2^{\mathbb N}\to H\times\mathbb N^{\mathbb N}`$ with image in $`F_p`$ and injective projection to $`H`$. The continuous maps form a standard Borel coding space with the uniform topology. Membership of the image in $`F_p`$ is a Borel test on a fixed countable dense subset of the domain, by closedness. Injectivity of the projection is also Borel. For each pair of disjoint basic clopen cylinders, their compact projected images must have positive distance, a condition testable on fixed dense subsets of those cylinders. Analytic selection chooses the code $`\varphi`$ from $`p`$. Use its projection as $`e_p`$. [Perfect sets, Baire measurable maps and analytic selection, Kechris, Theorems 29.1, 8.38 and 18.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

Now use one anchor for each of the $`l\le s`$ equivalence classes of positive sections of the target trace. From the anchor tuple construct a countable tree. The root and descendants impose all the anchored positives, with a finite list of additional exclusions at each descendant. At any node with a countable realizing set, use the countable-section list. At every node admitting a Cantor injection $`e_p`$, include the Borel parameterized trace core on its Cantor domain, with $`e_p`$ passed as a parameter and $`e_p(u)(y)`$ as evaluation. The VC dimension is at most $`d`$ on that domain. To define the descendants, for each nonempty basic clopen cylinder $`U`$ in the Cantor domain, list the equivalence classes of extra sections with comeager preimage in $`U`$. Extra means inequivalent to each of the $`l`$ anchored positive sections. At most $`s-l`$ such classes occur on any $`U`$. Otherwise representatives of $`s-l+1`$ of those classes together with the anchored positives would have uncountable intersection, since their preimages have comeager intersection in the perfect open cylinder and $`e_p`$ is injective.

For each cylinder, list one feature for each of these finitely many extra classes. For any length up to $`s-l`$, existence of a list of that length is an analytic test on the current parameters. The category quantifier tests comeagerness, and inequivalence is certified by a Cantor injection into the symmetric difference, using the analytic test above. Select a list of maximum length using those tests and Jankov–von Neumann, keeping the length and $`e_p`$ as parameters to the selector. Every extra class with nonmeager preimage is therefore represented on some cylinder. Create a child by adding the exclusion of each listed section, taken over all cylinders. The countable-section lists, Cantor-domain cores and child choices compose universally measurably at each finite tree address. Empty realization sets and absent children can return $`h_0`$.

To verify coverage, fix a proper realization $`h_*`$ of the target trace. If a negative section $`R_y`$ is equivalent to an anchored positive $`R_x`$, our $`h_*`$ belongs to the countable difference $`R_x\setminus R_y`$. Include the digit lists for these differences from the anchors and the extra observed feature $`y`$. Otherwise all negative sections are extra. The positives omitted from the anchors hold on the Cantor domain outside countable sets, since each is equivalent to a section imposed everywhere on that domain.

Follow only nodes whose constraints retain $`h_*`$. A countable node already contains that name on its list. At an uncountable node, if all negative sections have meager preimage, then the required trace is comeager in the Cantor domain and its core supplies a representative. Otherwise some negative feature $`y`$ has nonmeager preimage. On some cylinder it has a selected equivalent section $`R_z`$. If $`h_*\in R_z`$, then $`h_*\in R_z\setminus R_y`$, which is countable. Include at each node the digit lists for these differences between a selected section and a section at one additional observed feature. Each such list still uses only the anchors and that one additional feature. If $`h_*\notin R_z`$, follow the child excluding $`R_z`$. This retains $`h_*`$. At any later Cantor domain on that branch, all sections equivalent to $`R_z`$ have countable, hence meager, preimage. The descent thus removes at least one new equivalence class of negative requirements from further consideration. After finitely many descents one of the lists succeeds. No intermediate step reads any observed feature outside the $`l`$ anchors, and the countable-difference terminal branches each use at most one extra. This proves the claim with at most $`s+1`$ observed rows.

The class of points in the real plane, predicting membership in each feature line, shows why allowing the topology to depend on a feature is useful. Its VC dimension is two. Two distinct intersecting lines realize all four patterns. Three lines either have empty intersection or meet at a common point, and in the latter case two of the lines cannot be positive while the third is negative. Add the constant-zero predictor as a reference, which preserves the dimension. In any Polish topology on the named hypotheses, if a line has only countably many exceptional points then some basic open set with more than one point makes that line comeager. There are at most countably many isolated points, and a line contains uncountably many names. Two distinct lines cannot both be comeager in the same open set with more than one point, since their intersection has at most one point. Thus only countably many lines can have countable exceptional sections in a single Polish topology. Their countable mutual intersections still permit the two-feature construction above.
