# Measurable local reconstruction gives both agnostic VC bounds

Let $`X`$ and $`H`$ be standard Borel and let evaluation $`H\times X\to\{0,1\}`$ be Borel. Suppose the class has VC dimension $`d\ge1`$. For a nonnegative integer $`k`$, suppose there are countably many universally measurable proper reconstruction maps from at most $`k`$ features. Require that **every realized trace** on every finite feature sample is realized by a hypothesis reconstructed from some subtuple of at most $`k`$ of its features. Maps with no features are fixed candidates. A reconstruction map's index can include the proposed labels on its subtuple and any countable side information. No compressed representation needs to be chosen in advance.

Then one distribution-independent proper learner has expected empirical excess $`O(\sqrt{d/n})`$ on every labeled sample, and whole-output selector information $`O(d+k)`$ on every deterministic paired supersample. It also has ordinary iid CMI $`O(d+k)`$ for every iid law, using completed measurability. If the reconstruction maps are Borel, the learner is a Borel Markov kernel. In particular this yields both conjectured rates for classes with such reconstructions using $`k=O(d)`$. This is a written extension of the deletion proof.

## Learner

For each ordered sample $`T`$, its available candidates are indexed by a reconstruction map and an increasing tuple of positions of $`T`$ of the appropriate length. Order these witnesses by map index first and then lexicographically by their positions. The reconstruction is a function of the selected feature values alone. Deleting a row removes exactly the witnesses using that row, and the renumbering preserves the relative order of the remaining witnesses.

For each feasible trace $`v`$ choose the first witness for $`v`$ and denote its output by $`g_T(v)`$. Use the [multiscale entropy learner](../../results/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/PROOF.md#1-the-learner-and-its-empirical-error) on the finite set of feasible traces. If the optimizer draws trace $`v`$, return $`g_T(v)`$. The empirical argument uses only traces and gives the same bound. For any prescribed trace its feasibility is a countable union of measurable witness tests. The first fitting witness is measurable on the feasibility event. These statements hold in the universal completion when the reconstruction maps are universally measurable. Joint evaluation of a reconstructed name at any of the finitely many features remains measurable there because the target product is standard Borel. Finite trace optimizers are measurable in their random weights on each fixed set of feasible traces. The ensuing output law is a completed-measurable Markov learner for every iid law and admits the usual whole-output conditional law. For $`n\le d`$, the selector entropy is at most $`d\log 2`$.

## Deletion bound

Use the notation of the [deletion proof](../../results/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/PROOF.md#3-removing-one-example). For a full $`n`$-row block and fixed random weights $`U`$, write $`q`$ for the trace optimizer, $`r_i`$ for the optimizer after zeroing loss weight $`i`$, and $`r'_i`$ for the optimizer after also removing row $`i`$ from all restrictions. All three live on full traces. The first two comparisons use only the finite trace set. Thus, with squared Hellinger distance defined without the factor of one half,

```math
\mathbb E_U\sum_i\left(H_2^2(q,r_i)+H_2^2(r_i,r'_i)\right)=O(d),
\qquad
\mathbb E_U\sum_i H_2^2(q,r'_i)=O(d).
```

Lifting each law by $`v\mapsto g_T(v)`$ preserves these distances because different full traces have different hypothesis names. Let $`\mu_i`$ be the total $`r'_i`$-mass of all full traces in two-element deletion fibers. The same weighted unit-distance argument proves
$`\sum_i\mu_i\le4d+4\sum_i H_2^2(q,r'_i)`$.

Project $`r'_i`$ to short traces and then lift by the short-sample representative $`g_{T-i}`$. Call the result $`p_i`$. A full trace outside a two-element fiber has a unique extension of its shortened trace. If its first witness avoids the deleted row, it is still the first for the shortened trace after deletion. In this case the name of the hypothesis and its mass in this comparison agree. Let $`E_i`$ be the full traces whose first witness uses row $`i`$. Consequently, the two distributions compared here agree on a subprobability law of mass at least $`1-\mu_i-r'_i(E_i)`$. The sum of their remaining masses bounds their squared Hellinger distance, so the comparison costs at most $`2\mu_i+2r'_i(E_i)`$.

Each selected witness uses at most $`k`$ rows, which gives $`\sum_i q(E_i)\le k`$ for every choice of random weights. The square-root triangle bound gives

```math
\sum_i r'_i(E_i)\le 2k+2\sum_i H_2^2(q,r'_i).
```

The projected law on short traces before lifting is still the optimizer for the short objective with the $`-\tau\log 2`$ bonus on two-element fibers. The [same objective comparison](../../results/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/PROOF.md#3-removing-one-example) bounds its squared Hellinger distance to the actual short trace optimizer by $`(\log 2)\mu_i`$. Lifting both by $`g_{T-i}`$ preserves that comparison. The four successive comparisons give an expected summed deletion cost $`O(d+k)`$ by the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz. The unchanged [fixed-pairing transfer](../../results/steinke-zakynthinou-agnostic-vc/PROOF.md#4-information-in-the-returned-hypothesis) gives the claimed information bound. Averaging over the iid pool gives the iid CMI bound.

## The circle orbit class

This applies with $`k=1`$ to the standard Borel irrational circle-orbit class where every globally ordered representative rule fails completed measurability. For each observed feature with circle coordinate $`x`$, include every candidate $`x+\gamma`$ for $`\gamma`$ in the countable subgroup. A trace with any positive row is realized by a member of one such list. Include fixed candidates from countably many distinct cosets to realize the all-zero trace by avoiding the finitely many observed cosets. These are Borel maps, and the underlying trace-covering construction is already verified in Lean. The deletion extension above proves both rates for that class in writing. [Trace-covering construction](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitWitnesses.lean) · [Obstruction for ordered representatives](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitOutputAllSizes.lean)

The general standard Borel case would follow from an $`O(d)`$-row family of universally measurable proper reconstructions as above. For VC dimension one, the jointly Borel hypothesis alone suffices to construct measurable one-feature lists. The proof uses laminar disagreement sections and Borel linearization to choose the lists. [Written VC-one proof](vc-one-trace-reconstruction.md)

## Lean verification and constants

The Lean version proves a fixed-pool and an iid bound of $`197{,}120d+32k`$ nats, with expected empirical excess $`583\sqrt{d/n}`$ on every sample. For each deleted sample it lifts the short trace to the full trace of the **actual short hypothesis**. The existing trace-optimizer deletion bound of $`4928d`$ applies to any family of such lifts. For a lifted trace, the full and short hypothesis names agree if the full witness avoids the deleted row. Comparing the names costs at most ten times the squared Hellinger distance on traces plus eight times the full law's mass on witnesses using that row. The summed witness mass is at most $`k`$. The transfer to selector information multiplies the resulting estimate by four. [Trace lift and named-output comparison](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/BoundedReadout.lean) · [Fixed-pool theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/BoundedReadoutRates.lean)

The formal reconstruction rule orders witnesses first by their reconstruction index, then colexicographically by their row positions. This order is preserved by the increasing embedding of surviving rows. For completed iid laws, Lean builds the actual conditional kernel, proves measurability of its finite-support KL statistic, and integrates the pointwise bound. The same rule gives a globally Borel learner when the reconstruction maps are Borel. The circle instance is fully checked with $`k=d=1`$ and information bound $`197{,}152`$ nats. [Reconstruction rule](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/LocalReconstruction.lean) · [Measurable iid theorem](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/LocalReconstructionMeasurable.lean) · [Circle instance](../../lean/Conjectures/AgnosticVC/OrbitIidRates.lean)

For another application, suppose a jointly Borel class on standard Borel spaces has a reference hypothesis $`h_0`$ such that for every feature $`x`$, at most countably many hypotheses disagree with $`h_0`$ at $`x`$. Lusin–Novikov supplies countably many partial Borel maps enumerating those hypotheses, with Borel domains. Extend each map outside its domain by returning $`h_0`$. Any trace differing from $`h_0`$ has a disagreeing feature, and its original hypothesis occurs in the list for that single feature. The trace of $`h_0`$ uses the fixed candidate. Thus this entire class has a Borel reconstruction rule with $`k=1`$. The Lusin–Novikov application is a written corollary. [Kechris, Theorem 18.10](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

There is also a written construction for jointly Borel classes on standard Borel spaces whose hypotheses each differ from a fixed reference on at most $`s`$ features. Work in the disagreement coordinates relative to the reference. Use the distinct positive features of the target trace as the anchor tuple, of length at most $`s`$. For each such tuple build a countable tree of candidates. The root selects any hypothesis containing the anchor positives. At each node, the branch history lists additional features that must be avoided. For each positive feature of the current candidate outside the anchor tuple, make a child by adding that feature to the avoidance list and selecting a new feasible candidate, if one exists. Each node's chosen hypothesis has finite support in the disagreement coordinates, so its children can be enumerated after Borel enumeration of that support by Lusin–Novikov. The selections can be made universally measurable by Jankov–von Neumann applied to the Borel finite constraints. Invalid branches may return the reference. Each fixed finite branch is universally measurable by composition, and the branch index provides countable side information. If a target trace is feasible, a current candidate that violates one of its negative requirements permits following a child excluding that feature, with the target hypothesis still feasible. The process reaches a matching candidate after at most as many steps as there are negative features in the sample. All branches use only the positive anchors, so $`k=s`$ suffices. This includes the completed-iid side of the two-point Borel obstruction. [Uniformization theorems](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4) · [Two-point obstruction](borel-traces-and-randomized-selection.md)

The finite support bound can be dropped when the disagreement set of each hypothesis is countable. At most $`d`$ positives suffice to recover the entire positive list among countably many measurable candidates. The same tree construction then enforces the negative requirements. This yields $`k=d`$ even for countably infinite disagreement sets. [Written countable-disagreement proof](countable-disagreement-reconstruction.md)

If the product closure of the prediction functions has finite Cantor–Bendixson rank $`r`$, analytic rank tests give countable proper lists approximating its top derivative points. Each additional observed feature can eliminate a unique top point and lower the rank in the chosen clopen cell. This gives $`k=r`$, with $`r`$ at most the Littlestone dimension when the latter is finite. [Written finite-rank proof](finite-rank-reconstruction.md)

In any fixed Polish topology on the hypothesis parameters, one global countable list covers every finite trace with a nonmeager realizing set. A missed trace is supported on a union of meager exceptional sections, one for each observed feature. When those sections are countable, Borel enumeration gives $`k=1`$ in arbitrary finite VC dimension. The countable lists for nonmeager cells can also be chosen Borel in a parameter. [Written category reduction](category-trace-core.md)

For a class closed under finite intersections, a shattered positive closure basis and a minimal list of negative witnesses give $`k\le2d+1`$ with Borel finite-conjunction output codes representing proper functions. A VC-three example shows that taking a conjunction closure, even after any fixed bit switch, can grow the dimension without bound. [Written intersection proof and obstruction](intersection-reconstruction.md)
