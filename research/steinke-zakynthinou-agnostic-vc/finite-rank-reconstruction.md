# Measurable reconstruction from finite Cantor–Bendixson rank

Let $`X,H`$ be standard Borel with jointly Borel evaluation and VC dimension $`d\ge 1`$. Let $`K`$ be the closure in the product space $`\{0,1\}^X`$ of the predictors represented by $`H`$. Distinct names may represent the same predictor. Suppose $`r`$ is the largest integer with nonempty Cantor–Bendixson derivative $`K^{(r)}`$. There are countably many universally measurable proper reconstruction maps using at most $`r`$ observed features that realize every feasible finite trace. The [local reconstruction theorem](measurable-local-reconstruction.md) gives one proper learner with actual iid CMI at most $`197{,}120d+32r`$ nats and expected empirical excess at most $`583\sqrt{d/n}`$ on every sample. Finite Littlestone dimension implies that $`r`$ exists and is at most that dimension.

## Measurable queries about compact trace cells

Let $`p`$ denote a finite Boolean condition on predictions at a finite tuple of features. The data for $`p`$ include those features and a finite truth table describing the condition. Write $`K_p`$ for the clopen subset of $`K`$ where $`p`$ holds. This is the closure of the proper predictors satisfying $`p`$. Localization to a clopen cell commutes with each finite derivative. These facts follow from clopenness and density.

For every $`s\le r`$, the relation on $`p`$ that $`K_p^{(s)}\ne\varnothing`$ is analytic. For $`s=0`$, this is the existence of a proper hypothesis satisfying $`p`$, a projection of a Borel relation. Given the test at $`s`$, the existence of at least $`m`$ distinct points of $`K_p^{(s)}`$ is analytic. It is witnessed by a finite tuple of features and $`m`$ distinct prediction patterns on that tuple, each with nonempty $`s`$-th derivative in its clopen subcell of $`K_p`$. Indeed, finite sets of distinct predictors can be separated by finitely many feature evaluations. Since $`K_p^{(s)}`$ is compact, its next derivative is nonempty exactly when it is infinite. Hence the test at $`s+1`$ is a countable intersection of analytic tests over $`m`$. This establishes the claim by induction. The same queries give the finite top rank of any nonempty $`K_p`$ and the number of points in its top derivative. That derivative is a compact discrete space and is therefore finite.

One can universally measurably split any nonempty $`K_p`$ by a finite feature tuple so that each resulting cell contains at most one top-rank point of $`K_p`$. Compute the top rank $`s`$ and top size $`m`$ by the preceding tests. With $`s,m`$ as extra parameters, use Jankov–von Neumann to choose a tuple with at least $`m`$ distinct cells having nonempty $`s`$-th derivative. All $`m`$ points are therefore separated. The tests and choices use parameters in finite unions or countable disjoint unions of finite products of standard Borel spaces. Their compositions with universally measurable choices remain universally measurable. [Analytic selection, Kechris, Theorem 18.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

## Countable proper lists at the top derivative

For every nonempty $`K_p`$, construct from its parameters a countable list of proper hypotheses satisfying $`p`$ which meets every clopen neighborhood in $`K_p`$ of every top-rank point. Use the split just described to reduce to a cell with one top point $`f`$ of rank $`s`$. It suffices to find a countable list inside this cell meeting every clopen neighborhood of $`f`$ there. For $`s=0`$ the cell is a singleton predictor. Analytic selection returns a proper name for it.

For $`s>0`$, construct pairwise disjoint clopen subcells $`V_j`$ of rank exactly $`s-1`$, all excluding $`f`$. After removing the previous finitely many subcells, the remaining clopen cell still contains $`f`$ and has $`f`$ as its only $`s`$-th derivative point. Thus its $`(s-1)`$-th derivative contains a point distinct from $`f`$. Choose a feature $`x`$ and a bit $`b`$ with the following two properties. Intersecting the remaining cell with prediction $`b`$ at $`x`$ has nonempty $`(s-1)`$-th derivative, and intersecting the original cell with prediction $`1-b`$ at $`x`$ has nonempty $`s`$-th derivative. These conditions are analytic in the clopen parameters and the proposed $`x,b`$. A feature separating $`f`$ from the other derivative point gives a witness, so this choice can be made universally measurably. Use the first intersection as $`V_j`$. It excludes $`f`$, giving the asserted rank.

Apply the rank-$`s-1`$ construction in all the $`V_j`$. Any clopen neighborhood of $`f`$ in the original cell has complement of rank at most $`s-1`$. The $`(s-1)`$-th derivative of that compact complement is finite. Hence the neighborhood contains a top point from some $`V_j`$, since these cells are disjoint and each has at least one such point. Its corresponding lower-rank list meets the neighborhood. The union of those countably many proper lists proves the claim. Each representative at a fixed list index is universally measurable in the original condition by recursion in the finite rank and composition of the analytic selections.

## Covering a trace with observed features

Given a feasible trace, keep a proper realization $`h_*`$ for the coverage argument. Begin with the whole $`K`$. At any current nonempty clopen cell containing $`h_*`$, perform the finite split. Follow the subcell containing $`h_*`$. If it has lower rank than the current cell, recurse without reading another observed feature. Otherwise the subcell has a unique top-rank point $`f`$. If $`f`$ realizes the trace, that trace defines a clopen neighborhood of $`f`$ in the subcell and one of the countable list entries realizes it properly. Otherwise, choose one observed feature at which $`f`$ differs from the required trace, and intersect the subcell with that required prediction. This leaves $`h_*`$ in the cell and excludes $`f`$, so the rank drops strictly. Every such step reads one observed feature, and at most $`r`$ steps are possible. Rank zero always terminates in a proper representative after the split.

To define the reconstruction maps, enumerate the finite paths of split-cell choices, choices of when to use an observed feature and of its required bit, and the final list index. Along a fixed path, the split features and the approximating lists are selected by the measurable rules above from the previous conditions. The only observed features used are the explicit at most $`r`$ anchors. Invalid paths may return a fixed proper hypothesis. If a path reads the anchors in a different order from their occurrence in the sample, include that finite permutation in the path index. This is a countable family of universally measurable proper maps, and the trace argument identifies a successful index on each feasible sample.

To compare with Littlestone dimension, if $`K^{(s)}`$ is nonempty for $`s>0`$, then $`K^{(s-1)}`$ has two distinct points. Choose a feature separating them. Each clopen branch has nonempty $`(s-1)`$-th derivative. Induction constructs a full binary Littlestone tree of depth $`s`$, where the feature at each internal node may depend on the path to it. Every finite path realized in $`K`$ is realized by a proper hypothesis because it defines a nonempty clopen cell. Thus $`\mathop{\mathrm{Ldim}}(H)\ge s`$. The finite-rank theorem gives the conjectured scaling when $`r=O(d)`$. A Polish parameter space for $`H`$ with Borel evaluation can have a perfect product closure, so a finite rank bound is an additional hypothesis.
