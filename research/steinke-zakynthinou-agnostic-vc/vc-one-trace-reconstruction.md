# Measurable one-feature reconstruction for jointly Borel VC-one classes

Let $`X`$ and $`H`$ be standard Borel spaces and let evaluation $`H\times X\to\{0,1\}`$ be Borel. Assume the class has VC dimension one. There is a countable family of universally measurable proper reconstruction maps using one observed feature, together with a fixed hypothesis, that realize every feasible trace on every finite sample.

Consequently, the [local reconstruction theorem](measurable-local-reconstruction.md) gives one proper learner with empirical excess at most $`583/\sqrt n`$ on every sample and whole-output iid CMI at most $`197{,}152`$ nats for every iid law, using completed measurability. These conclusions hold in ZFC.

## Ordering the sections containing an anchor

Fix $`h_0\in H`$ and put $`R_x=\{h:h(x)\ne h_0(x)\}`$. The nonempty disagreement sections form a laminar family. If two intersecting sections were not nested, the intersection, the two differences, and $`h_0`$ would realize all four traces in disagreement coordinates. For a fixed $`a\in H`$, the sections containing $`a`$ therefore form a chain.

There is one Borel map $`F:D\to 2^\alpha`$ for some countable ordinal $`\alpha`$, where $`D=\{(a,y):a\in R_y\}`$, such that for sections containing $`a`$,

```math
R_y\subsetneq R_z \quad\Longrightarrow\quad F(a,y)<_{\mathrm{lex}} F(a,z).
```

To construct it, order $`D`$ first by a Borel linear order on $`a`$, obtained from a Borel embedding of $`H`$ into the reals. Within a single $`a`$, order by inclusion of sections. This relation $`\preceq`$ is a coanalytic linear quasi-order on the Borel space $`D`$. The complement of inclusion is analytic since failure is witnessed by a hypothesis in the set difference. The strict part of $`\preceq`$ is analytic by totality.

For clarity, the Borel refinement used here follows from analytic separation. Start by separating the strict part of $`\preceq`$ from the complement of $`\preceq`$ by a Borel relation $`B_0`$ contained in $`\preceq`$. Given such a $`B_i`$, adjoin the pairs incomparable for $`B_i`$, which must be equivalent for $`\preceq`$. The reflexive transitive hull is analytic by finite paths, remains contained in $`\preceq`$, and is total. Separate this hull from the complement of $`\preceq`$ to obtain $`B_{i+1}`$. The increasing union of the $`B_i`$ is a Borel linear quasi-order with every strict comparison of $`\preceq`$ still strict. The Borel linearization theorem of Harrington, Marker and Shelah gives an order-preserving map from this Borel order to the lexicographic order on $`2^\alpha`$, preserving strict comparisons. This proves the required property of $`F`$. [Borel refinement in the proof of Corollary 6](https://www.impan.pl/shop/publication/transaction/download/product/91557) · [Borel linearization, Theorem 3.1](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95596/215.pdf)

We will use the following uniform consequence. Let an analytic relation have sections $`Y_p\subseteq X`$ parametrized by a standard Borel space, and let $`f(p,y)`$ be a Borel map into $`2^\alpha`$ defined on those pairs, or on a Borel superset of them. There is a universally measurable list of elements of $`Y_p`$, allowing empty entries, whose $`f`$-values are cofinal in the set of all $`f`$-values on $`Y_p`$. To see this, construct $`b(p)\in 2^\alpha`$ by recursion, setting the bit at $`\beta`$ to one exactly when a value on $`Y_p`$ extends the previous prefix with a one at $`\beta`$. With the prefix as an extra parameter, the existence test is analytic. Each prefix is universally measurable by countable transfinite recursion. For every one bit choose a witness for that prefix and bit, and also choose a witness realizing $`b(p)`$ if one exists. Jankov–von Neumann applied with the prefixes as separate parameters makes each witness universally measurable. Every $`f(p,y)\ne b(p)`$ first differs at a one bit of $`b(p)`$ and has a zero there, so the witness for that bit has strictly larger value. The witness for equality covers the remaining possibility. This argument also works for empty $`Y_p`$. Here and below, universal measurability is preserved by composition between standard Borel spaces. Under any given Borel probability the first map has a Borel version, and its distribution can be used to take a Borel version of the second map. [Analytic selection, Kechris, Theorem 18.1](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4612-4190-4)

## Selecting representatives in a section

Let $`V=R_x`$ be nonempty. Declare two points in $`V`$ equivalent if they are equal or they belong to a common section strictly contained in $`V`$. Transitivity follows from laminarity. Each nonempty proper subsection of $`V`$ is contained in one of its equivalence classes.

Fix an anchor $`a\in V`$. The condition on $`(x,a,y)`$ that $`a\in R_y\subsetneq V`$ is analytic on $`a\in R_x`$. Indeed, once $`a\in R_y`$ the containment is strict in the correct direction exactly when some point belongs to $`R_x\setminus R_y`$, by laminarity. Apply the preceding measurable cofinal construction with parameter $`(x,a)`$ and values $`F(a,y)`$. This gives a list $`(y_j)_j`$ of features (allowing empty entries) whose sections are cofinal under inclusion among the proper subsections through $`a`$. In fact, those sections are cofinal among all the proper subsections contained in the class of $`a`$. If such a subsection $`L`$ lacks $`a`$, choose any $`b\in L`$. A proper subsection containing $`a,b`$ then contains $`L`$ by laminarity. The class of $`a`$ now has the explicit representation

```math
C(x,a)=\{a\}\ \cup\! \bigcup_{j:\,y_j\text{ present}} R_{y_j}.
```

Choose representatives at $`V`$ as follows.

1. Choose a sequence of anchors by choosing an arbitrary point of $`V`$ outside all classes of earlier anchors. Use the empty entry if there is no such point. Include all present anchors among the representatives. Compute the cofinal lists above for all present anchors.
2. For each subsection $`R_{y_j}`$ of an anchor $`a`$, also choose a point of $`C(x,a)\setminus R_{y_j}`$ if one exists, and add it to the representatives. Create a child with feature $`y_j`$ for every present subsection in every anchor list.
3. Repeat at all children. Use the same rule at every node of this countably branching tree.

These choices use analytic selection. In step 1, pass the present anchors and their cofinal lists as extra parameters, so membership in any of their classes is expressed by the displayed countable Borel condition. The candidate relation for the next anchor is Borel in those parameters and $`x`$. In step 2, use the same representation of $`C(x,a)`$ and include $`y_j`$ as a parameter in a Borel difference test. All empty entries are handled by adjoining an isolated symbol to the relevant standard Borel space. For each anchor or representative index and each finite address in the tree, the output depends universally measurably on the original root feature by composition. The collection of all these indexes is countable. Turn absent representatives into $`h_0`$, also using $`h_0`$ when the original root is empty.

We prove that, for any root $`U=R_x\ne\varnothing`$, its tree outputs a point in every nonempty residual $`U\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^t L_i`$, where the $`L_i`$ are nonempty proper subsections of $`U`$. Fix a point $`h`$ in that residual. Suppose all chosen representatives are covered by the forbidden $`L_i`$. At any node $`V`$ containing $`h`$, a forbidden section meeting $`V`$ is strictly contained in $`V`$, by laminarity and because it does not contain $`h`$. It lies in just one class of $`V`$. If infinitely many anchors were chosen here, the finitely many forbidden sections could not cover all of them. Thus there are finitely many classes, and each has its anchor.

Let $`C`$ be the class containing $`h`$. If no forbidden section lies in $`C`$, its anchor escapes. Otherwise the finitely many forbidden sections in $`C`$ are covered by a single subsection $`R_{y_j}`$ from that anchor's cofinal list. This follows from cofinality and the fact that sections through the anchor are nested. If $`C\setminus R_{y_j}`$ is nonempty, step 2 supplies an escaping representative. The only remaining possibility is $`C=R_{y_j}`$, in which case we descend to this child containing $`h`$. Since the child is strictly inside $`V`$, an anchor in another class of $`V`$ must be covered by a forbidden section in that other class. That section is disjoint from the child. Each descent uses a distinct forbidden index in this way, so after at most $`t`$ descents the assumption that every representative is covered gives a contradiction.

## Exact reconstruction and the rates

For a feasible finite trace, express its labels in disagreement coordinates relative to $`h_0`$. The all-zero trace is realized by $`h_0`$. If the trace has positive features, their sections contain a common realizing hypothesis, so they are nested. Choose a feature $`x`$ with a smallest positive section $`R_x`$. Every hypothesis in it matches all positive coordinates. A negative feature's section is disjoint from $`R_x`$ or properly contained in it. Otherwise that section would contain every possible realization. The tree for $`x`$ supplies a hypothesis avoiding the finitely many forbidden subsections, so it realizes the trace. Apply the local reconstruction theorem with $`k=d=1`$ to get the asserted bounds. The proof of laminarity uses VC dimension one. The construction leaves the general finite-VC completed-iid problem open.
