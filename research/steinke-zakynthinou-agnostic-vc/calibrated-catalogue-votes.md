# Calibrating catalogue information to the original empirical rate

For every jointly Borel VC class of dimension $`d\ge1`$ on standard Borel spaces, one distribution-independent improper learner attains the empirical excess $`O(\sqrt{d/n})`$ on every sample, with whole-output CMI bounded by a function of $`d`$ alone. More precisely, if $`r=\max(1,\mathop{\mathrm{VC}}(H^*))`$ for the dual class, the information bound is $`O(d r^2\log^4(er))`$. It holds on every fixed paired pool and for actual iid CMI, even when the returned output is the entire ordered tuple of hypotheses comprising the vote. For bounded dual VC dimension the bound is linear in $`d`$. General dual VC dimension can be exponential in $`d`$, and the vote can be improper. [Scope of Conjecture 6 and its bounty](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v125/steinke20b/steinke20b.pdf)

A [measurable high-agreement projection](majority-projection.md) gives a proper catalogue and avoids charging the VC dimension of the vote class. Exact pointwise projection into the given proper name space has a separate descriptive-set-theoretic obstruction.

## A catalogue bound with separate accuracy and trace dimensions

Consider a local catalogue of a jointly Borel class with VC dimension at most $`D`$, generated by countably many universally measurable proper reconstruction maps reading at most $`k`$ observed features. Include a fixed candidate. For any $`1\le d\le D`$ and $`n\ge d`$ one can attain empirical excess $`O(\sqrt{d/n})`$ relative to the catalogue optimum, with information

```math
O\!\left(k+\frac{D^2}{d}\bigl(1+\log(D/d)\bigr)^2\right).
\tag{1}
```

Use the [hidden-mark catalogue schedule](hidden-restriction-marks.md#schedule-and-weighted-loss-stability) with $`D`$ as the trace dimension, but set its first temperature to $`b_0=\sqrt{nd}/D`$. Scores are ordinary mistake counts. Write $`t=n/D`$ and $`L=1+\log t`$ when $`n\ge D`$. When $`L\ge100`$, the terminal temperature is $`\tau=b_0/L`$ and the other temperatures and mark probabilities have the same ratios as before. When $`L\lt100`$, the only temperature is $`\tau=b_0`$. Use a measurable catalogue ERM if $`n\lt D`$ or $`\tau\lt1`$. Its CMI is at most $`n\log2`$, which is within (1) in these parameter ranges. In detail, $`n\lt D`$ suffices directly, and for $`n\ge D`$ the condition $`\tau\lt1`$ implies $`t<(D/d)(1+\log t)^2`$. If $`L\ge16`$, then $`2\log L\le L/2`$, so taking logarithms gives $`L\le2(1+\log(D/d))`$. When $`L\lt16`$, use $`L\le16(1+\log(D/d))`$ instead. Substitution bounds $`n`$ as required.

In the entropy learner the expected bonus is $`O(D b_0)=O(\sqrt{nd})`$, by the [same Sauer bounds](hidden-restriction-marks.md#schedule-and-weighted-loss-stability). Comparing to a point mass on a catalogue ERM gives the stated empirical accuracy directly.

### Score deletion with ordinary mistake counts

Fix the marks and let $`q`$ be the full trace optimizer. Keep the full support and partitions, and let $`q_{i,s}`$ be the law obtained by subtracting $`s\ell_i`$ from the trace costs, for $`0\le s\le1`$, where $`\ell_i`$ is the binary loss on row $`i`$. Thus $`q_{i,0}=q`$ and $`q_{i,1}=r_i`$ in the notation of the hidden-mark proof. Write $`\delta_v`$ for the change of value in a subtree at $`v`$. We have $`-s\le\delta_v\le0`$ from monotonicity and translation invariance of the soft minimum. On a path $`v_0,\ldots,v_m`$, with split temperature $`\beta_j`$ at $`v_j`$, multiplying conditional probability ratios gives the log ratio

```math
\frac{\delta_{v_0}}{\beta_0}
+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\delta_{v_j}
 \left(\frac1{\beta_j}-\frac1{\beta_{j-1}}\right)
-\frac{\delta_{v_m}}{\beta_{m-1}}.
```

All terms except the last are nonpositive. Hence $`q_{i,s}\le e^{s/\tau}q\le e q`$ pointwise, since this branch uses $`\tau\ge1`$.

Differentiating the soft minima gives the subtree derivative $`-\mathbb E_{q_{i,s}}[\ell_i\mid\text{subtree}]`$. The log-probability derivative is therefore the sum of the increments of these conditional means divided by their split temperatures. The increments are orthogonal in $`L^2(q_{i,s})`$ by the nesting. Four times the squared speed of the square-root law is the sum of the squared norms of the increments divided by the squared temperatures. For a band after partition $`\mathcal F_j`$, its squared increment norm is at most

```math
\inf_{g\ \mathcal F_j\text{-measurable}}\mathbb E_{q_{i,s}}(\ell_i-g)^2
\le e\,\mathbb E_q\bigl(\ell_i-\mathbb E_q[\ell_i\mid\mathcal F_j]\bigr)^2 .
```

In the final expression the law $`q`$ is common to all $`i`$. Summing these residual variances over the rows gives at most $`n\Delta(M_j)`$, with $`\Delta(M_j)`$ the maximum disagreement fraction within a trace cell. This follows by taking two independent conditional draws in each cell and counting the coordinates where their predictions disagree. The first band contributes at most $`n/(4b_0^2)`$ to the sum of band variances divided by squared temperatures. Integrating the squared speed and applying the square-root triangle and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities bounds squared Hellinger distance. The [same restriction-diameter bounds and summation](hidden-restriction-marks.md#schedule-and-weighted-loss-stability), scaled by the new $`b_0`$, now give

```math
\sum_i\mathbb E_{\rm marks}H_2^2(q,r_i)
=O(n/b_0^2)=O(D^2/d).
\tag{2}
```

### Restriction marks, pruning and names

The temperature ratios and mark probabilities in the [hidden-mark comparison](hidden-restriction-marks.md#summing-the-hidden-mark-comparisons) are unaffected by the rescaling. Put $`B=D^2/d\ge D`$. At each level the summed minority-mass budget is $`O(D+B)=O(B)`$, by the weighted edge bound on the common full projected law and (2). Jensen's inequality in the mark comparison gives $`O((D u_\ell)^{\gamma_\ell}B^{1-\gamma_\ell})\le O(Bu_\ell^{1/2})`$. The original exponential factors, depending only on temperature ratios, still sum this to $`O(B)`$. Thus the full law averaged over the deleted row's mark differs from the zero-score, omitted-mark law by summed expected squared Hellinger distance $`O(B)`$.

The [double-fiber and support comparisons](hidden-restriction-marks.md#removing-the-row-unavailable-traces-and-names) now have budgets $`O(D+B)`$ for the double fibers and $`O(k+B)`$ for witnesses using the deleted row. The terminal entropy bonus comparison and the nested pruning comparison have the same constants. Lifting to first-witness proper names and applying the selector transfer gives $`O(B+k)`$ information on this branch, which completes (1). Evaluation and witness feasibility use the same completed measurability as the original catalogue theorem.

## A measurable catalogue of exact votes

Fix a base consistency map on each finite labeled tuple of length at most $`s=C_0d`$. On every realizable tuple choose a proper consistent hypothesis by analytic uniformization, using fixed defaults otherwise. The universal constant $`C_0`$ is large enough for the usual VC estimate at constant accuracy for consistent learners on a finite distribution. The maps are universally measurable. In catalogue maps the labels can be passed as finite side information, so the maps read only features.

We give the [Moran–Yehudayoff majority argument](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1503.06960) with these base maps. Fix a finite feature tuple and a target trace realized by $`H`$. For every distribution on its positions, there is a subtuple of at most $`s`$ positions labeled by the target whose base-map output makes at most one third error for that distribution. Indeed, iid sampling from the positions gives an agreement net at constant accuracy with positive probability by the VC estimate. Drop duplicate positions before applying the consistency map. The finite minimax theorem applied to all such base-map outputs supplies a mixture with probability of correct prediction at least two thirds at every position. On the finite set of outputs, the family of correctness tests at sample positions is a subfamily of the dual tests and their complements. Adding complements at most doubles the growth function, so its VC dimension is $`O(r)`$. A sample of an odd length $`a=O(r)`$ from the mixture approximates all those correctness probabilities to within one eighth with positive probability, by the fixed-accuracy VC estimate. Its majority realizes the entire target trace. Choose a fixed odd $`a`$ large enough in the uniform bound.

Use the class of $`a`$-term majority votes, represented by the standard Borel output space $`H^a`$ with jointly Borel majority evaluation. The catalogue takes all votes of base-map outputs on the permitted subtuples with all possible proposed labels. A vote reads at most $`k=as=O(dr)`$ observed features. A map on the union of the input positions can use its side information to reconstruct the ordered constituent subtuples and their labels. All such choices form a countable collection of universally measurable maps. The argument above gives a vote for every feasible trace of the original class, so the catalogue optimum is at most the original optimum for any labeling.

The vote class has dimension $`D=O(d a\log(ea))`$. On $`m`$ positions its trace count is at most the original trace count raised to $`a`$; the Sauer bound and $`2^m\le(em/d)^{da}`$ for any shattered $`m\ge d`$ give the estimate. Apply (1) with this enlarged trace dimension and the original $`d`$ for the accuracy. The information bound simplifies to $`O(dr^2\log^4(er))`$ and does not depend on $`n`$. The bound is for the full tuple name, so it also controls any measurable readout of the returned classifier. A uniform bound $`r\le 2^{d+1}`$ follows from dual shattering: shattering $`2^{d+1}`$ hypotheses and indexing them by bit vectors of length $`d+1`$ would produce $`d+1`$ features shattered in the original class.
